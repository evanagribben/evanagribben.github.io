The Lead

Arteta Rolls the Dice for Bruno — and Redraws the Champions’ Summer

With the Premier League trophy in the cabinet for the first time in twenty-two years, Arsenal are chasing the one profile they still lack: a ball-carrying, tone-setting midfield general. Bruno Guimarães is that man — and the pursuit has become the through-line of the entire window.

Champions are supposed to enjoy their summers. Instead, Arsenal have spent theirs in a state of restless ambition. The 2025/26 title — sealed with an authority that ended a generation of near-misses — did not soften Mikel Arteta’s appetite so much as sharpen it. The message from the manager’s office this month has been unambiguous: a first crown in twenty-two years is a floor, not a ceiling. And the clearest expression of that ambition is the name that has dominated every Arsenal back page this week — Bruno Guimarães.

The interest is real, advanced, and unusually public. Fabrizio Romano reported on July 23 that Arsenal have reached an understanding with the player on personal terms, framing Guimarães as keen on a move to north London and a manager “pushing like crazy” to land him. The obstacle is the one that always governs these sagas: the fee. Newcastle United value their captain in the region of £80m–£100m and have insisted, loudly, that he is not for sale — Sky Sports’ Keith Downie reported the Tyneside club were “frustrated” that Arsenal had probed the terms of a deal through intermediaries rather than lodging a formal offer, and had asked, in effect, whether the Gunners were serious.

They are. Reporting across the week — Romano and The Times among them — converged on Arsenal readying a formal bid of around £70m, rising toward the €82m mark, with the expectation that a first offer would be rebuffed and used chiefly to “move negotiations much closer.” This is how the biggest deals begin: an agreement with the player, a valuation gulf with the selling club, and a war of nerves over who blinks. Chelsea, per Football365, have been floated as a possible gate-crasher, though nothing there has hardened. For now, treat this as advanced interest with personal terms in hand and a fee still to be bridged — not a done deal, and emphatically not a “here we go.”

“A first crown in twenty-two years is a floor, not a ceiling. The champions want the one profile they still don’t own.”

Why Guimarães, and why now? On paper Arsenal’s engine room is a strength, not a need. Declan Rice is among the world’s best all-rounders, Martin Zubimendi gave the base a positional intelligence that underpinned the title, and Mikel Merino offers goals and physicality from deep. But listen to what Arteta’s side lacked in the tightest games: a midfielder who can take the ball under pressure, drive through the lines, and change a match’s tempo on his own initiative. That is precisely Guimarães’s signature — a press-resistant carrier who defends the middle and dictates the front foot. In a squad built to control games, he is the accelerator.

There is a squad-management logic, too. Arsenal are back in Champions League Pot 1 as English champions, with a league-phase campaign that will again stretch the group to breaking point across the winter. A midfield of Rice, Zubimendi, Merino and a fit-again Martin Ødegaard is formidable; a midfield that adds Guimarães is one that can rotate without dropping a decibel of intensity. That is the difference between winning a title and defending one across fifty-plus games.

It is not without risk. A fee north of £70m for a midfielder turning twenty-nine, in a market where Arsenal have already been priced out of one marquee target (more on Morgan Rogers below), invites the obvious question of value. Sporting director Andrea Berta has spent the summer balancing ambition against Profit and Sustainability headroom, and a splash here likely accelerates a sale elsewhere — the club are known to be open to moving Gabriel Martinelli as part of a wider reshape. The pursuit of Guimarães is therefore not a single transfer but the first domino in a summer of them.

What happens next is a test of nerve on both sides. Newcastle do not want to sell and are under no financial compulsion to; Arsenal know that once a captain has told his club he wants to leave, the ground shifts. The likeliest path is a rejected opening bid, a public standoff, and a resolution — one way or the other — only as the window tightens in August. It is the defining thread of the champions’ summer, and every other piece of Arsenal business is being read through it.

The Saliba Blow: Champions Lose Their Cornerstone for ‘an Extended Period’

A back injury suffered on World Cup duty leaves Arteta rebuilding his defence before a ball has been kicked — and reframes Arsenal’s summer priorities.

If the Guimarães chase is the summer’s ambition, the injury to William Saliba is its complication. The France centre-half hurt his back in the World Cup semi-final against Spain on July 14, withdrawn inside the opening half hour. On July 22, Arsenal confirmed the outcome the club had feared: surgery is not recommended, but Saliba will undergo a “managed recovery programme” and be out “for an extended period,” with no timeline attached. Estimates of four-to-five months have circulated in the press; the club has pointedly declined to put a date on it, and this Digest will not either until one is confirmed.

The consequences ripple outward. Saliba is not merely a very good defender; he is the axis around which Arteta’s back line is organised. His absence thrusts responsibility onto Gabriel Magalhães, the returning Ben White (who reported for pre-season declaring his troublesome knee “all good”), summer buy Piero Hincapié, and the versatile Cristhian Mosquera and Jurriën Timber, the latter still easing back from a groin problem. It also explains the flurry of centre-back inquiries this week — Real Sociedad’s Jon Martín and Como’s Jacobo Ramón among them — as Berta weighs whether cover has quietly become a need. For a side built on the meanest defence in the country, losing its cornerstone before August is the sort of blow that shapes a season.

 

Transfer News

Tzolis Lands: Greece Winger Signs for £34m as the Trossard Replacement

Status: OFFICIAL · Five-year deal · No. 17

The headline arrival of the week is Christos Tzolis, the 24-year-old Greece international, signed from Club Brugge for a reported £34m on a five-year contract and handed the No. 17 shirt. A left-sided forward comfortable across the front line, Tzolis leaves Bruges as a Belgian champion and their record sale after a season of 22 goals and 29 assists that earned him Belgian Footballer of the Year honours. Berta framed him as “an extremely versatile attacking player who naturally operates on the left but is comfortable across the front line” — the direct replacement for Leandro Trossard, sold to Beşiktaş. He is a rotation-and-depth signing rather than the marquee wide addition Arsenal are still hunting, but a shrewd one at the price.

A Cross-London Coup: Elijah Upson Joins from Spurs

Status: OFFICIAL · Free transfer · No. 57

Few deals sting a rival like signing their brightest teenager. Elijah Upson, an 18-year-old centre-half and the son of former Gunner Matthew Upson, joined from Tottenham on a free after declining a new contract at his boyhood club. Romano called it a “here we go” on July 18; Arsenal confirmed it on July 21–22. An England youth international who was a fixture in Spurs’ U18 and UEFA Youth League sides, Upson chose Arsenal over reported interest from Chelsea, Manchester City, Bayern Munich and others — a genuine statement capture for Hale End.

The Wide Search: Nico Williams Revived, Vinícius Explored

Status: Concrete interest (Williams) · Preliminary/exploratory (Vinícius)

Having lost their No. 1 forward target (see below), Arsenal have turned back to Nico Williams. Berta is reported to have reopened dialogue over the Athletic Club winger, whose release clause sits around £77m — a figure Arsenal are said to be capable of meeting, with Liverpool also monitoring. Treat this as concrete interest at the early-talks stage; the sourcing is a rung below Tier-1 and no fee has been triggered.

The eye-catcher, broken by David Ornstein and James McNicholas on July 25, is Vinícius Júnior. The reporting is careful and should be read carefully: the idea of pursuing the Real Madrid forward — whose deal expires in 2027 — has “approval at all levels” inside Arsenal, but no bid has been made and no negotiations have begun. This is a monitoring exercise contingent on Madrid’s renewal talks stalling, not a live transfer. Any aggregator headline claiming Arsenal have “launched a bid” is overstating what Ornstein actually reported.

The One That Got Away: Rogers Goes to Chelsea for a British Record

Status: DEAD for Arsenal

Arsenal’s stated No. 1 forward target for the summer, Morgan Rogers, is Chelsea-bound. Chelsea agreed a British-record deal reported at £117m with Aston Villa, with a medical set to follow the England man’s World Cup exertions. Sky’s Kaveh Solhekol framed it as Arsenal being priced out rather than hijacked — the Villa asking price had climbed toward £130m and the Gunners declined to match. Either way, the miss is what set the rest of Arsenal’s attacking business (Tzolis, Williams, the Vinícius musing) in motion.

The Rumour Mill

The smaller links and simmering sagas — certainty labelled.

Martinelli, the exit to watch. Arsenal are reported open to selling Gabriel Martinelli at around £38m as part of the wide-forward reshape, with several unnamed suitors circling. Mid-sourced and unconfirmed — no Tier-1 verification — but consistent with the club funding a marquee arrival.

Centre-back cover. With Saliba out, Arsenal have inquired about Real Sociedad’s Jon Martín (20, ~€60m clause; the player has just been offered fresh terms and prefers to stay) and Como’s Jacobo Ramón (complicated by a Real Madrid buy-back). Both at the low-certainty inquiry stage.

Barcola, cooled. Ornstein reported PSG do not want to sell Bradley Barcola and would demand a fee north of the £116m City paid for Elliot Anderson; Arsenal have made no fresh contact. Stalled.

Diomande, an opening? RB Leipzig’s Yan Diomande has agreed terms with PSG, but with the clubs apart on a fee (Leipzig want €120m+), German outlet Bild frames a possible opening. Speculative and second-hand for Arsenal.

Nwaneri, murky. Conflicting reports: some list Ethan Nwaneri among players who could depart seeking guarantees of minutes; others insist he is tied to a long-term deal. Unresolved — his pre-season will tell.

 

The Squad

A Staggered Return, a Title to Defend

Pre-season has begun in fits and starts, dictated by a World Cup that ran deep into July. The first wave reported to London Colney around July 20 — Myles Lewis-Skelly, Riccardo Calafiori, Cristhian Mosquera, Gabriel Jesus, Reiss Nelson, Ben White and Jurriën Timber among them — while the tournament’s protagonists filter back over the coming fortnight. Viktor Gyökeres, Kai Havertz, Martin Ødegaard, Gabriel and the Spain and England contingents are being reintegrated on individual schedules.

The tone from White was encouraging — knee “all good” after a summer of daily training — while Timber offered the more measured “I’m getting there slowly” on his groin. Ødegaard remains club captain; the low-grade tabloid noise about an armband change carries no credible sourcing and is not treated as a story here. The genuine tactical question of the summer is not formation but personnel: how Arteta reshuffles his defence around Saliba’s absence, and whether a new midfield dimension in Guimarães changes the team’s centre of gravity. Note, too, a housekeeping point on fitness management — Bukayo Saka, who has managed Achilles tendinopathy, will have his pre-season minutes handled with care after a heavy international summer.

On the field, the week’s only action was a behind-closed-doors friendly at London Colney, reportedly against Norwich City — no public lineup or score. The first open test comes at Girona on August 1. (For the record: the “Arsenal 3-0 Athletic” Emirates Cup result circulating in some feeds is from the summer of 2025, not this window, and has no bearing on 2026 pre-season.)

Hale End

The Kids Come Back: Boreham Wood, a Coup, and a Loan on Fire

The young Gunners were first back to work, opening pre-season with a 1-1 draw at Boreham Wood on July 17, Bowen Phillips lashing home a late equaliser. The U21s are under interim charge of David Horseman after Max Porter left for Stoke City, with U18 coach Adam Birchall assisting; first-year scholar Hakeem Abubakar, plus Teshaun Murisa and Ife Ibrahim, caught the eye.

Off the pitch it has been a bumper week for recruitment and retention. Beyond the Elijah Upson capture from Tottenham, Arsenal secured the commitment of Emerson Nwaneri — the younger brother of Ethan — who agreed a scholarship plus a pre-arranged first professional deal, snubbing Chelsea and Manchester City (per Sam Dean of The Telegraph). Left-sided defender Josh Ogunnaike also signed pro terms, correcting earlier reports that he had declined. Winger Alex Marciniak’s contract, by contrast, remains under negotiation.

The name to watch remains Max Dowman, the 16-year-old attacker who trained with the seniors rather than the U21s — a telling sign he is now viewed as a first-team asset, particularly with Saka’s minutes to be managed. And the standout loan story is Charles Sagoe Jr at Kalmar FF in Sweden, who is in blistering form — two assists in a 3-0 win over Örgryte and another in a 2-2 draw with Malmö on July 21, taking him to a reported two goals and eight assists on the season. Swedish outlet Expressen reports AIK want to make the move permanent — unconfirmed, but one to track given his 2027 contract expiry.

 

Arsenal Women

Slegers’ Great Rebuild: Seven In, Six Out, and a Statement of Intent

A response to finishing third: the Champions League winners of 2025 are reloading with proven internationals.

After a 2025/26 in which Manchester City ended Chelsea’s six-year reign and Arsenal came home third in the WSL, Renée Slegers has overseen one of the most aggressive squad overhauls in the women’s game. The incomings read like a European all-star team: England’s Georgia Stanway from Bayern Munich; Spain World Cup winner Ona Batlle from Barcelona; Switzerland’s Géraldine Reuteler from Eintracht Frankfurt; Selina Cerci from Hoffenheim; young winger Lisa Baum from RB Leipzig; and two goalkeepers — Spain international Misa Rodríguez (a free from Real Madrid, confirmed July 22) and Danish teenager Isabella Damm.

The departures are just as eye-catching: Katie McCabe, Beth Mead (to Manchester City), Laia Codina, Victoria Pelova, Manuela Zinsberger and Naomi Williams have all moved on, with Rosa Kafaji sold to London City Lionesses. The goalkeeper churn is deliberate: with Daphne van Domselaar recovering from summer knee surgery (timeline unknown), Arsenal have stocked the position rather than gamble on fitness. This week’s outgoing news was the season-long loan of 19-year-old forward Jessie Gale to Newcastle (WSL2), where she links up with Jordan Nobbs.

A storyline to hold onto: Michelle Agyemang, the teenage England breakout, is rehabbing an ACL rupture, her season a question mark. Slegers, secure on a contract reported through 2029 and captained by Leah Williamson, has the tools to reassert Arsenal at the top. The WSL is expected to kick off around September 5–6, with a Champions League league-phase campaign to come; a pre-season training camp in Spain is under way. The exact opening-weekend fixtures were not yet confirmed at the time of writing.

 

Gunners Abroad

World Champions in Red & White — and a Saka Hat-Trick

The 2026 World Cup ended on July 19 with Spain beating Argentina 1-0 after extra time, Ferran Torres settling it in the 105th minute at MetLife Stadium — and three Gunners came home world champions. Mikel Merino was outstanding, scoring twice across the run; Martín Zubimendi was a squad contributor; and David Raya collected a winner’s medal as understudy to Unai Simón, who kept goal throughout and won the Golden Glove. Spain’s Rodri took the Golden Ball; no Arsenal player won an individual award.

For England, third place carried an unmistakably Arsenal signature: Bukayo Saka struck a hat-trick in a wild 6-4 win over France in the bronze-medal match, having been controversially benched in the semi-final. Declan Rice logged the most minutes of any Gunner. The France camp, meanwhile, is where the summer’s bad news originated: William Saliba’s tournament ended with the back injury detailed above. Elsewhere, Martin Ødegaard (Norway) recorded three assists, Gabriel (Brazil) one, Kai Havertz (Germany) three goals, and departing Leandro Trossard (Belgium) two — figures drawn from a single aggregated source and best treated as indicative. On the women’s side there is no international tournament this summer, so Arsenal’s internationals are in club pre-season rather than national colours.
