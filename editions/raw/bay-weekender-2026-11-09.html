At a Glance

Five weeks, two wet weekends, and the first playoff night PayPal Park has ever had

This edition covers five weeks instead of two, because The Bay Weekender now arrives every other Monday. The practical effect: everything from tonight's Warriors game to the Geminids on December 13 is in here, sharpest across the next three weeks.

Three things anchor it. The Golden State Valkyries won the WNBA championship at Chase Center on October 31, in only their second season. The Earthquakes host a playoff match at PayPal Park for the first time in the stadium's history on November 24. And the holiday market season starts in earnest, with something free on almost every weekend from here to Christmas.

We have also cut every event description in half and hidden the detail behind a disclosure arrow. Readers told us the old editions read like homework. The hook is now the whole collapsed state. Open only what you want.

Pick of the Month

The first playoff night in PayPal Park history

San Jose Earthquakes vs Houston Dynamo, Round One Game 1. Tuesday, November 24, 7pm, PayPal Park, 1123 Coleman Avenue, San Jose.

The reason is simple: it has never happened. PayPal Park opened in 2015, and the club confirms this is the first home playoff game the stadium has ever hosted. The Earthquakes reached the playoffs in that span and every time the bracket sent them somewhere else.

The match itself is worth the trip on merit. San Jose finished fourth in the West on 52 points and drew fifth-seeded Houston, which is as close to an even series as Round One produces. It is best-of-three: Game 1 in San Jose, Game 2 in Houston on November 28, and Game 3 back at PayPal Park on December 2 if the first two split.

Practical notes. Tickets for both possible home games are on sale now through Tixr, there is a stadium-wide playoff T-shirt giveaway at Game 1, and the stands have partial roof cover. From San Francisco it is a Caltrain ride plus a connection, which on a Tuesday night means planning the return leg before you go.

Food, Drink and Festivals

Markets almost every weekend from here to Christmas, a pay-what-you-can taco day, and the restaurant news that actually matters.

Opening soon and just opened

Five places worth knowing are already serving. Shinka, chef Funio Yonezawa's modern Japanese room with French technique, is on the ground floor of The Jay at 333 Battery Street. Athanor, from chef Mike Lanham, runs a ten-course fermentation-led tasting menu at 2600 Sutter Street in Pacific Heights.

Thai chef Thitid Tassanakajohn's Saam has opened at 415 Brannan Street in SoMa. The Mess Hall at 201 Halleck Street in the Presidio is a three-restaurant food hall with a raw bar. And chef Susan Dunn's Esme, a French bistro, is inside the Metro Hotel at 311 Divisadero Street.

Announced, but no confirmed date

Four names to watch, none with a date we would put in a calendar. Sutro Hall takes the old Mourad space at 140 New Montgomery Street. Monami, a Korean steakhouse from the Ssal team, goes into the former Noosh at 2001 Fillmore Street.

Jupiter Room, a martini bar, is bound for 2065 Polk Street, and Bayview Oyster Bar at 5114 Third Street comes from the chef behind the closed Auntie April's. Restaurant opening dates slip constantly. Treat all four as fall or winter intentions rather than appointments.

Trending and gone viral

The matcha queues are real, and so is the backlash

The Infatuation's SF writers clocked 25-minute lines for matcha at both Kissaten HiFi and Nagomi, and a queue running down the block at Q Specialty Coffee. Why it caught on: the drink is cheap, it photographs well, and the rooms are tiny, so a modest crowd reads as a phenomenon from the pavement.

Here is the honest part. The same writers say the drinks are not worth more than about 15 minutes of waiting, and suggest letting the crowds thin. We are reporting the queue, not endorsing it.

Walk-in only is spreading

La Cigale, the wood-fired place in Glen Park, takes no reservations and no tips. Khao Tiew has gone walk-in only too. The pitch is fairness: no insider booking advantage. The cost is that you stand outside. Arrive early or eat late.

Baklava, onigiri, and Yemeni coffee after 10pm

Two handheld formats are showing up everywhere. Turquaz carries about a dozen baklava and Cafe Velora does pistachio and chocolate cherry versions. For onigiri, The Wild Fox makes a miso salmon and Do-Re-Mi makes them to order.

Separately, Yemeni cafes have become the city's late-night answer. Delah Coffee, Haraz Coffee House and Heyma all stay open past 10pm, some until 1am at weekends, with no pressure to order a second dinner.

Up and coming

Three pop-ups are graduating to permanent addresses. La Kalidad, a Filipino bakery that began as a pop-up and farmers market stall, is taking its first bricks-and-mortar space at 680A Second Street in SoMa. Lorella, from the Octavia and Saturbake team, has 2139A Polk Street.

Al Carajo is opening a dedicated breakfast burrito counter at 683 Geary Street in Lower Nob Hill. Across the bay, the former Egg Pals pop-up becomes Egg Palace at 4101 Broadway in Temescal, Oakland. None of the four has published a firm opening date.
