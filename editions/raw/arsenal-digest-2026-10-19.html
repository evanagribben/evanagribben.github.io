The Lead

Fourteen in a row, and nothing conceded: Arsenal take their European machine to Munich

Two more wins, two more clean sheets and a 13th 1-0 in a year. On Wednesday Arteta brings the best defensive record in the Champions League to the Allianz Arena, where Harry Kane is waiting and Michael Olise is suspended.

There is a version of this season in which Arsenal are quietly frightening and nobody has quite said it out loud. They have won their last 14 Champions League league-phase matches. They have not conceded a goal in this season’s competition. They have taken 18 points from seven league games, lost once, and gone to the City Ground and won without making a single meaningful chance until the 80th minute. On Wednesday they go to Bayern Munich, which is the first proper examination of the lot.

The week was a study in two kinds of victory. Against Lille last Tuesday the game was goalless and sluggish for an hour before Arteta sent on Bukayo Saka, Martin Ødegaard and Kai Havertz together and the thing broke open in seven minutes: Jurriën Timber on 64, Eberechi Eze on 67, Saka on 71 from Havertz’s cut-back. It finished 3-0 and Lille did not have a shot on target. Olivier Giroud, 40, came on for the visitors on 78 and got the reception he deserved.

Sunday at Nottingham Forest was the other kind. Arsenal had roughly 75 per cent of the ball and generated 1.07 expected goals from it. Noni Madueke, on at half-time for Eze, scored the only goal on 80 minutes with a shot from a tight angle that deflected off Jair Cunha and past Matz Sels. It was Arsenal’s first shot on goal. Declan Rice completed 57 of 58 passes, had 113 touches and made the assist. Bruno Guimarães won 10 of 12 duels before he came off.

That is the 13th time in 366 days Arsenal have won a match 1-0. Arteta was candid about what it means and what it does not. He acknowledged the need to be more clinical, then gave the afternoon to his centre-backs: “The win was not possible without them.” Ezri Konsa, the £55m signing from Aston Villa, has now helped keep five clean sheets in nine Arsenal appearances while William Saliba is absent, and blocked Liam Delap’s shot deep into stoppage time.

What makes Wednesday awkward is that Bayern are not in the form their name implies. They drew 3-2 up and then 3-3 away at Viking in Stavanger six days ago, conceding a 90th-minute equaliser after Olise was sent off for a second yellow on 74. Harry Kane scored twice in that game, a penalty and a header, and Luis Díaz added a third. Four points from two matches is not a crisis, but it is not the serene Bayern of the group-stage era either.

The matchup itself is unusually legible. Arteta told his players to expect “96 minutes of man-to-man,” which is both a description of how Bayern defend and a warning about what Arsenal will face when they try to build. Vincent Kompany’s side press high and individually; Arsenal have spent two months being slow and tidy in first halves and dangerous in second halves. If the Munich pressure forces the game to be quick from the first whistle, that may suit Arsenal better than Lille sitting off them did.

There is a practical wrinkle in that. Man-marking across the pitch asks a side to beat its opponent one duel at a time, and Arsenal are built for it in a way they were not two years ago. Rice and Bruno Guimarães are both physical ball-carriers rather than pure circulators, Timber and Lewis-Skelly can carry out of the back, and Raya is comfortable playing the first pass long when the press is man-oriented. Against Forest on Sunday, Bruno won 10 of his 12 duels before coming off on 66 minutes, which is the kind of evening the Allianz will demand for rather longer.

The key battle is the obvious one. Kane against Konsa and Gabriel Magalhães is a test the pairing has not yet had: Arsenal’s European clean sheets this season came against Napoli and a Lille side that managed nothing on target. The second is at the other end, where Olise’s suspension removes Bayern’s most reliable creator and hands Lennart Karl a night he will remember either way.

Selection is the live question. Zubimendi is available again after the freak ankle injury in the warm-down after the Leeds United game, which gives Arteta a way to protect Rice in a week with three matches. Madueke, who was being written about as a departure a fortnight ago, has gone from five appearances and no league start to a match-winner, and is expected to start ahead of Eze. The one that will not resolve itself is the centre-forward: Viktor Gyökeres is still without an Arsenal goal this season, while Havertz keeps doing the work Arteta values and not scoring much either.

The stakes are structural rather than dramatic. The expanded league phase rewards teams who bank points before the hard fixtures arrive, and Arsenal’s remaining draw is heavy: Slavia Praha away, then Borussia Dortmund and Real Madrid at home before Christmas. Nine points from Munich would make a top-eight finish, and the bye it brings, close to a formality. A defeat would make December matter in a way nobody in north London wants it to.

None of which changes the thing that has quietly defined this season so far. Arsenal are not scoring freely. They have 11 league goals in seven matches and have not scored from a set play all season, from a side that managed a record 19 league goals from corners alone last year. They are winning anyway, because almost nobody scores against them. At some point that combination meets a team good enough to score first, and Wednesday in Munich is the likeliest candidate yet.

Ken Friar, 1934 to 2026: the boy with the tennis ball who became Arsenal’s memory

He walked into Highbury as a child in the 1940s, joined the staff in 1950 and left the board in 2020. For eight decades, if you wanted to know how Arsenal actually worked, you asked him.

Arsenal announced last Tuesday that Ken Friar OBE, the club’s life president, had died aged 92. Born in Islington on 13 August 1934, he told for the rest of his life the story of the tennis ball that rolled under a car belonging to the manager, George Allison, outside the ground. Allison asked the boy what he was doing. The boy ended up running messages for the box office and joined the permanent staff in 1950.

He became club secretary in 1973 and managing director in 1983, a job he held until 2000 and which in that era meant very nearly everything: player contracts, manager contracts, the relationship with supporters, the relationship with Islington Council. He then spent two decades as the club’s institutional memory, working with Danny Fiszman on the move from Highbury to the Emirates. He stepped down from the board in September 2020 and was made life president, having received an OBE for services to football in 2000 and the Freedom of the Borough of Islington in 2004.

The span is hard to overstate. Friar was at Arsenal for the Compton brothers, for Bertie Mee, for George Graham, for Arsene Wenger and for Arteta, and saw 11 league titles, the most recent in May. A bridge at the Emirates carries his name, with a statue at the foot of it of a small boy playing football outside Highbury.

The club’s statement said he “will never be forgotten by all those who knew him,” and dwelt less on the titles than on the contract negotiations, the council meetings and the stories he told the staff. His own summary, given to Nick Ames, was characteristically unbothered by grandeur: “It is the people that make the Club.” He said he kept going “because I love the place and the people.” His wife Gwendoline, whom he married in 1957, died in December 2020.

Transfer News

The Alvarez thread acquires an agent, and a complication

Arsenal were said to have overtaken Barcelona on Monday. By Saturday the Spanish press had them worried again. Status: reported interest, no bid, no agreement.

The Julián Álvarez saga had a strange week. On 12 October Football365 reported that Arsenal had moved ahead of Barcelona in the race for the Atlético Madrid forward after he parted company with his agent, Fernando Hidalgo. By 17 October the same outlet was carrying a report from Sport that Álvarez is close to signing with Pablo Barquero, the agent who handled Rodri’s move to Barcelona, and that the connection has caused concern inside Arsenal.

Treat all of it as second-hand. Neither club, nor the player, nor either agent has confirmed anything, and the underlying position has not changed since September: Arsenal want a forward, Álvarez has not started a La Liga match this season, and the two clubs have talked. What has changed is the noise level, which in a window still ten weeks away is not the same as momentum.

Fabrizio Romano has been the useful corrective. He has said he does not see a January move as “imminent or urgent”, because the club are happy with the strikers they have and are scoring enough, and that he expects a new centre-forward in the summer rather than the winter. He named Álvarez and Bournemouth’s Eli Junior Kroupi among the names, and flagged right-back as the position to watch, most likely in summer 2027. David Ornstein’s standing caveat also applies: Arsenal are close to the UEFA financial line, so a nine-figure arrival almost certainly needs a significant sale beside it.

The quieter business: Rice first, then Odegaard and Timber

Three contracts that all expire in 2028, and a club trying to close them in order. Status: advanced talks, nothing signed.

BBC Sport reported on 7 October that Declan Rice is close to a new long-term deal with a significant rise on his current wages. Football Insider’s Pete O'Rourke followed on 12 October with the sequencing: Martin Ødegaard and Jurriën Timber are both due new deals once Rice’s is finalised. All three current contracts run to 2028, with an option on Rice’s for a further year. Nothing has been signed.

None of this is glamorous and all of it matters more than a January striker. Rice joined from West Ham United in 2023 for an initial £100m plus £5m in add-ons and has 165 Arsenal appearances with 21 goals and 33 assists. Ødegaard is the captain and opened the European scoring at Napoli. Timber scored the first against Lille and has been asked to cover both full-back positions this season. Add Arteta’s own extension to 2030 and the club has spent October locking down the people who won the title. Ben White is also reported to be in line for fresh terms.

Madueke answers the exit stories in the only way that counts

A fortnight ago Madueke looked like a £48.5m squad player drifting out of view, with The Telegraph reporting that Atlético Madrid and Juventus were monitoring him. He had five appearances, 176 minutes, one EFL Cup goal and no place in Thomas Tuchel’s England squad.

Since then he has started against Leeds United and crossed for Calafiori’s equaliser, started against Lille, and come off the bench at the City Ground to win the game. “We showed grit, determination” and a champion’s spirit, he said afterwards. The awkward part is that he remains the player Arsenal are reportedly most willing to include in an Álvarez package. With Tzolis still out, they are also short of exactly what he offers.

The Squad

A team that has stopped conceding and not started scoring

Five clean sheets in Konsa’s nine games. Eleven league goals in seven matches. Zero from set plays, from the side that broke the corner-goal record six months ago. The win rate says one thing and the process says another.

The defensive numbers have moved past impressive into something odd. Arsenal have conceded five league goals in seven matches, three of them at Brighton in the one defeat. In Europe they have conceded none at all. The 1-0 at Nottingham Forest was the 13th time in 366 days they have won a game by that exact scoreline, which is the statistical signature of a side that trusts its back four to make one goal enough.

Konsa is the quiet reason it has held up without Saliba. Five clean sheets in nine appearances, a stoppage-time block on Delap, and Arteta naming him and Gabriel as the reason for the three points. Forest had 14 attempts on Sunday and not one of them was classified as a big chance. Two were on target. David Raya has had quiet afternoons and said afterwards that clean sheets are what win games, which from a goalkeeper is both true and self-serving.

The attack is the project

Eleven league goals in seven games is a modest return for a champion, and the detail is more uncomfortable than the total. Arsenal have still not scored from a set play this season. The numbers behind the drop across the opening run were stark: set-play attempts down 34.6 per cent, set-play expected goals down 48 per cent, the share of total xG coming from dead balls down from roughly 47 per cent to roughly 21. Last season they scored a Premier League record 19 goals from corners alone.

Arteta’s account has been more nuanced than the headline. He claimed Arsenal have “done better things than last year in the first five games, but we have scored no goals” from corners, and said plainly: “this season we should have scored four and we haven’t.” On the revised corner laws he was matter of fact: “You have to adjust to the rules.” Opponents are defending set pieces better, so Arsenal will have to score in other ways.

Who is actually playing well

Saka remains the most productive attacker, with three league goals and a Champions League goal against Lille that took him to 19 in major European competition for Arsenal, second only to Thierry Henry. He also has nine Champions League assists for the club, behind only Henry and Cesc Fàbregas. Tim Stillman’s analysis makes a persuasive case that the shot volume is deliberate: he is being moved into central finishing positions and running off his full-back’s shoulder.

Havertz is the more interesting argument. Arteta spent last week publicly backing him as a “giver” who can still become a bigger goalscorer, which is the kind of endorsement a manager offers a player whose contribution the numbers undersell. He has two league goals and made the cut-back for Saka against Lille. Gyökeres, meanwhile, has none, which is the single unresolved question in the squad and the one Munich may yet answer.

The midfield is the part of the team that has actually changed. Bruno Guimarães, the £75m arrival from Newcastle United, has two league goals including the winner against Leeds United, and his duel numbers at the City Ground were the best on the pitch. Rice has been used deeper and more conservatively than last season, which is partly workload management after a World Cup and partly a consequence of Zubimendi being unavailable for stretches. The pairing is the thing Arseblog flagged in early October as pivotal to the season, and it is only now getting a clear run.

The rotation question eases this week. Zubimendi is fit again, which gives Arteta a way to protect Rice across three matches in eight days. Myles Lewis-Skelly keeps left-back while Hincapié is out. Timber is covering both flanks and scoring. The group is thinner than it was a fortnight ago, and it is still winning every week.

Hale End

Mylo Bernard beats Tottenham in the 97th minute, five weeks after signing

A derby won by a brace from a boy who only completed his move last month, a goalkeeper crisis solved by an outfield 16-year-old, and a 14-year-old creating in the Youth League. It was a good week downstairs.

The under-18s beat Tottenham Hotspur 2-1 at home and Mylo Bernard scored both, the winner arriving in the seventh minute of stoppage time. He only officially completed his move from Crystal Palace last month. The win was a fourth consecutive league victory, lifted Josh Smith’s side to fourth, five points behind Chelsea with the leaders holding a game in hand, and ended Tottenham’s unbeaten start to the campaign. Arsenal have conceded only seven league goals at this level.

The other story from that afternoon is better than the scoreline. Emerson Nwaneri, 16, finished the match in goal as an outfield player after Jack Talbot suffered a concussion and his replacement Max Lo Presti also went off injured. He is normally a right winger.

Three days earlier the under-19s lost 3-2 to Lille in the UEFA Youth League, their first defeat after beating Napoli in the opener. Defenders Josh Ogunnaike and Elijah Upson scored, Jack Porter made several superb saves in goal, and Luis Muñoz, who is 14, delivered the corner for Upson’s goal. Joshua Sesay, Hakeem Abubakar and Theo Odaji-Ukueku all made their Youth League debuts off the bench. Arsenal were short of the creativity of Ceadach O'Neill, who was on the first-team bench that night. They go to Bayern Munich this week.

The under-21s were idle at the weekend, which spares them a result for once. They remain without a league win and sit 21st of 31 in Premier League 2, a standing that owes something to the structural oddity of the moment: on the night the under-19s played Lille, four of the best young players at the club were on the first team’s bench at the Emirates.

The Dowman decision, and the date that matters

Max Dowman spent the previous week being handed hardware: three Guinness World Records for youngest Premier League title winner, at 16 years and 144 days; youngest Premier League goalscorer, at 16 years and 73 days; and youngest player in Champions League history, at 15 years and 308 days. He came on late against Lille last Tuesday.

The practical question is unresolved. Football Insider reports that Arsenal have not decided whether to let him leave on loan in January, with as many as 12 clubs interested and Rangers preparing an approach. Ornstein has reported that Arsenal have agreed to tie him to a professional contract when he turns 17 on 31 December, one day before the window opens. That sequencing is not a coincidence. With Tzolis out and Gyökeres still goalless, a loan that looked sensible in September looks less obvious now.

Loan watch

Ethan Nwaneri’s season at Borussia Dortmund is accumulating minutes rather than moments. He has five Bundesliga appearances, four of them starts, 310 minutes, no goals and no assists, plus two Champions League outings. He played 62 minutes in Saturday’s goalless draw at Union Berlin and 30 off the bench as Dortmund lost 1-0 at Bodo/Glimt in midweek. The week before he had a bicycle kick ruled out against Werder Bremen for a soft foul in the build-up, in what German reporting called his best performance yet.

Elsewhere, Tommy Setford is getting games in goal at Stevenage, Ismeal Kabia is playing at St Mirren and Charles Sagoe Jr is seeing out his spell at Kalmar FF. On the women’s side, Jessie Gale is at Newcastle United, Sophie Harwood at Sheffield United and Cecily Wellesley-Smith at FC Rosengard.

Arsenal Women

Twenty-seven shots, four on target, one point: Slegers says a change may be needed

Arsenal Women drew 1-1 at home with newly promoted Birmingham City and sit eighth with six points from six. Their manager used the word that managers do not usually volunteer themselves.

Saturday at the Emirates was the clearest statement yet of what is wrong. Arsenal had 27 shots and four of them were on target. In the first half they had 76 per cent of the ball, eight shots and one on target, and the nearest thing to a goal was Alessia Russo’s header off the inside of the post. Océane Hurtré put Birmingham City ahead on 53 after running clear of Leah Williamson and Lotte Wubben-Moy. Olivia Smith curled in the equaliser on 66. It finished 1-1.

Renee Slegers did not hide behind it. She called the counter-attack goal “not good enough,” said Arsenal spend a lot of time on the ball and get caught when they lose it, with poor positioning and transition defending, and admitted the ball circulation was excruciatingly slow. On Birmingham’s switch of Hurtré from left to right to attack Arsenal’s left side, she said: “that’s the one player we needed to stop and we didn’t in that moment.”

I still believe I can help this team move forward. If that is no longer the case, a change needs to happen.

Renee Slegers, after the Birmingham draw

That sentence is the story. Managers are rarely the first to put the word on the table, and Slegers put it there herself, framing the problems as small margins that are costing the team. Arseblog’s Tim Stillman reached for the comparison most Arsenal supporters were already making privately, to Joe Montemurro’s final months in 2020, and made the sharper point underneath it: nobody watching can currently say whether this team presses or passes, whether it is fast or technical or direct.

A rebuilt squad and a long injury list

Context cuts both ways. Slegers won the Women’s Champions League in 2025 and finished second in the WSL last season, and the squad she works with is substantially new. In came Georgia Stanway from Bayern Munich, Ona Batlle from Barcelona, Lisa Baum from RB Leipzig, Géraldine Reuteler from Eintracht Frankfurt, Selina Cerci from Hoffenheim, Élisa De Almeida from Paris Saint-Germain and the keepers Misa Rodríguez and Isabella Damm.

Out went Beth Mead to Manchester City, Katie McCabe to Chelsea, Victoria Pelova to Tottenham Hotspur, Laia Codina to West Ham United and Manuela Zinsberger to Borussia Dortmund. Those arrivals were meant to prevent exactly this start. De Almeida finally made her debut on Saturday and had two late efforts.

The absences are not trivial either. Mariona Caldentey is out with the hamstring injury from Manchester City. Frida Maanum picked up a quad problem on international duty and may be out two to three weeks. Kim Little was unwell and missed the match. Chloe Kelly (hamstring) and Daphne van Domselaar (knee) remain out, and both Michelle Agyemang and Katie Reid have ruptured cruciate ligaments. With Caldentey, Little and Maanum all unavailable, Reuteler played as the number 10 and Stina Blackstenius led the line.

Europe, and a derby on Saturday

Europe remains the refuge. Arsenal have four points from two Women’s Champions League matches, a 1-0 home win over HB Koge settled by a Caldentey penalty in stoppage time and a 2-2 draw in Paris in which they threw away a two-goal lead. Benfica visit on 28 October, and then it gets serious: Barcelona away on 11 November and OL Lyonnes at home on 19 November. Before any of that, Tottenham Hotspur host the north London derby on Saturday lunchtime, live on BBC One, and the margins Slegers keeps describing have to start falling the other way.

Gunners Abroad

Russo scores twice as England romp through, and Nwaneri gets his European minutes

No men’s international window this week, so the Gunners abroad were in Europe: a World Cup play-off settled in Leicester, a Youth League trip to Lille and a loanee in the Champions League for somebody else.

Alessia Russo scored twice as England beat Greece 5-0 at the King Power Stadium last Tuesday to go through 8-1 on aggregate in the first round of the World Cup play-offs. She opened on three minutes and added her second on 75, with Georgia Stanway scoring on 30 and Lauren James getting two. Sarina Wiegman’s side now face Ukraine over two legs between 26 November and 5 December for a place at Brazil 2027.

In the Champions League proper, four Arsenal players scored in the same European night for the first time this season: Timber, Eze and Saka against Lille, after Ødegaard’s winner in Naples. Saka’s goal took him to 19 in major European competition for the club, behind only Thierry Henry’s 42.

The oddest split in the squad remains Viktor Gyökeres. He has no goal for Arsenal this season and has scored in seven consecutive UEFA Nations League matches for Sweden, a competition record, most recently the only goal of a 1-0 win in Romania. He has 25 Sweden goals and passed Marcus Berg on the way. The next chance to reconcile the two versions comes in Munich.

Ethan Nwaneri played 30 minutes off the bench as Borussia Dortmund lost 1-0 away to Bodo/Glimt on Wednesday, his second Champions League appearance of the loan. In Brief

Saliba’s return slips to December

The back problem that flared up at the World Cup has kept William Saliba out all season. He needed no surgery and is reported to be running at London Colney, but December is now the realistic marker, not the November international break.

No 80,000-seat Emirates

Arsenal have ruled out a major expansion and committed instead to the “continued evolution” of a 60,704-seat ground, after an Advisory Board meeting attended by Josh Kroenke and chief executive Richard Garlick. The Arsenal Supporters’ Trust told members there will be “no significant capacity changes”. The money goes instead on hospitality, catering, retail and connectivity, plus safe standing at the Clock End this season and the North Bank in 2027/28.

Manchester City found guilty

An independent commission found Manchester City guilty of all charges relating to serious breaches of financial rules, including inflating revenue by £830m between 2009/10 and 2017/18. No sanction has been set, and City appealed on 1 October, calling the decision “unsafe.” The appeal hearing must conclude within 12 weeks of filing.

Arteta on his City years

Asked whether his conscience is clear about his time as Pep Guardiola’s assistant during the period investigated, he answered “Yes”, adding that he cannot say more while the process is unresolved.

Arsenal are going back to Highbury

The EFL Cup fourth round sends Arsenal to Fleetwood Town a week on Tuesday, and the League Two side’s ground is called Highbury Stadium.

Arteta to 2030

The extension confirmed on 7 October will take him to a decade in charge. He was appointed in December 2019 and won the league in May with 85 points, seven clear of Manchester City.

A long-serving executive departs

Arsenal’s General Counsel has left after 17 years, a change that passed almost unnoticed.
