The Lead

Venezia at home, and no more room for drift

Juventus come back from the international break seventh, 11 points off the top, with the kindest-looking fixture of the autumn and every reason to fear it.

Two weeks without a match have not cooled anything in Turin. Juventus return on Sunday lunchtime against Venezia at the Allianz Stadium, seventh in Serie A on 18 points from 11 games. They are six points behind fourth-placed AC Milan and 11 behind leaders Lazio, who won 3-0 in Turin in October (Sky Sport table).

The game itself has moved. It was first scheduled for Monday, November 23 at 20:45, but now kicks off at 12:30 in Turin on Sunday, November 22, which is 3:30 a.m. Pacific (ilBianconero, Fantacalcio). Some fixture sites still show the old slot.

The pattern Spalletti has to break

Since the start of October, Luciano Spalletti's side have alternated almost perfectly in the league: a draw at Cagliari, a 3-0 home defeat by Lazio, a win at Lecce, a 2-0 loss at Genoa, a 2-0 win over Napoli and a 2-2 draw at Fiorentina. That is eight points from six games, the form of a mid-table side rather than a contender.

Europe has been the steadier stage. Juventus are unbeaten in the Europa League with three wins and a draw, and Tuttomercatoweb calculates that about seven more points should secure a top-eight finish and a direct path to the round of 16 (TMW). Omonia visit Turin on Thursday, November 26.

The attack is the sore point. Calciomercato's analysis after Florence was blunt: a centre-back, Gleison Bremer, is the club's top Serie A scorer with three goals, which it called a symbol of the failed striker market (Calciomercato). Across all competitions Randal Kolo Muani and Nick Woltemade have four goals each.

A back three, by necessity

Tuttosport expects Spalletti to switch to a back three against Venezia because of the injury list, with a return to a back four planned once his full-backs are fit. Its probable XI: Vicario; Gatti, Lucumí, Bremer; McKennie, Koopmeiners, Douglas Luiz, Cabal; Kolo Muani, Alajbegović; Woltemade (Tuttosport).

There is some relief. Edon Zhegrova, who left Kosovo's camp with a muscle problem, is back in training, and Francisco Conceição has recovered from the dislocated shoulder he suffered against Lazio (Goal). Zeki Çelik's hamstring flared up with Türkiye, and Pierre Kalulu remains out.

Kenan Yildiz, out since August with a fractured metatarsal, is the missing piece. Reports disagree on his return: beIN Sports says he is aiming for December, while La Stampa, cited by Il Fatto Quotidiano, points to January (beIN Sports, Il Fatto Quotidiano).

The ghost in the room

Spalletti has not spoken publicly this week; his pre-match press conference is expected on Saturday. Others have filled the silence. Former Juventus coach Antonio Conte, in an interview with Cronache di Spogliatoio reported by LaPresse on November 18, talked openly about returning one day (LaPresse).

“I think that sooner or later that circle will have to be closed.”

Antonio Conte, via LaPresse, November 18

Conte added that he would come back "when the opportunity arises, when certain circumstances align." Fanpage, reporting on the club's reaction, notes that Spalletti's contract runs to June 2028 (Fanpage). No one at the club has suggested a change, but the timing of the remarks will not have gone unnoticed.

Why Sunday matters

Venezia are 19th with six points, have scored only eight league goals and have conceded 21. On paper this is three points. In practice, Juventus have struggled to impose themselves after conceding first, and Sunday starts a run of Venezia, Omonia and then Como away on November 29.

Nine days that will decide whether this squad goes into December within reach of the top four, or whether January becomes a repair job. A win against Venezia does not answer every question. A slip would make every one of them louder.

Second feature

200 million euros, and no room for stars

The capital increase is under way and the shares have fallen, while the chief executive admits UEFA's settlement agreement is the biggest obstacle to rebuilding.

On November 12 the Juventus board approved a capital increase of about 200 million euros, part of the 250 million euros shareholders authorised on November 3. Up to 208.5 million new shares are offered at 0.96 euros each, one new share for every two held, a 28.36% discount to the theoretical ex-rights price (Calcio e Finanza).

Rights trade from November 16 to 25 and subscriptions close on December 1 (Borsa Italiana). Exor, with 65.4%, has committed about 131 million euros, including 60 million already advanced, and will take up any unsold shares. Tether, the second-largest shareholder at 11.527%, has not said publicly whether it will subscribe (Affaritaliani).

The market's first verdict was cool. On November 16, the first day of the rights issue, the shares fell 6.5% to 1.15 euros and the rights lost 36.1% (Soldionline). The club has said it expects a loss in 2026-27, mainly because it is not in the Champions League; 2025-26 ended with a consolidated loss of 66 million euros (Teleborsa).

Carnevali's candour

Speaking at the RCS Sport Industry Talk on November 18, chief executive Giovanni Carnevali was unusually direct. "Blocked by the settlement agreement, we cannot make big signings, but I am confident," he said, adding that the recapitalisation was "the demonstration that mistakes were made" (Calciomercato).

He promised "nothing special" in the short term (Calcio e Finanza). The agreement, signed with UEFA in June after Juventus breached its football earnings rule, runs from 2026 to 2028, carries a fine of up to 20 million euros and can bring squad-list limits if interim targets are missed (Corriere dello Sport).

The practical meaning for January is clear in Tuttosport's reading: a striker and a left-back are the priorities, with a midfielder dependent on how Thuram and Locatelli recover, and sales needed to balance the books (Tuttosport).

Scouting the Next Opponent

Venezia: bottom three, but dangerous down the flanks

Davide Nicola's side arrive with the joint-fewest goals in Serie A and a win over Udinese behind them.

Juventus face Venezia sitting 19th on six points, with two wins and nine defeats; only Udinese, on five, are below them (Sky Sport). Their eight goals scored is the joint-lowest total in the league, and they have conceded 21.

They head to Turin with a little momentum. Their last five league games produced two wins, a 1-0 over Napoli and a 2-1 over Udinese just before the break, when Kike Pérez struck a long-range winner (Fantacalcio).

Coach Davide Nicola is expected to set up with a back three and wing-backs (single-sourced preview). The width is where the threat lives: wing-back Antoine Hainaut has two goals and an assist, striker Akor Adams has two goals, and Gianluca Busio leads the side with two assists (Calciomercato stats).

Kike Pérez is the ball-carrier who draws fouls, and he is one of five Venezia players on three yellow cards. For Juventus, the lesson of the autumn applies: stop the wing-backs early, and do not concede first.

History favours the hosts. Juventus's all-time Serie A record against Venezia is 17 wins, eight draws and two defeats (Juventus.com), but the last two meetings were tight: 2-2 in Turin in December 2024 (Football Web Pages) and 3-2 to Juventus in Venice in May 2025 (90min).

Transfer News

The striker hunt: Sørloth leads a short, careful list

Juventus want a centre-forward in January, but the money has to come from somewhere.

Atlético Madrid's Alexander Sørloth is the most workable option for January, according to Niccolò Ceccarini of Tuttomercatoweb, who reports good relations between the clubs (TMW). QS also lists him as a priority (QS). Certainty: concrete interest; nothing agreed.

How a deal might be structured is unclear. Get Italian Football News reported a swap worth about 25 million euros involving an Argentine, read by others as Nico González, who only renewed to 2030 in October (Yahoo Sports). That is single-sourced; QS instead mentions Jonathan David, on loan at Atlético from Juventus.

Behind Sørloth, Monaco's Folarin Balogun is described by TMW as a first choice in principle but almost impossible in January. Mateo Retegui's wages are a problem, and Gianluca Scamacca would be expensive (Goal, citing Gazzetta). In October, Frederic Massara said there was "still plenty of time" (Di Marzio).

Left-back: Miranda, Aït-Nouri or Udogie

Tuttosport's Nicolò Schira reports that Massara is going back for Bologna's Juan Miranda in January after Bologna rejected an approach in August; his contract runs to 2028 and renewal talks have stalled (Tuttosport). Certainty: concrete interest. Bologna are reluctant to sell mid-season.

Gazzetta lists Manchester City's Rayan Aït-Nouri as a top target, possibly on a loan with an option; Napoli are also linked (Napoli Magazine, citing Sky Sport Switzerland). Tottenham are open to selling Destiny Udogie, with a loan and delayed purchase floated (TMW, citing Gazzetta). Both: interest.

Midfield: Højbjerg now, Bakola for later

Marseille's Pierre-Emile Højbjerg, valued at about 15 million euros with a contract to 2028, is a live option if the injuries to Khéphren Thuram and Manuel Locatelli demand it; Fiorentina are also interested (TuttoJuve). Certainty: interest, no bid.

The longer play is Sassuolo's Darryl Bakola, born in 2007. Gazzetta reports Juventus could invest 25 to 30 million euros and leave him at Sassuolo until June, with Vasilije Adžić possibly involved (SassuoloNews), but Calciomercato says Sassuolo do not want to sell in January (Yahoo Sports).

Going the other way

Zhegrova would consider leaving for the right offer, per Fabrizio Romano; all nine of his appearances have come off the bench. Calciomercato values him at about 15 million euros (Calciomercato), and Tuttosport names Al-Ittihad and Monaco among the interested clubs (TMW).

Weston McKennie closed down the Manchester United talk himself on November 18: "I have no intention of leaving Juventus... my words were misunderstood" (Calciomercato). TEAMtalk reports United are not pursuing him (TEAMtalk).

The Squad

Bodies back, but the treatment room is still full

Zhegrova and Conceição return; Yildiz, Locatelli, Thuram and Kalulu do not.

Spalletti's preferred shape this season has been a back four, but Tuttosport expects a 3-4-2-1 on Sunday because of the full-back shortage (Tuttosport). Federico Gatti, who did not play for Italy in the break, is pushing to start.

The captain, Manuel Locatelli, and Khéphren Thuram are both expected back only in 2027 (Goal). Without them, Douglas Luiz and Teun Koopmeiners carry the central midfield, with McKennie used on the right.

Kalulu injured a hamstring at Fiorentina. Gazzetta, via SOSFanta, says he is aiming for Como on November 29, while other reports suggest mid-December (SOSFanta). Çelik was pulled from Türkiye's XI with tightness in the same left hamstring he injured in October (JuveFC).

Next Gen

Next Gen breathe again; a Montero scores for Juventus

A 3-0 win over Treviso lifted Brambilla's side out of the relegation zone, and a familiar surname got on the scoresheet.

Juventus Next Gen beat Treviso 3-0 at the Moccagatta in Alessandria on November 15, with goals from Konaté (14'), Alfonso Montero (55') and Merola (74') (Juventus.com). Massimo Brambilla's side are 13th in Serie C Group A with 14 points from 14 games (TMW).

Montero, a Uruguayan centre-back born in 2007 and the son of former Juventus defender Paolo Montero, scored his first professional goal (TMW). He trained with the first team in October. Next Gen visit Giana Erminio on Saturday.

The Primavera, coached by Simone Padoin, beat Empoli 2-0 on November 7 thanks to a Paolo Ceppi free kick and a Gabriel Repciuc counter-attack goal (Juventus.com). They travel to Inter on Saturday.

Loan watch

Vasilije Adžić started fast at Sassuolo with four goals and an assist in six games but has since gone six without either (TMW). He then went off after 39 minutes with a knee problem in Montenegro's 4-1 win over Cyprus on November 15 (Pazzidifanta).

Jonathan David scored in each of his first three La Liga games for Atlético Madrid and came on late in a 2-1 loss to Barcelona on November 8. Fabio Miretti has four league appearances without a goal at Beşiktaş. Loïs Openda's loan at Lyon is reportedly in doubt, a single-sourced claim.

Juventus Women

Three goals in six minutes, then a night of relief in Biella

Isaac Guerrero's side came from 2-0 down at Inter and followed it with a Champions League win.

Juventus Women were losing 2-0 at Inter on November 15 after two Brittany Raphino goals, then scored three times in six minutes: Sarriegi on 88, Chiba in the 92nd minute and Alba Redondo in the 94th (Juventus.com). It was a third straight league win.

Remina Chiba had a goal and two assists off the bench, and Redondo now has six league goals, five of them in her last five appearances, per the club's statistics (Juventus.com stats). Martina Rosucci said the team had bounced back after "a heavy Champions League defeat" (Juventus.com), the 6-0 loss to OL Lyonnes on November 11 (Juventus.com).

On Wednesday Juventus beat OH Leuven 2-0 in the Champions League with two late goals (Juventus.com). Futboltotal credits Serturini (86') and Pavan (90+3'), the second set up by Redondo; the scorers are single-sourced (Futboltotal).

Next: Lazio away on Sunday, November 22, at 11:30 a.m. Pacific, then a three-week gap until Como visit on December 12 (Juventus.com). The Champions League resumes at PSG on December 16.

Players Away

McKennie the standout as the break leaves bruises

Eleven Juventus players were called up; two came back hurt.

Weston McKennie headed in during the USA's 6-0 win in Haiti in the CONCACAF Nations League quarter-final, then added two assists in the 2-0 second leg, one for a Christian Pulisic overhead kick (Juventus.com, TuttoJuve). The USA meet Mexico in the semi-final in March.

Kerim Alajbegović assisted in Bosnia and Herzegovina's 3-2 defeat in Sweden and set up the late winner against Romania. Pape Matar Sarr assisted off the bench in Senegal's 4-1 win over Sudan and started the 4-2 return. Teun Koopmeiners played in the Netherlands' 7-0 win over Greece and their 5-0 defeat by Germany (Juventus.com).

Jeff Ekhator came on and scored an 87th-minute penalty in Italy Under-21s' 2-2 draw with Belgium. Guglielmo Vicario and Federico Gatti did not play as Italy beat France 3-1 and lost 4-1 in Belgium (Tifojuventus). Jhon Lucumí and Juan Cabal were with Colombia for two friendlies against Chile; reports differ on their minutes.

The bruises: Zhegrova played 82 minutes of Kosovo's 0-0 draw with Israel before leaving camp with a muscle problem, and Çelik's hamstring tightened before Türkiye played France. Juventus Women's call-ups for the late-November window had not been published when this edition went out.
