The Lead

Ten New Voices, One Compressed Summer

England restarts on Friday, 33 days after a World Cup final, with half its clubs under new management, Pep Guardiola gone after a decade and a financial rulebook nobody has yet tested. Europe follows within the week. This is the most thoroughly rebooted season the continent has staged in a generation.

The strangest thing about the 2026-27 Premier League is how little of last season is still standing. The competition kicks off on Friday 21 August, a start date the league pushed back so that players would get 33 days between the World Cup final on 19 July and the first whistle. The Premier League confirmed the calendar months ago. What nobody could have scheduled was the turnover that filled the gap.

Ten of the twenty clubs will line up on Friday with a manager they did not have this time last year. Pep Guardiola is gone from Manchester City after ten years and seventeen major trophies, replaced by Enzo Maresca on a three-year contract, with City paying Chelsea more than £17m in compensation to prise him away, per Sky Sports. Andoni Iraola has succeeded Arne Slot at Liverpool on a two-year deal.

Roberto De Zerbi has Tottenham, Xabi Alonso has Chelsea, and Matthias Jaissle took Newcastle on 5 August after Eddie Howe left on the last day of July. Add Oliver Glasner at Nottingham Forest, Pierre Sage at Crystal Palace, Marco Rose at Bournemouth, Gary O'Neil at Ipswich and Alvaro Arbeloa at Fulham, and you have half a division learning a new voice in the same fortnight. The league published the complete list on 5 August.

The money moved just as violently. Morgan Rogers left Aston Villa for Chelsea at £117m, the most expensive British player of all time, a record that had stood for twenty days after Elliot Anderson went from Forest to City for £116m.

Newcastle sold Sandro Tonali for a club-record £100m and Bruno Guimaraes for £75m, and Mohamed Salah ended nine years at Anfield with 255 goals before joining Trabzonspor on a free. A league that spent a decade arguing about whether it could afford itself has just had its most expensive summer, and the window does not close until 23:00 BST on 1 September.

We want more, we need more. We keep looking forward to how we can do that.Michael Carrick, Manchester United head coach, 13 August

Manchester City are the most interesting case, because they have spent £116m on a midfielder and simultaneously sold the one who made the whole thing work. Rodri completed a move to Barcelona on 18 August. Maresca's only competitive outing so far was the Community Shield in Cardiff on 16 August, a 3-0 defeat in which City conceded inside the first minute and never recovered. "I think a big part of the game was in the first goal we conceded," Maresca said afterwards. "Unfortunately after 30 seconds we conceded the first one."

City's own match report lists Rodri as absent after recent back surgery. He will now be absent permanently, and the club's answer appears to be an 18-year-old from Lille (see Transfer News). Maresca inherits a possession model built around a single controlling pivot and has to rebuild that pivot in the last ten days of a window.

Liverpool's problem is more immediate and more structural. Iraola arrives to find a centre-back department that has essentially collapsed: Jeremy Jacquet, Giovanni Leoni and Joe Gomez all injured in pre-season, and Conor Bradley described by the manager as months away, Sky Sports reported on 11 August.

Liverpool used Wataru Endo and Luke Chambers as an emergency central pairing, then borrowed Ronald Araujo from Barcelona on a season loan with a £47.14m option and no loan fee, the player having taken a pay cut to make it work.

Pre-season brought 3-2 defeats to Leeds and Monaco, both after leading 2-0. "Right now probably we don't have much more in terms of the level required to play like this," Iraola said. They open away at Newcastle on Sunday.

Behind those two, Chelsea are the summer's biggest spender and its most opaque squad. Alonso has added Rogers and the Rayo Vallecano left-back Pep Chavarria, signed on 12 August in a deal reported at €19m plus add-ons. He has also left a long list of senior players out of pre-season friendlies, which English and Italian outlets have read as a clear-out, though the club has said nothing.

Tottenham spent enormously and sold their captain, Cristian Romero, to Atletico Madrid on 15 August. Manchester United gave Michael Carrick a contract to 2028 and, in his own words on 13 August, still want more.

All of this happens under rules nobody has stress-tested. Profitability and Sustainability Rules are gone. From this season the league runs Squad Cost Ratio and Sustainability and Systemic Resilience: on-pitch spending capped at 85 per cent of football revenue for clubs outside Europe and 70 per cent for those in it, plus three balance-sheet tests including a £12.5m monthly working-capital floor and the ability to absorb an £85m shock.

Financial penalties for squad-cost breaches only become enforceable in 2027-28, so this is the year clubs find out where the lines actually sit. Referees arrive with a new instruction set too: red cards for hair-pulling, a five-second countdown on restarts, a one-minute exile for treated players, and a VAR that may now overturn a second yellow but may not invent one.

The models are unimpressed by the chaos. Opta's pre-season projection, published by Al Jazeera on 18 August, still puts the reigning champions well clear at 38 per cent, with Manchester City at 20.5, Liverpool at 9.2 and Manchester United at 6.2, and makes Hull City the likeliest to go down.

Those are guesses dressed as arithmetic. What is certain is that on Friday night ten managers find out whether a compressed pre-season, a record wage bill and an untested rulebook add up to anything at all.

 

Jamal Musiala Chooses to Keep Playing

Twice in four days the Bayern forward went down with nobody near him. On Tuesday he told everyone why, in his own words, and said he intends to carry on.

The most important story in German football this week has nothing to do with the transfer window. Jamal Musiala, 23, collapsed off the ball in two consecutive Bayern Munich friendlies: first against RB Leipzig in the Telekom Cup on 15 August, then again on 18 August at Heidenheim, shortly after coming on, before being withdrawn. Both incidents were shown live. Neither involved contact.

On Tuesday evening Musiala published a statement on Instagram, carried in English by bundesliga.com, that was notable for how much he chose to disclose and how calmly he did it.

I have been diagnosed with brief, temporary but readily treatable absences, which are caused by a neurological dysfunction. I know that they may seem scary at first glance, but for me, they are currently part of my everyday life.Jamal Musiala, 18 August

He went further. "The important thing is that there is no further health risk beyond this," he wrote, adding that it was his own wish, with the approval of the neurological specialists, to keep "pursuing my passion of playing football. I have taken responsibility for that decision." He closed by asking that the matter otherwise stay "within the circle of my closest confidants, the club and the experts."

Bayern have said very little, which in the circumstances reads as discipline rather than evasion. Sporting director Max Eberl told Abendzeitung Muenchen on 16 August only that "the most important thing is that he is doing well," and Manuel Neuer added: "Of course we're keeping our fingers crossed."

Early German coverage floated the heat in Munich, above 30C that weekend, as a possible explanation. Musiala's own statement has superseded that, and it should not be repeated.

Bayern play Borussia Dortmund in the Franz Beckenbauer Supercup on Saturday and open the Bundesliga against VfB Stuttgart on 28 August.

Whether Vincent Kompany uses Musiala at Signal Iduna Park is a matter for the club and its doctors, and nobody outside that room is in a position to say. What is already clear is that a 23-year-old has handled a frightening, public medical episode with more grace than most of the industry manages on an ordinary Tuesday.

Rodri Goes Home

Barcelona have the best holding midfielder of his generation for a guaranteed fee well below what he would have cost a year ago. Manchester City have a hole where their team used to start.

Status: OFFICIAL. Rodri signed for Barcelona on 18 August on a contract to 30 June 2030. Sky Sports put the fee at £65.4m; ESPN reported the total package at €76.5m, and Spanish reporting has consistently described a structure of roughly €60m guaranteed with €16.5m in variables. Barcelona's first two offers, of about £40m and £55m, were rejected. He is 30 and had one year left.

The context matters more than the number. Rodri was central to Spain's World Cup win last month, and Al Jazeera described him as crucial to that campaign. He is a former Ballon d'Or holder. Barcelona were forced into the market partly by an injury to Frenkie de Jong, picked up on international duty, whose diagnosis and timeline the club has not published.

The open question is registration. Sports Illustrated Futbol reported on 14 August that Barcelona are back under LaLiga's 1:1 spending rule and that Anthony Gordon and Karim Adeyemi were still unregistered and without squad numbers.

Rodri is expected to take the number 16. Barcelona's first league match is not until 27 August, at home to Athletic Club, which buys them time. This newspaper could not confirm that any of the three has been registered, and readers should treat any claim that they have as unverified.

Rodri's parting line to City, carried by Sky, was better than most: "City didn't just make me grow as a player. They made me grow as a human."

City's Teenage Answer, and the Deal Chelsea Priced Out

Manchester City are expected to replace a 30-year-old world champion with an 18-year-old from Lille. A parallel move for Enzo Fernandez has already missed one deadline.

Status: advanced, not announced. Ayyoub Bouaddi, 18, is expected to leave Lille for Manchester City. The fee is where the reporting diverges: beIN Sports put it at up to €100m on 17 August, while Matteo Moretto, relayed by Eurosport France the same day, has it nearer €130m, which would make it the biggest transfer of the French window by a distance.

ESPN reported City were near a deal. Neither club has announced anything, and this newspaper is printing the range rather than a single number.

Enzo Maresca was asked about it on 17 August and gave the answer of a manager who knows the deal is close and cannot say so. "He is still a Lille player," he told beIN Sports. "But with any new signings, the sooner they arrive, the better."

He added: "We are quite clear. For sure, we are going to do something before the transfer window closes." On Rodri, in the same session: "Rodri is a player that just won the World Cup, who won the Ballon d'Or. He's a top player."

The other City midfield thread is stalled. Chelsea set a €140m deadline of 17:00 BST on 14 August for Enzo Fernandez and City did not meet it, with Fabrizio Romano reporting that discussions continue and a summer exit remains possible. Chelsea's valuation has been put nearer £120m. Separately, The Sun claims City are looking at Adam Wharton of Crystal Palace, which sits firmly in the rumour tier.

 

Liverpool Go Shopping in Paris

Two PSG forwards have agreed personal terms with Liverpool. Neither club-to-club deal is done, both players were left out of the squad, and the fee reporting is all over the place.

Status: player terms agreed, clubs not agreed. On 14 August Fabrizio Romano reported a "verbal agreement" between Liverpool and Bradley Barcola, saying the player "wants to go" and that Liverpool were "ready to go strong" (via Football365).

Nicolo Schira has put a possible fee at €125m; Paris Saint-Germain had earlier been reported as wanting closer to £145m, and Romano had previously described PSG seeking roughly €200m for Barcola and a team-mate together. Print the range, not a figure.

The team-mate is Ibrahim Mbaye, 18. Fabrice Hawkins of RMC Sport reported on 18 August that Liverpool and Mbaye have agreed the financial terms of a five-year contract and that the player has decided against Bayer Leverkusen. No club-to-club agreement has been reported, and PSG's asking price is not established.

The tell is the team sheet. Foot Mercato reported on 17 August that Barcola had been sidelined by PSG, and both players were left out ahead of fresh talks. PSG have not been idle in the other direction: Mika Godts arrived from Ajax for a reported €55m and Ferran Torres from Barcelona for a reported €50m, both announced on 15 August.

 

Amorim Draws a Line Through Half of Milan

After a meeting with Gerry Cardinale at Milanello, the new head coach has told a large group of senior players they are outside the project. Rafael Leao is among the names on the market.

Status: reported by multiple Italian outlets, not confirmed by the club. Corriere dello Sport reported on 14 August that Ruben Amorim, appointed at AC Milan on 16 June, has communicated to Christopher Nkunku, Fikayo Tomori, Youssouf Fofana and others that they are out of the plan or at risk.

Sportmediaset and Fanpage carried the same story in outline, with Fanpage putting the number of excluded players at eight. The exact list is not consistent across sources and should be read as reporting rather than a club statement.

The headline name is Rafael Leao. Corriere dello Sport reported on 15 August that Leao missed the trip to face Manchester United with a muscle problem, but that the absence was not only medical: Amorim has concluded that Leao does not fit a 3-4-2-1 built on possession and short combinations.

The asking price is reported at around €50m including bonuses by Corriere dello Sport, and at €40m to €45m by Pianeta Milan citing Fabrizio Romano and Matteo Moretto. Galatasaray and Chelsea have both been named. Agent Jorge Mendes is reported to have offered Leao to Roma, where a net salary of €7m collided with an ownership-imposed wage ceiling of €4m.

Milan are buying as well as culling. Diego Moreira, the 22-year-old Belgium international, is arriving from Strasbourg after a medical, with Sky Sport Italia's 19 August live blog giving the structure as €45m plus €20m in bonuses. Foot Mercato, which broke the verbal agreement through Samuel Zemour, reported a package above €50m with a sell-on clause.

Moreira had previously agreed terms with RB Leipzig and turned them down, which is why Martin Demichelis spent the weekend in front of Magenta TV demanding more signings.

 

The Liquidity Index Writes the Deal

Lazio could not sign Davide Frattesi on the terms both clubs wanted. So Italy's financial rules rewrote the contract instead. It is the most instructive transfer of the Italian summer.

Status: OFFICIAL, 14 August. Davide Frattesi joined Lazio from Inter on a €5m loan with a €10m option to buy, Inter retaining 50 per cent of any future sale. Sky Sport Italia broke the terms and Romano confirmed the green light.

The reason the formula looks odd is that the obvious version was illegal. Serie A's indice di liquidita requires constrained clubs to keep a zero or positive transfer balance. An obligation to buy would have breached it and Lazio would not have been able to register the contract at all.

Restructuring the deal as a high loan fee plus a right rather than an obligation kept the balance positive and unlocked it. It is also why Lazio are running a zero-net-spend summer, and why their post-cup shopping list reads like a list of loans.

The same regulatory pressure shows up at Roma, where the Friedkin ownership has imposed a €4m net wage ceiling. Roma's business instead went into Rodrigo Mora, the 19-year-old Porto forward, who arrived in Rome on 18 August for a medical.

Corriere dello Sport reported a fee of €25m plus bonuses with Porto keeping 50 per cent of any resale, a clause allowing Roma to buy that half back for a further €20m, and a €120m release clause in Mora's new contract. Gian Piero Gasperini met the player personally. On 18 July he had told the club: "I need two players in the attacking-midfield line."

 

Naples, Turin and the Quiet Business

Napoli borrowed a Chelsea centre-back, Juventus took Tottenham's goalkeeper on a free loan, and Inter have a verbal agreement for a Liverpool midfielder.

Status: medical completed. Benoit Badiashile, 25, took his medical at Villa Stuart in Rome on the morning of 19 August ahead of a move from Chelsea to Napoli.

The structure reported by Corriere dello Sport and Sky Sport Italia is a €3m loan plus €1m in bonuses with a €27m purchase option to be discussed at the end of the season; Chelsea would have preferred an outright sale.

Asked about the move, Badiashile said simply: "Sono felice." Napoli, now managed by Massimiliano Allegri on a contract to 2029, also took Costantino Favasuli on a €1m loan with a €6.2m option.

Status: OFFICIAL. Juventus signed Jhon Lucumi from Bologna on 17 August for €20m plus bonuses to 2030, then took Guglielmo Vicario from Tottenham on 18 August on a free loan to June 2027 with an option at €8m plus up to €2m, per Tuttosport.

Vicario takes the number 25 and said he could not wait "to see everyone celebrating together at the Allianz Stadium." His arrival ends Michele Di Gregorio's time in Turin, with Marseille reported to be interested.

Status: verbal agreement, not signed. Gianluca Di Marzio reported on 19 August that Inter and Curtis Jones of Liverpool have a verbal agreement worth €30m plus €4m to €5m in bonuses, with a sell-on percentage still being negotiated, and that Inter sent formal documentation to Liverpool at 18:30 that evening (Di Marzio, matched by Sky Sport Italia).

Inter first made contact in January and identify Jones as the replacement for Frattesi. Other outlets have quoted €32m plus €4m and €36m total; the Di Marzio and Sky figure is the best-sourced. Inter had already bought Djed Spence from Tottenham on 15 August on a deal to 2031, reported at around £30m, and John Stones from Manchester City on a free.

 

Two Club Records in a Single American Day

On 18 August Real Salt Lake sold their homegrown star to Crystal Palace for a club record and Sporting Kansas City broke their own record to sign a Brazilian from Olympiacos.

Status: OFFICIAL, both. Zavier Gozo, 19, moved from Real Salt Lake to Crystal Palace for a reported $15m, a club record, with RSL keeping a sell-on percentage.

Gozo came through the academy, has 50 appearances since debuting in 2023, was a 2026 MLS All-Star and leads RSL this season with six goals and five assists in 16 matches. "As a homegrown kid from Utah, Real Salt Lake has been part of my life for as long as I can remember," he said.

The same day, Sporting Kansas City signed the 24-year-old Brazilian winger Andre Luiz from Olympiacos for $18m plus add-ons on a five-year Designated Player deal, also a club record, despite sitting bottom of the Western Conference on 14 points.

It is a sign of how the extended MLS window, open until 2 September for the first time in nearly two decades, has changed the league's appetite. Charlotte also handed Allan Saint-Maximin, signed from Lens, his debut in a 3-1 win over Columbus.

 

The Rumour Mill

Everything still simmering, with the certainty of each item stated plainly. Nothing here is done.

Ollie Watkins to Al Hilal is the loudest live saga in England. Sky's Paper Talk on 19 August, crediting Fabrizio Romano, reports a £38.5m bid and a player who is keen; Read Aston Villa says a third bid of around £38m has been rejected and that Villa expect a fourth, per Simon Jones of the Daily Mail.

Villa will not sell without a replacement. Several lower-tier outlets have reported that personal terms are agreed. Sky and Romano's own wording do not support that, and this newspaper will not print it as fact. Tier: bids rejected, talks live.

Moise Kean to Como is Italy's best-sourced unresolved deal. Como made a formal offer of a €5m loan with a €25m obligation, a €30m total, and Fiorentina rejected it and want €40m, per Corriere dello Sport, Sky Sport Italia and Tuttosport. Kean is receptive: Champions League football under Cesc Fabregas and a reported €4.5m salary. Tier: offer made and rejected, gap of €10m.

Jack Grealish to Everton on a second loan has moved from speculation to acknowledged contact. Everton chief executive Angus Kinnear told The Times, quoted by Goal: "Jack is a player we've remained in contact with. He's a player who's fallen in love with the right shade of blue but ultimately he's a Manchester City player at the moment and we need to respect that." Tier: contact confirmed, no agreement.

Giannis Konstantelias to Borussia Dortmund has reached the medical stage and a fan revolt. The 23-year-old flew to Germany on 16 August, with fees reported between €25m and €32m including add-ons, the story originating with Ruhrnachrichten.

PAOK's ultras group Gate 4 called the club's decision-makers "crooks and traitors" and gathered outside the stadium. There is precedent: in 2025 owner Ivan Savvidis killed a move to Stuttgart under identical pressure. Tier: medical stage, with a history of collapse.

Malick Fofana to Roma had personal terms agreed on 17 August, per Romano via Foot Mercato, at a fee Italian sources put near €40m, contingent on Roma completing the Mora deal.

Christopher Nkunku to RB Leipzig on loan with a €30m to €35m option is pointed at from both sides but has no agreed fee, and Nicolas Jackson of Chelsea, price cut to €70m, has interest from Villa, Tottenham, Atletico and Napoli, with nobody yet willing to pay it.

Two collapses worth recording. Nayef Aguerd's move from Marseille to Real Sociedad died on 13 August, and the two best-connected reporters in Spain tell opposite stories: Santi Aouna of Foot Mercato says Aguerd passed the medical and withdrew after a conversation with Pellegrino Matarazzo, while Matteo Moretto says he failed it.

On 15 August Aguerd said publicly that his medical "went perfectly" (El Desmarque). And Julian Alvarez is staying at Atletico Madrid, Diego Simeone saying on 18 August: "there is still market left and you know nothing surprises me."

Around the Leagues

Premier League

With no football played, the week belonged to the boardroom and the treatment table. The largest story is at Chelsea, where the Financial Times reported on 18 August that Todd Boehly and Mark Walter are in talks to sell their 12.8 per cent stakes each to majority owner Clearlake Capital, which holds 61.5 per cent and is led by Behdad Eghbali.

The club is valued at £5bn, which would roughly double the £325m each man put in four years ago. Boehly is set to step down as chairman at the end of this season, at the close of his five-year term. Ownership terms bar co-owners from selling to outside investors for the first ten years, and a rift over stadium plans has been reported since 2024. No party has commented.

At Everton, chief executive Angus Kinnear spent the week conducting an unusually frank public audit. He confirmed the club will not have a right-back available for Saturday's opener against Crystal Palace, said "I'm confident we'll bring a right-back in," and, on Iliman Ndiaye, offered the sentence every Everton supporter has learned to read carefully: "Our vision is very much that our best players like Ili are retained and they help us achieve what we all want."

Everton and Palace have already done business, swapping Brennan Johnson and Dwight McNeil straight across on 11 August, both on four-year deals, both fees undisclosed.

It is a genuinely rare structure, and it tidies up two awkward January stories at once: Johnson had joined Palace in January for £35m and made 26 appearances without scoring, while McNeil's move the other way had collapsed when paperwork was not filed in time.

The promoted clubs are not behaving like promoted clubs. Ipswich Town and Coventry City spent a combined £49.2m in a single day on 17 August, Ipswich taking Julio Enciso and Abdoul Ouattara from Strasbourg and Coventry signing Taiwo Awoniyi from Forest for £9m.

Newcastle added Amar Dedic from Benfica for a reported £30m on 18 August, having already sold Tonali and Guimaraes. Elsewhere, Manchester City's 115 charges remain unresolved, with no verdict since the hearing ended in December 2024, and the Women's Super League returns on 4 September expanded from 12 clubs to 14, Manchester City defending the title.

La Liga

Spain is the only one of the six leagues actually playing, and even then only partly. Matchday 1 has been split: five matches went ahead between 15 and 17 August, while the openers of clubs with players who reached the World Cup semi-finals or beyond were deferred to between 19 and 27 August, an agreement between LaLiga, the RFEF and the players' union to reduce injury risk. Espanyol lead the table on goals scored after a 3-0 win over Levante, Roberto Fernandez scoring twice inside 40 minutes.

Alaves beat Getafe 3-0 and Deportivo marked their return to the top flight after eight years with a 1-1 draw against Elche in which Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang scored on debut. Atletico Madrid host Malaga as this edition goes out.

Two rows are already running. The opening night produced an immediate refereeing controversy at Sevilla, where Ricardo de Burgos Bengoetxea awarded a penalty, saw it overturned by VAR, then gave another in the seventh minute of stoppage time that El Debate and others reported the replays did not support. Sevilla won 2-1. Rayo Vallecano's Alvaro Garcia said afterwards: "Three penalties. The match was even, but too much of a leading role for the person who should not be a lead character."

Separately, Celta against Osasuna was postponed on 13 August because of a problem with the Balaidos turf, identified in Spanish reporting as a fungus. Osasuna issued a statement expressing "absolute disagreement" with the new date of 27 August, saying both clubs had agreed the September international break would work and that the team most harmed by the postponement is once again the team most harmed by the fix.

Meanwhile Real Madrid open their season at Espanyol on Saturday under Jose Mourinho, their third head coach in eight months, after beating Schalke 3-0 in Gelsenkirchen to finish pre-season unbeaten.

Bundesliga

Germany starts last, on 28 August, but Saturday brings the Franz Beckenbauer Supercup at Signal Iduna Park, where Borussia Dortmund host Bayern Munich as last season's runners-up.

Bayern arrive as champions of a league they won on 19 April with four games to spare while scoring a record 122 goals, and with Harry Kane collecting the European Golden Shoe in Munich on 19 August for 36 Bundesliga goals, ahead of Erling Haaland and Kylian Mbappe. Bayern beat RB Leipzig 3-1 and Heidenheim 4-2 in friendlies, with Kane and Michael Olise returning as substitutes after extended post-World Cup breaks.

Below Bayern the picture is uneven. Eintracht Frankfurt lost 7-0 at Brentford on 15 August in their final friendly, captain Robin Koch going off injured at half time; the club's own report called it an expensive education. RB Leipzig lost their record signing Yan Diomande to Real Madrid for a reported €140m including bonuses and their replacement target Moreira to Milan, and Martin Demichelis said publicly that his sporting director "has work to do."

Sebastian Hoeness of Stuttgart, contracted to 2028, acknowledged on 19 August that clubs including Manchester City and Milan have made enquiries about him, saying it would "not now be surprising if there were perhaps one or two clubs that made an enquiry about me," as reported by sport.de on 19 August.

And the Bundeskartellamt delivered its final assessment on the 50+1 rule on 12 August, accepting the rule's aims while ruling that enforcement must be consistent and naming Leverkusen, Wolfsburg, RB Leipzig and Hannover 96 as needing to change. That is currently single-sourced in English and the authority's own document is worth reading before anyone declares 50+1 saved.

Serie A

Italy kicks off on Saturday and has already produced its first disaster. Lazio, beaten finalists in last season's Coppa Italia, went out of this season's competition at the first hurdle, losing 2-0 at home to Serie B side Mantova in Gennaro Gattuso's competitive debut, Ruocco scoring on 75 minutes and Cajazzo in stoppage time.

Gattuso was without six players through injury or fatigue. "It's a devastating blow," he said, per ANSA. "We must apologise and move forward with focus and unity rather than seeking excuses."

Il Messaggero and Leggo both marked him four out of ten for a nightmare debut. In the same round Palermo knocked out Lecce 2-0, Pisa went through on penalties against Empoli, and of the Serie A clubs playing on 17 August only Sassuolo won.

The league also arrives with a new refereeing regime. On 18 August the AIA held an online meeting with all 20 clubs, run by technical director Domenico Messina and the new CAN designator Daniele Orsato, whose predecessor Gianluca Rocchi self-suspended in late April while under investigation for sporting fraud over allegedly steered appointments. Calcio e Finanza set out the changes, which broadly mirror England's: VAR confined to clear and obvious error, a one-minute exile for treated players, a visible five-second count on restarts, and a strengthened captain protocol. "The legacy of the World Cup is that we saw how the central figure was the referee," Orsato said.

Not everyone is charmed by the calendar: Maurizio Sarri, now at Atalanta, called the transfer window dates "madness" and said that discussing the market endlessly is "an insult to my profession," per Tuttosport.

Ligue 1

France produced the result of the week. RC Lens beat Paris Saint-Germain 1-0 at Stade Bollaert-Delelis on 16 August to win the Trophee des Champions for the first time in the club's history, Florian Thauvin scoring on 32 minutes and Lens then playing roughly 47 minutes of normal time with ten men after Kyllian Antonio was sent off. Nuno Mendes was dismissed late for PSG, who had 69 per cent of the ball and 22 shots to Lens's eight.

Goalkeeper Robin Risser made six saves. Zinedine Zidane, confirmed as France head coach on 28 July, watched from the stand. Lens head coach Dino Toppmoller's dressing-room speech, published the next day by RMC, is the quote of the French week: "Lens is this: we fear no one.

We said it before the match, we can beat everyone here." Luis Enrique's verdict, on PSG's own site, was one line: "I'm pleased with what I saw, but frustrated."

Four days earlier PSG had won a second consecutive UEFA Super Cup, beating Aston Villa 2-1 in Salzburg, Khvicha Kvaratskhelia and Desire Doue scoring and Villa's 17-year-old Brian Madjo becoming the youngest player to start and score in the fixture, which was refereed by Omar Artan of Somalia, the first non-European to take a Super Cup. Elsewhere Monaco and Paul Pogba ended their partnership by mutual consent on 19 August, the club saying "the objectives initially set were only partially achieved" and Pogba replying that he would "always be grateful."

Nice signed the 37-year-old Axel Witsel on a free from Girona on 18 August as emergency cover after a serious knee injury to Laurent Abergel. And the league itself changes shape commercially: with beIN Sports declining to renew, Ligue 1+ becomes the sole broadcaster of every match, with the subscription rising to €19.99. The season also has zero midweek matchdays, a deliberate scheduling choice.

Major League Soccer

America is the only league of the six in the thick of it, and Nashville SC have taken it over. They sit on 43 points from 19 matches with a goal difference of plus 24 and only 15 conceded, five clear of Inter Miami, and they demolished them 4-1 on 15 August, Hany Mukhtar scoring twice in the second half after goalkeeper Brian Schwake saved a first-half Lionel Messi penalty.

Miami's fortnight has been grim: they went out of the Leagues Cup in the league phase on 12 August, losing 3-2 at home to Club Leon with three points from three matches, their worst campaign of the Messi era.

Messi came on at half time to a standing ovation, days after the death of his father and long-time agent Jorge Messi at 68. "For people to understand what Leo Messi is, I think it was proven today," Leon coach Javier Gandolfi said. "He's a competitive animal, a player who, in my opinion, is the best in history."

In the West, Vancouver Whitecaps lead on 37 points from 18 with the best goal difference in the league, and clinched a record ninth Cascadia Cup by winning 2-0 at Seattle Sounders on 17 August, Rayan Elloumi and Thomas Muller scoring in front of 30,026. Seattle are in freefall, that defeat extending a league losing run to seven, with Jesus Ferreira going off injured after 36 minutes and Brian Schmetzer conceding his team managed only "30 to 35 good minutes." Petar Musa of FC Dallas leads the Golden Boot race on 14 goals.

Chicago Fire have won five straight in all competitions for the first time since 2000. And at Orlando City, Antoine Griezmann has two goals, one assist and 14 chances created in his first six appearances since arriving from Atletico, team-mate Griffin Dorsey telling MLSsoccer that "as much as he's a star, it's very easy to be in a room with Grizi and feel like he's just another human being."

In Brief

  • Borussia Dortmund signed Joey Veerman from PSV on 18 August after activating a €21.5m release clause. Managing director Lars Ricken said the club had "deliberately focused our transfer activities this summer on the central midfield."
