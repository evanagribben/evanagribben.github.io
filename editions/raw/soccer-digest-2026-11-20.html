The Lead

Pochettino Walks Into The Oldest Fight In England

Four days into the job, with a squad he has barely met and a club sitting in the relegation zone, Manchester United's eighth permanent manager since Alex Ferguson begins at Anfield. The tactical problem, the personnel, and why the hardest possible start may also be the kindest.

There is no gentle way to begin at Manchester United, but there are degrees of cruelty, and the fixture computer has chosen the far end of the scale. Mauricio Pochettino officially became United's head coach on November 18. On Tuesday he was still on a touchline in another hemisphere, finishing his last assignment as head coach of the United States. On Sunday at 8:30am Pacific he walks out at Anfield.

He will have had roughly three days of training with his new players. Three of his staff, including long-time assistant Jesús Pérez, went ahead to Manchester after the first leg of the United States' double-header so that something resembling preparation could begin without him.

How United got here

Badly, and in public. Michael Carrick was dismissed on November 12 after one win in ten league matches. United sit 18th with eight points from ten games, a record of one win, five draws and four defeats, and a goal difference of minus five. It is the club's worst start to a league season in forty years.

The underlying numbers are worse than the table. United have not kept a clean sheet all season. They drew 4-4 at home with Aston Villa on November 8, a scoreline that functions as a diagnosis rather than a result. Only one league win separates them from the bottom two.

The buyout, and the hurry

Pochettino had been under contract with US Soccer through the 2030 World Cup following an extension. United activated his buyout clause in November and agreed a deal reported at two and a half years. He becomes the eighth permanent manager at Old Trafford since Ferguson left in May 2013, and the club have now circled him three times across a decade, passing him over for José Mourinho and then Erik ten Hag before finally moving.

United's statement leaned on his reputation for high-intensity attacking football, cohesive team-building and player development. All three are long-horizon qualities. None of them is likely to be visible on Sunday.

The tactical problem

A Pochettino side presses high and runs a lot, and both of those things require a level of conditioning this United squad has not demonstrated. Jamie Redknapp has argued that the first job is simply to get the group fit and to repair the back four. Gary Neville, long an advocate of the appointment, has been blunter still, saying Pochettino must take control of recruitment for any of it to matter.

There is, though, one asset a brand-new manager carries that nobody can scout. Liverpool head coach Andoni Iraola conceded this week that United arrive with an edge nobody at Anfield can prepare for, because nobody knows how Pochettino will set them up.

Only one of United's last nine managers, caretakers included, has lost his first Premier League match in charge. The Analyst, on the one statistic working in Pochettino's favour

Liverpool are not what they were

This is the part of the equation that makes Sunday live. Liverpool sit fourth on 18 points with five wins, three draws and two defeats, a goal difference of plus four from ten games. That is a long way from a side cruising.

Iraola, appointed in June after Arne Slot was dismissed at the end of last season, has not yet made Anfield the fortress it was. His team have created plenty at home without converting it, and their clearest advantage on Sunday is from set pieces. Virgil van Dijk, Dominik Szoboszlai and Ryan Gravenberch are all fit. Alexander Isak leads the line.

The record, and the history

Pochettino's personal history against Liverpool is poor: three wins from nineteen meetings. His first match as Chelsea manager was also against Liverpool, which is either a pleasing symmetry or an omen, depending on temperament.

He is the first United manager whose opening league fixture is against Liverpool. Against that, eight of the previous twelve United managers won their first match in charge, and only one of the last nine lost. The Analyst's pre-match model ran ten thousand simulations and gave Liverpool a 57.3 percent chance of winning to United's 20.5 percent.

What he actually has available

Harry Maguire and Patrick Dorgu both trained on Thursday. Lisandro Martínez returns from suspension. That is a stronger defensive hand than looked likely a week ago, which matters for a side yet to keep a clean sheet.

Chief executive Omar Berrada has insisted publicly this week that the club is heading in the right direction, a sentence that lands differently from 18th place. The Manchester United Supporters' Trust responded to the appointment by calling on the club to back its new manager properly. January, as the transfer section below sets out, is where that promise gets tested.

The Other Manchester Story Is A Courtroom One

City were found guilty on 114 of 115 charges. The appeal is set for mid-December, the sanction will not land until 2027, and in the meantime the team has lost three league matches in a row.

On September 29 the Premier League announced that Manchester City had been found guilty of all charges relating to serious breaches of its financial rules across the nine seasons from 2009-10 to 2017-18, and of the majority of charges relating to a failure to cooperate with the investigation. An independent commission upheld 114 of the 115 charges and described sponsorship arrangements as sham deals, finding that the club benefited from more than £900 million of disguised funding. Chief executive Richard Masters called it the most significant disciplinary case in the league's history.

City lodged what they termed a comprehensive appeal at 7pm on October 1, calling the verdict unsafe and arguing it contains material errors of law, principle and fact. Part of the appeal is expected to rest on the claim that the money came from the Abu Dhabi government as a sovereign body rather than from the club's owners.

The timetable that emerged this week

The Financial Times reported on November 13 that the appeal will be heard in mid-December and could run for several days. Football finance analyst Stefan Borson told talkSPORT this week that he expects the hearing to last between five and ten days, with a decision perhaps thirty to forty-five days afterwards, and he stressed that no sanction hearing has taken place at all yet. That comes after the appeal.

The available sanctions range from a reprimand and a fine through transfer bans and points deductions to expulsion from the competition. Rival clubs have briefed that they do not expect City to be in the Premier League next season if the appeal fails. Under Premier League rules the club cannot take a sanction to the Court of Arbitration for Sport.

And the football, which has not helped

City opened the season with seven straight wins and have since lost three consecutive league matches, the last a 3-1 defeat at Nottingham Forest. They are third on 21 points, six behind the leaders. Enzo Maresca, who replaced Pep Guardiola in the summer after Guardiola resigned in May, hosts Fulham on Saturday, Napoli in the Champions League on Tuesday and travels to the Emirates a week on Sunday.

Erling Haaland addressed the case during the international break and pushed back firmly. He pointed to a five-year contract signed in 2022 and a further extension in late 2024 that runs to 2034, and said plainly that he is a City player and is happy there. Reports in Spain that Florentino Pérez expects to sign him eventually are, for now, exactly that: reports.

Transfer News

United's January Is Already Being Written

A new manager, two named priority positions, one departure that looks settled and a Barcelona left-back whose situation has become untenable.

The message coming out of Old Trafford, relayed by Fabrizio Romano, is unambiguous: in January, United will do things. Pochettino will be part of those conversations rather than a bystander, and two positions have been identified as priorities. The first is centre-back. The second is a striker.

On the striker, Romano reported on Friday that United have already started exploring opportunities regardless of what happens with Joshua Zirkzee, whose exit would accelerate matters rather than cause them. Left-back could be added once Pochettino completes detailed recruitment discussions with the club.

Balde: serious and concrete, but not yet advanced

The name to watch is Alejandro Balde. The Barcelona left-back is barely playing, and Romano's framing has hardened over three weeks from a situation to watch into a chance he described as serious and concrete. Certainty tier: concrete interest. No fee has been agreed and no talks between clubs have been reported.

Gabriel to Madrid looks closest to done

The deal furthest along involves a player leaving. Romano has said that Real Madrid are set to make United teenager JJ Gabriel their first signing of the January window, with a here-we-go on the way. Gabriel cannot sign anything or complete a transfer before January 1. An earlier link to Paris Saint-Germain is dead. Certainty tier: agreement reported, not yet official.

And one that is not happening

Italian outlets linked Benjamin Šeško with Juventus in January. Romano has reported that Šeško wants to stay at United. Gazzetta dello Sport has also connected Pochettino with his former United States midfielder Weston McKennie, though Juventus's stance makes a January departure unlikely. Both sit at the rumour tier.

Milan Look North For A Leader

Italy's winter planning has begun early, and the most striking name on it plays at Anfield.

Reports in Italy this week, led by Luca Bianchin of Gazzetta dello Sport, have AC Milan considering Virgil van Dijk their number one defensive target ahead of next summer, with the captaincy floated as part of the pitch. It is a summer idea rather than a January one, and it sits firmly at the rumour tier: no approach, no valuation, no indication from Liverpool that the player is available.

The wider context is that Milan have set guidelines for their 2027 business under sporting leadership that Gazzetta characterised as ambitious, surprising and Italian. Milan sit fourth in Serie A on 24 points and host Frosinone on Sunday.

Juventus want another striker anyway

Juventus signed Randal Kolo Muani from Paris Saint-Germain and Nick Woltemade from Newcastle United in the summer and may still enter the market for a forward in January. They are seventh on 18 points, which is not where the club expects to be in late November, and the January window is the only lever available before the spring.

Germany Is Where The Prices Get Set

Leipzig are holding, Dortmund have a clause that does not bite until summer, and Bayern have not let go of a Liverpool teenager.

RB Leipzig have made their position clear on Antonio Nusa. Romano reports that Leipzig are not pushing to sell, are demanding a substantial fee and would prefer to keep the Norway winger until the summer. Their reasoning is commercial and blunt: having already generated a large sum last summer from the sale of Ousmane Diomandé, selling Nusa in January would gut the attack mid-season. Romano expects him to move in 2027. Certainty tier: interest, with the selling club resisting.

Mundo Deportivo reports from Germany that Nico Schlotterbeck, following his contract renewal at Borussia Dortmund last April, carries a release clause that only becomes affordable in next summer's window. Not a January story.

Two to file away

Christian Falk of Bild reports that Bayern Munich retain their interest in Liverpool teenager Rio Ngumoha after exploring a deal in the summer, with a vacancy possibly opening on their left side. Separately, Bild reports Liverpool among the clubs interested in Wael Mohya, the 17-year-old Mönchengladbach attacking midfielder, with a price point around 20 million euros shaped by his contract position and his club's dire league situation. Both are rumour tier.

The Rumour Mill

Everything still simmering, sorted by how close it actually is. The ladder runs: rumour, concrete interest, advanced talks, fee agreed, personal terms, medical, official.

Alexander-Arnold to Liverpool, ruled out. Romano reported on November 18 that Trent Alexander-Arnold has no intention of leaving Real Madrid in January and that a return to Anfield is not being considered by any party. Mourinho wants him to stay. No concrete discussions between the clubs have taken place. Treat the loan reports as dead.

Ronald Araújo's buyout clause, decision taken. Romano has reported Liverpool's position on whether to trigger the Barcelona defender's clause, reported in England at around £46 million. Advanced interest has not materialised into action.

Luka Vušković to Liverpool, summer. Florian Plettenberg of Sky Germany reports the centre-back is on Liverpool's shortlist for a summer move, with Brighton valuing him at over 100 million dollars. Plettenberg adds that the Croatian's own ambition is eventually to play for Real Madrid. Rumour tier, long horizon.

Nico Paz to Manchester United. Romano has addressed United's chances of signing the Como playmaker in January. Como's own situation complicates it: they are fourth in the Champions League league phase and fifth in Serie A, and forward Moise Kean is now out for at least two months. Selling their creative hub mid-season would be extraordinary. Rumour tier.

Also live. United are exploring a deal for Rayan Aït-Nouri. Romano flags Johan Bakayoko as a genuine January possibility. Bournemouth's Alex Scott has multiple suitors. And the perennial: Real Madrid and Barcelona are both described as monitoring Haaland's situation should City's sanction change his calculus, which is speculation stacked on an appeal that has not been heard.

Around the Leagues

Six Competitions, Restarting At Once

Where every league stands as domestic football returns from the November international break.

Premier League

The table has a settled look at the top and chaos everywhere else. Brighton are second on 22 points with a goal difference of plus nineteen, the best in the division, built on results like a 5-0 home win over Crystal Palace in October and a 5-0 win at Coventry in September. For a club that has spent a decade being described as well-run, this is the season the description has started to look like an understatement.

Behind them, Manchester City have gone from seven straight wins to three straight defeats and sit third on 21. Liverpool are fourth on 18 under Andoni Iraola, who replaced Arne Slot in June. Chelsea are fifth on 16 in Xabi Alonso's first season, a campaign that has already produced wins over Brighton and Manchester United alongside defeats to Tottenham and Sunderland, a League Cup exit to Liverpool and a 5-1 thrashing of Bournemouth. Alonso also inherited a change of ownership: Clearlake Capital took full control in September, ending Todd Boehly's chairmanship in a deal valuing the club at £5 billion.

The bottom is where the managers are changing

Two clubs have changed manager this season and both did it on the same day. United sacked Carrick on November 12 and appointed Pochettino. Crystal Palace sacked Pierre Sage on the same date with the club bottom on seven points, after four straight league defeats and a sixteen-game tenure. On November 16 Palace appointed Thomas Frank on a deal running to June 2029, chairman Steve Parish citing his Premier League experience. Frank, who took Brentford into the top flight and then endured a bruising eight months at Tottenham, begins away at Coventry City on Saturday. Coventry are themselves back in the Premier League after a twenty-five-year absence, the longest of any returning club in the competition's history.

The relegation picture is genuinely open: Nottingham Forest on nine, Coventry on nine, Manchester United on eight, Sunderland on eight and Palace on seven are separated by two points. Hull City, promoted this season, sit 13th on eleven and have the longest winless run in the division alongside United.

La Liga

Three clubs, twelve games, five points between them. Barcelona lead on 33 points with eleven wins and one defeat, a goal difference of plus thirty-five that dwarfs everything else in Spain. Hansi Flick's side host Villarreal on Sunday with Jules Koundé, Dani Olmo, Pau Cubarsí and Frenkie de Jong all back in training.

The story of the autumn, though, has been the club in second. José Mourinho returned to Real Madrid this season and, after a start one ESPN writer described as flawed, won his first Clásico since coming back, 2-1 at Camp Nou on October 25. Madrid are second on 30 points and have a perfect record in the Champions League league phase. The next Clásico is not until May 9. Atlético Madrid are third on 28 under Diego Simeone, who has not won the league since 2020-21 and has a case this time.

Elsewhere, Real Betis are fourth on 26 and Real Sociedad fifth on 22. Saturday brings the Seville derby, Sevilla against Betis, with sixth-placed Sevilla eight points adrift of their neighbours. At the other end, newly promoted Málaga are last on five points and still looking for a first win of the season from twelve attempts.

Bundesliga

Nine matches in, Germany has a title race. Bayern Munich and Borussia Dortmund are level on 21 points, both unbeaten with six wins and three draws, separated only by goal difference: Bayern plus twenty, Dortmund plus nine. They drew 2-2 in the first Klassiker on October 31. SC Freiburg are a point back in third on 19, then RB Leipzig on 18 and Bayer Leverkusen on 16.

Bayern opened Matchday 10 at home to 1. FC Köln on Friday evening in Munich, a match under way as this edition went to press. Vincent Kompany returned to the touchline for it after recent health problems. Dortmund travel to Borussia Mönchengladbach on Saturday morning Pacific; Gladbach are bottom on four points with one win in nine, which gives the fixture an edge it would not otherwise have.

The season's genuine curiosity is SV Elversberg, who are making their Bundesliga debut as the 59th club in the competition's history and sit seventh on 13 points, ahead of Stuttgart, Hoffenheim and Eintracht Frankfurt. They host Freiburg on Saturday in a 10,000-capacity stadium. Schalke 04, back after three years away, are 17th.

Serie A

The best story in European football right now is in Rome, and it is not the one you would expect. Lazio lead Serie A on 29 points from eleven games, unbeaten with nine wins and two draws. They went alone to the top on November 1 with a 2-1 comeback win over Cagliari, Andrea Pinamonti scoring in the 83rd minute. It ended a wait of 5,836 days since the club last stood alone at the summit of Italian football, in 2010 under Edy Reja.

What makes it improbable is the starting point. Lazio were banned from transfer activity last summer for failing financial tests, sold two key players in January without replacing them adequately and finished ninth, their worst in twelve years. Gennaro Gattuso arrived in June and has asked his players not to look at the table, saying they might get dizzy, while setting a public target of forty points. TNT Sports this week ran the Italian Leicester City comparison seriously.

Behind them: Inter Milan, the reigning champions, second on 28 with a perfect home record. Roma third on 27 under Gian Piero Gasperini, one of only four teams in sixty-five years to score more than thirty goals in their opening eleven Serie A matches. AC Milan fourth on 24. Como fifth on 23, which understates them: they also sit fourth in the Champions League league phase, having beaten Lens 9-1. Napoli, champions two seasons ago, are tenth on 15 and lost 2-0 at Juventus on November 1.

Ligue 1

Paris Saint-Germain lead on 23 points and arrive at Nice on Saturday having won their last eleven matches in all competitions. Luis Enrique's side are the reigning European champions and are top of the Champions League league phase with a perfect twelve points and a goal difference of plus fifteen, including a 6-0 win over Villarreal and a 4-2 win over Barcelona.

The chasing pack is unfamiliar. Lille are second on 21 and host fifth-placed Lyon on Saturday. Paris FC, in only their second season back in the top flight, are third on 20. Marseille are fourth on 18 under Bruno Génésio, having won their last five. Nice, PSG's opponents, are sixth on 17 and have won five of their last six.

France has also had the busiest managerial month in Europe. Lyon's Paulo Fonseca resigned on November 4 with the club third, replaced by Carlos Vicens on November 9. Lens sacked Yannick Cahuzac on November 10 and hired Didier Digard on November 13, which is notable because Le Havre had sacked Digard on October 31. Le Havre, bottom at the time, appointed Valérien Ismaël on November 12.

Major League Soccer

The regular season ended on November 7 and the postseason began on Thursday. Both wild-card matches went to the visitors. FC Cincinnati came from a goal down to win 3-1 at Orlando City, Evander scoring twice within five minutes of the restart to eliminate Antoine Griezmann's side. In the West, Real Salt Lake drew 1-1 at Seattle and won 4-2 on penalties at Lumen Field, goalkeeper Rafael Cabral saving twice.

Round One is a best-of-three series and starts this weekend. Nashville SC, Supporters' Shield winners, face Cincinnati. Inter Miami, the defending MLS Cup champions with Lionel Messi, face Columbus Crew. Real Salt Lake meet Vancouver, last season's runners-up. The conference semifinals are December 5 and 6, the conference finals December 11 and 12, and MLS Cup is on Friday, December 18. One footnote from the regular season: for the first time since 2009, no club from the New York metropolitan area qualified.
