If you read nothing else

Arsenal won 1-0 at Anfield on Sunday, a first league win there since 2012, and lead the Premier League by three points on 24.

William Saliba is back in full training and in the squad at Slavia Prague after three months out; Bukayo Saka's thigh injury still has no return date.

The Lead

Fourteen Years, One Goal, And A Defensive Masterclass At Anfield

Arsenal did not out-play Liverpool on Sunday. They out-organised them, and in the process they took the league lead and confirmed the identity that will decide their season.

T he goal that ended fourteen years of waiting was not a work of art. Arsenal pressed the ball high, Liverpool goalkeeper Alisson Becker misplaced a pass to Jérémy Jacquet, and the ball squirted loose. Noni Madueke and Declan Rice worked it down the right, Rice overlapped and drove a low cross through the six-yard box, and Kai Havertz tapped it in at the far post. Sixteen minutes gone.

It was the only goal Arsenal needed, and the only goal they got.

The bare facts undersell what followed. Mikel Arteta's side had not won a league match at Anfield since 2012. They had lost a League Cup tie at League Two Fleetwood Town four days earlier, on penalties, with a sixteen-year-old sent off in the closing minutes.

The response was a performance of almost pathological discipline: 74 minutes of a compact back four, a shrinking pitch, and a Liverpool side that finished the match without registering a single shot on target.

Liverpool had 56 per cent of the ball and 17 shots to Arsenal's 10. The expected-goals numbers were almost level, 1.05 to 0.98. The number that matters is 52 against 24: touches in the opposition box, Liverpool's figure double Arsenal's. The hosts got into the area constantly and found nothing there.

Florian Wirtz struck the crossbar from a free-kick. Alexis Mac Allister headed over. David Raya did not make a save of consequence until he held Víctor Muñoz's late header.

The back four is the project now

Five goals conceded in nine Premier League matches is the best record in the division. Two conceded in three Champions League games is jointly the best in that competition. Arsenal have done this without William Saliba, who has not played for the club since the Champions League final defeat to Paris Saint-Germain in May.

Ezri Konsa, the £55m summer signing from Aston Villa, has instead formed a partnership with Gabriel Magalhães that Arteta has had no reason to break up.

On Sunday that pair were flanked by Jurriën Timber and Riccardo Calafiori, with Rice and Bruno Guimarães screening in front. Arteta was candid afterwards that this was not the plan. Liverpool's verticality, he said, forced Arsenal into defending far more than they wanted, and he admitted "we had an element of luck as well."

"I don't think there is a better team in world football that defends quite like Arsenal defend."

Alan Shearer, on Match of the Day

Havertz keeps answering the question

Havertz's finish was his 46th Premier League goal, which took him past Ilkay Gündogan as the competition's highest-scoring German. It is the kind of record that tends to be filed under trivia, but it speaks to something Arsenal have relied on heavily this autumn.

With Saka injured and Viktor Gyökeres yet to start a league game, Havertz has led the line in all nine Premier League matches and is the club's joint-top scorer.

Around him, the summer rebuild is bedding in at different speeds. Rice now has four league assists, more than any Arsenal player. Christos Tzolis, back from a hamstring problem, drew Alisson's best save of the afternoon with a first-time shot. Bruno Guimarães, the £75m arrival from Newcastle United, curled an effort just wide and otherwise did the unglamorous work of holding the middle of the pitch for 62 minutes.

What it was worth

Arsenal arrived at Anfield level on points with Manchester City. They left three clear, because City lost to Brighton & Hove Albion the same day.

Twenty-four points after nine games is a total Arsenal have bettered at this stage only twice in Premier League history, in 2004/05 and 2007/08. Arteta, asked about the title, said what managers say: "it's too early." He then named the thing actually in front of him, "another big, big, big fight in Prague."

There is a case that this was the single most instructive 90 minutes of Arsenal's season. Last year's title was won on control.

Sunday was won on something harder to coach and easier to lose: the willingness of eleven players to defend a one-goal lead at Anfield for 74 minutes without panicking. Gary Neville called it "a defensive masterclass." Theo Walcott, watching, said the quiet part out loud: "They are going to be difficult to stop this year."

The caveats are real. Arsenal were fortunate that VAR did not look again at Alexander Isak's shirt being tugged by Timber in the first half, an incident Liverpool manager Andoni Iraola said would have been a penalty "100 per cent" had Isak gone down. Rice's own verdict on the Anfield atmosphere, "It's nothing I've ever seen before," hints at how close it ran. But the table does not record hints.

Saliba Walks Back Into A Side That Learned To Live Without Him

After a three-month absence, the centre-back is back in training and in the squad in Prague. Arteta's problem is now a pleasant one.

William Saliba has not played for Arsenal since they lost the Champions League final to Paris Saint-Germain on penalties in May. A back problem he had been managing late last season worsened at the World Cup, where he was substituted in France's semi-final defeat to Spain. On Tuesday, Mikel Arteta confirmed he had completed full first-team training and travelled to Prague.

"He played 45 minutes in closed door last week, and he feels ready to go," Arteta said. "He's in a really good place." Pressed on whether he would actually use him, the manager was deliberately non-committal: "Whether we use him or not is now a matter of what the team needs."

That line matters, because the team does not obviously need him. Ezri Konsa and Gabriel Magalhães have conceded five league goals between them. What Arsenal need is bodies: ten matches are scheduled between the coming international break and the end of the year, including Manchester City on November 29, Tottenham Hotspur on December 6 and Real Madrid in the Champions League on December 9.

Arteta framed the return in exactly those terms. "He's built the capacity that we needed, and now it's about giving him minutes," he said, noting that "a centre-back normally has to be able to play 90 minutes."

The useful detail is positional flexibility: Konsa can fill in at either full-back, and Saliba has played on both sides of a back four for club and country. Four senior centre-backs for ten games in eight weeks is not a luxury. It is the minimum.

Transfer News

Rice Is About To Become The Best-Paid Player In Arsenal's History

Arteta says a new deal is "a matter of time." The reported figure is £350,000 a week, and the timing is not accidental.

The most consequential piece of Arsenal business this winter may not involve a signing at all. Declan Rice is in the final two years of the contract he signed on joining from West Ham United in 2023, and Mikel Arteta used his pre-Liverpool press conference to confirm that an extension is coming.

"Yes, my feeling is that he is very happy here," Arteta said when asked whether he expected an agreement. "Every conversation I have with him, he wants to continue here for the long term. It will be a matter of time when we resolve that."

The numbers, with the usual caution

Rice's current deal runs to the summer of 2028, with a reported club option for a further twelve months. He is one of nine Arsenal players whose contracts expire that summer. Capology estimates his present salary at around £260,000 a week. The new terms being reported are £350,000 a week, which would move him past Bukayo Saka, on a reported £300,000, as the club's highest earner.

Two caveats are worth holding on to. First, the only wage figures in the public domain are estimates and reports, not confirmed terms.

Second, the £350,000 benchmark has been reached at Arsenal before, by Mesut Özil and Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang, and in both cases the headline number reportedly included performance add-ons. Certainty tier: advanced talks. Fabrizio Romano reported in October that Arsenal were closing in; Arteta has now effectively confirmed the direction. Nothing is signed.

Why it is the right player to pay

Rice has started all nine Premier League games and leads the squad with four league assists, including the cross for Kai Havertz's winner at Anfield. Jamie Carragher described him as "world class" after Sunday's match. For a club whose defensive record is now its main competitive asset, paying a record wage to the player who anchors the midfield in front of that defence is an easier sell than most record wages are.

Sources: Arseblog News, Sports Mole.

Gyökeres Has Not Started A League Game, And His Camp Has Noticed

Last season's 21-goal striker is a substitute in the title race he helped win. January is the obvious pressure point.

Viktor Gyökeres scored 21 goals in all competitions last season as Arsenal won the league. This season he has not started a single Premier League match. Kai Havertz has played all nine as the centre-forward, and Gyökeres has come off the bench, including for the final six minutes plus stoppage time at Anfield.

TEAMtalk reported in mid-October that the Swede is prepared to push for a move in January, with his frustration centred specifically on the absence of league starts rather than on his overall minutes.

Atlético Madrid are described as retaining their interest, with unnamed clubs in Italy, Germany and the Premier League monitoring. Certainty tier: concrete interest, with no bid reported and no fee in the public domain. This is a single-sourced report at origin, picked up widely since.

The complicating factor

Arsenal's own reported interest in Atlético's Julián Álvarez has, according to the same reporting, hardened doubts in Gyökeres's camp about his long-term role. That is the sort of circular transfer logic that often precedes nothing at all. It is also exactly the kind of signal worth logging in early November, because it is the shape a January saga takes before it becomes one.

The counter-argument is simple arithmetic. Arsenal have ten fixtures between the international break and New Year's Day, Saka has no return date, and Havertz has started every league game. Selling a proven 21-goal striker in the middle of that run would require a replacement Arsenal are not obviously positioned to buy. Gyökeres did start the Fleetwood tie, and scored.

Sources: CaughtOffside, reporting TEAMtalk; ESPN.

The January Attacker Question, And What Arsenal Can Actually Afford

David Ornstein's read is that Arsenal left themselves room in the summer. The financial trackers suggest the room is real but not unlimited.

The structural case for an Arsenal forward signing in January was made in the summer, by omission. Gabriel Martinelli left for Al Hilal and was not replaced. Gabriel Jesus joined Barcelona and was not replaced. Christos Tzolis arrived from Club Brugge, but principally as cover for the departed Leandro Trossard. Ethan Nwaneri went to Borussia Dortmund on loan, with a recall option written in.

David Ornstein, in an Athletic question-and-answer session, put it this way: Arsenal "have given themselves the opportunity to" sign an attacker, and "I could see something happening because they wanted to." He also said he knew of nothing "brewing," and that on current evidence the squad "doesn't look like they need it." His stated condition was blunt: "the key for them is to keep people fit."

The financial ceiling

Ornstein's note of caution was that Arsenal are "close to UEFA's financial rules" and "have got to be very careful on the financial side of things."

The independent Bean Counter tracker, whose figures are estimates rather than official numbers, puts Arsenal's UEFA squad-cost ratio for calendar 2026 at 58.4 per cent against the 70 per cent limit, which it reads as roughly £95.8m a year of squad-cost headroom after the Martinelli sale. On Premier League PSR it estimates Arsenal have used close to none of the £105m three-year loss allowance, with the final assessment due in January 2027.

In plain terms: the constraint on a January signing is not a hard ceiling. It is the recurring annual wage bill, which is the number squad-cost ratios actually measure, and which a record Rice contract will push upward. That is the trade-off Arsenal are managing, and it is why "we could, if we wanted to" is a more accurate summary of the position than "we will."

Sources: Football365, reporting David Ornstein for The Athletic; The Bean Counter.

The Rumour Mill

Six live Arsenal threads, with the certainty on each stated plainly. None of these is close.

Endrick, Real Madrid forward

Italian outlet Calciomercato placed Arsenal among the clubs considering a winter move for the 20-year-old, alongside AC Milan, Roma and Aston Villa.

The underlying facts are checkable: Endrick has played around 112 minutes this season under José Mourinho, sits behind Arda Güler and Yan Diomande on the right and behind Kylian Mbappé through the middle, and scored eight goals in 21 games on loan at Lyon last spring. No fee or loan structure has been reported. Certainty tier: rumour and link, single-sourced, and treated sceptically even by the outlets reporting it.

Eli Junior Kroupi, Bournemouth forward

Reports have Arsenal considering a January approach, with Bournemouth described as reluctant to sell mid-season. Kroupi has recently returned from a broken foot and has three substitute appearances to his name. Manchester United, Tottenham Hotspur, Bayern Munich and Paris Saint-Germain have also been linked. Certainty tier: rumour and link. No fee reported, no bid reported.

Kenan Yildiz, Juventus winger

A recurring link with no movement behind it. Juventus have shown no inclination to sell, and head coach Luciano Spalletti dismissed the idea in the summer. One report suggests even a €100m offer would not change the club's position.

The theoretical opening is that Juventus sit seventh in Serie A, six points off the Champions League places, and may need to sell if they miss out. Yildiz has one Serie A appearance this season and has been injured. Certainty tier: rumour and link, with the selling club actively resisting.

Martin Ødegaard, and Bayern's standing interest

Sky Germany reported that Bayern Munich are monitoring the Arsenal captain's contract situation. Martin Ødegaard's deal runs to 2028 and Arsenal have held early, informal talks about an extension. Other European clubs have reportedly asked about him. The reporting itself is careful to say a move is not on the cards in the near future, and that is the right reading. Certainty tier: monitoring, no talks with the player reported.

Julián Álvarez, Atlético Madrid forward

Álvarez belongs in this edition less as a transfer story than as a piece of context. Ornstein cited him, alongside Vinícius Júnior and Morgan Rogers, as evidence that Arsenal's ownership has been willing to fund major attacking moves when the player was open to it. He has also been named in reporting as the signing whose prospect unsettled Gyökeres. Certainty tier: historic interest, cited as evidence of capacity rather than as a live pursuit.

The smaller threads worth a line

Arsenal have been linked with Eduardo Pape, a Brazilian teenager, and with Mohamed Belloumi of Hull City, reportedly for 2027 rather than this window. A separate report has Victor Ozhianvuna of Shamrock Rovers describing his exit in January. Certainty tier on all three: rumour and link, single-sourced, and reported by outlets this Digest treats as leads rather than proof. They are listed here for completeness, not because any of them is close.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Slavia Prague: Champions At Home, Cursed In Europe

One win in 23 Champions League matches, and the record for the longest winless run is now within reach. Arsenal arrive on Wednesday.

Slavia Prague are the most comfortable team in Czech football and the least comfortable in this competition. They lead the Czech First League by seven points, having won their last two domestic matches. In the Champions League they have one point from three games and sit 32nd of 36.

The longer record is stranger still. Slavia have won one of their 23 Champions League matches, a debut victory over Steaua Bucharest in September 2007. Since then: eight draws and 14 defeats across four campaigns. AEK Athens hold the competition record of 24 games without a win, set between 1992 and 2006. Slavia's current run is 22, so two more without a victory would match it.

Jindřich Trpišovský has managed the club since 2017 and was candid about what the streak does to him. The longer it runs, he said, "the more unpleasant it is," and he admitted he broods over it more than he used to. His assessment of Wednesday was unsentimental: everything would need to go Slavia's way, and the team must give 100 per cent to have a chance.

Who Arsenal must stop, and where Slavia leak

The principal attacking threat is David Moses. Behind him, defender David Zima has set the tone publicly, calling the competition "a football university" and arguing the team cannot stress about needing a result now. Tomáš Chorý is not expected to play for the club again. Lukáš Provod and Ange N'Guessan are doubts, and Ivan Schranz is recovering from a long illness. Previews put Slavia's absentees at five first-team regulars through injury or suspension.

The vulnerability is straightforward: Slavia have conceded seven goals in three European games. They were reduced to ten men against Lens and led 2-1 in the 89th minute before losing 3-2. They then lost 2-0 at Sabah in Baku, a result Vladimír Šmicer, the former Slavia striker and 2005 Champions League winner, singled out as the real failure. Losing to Arsenal and Bayern Munich, Šmicer argued, is not the problem. Losing in Baku is.

The head-to-head offers Slavia nothing. Arsenal won 3-0 in Prague in last season's league phase and 7-0 in London in 2007, a result that remains among Arsenal's largest European wins. Slavia have not beaten them. The one genuine risk is the one Mikel Arteta named himself: a trip to Prague four days after Anfield, with rotation likely, against a side with a record to break and a crowd that will treat it as a final.

The Squad

Ten Games, Four Centre-Backs, And One Winger Nobody Can Date

Arteta's squad is deeper than it was a month ago in every department except the one that matters most.

The Saka problem has no timeline

Bukayo Saka picked up a thigh injury in the Champions League draw at Bayern Munich on October 21. Arsenal have never graded it publicly. A second MRI was required two days later because of fluid around the injury, and Mikel Arteta described the signs then as "not good."

By October 30 the manager said Saka was "feeling much better than he was a few days ago," but had not been cleared, and that a further scan was needed to see how the fluid was resolving and whether the tendon was involved. "Afterwards we'll know more about it," he said. As of this edition, no result and no return date have been published.

One injury tracker places a possible return at the Brentford game on December 2. Asked whether Saka would miss England's November internationals, Arteta said: "Looks like it, but we'll have to wait and see next week." Saka has three goals in seven league appearances this season and remains the squad's only genuine right-sided specialist, which is why Noni Madueke has started the last two matches there.

The defensive record is the tactical story

Five goals conceded in nine league games is the best figure in the Premier League, and two in three Champions League games is jointly the best in Europe. The structure has been consistent: a back four with Ezri Konsa and Gabriel Magalhães central, Declan Rice and one of Bruno Guimarães, Mikel Merino or Martín Zubimendi in front, and a high press that Arsenal are now willing to abandon mid-match when the game demands it.

Sunday was the clearest example. Arsenal pressed to force Alisson's error for the goal, then spent 74 minutes in a low-to-mid block. Alan Shearer's verdict, that no team in world football defends quite like Arsenal, is the kind of pundit hyperbole that happens to be supported by the table.

Individual form, and the Dowman question

Rice leads the squad with four league assists. Kai Havertz has three goals and has started every league game. Riccardo Calafiori has a goal and two assists from left-back, and Ben White three assists without a goal, which makes the two full-backs Arsenal's most productive defenders. Viktor Gyökeres and Eberechi Eze, two of the squad's most expensive attacking options, have no league goals between them.

Then there is Max Dowman, who is 16 until December 31 and has become a genuine selection question rather than a curiosity.

He scored twice in the 4-2 Carabao Cup win over Ipswich Town in September. Arteta has publicly backed him since the red card, and two of football.london's writers picked him to start in Prague. The three-match domestic ban is a reminder of how exposed a 16-year-old is in cup football, not a judgement on whether he is ready.

Sources: The False 9, Sports Mole, ESPN, Daily Cannon.

 
Hale End

A Debut, A Dismissal, And A New Man Running The Place

The first team keeps promoting from Hale End. The under-21s, meanwhile, are still looking for a league win.

Ceadach O'Neill, in at the deep end

Ceadach O'Neill, 18, made his senior Arsenal debut in the Carabao Cup tie at Fleetwood Town, and it was a first start rather than a cameo. He lasted 60 minutes in a 4-2-3-1 that also contained the 17-year-old Marli Salmon at centre-back and the 16-year-old Max Dowman. Arsenal went out on penalties. O'Neill had scored the under-21s' only goal in their 4-1 defeat at Leicester City two days earlier.

The under-21s are the problem

David Horseman's side are without a Premier League 2 win this season: four draws and two defeats from six games. Arseblog's assessment was unusually direct, attributing the struggles partly to constant rotation hurting cohesion, and arguing that some squad members should have been loaned out in the summer to clear space for under-18 players. The team, it concluded, lacks dynamism.

There was better news in the EFL Trophy, where the under-21s progressed in late October despite the league form, with an away tie at AFC Wimbledon next in that competition. The split is a familiar one at this level: cup football rewards a side that can raise itself for one night, league football exposes a side that changes every week.

The new academy director gives his first long interview

Pascal De Maesschalck, 52, took over as academy director roughly three months ago, arriving from Strasbourg, where he was technical director. He succeeded Per Mertesacker, ending a club association that went back to 2011. His first substantial interview, published this week, was striking mostly for its humility: "I actually still have no idea how they ended up with me."

On priorities he was unambiguous. "You can have the best facilities or operations, but with the wrong players, it won't work," he said, describing the job as watching a great many matches and building relationships with players and their families, because "it remains a 'people's business'."

On Arteta: "His training sessions, I have never seen anything like that anywhere else." And on ambition, a sentence worth keeping: "Our ambition is to write the definitive training story of England."

Arsenal Women

Seventh, Twelve Points Back, And Conceding To The First Shot Again

Arsenal are flying in Europe and sinking in the league. The gap between those two things is now the whole story of Renée Slegers' season.

Another late rescue, another two points dropped

Arsenal drew 1-1 with Aston Villa at the Emirates on Sunday. Lucia Kendall released Chasity Grant on 25 minutes, Grant's shot deflected off Steph Catley and looped over Misa Rodríguez, and that was Villa's only shot of the match. Alessia Russo headed in Stina Blackstenius' cross off the left post on 85 minutes.

It was the second consecutive Women's Super League match in which Arsenal conceded from the opponent's first shot. They are seventh with 10 points, twelve behind leaders Chelsea, and have won one of their last five league games. Arsenal have now trailed 1-0 in three consecutive home league games for the first time since 2015.

The diagnosis is no longer about the low block

Arsenal dominated the ball and the shot count against Villa and created almost nothing of quality. Tim Stillman's analysis described a "horseshoe" attacking shape around a deep defence: volume without incision. Opta logged one big chance from a high shot count, a ratio that has become the season's signature.

Daily Cannon's tactical read found the more alarming pattern at the other end. In three of Arsenal's four home games, the opposition have had one shot on target and scored from it. The Villa goal came from a long pass over a six-player press, a quick switch wide, and a simple pass that left defenders isolated one against one.

The selection question, and the manager

Renée Slegers had built new partnerships before the October international break: Taylor Hinds and Caitlin Foord left, Riola Reuteler and Ona Batlle right, Russo behind Olivia Smith. A double half-time substitution broke them up, removing Hinds and Reuteler, the two players who had only recently begun earning minutes. Blackstenius did not come on until the 82nd minute.

Stillman's conclusion is the sharpest thing written about Arsenal Women this season: managers reveal who they trust under pressure, and Slegers' circle of trust is shrinking even as her squad grows. Victoria Pelova is in limited-trust limbo. Batlle, signed largely as a left-back, had not played there since the Birmingham game. Slegers was visibly frustrated at the final whistle, which is out of character.

Daily Cannon suggests a decision on the manager could come around the end of November, during the international break. That is speculation, clearly labelled as such, and it is worth separating from the facts. The facts are that Arsenal are mid-table in the WSL and top-eight in Europe, and that those two things cannot both be true for much longer.

In Brief

Four men on the FIFPRO shortlist. David Raya, Gabriel Magalhães, William Saliba and Declan Rice are among the 26 men nominated for the FIFPRO World 11, voted on by 26,317 professionals from 75 countries. The winning teams are announced on Monday, November 9.

The Mac Allister flashpoint. Alexis Mac Allister was booked late at Anfield after a confrontation with Noni Madueke, going down holding his face following forehead contact. VAR did not send Madueke off. Madueke told Viaplay it was "a natural movement." Gary Neville called the reaction "a bit over the top"; Jamie Carragher called it "a joke from Mac Allister."

The penalty Liverpool did not get. Jurriën Timber pulled Alexander Isak's shirt in the first half and VAR did not intervene. Andoni Iraola said it would have been given "100 per cent" had Isak gone down. Virgil van Dijk's verdict was more resigned: "In the end, it wasn't given and we lose the game unfortunately, so well done to Arsenal."

No major Emirates expansion. Following an Advisory Board meeting attended by co-chair Josh Kroenke and chief executive Richard Garlick, Arsenal confirmed in early October that they will pursue "continued evolution of Emirates Stadium" rather than a large-scale rebuild. Current capacity is 60,704. A working group had reportedly examined an increase toward 80,000, which would have required moving home games during construction.

Safe standing, in phases. The Clock End gets safe standing this season and the North Bank in 2027/28. Arsenal have said neither will increase capacity. Legends Global became official caterer for 2026/27 under a new "1886 by Arsenal" food and beverage identity.

The financial position, per the trackers. Independent estimates put Arsenal's UEFA squad-cost ratio at 58.4 per cent against a 70 per cent limit, with roughly £95.8m a year of headroom, and close to zero of the £105m Premier League PSR allowance used. These are estimates, not club figures, and the final PSR assessment falls in January 2027.

Bayern are watching Ødegaard. Sky Germany reported that Bayern Munich have kept contact with the Arsenal captain's representatives. Martin Ødegaard's contract runs to 2028 and early, informal extension talks have taken place. No move is reported to be imminent.

Arsenal's remaining European draw. After Prague come Borussia Dortmund on November 24, Real Madrid on December 9, Real Betis on January 20 and Sabah on January 27. All four are currently scheduled for 12:00pm Pacific and carried by Paramount+ in the United States, per ESPN's fixture list.
