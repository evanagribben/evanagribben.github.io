The Lead

Four without a win, and De Bruyne is coming back

A home draw with Fulham left City six points off the top and booed off. Now Napoli, missing eleven players, bring an old friend to the Etihad before the trip to Arsenal.

One week was enough to show Manchester City how thin the line is between a bad run and a crisis. Saturday's 1-1 draw with Fulham at the Etihad was the fourth league game in a row without a win, and the boos at full time (The False 9) said the rest.

On Tuesday Enzo Maresca's side host Napoli in the Champions League (8:00 p.m. UK, 12:00 p.m. Pacific, on Paramount+ in the US). On Sunday they go to Arsenal, six points clear. Kevin De Bruyne returns to the ground where he won six league titles, with a weakened Napoli and something to prove.

What went wrong on Saturday

The pattern was familiar. Rodrigo Muniz curled Fulham ahead on 16 minutes after Oscar Bobb, once of City, cut inside Elliot Anderson. City have now gone 1-0 down in four league games in a row for the first time since 2012, and trailed at half-time in four straight for only the third time (Opta Analyst).

They dominated the ball after that: 15 shots to five and 1.88 expected goals to 0.72. Anderson volleyed in a half-cleared corner on 57 minutes, his first league goal for the club. But only three of City's shots came after half-time, and Erling Haaland had his best moment ruled out.

The disallowed goal was the talking point. Referee Tom Bramall judged that Bernd Leno had the ball under control when Haaland poked it in after a fumble, and VAR did not intervene (ESPN). Maresca said that from his view "it's quite clear that it is not a foul" (NBC Sports).

Again we are conceding many goals, especially in the beginning of the game... we need to try to find a way to again keep a clean sheet and win games.

Enzo Maresca to TNT Sports, via NBC Sports

The bigger problem: nine without a clean sheet

City have now conceded in nine consecutive games in all competitions, their longest run since December 2019 (Opta). They have won one of their last six. Fulham's point also ended a 20-match winning run against them; the London side had not avoided defeat against City since September 2011.

Maresca's response has been to talk about time. "For me, you know, this is like a reset, it's like a restart," he said on Monday, pointing to ten years of the same manager and methods under Pep Guardiola (AFP via Yahoo Sports). He also called the four-game run "a shame" (PA via ESPN).

Napoli: the matchup

On paper this is the easier game of the week. Napoli are 27th of 36 in the league phase on three points, and Yahoo Sports counts eleven players unavailable, including four of their six centre-backs and captain Giovanni Di Lorenzo. Opta's model gives City a 71.2 per cent chance of winning (Opta Analyst).

The key battle is the one City lost on Saturday: the first 20 minutes. Napoli's Scott McTominay has seven goals in his last nine Champions League games, and Rasmus Højlund and Noa Lang attack space behind full-backs. Maresca named all three on Monday, adding that Napoli "create lots of chances, even in games they don't win" (PA via ESPN).

Massimiliano Allegri was quoted in Italy calling the trip "prohibitive". Maresca was unimpressed: "I think in Italy we are pretty good at crying at our lot in life. I don't believe he thinks he can't win" (PA via ESPN).

Why the week matters

A win on Tuesday would put City on 12 points with three league-phase games left (Barcelona away, Lens away, Sporting at home) and on course for the automatic top eight that Maresca says is the aim. Lose, and the January games become a scramble.

Then the Emirates. Arsenal drew 2-2 at Newcastle on Saturday, so the gap stayed at six points (The Herald). Win on Sunday and City cut it to three. Lose and it is nine, before December has started.

Scouting the Next Opponent

A wounded Napoli, built around McTominay and an old friend

Allegri's side are eighth in Serie A and short of defenders. They still score goals, and they still collect cards.

Napoli arrive eighth in Serie A on 18 points after a 1-0 home win over Torino on Saturday, settled by Scott McTominay with about 17 minutes left (Flashscore). Before that they drew 1-1 with leaders Lazio and lost 2-0 at Juventus.

Europe has been worse. They have one win in four (4-3 against Bodø/Glimt) and defeats to Arsenal, Villarreal and Porto, the last a 4-1 hammering on November 4. They have scored six and conceded eleven (Wikipedia league-phase table), and Opta says no side has collected more cards in this season's competition (13).

System and the players to stop

Allegri is expected to use a back three, with Mathías Olivera, Amir Rrahmani and Sam Beukema the likeliest trio (Yahoo). Italian reports say he is choosing between a 3-5-1-1 and a 3-5-2, with De Bruyne and Noa Lang competing for the role behind the striker (Mondonapoli).

McTominay is the threat from midfield, arriving late into the box. Rasmus Højlund runs the channels and Lang is in form, Maresca said. De Bruyne has four goals and two assists in 15 games this season, according to one report (Telecomasia), and is linked with a January move to PSV.

Where City can hurt them

The absentees are the story. Stanislav Lobotka, Alessandro Buongiorno, Di Lorenzo, Alex Meret and David Neres are injured, Rafa Marín is suspended, and Allegri suggested after the Torino win that Napoli might not travel at full motivation. A makeshift back line should be there to be pressed and turned.

History favours City too. They have won all three of their last Champions League meetings, including a 2-0 win at the Etihad last season when Di Lorenzo was sent off and De Bruyne was withdrawn after 26 minutes, and they are unbeaten at home against Napoli (W2 D1) (Opta).

Transfer News

Aït-Nouri says yes to Juventus, but the clubs are not there yet

The left-back's January exit moved a step closer this week. Nunes's new deal is still unsigned, and Kovačić's future is drifting towards the summer.

Rayan Aït-Nouri: personal terms with Juventus

Juventus have an agreement in principle with Rayan Aït-Nouri for a January move, Gazzetta dello Sport reported on Friday, but there is no deal between the clubs (AfricaSoccer, relaying Gazzetta). Juventus want a loan; City would prefer a permanent sale to reinvest the money.

Manchester United remain interested. Graeme Bailey says the player has been offered to them (TEAMtalk). Juventus are constrained by their UEFA settlement and may need to sell Andrea Cambiaso first. His City contract runs to 2030. Certainty: personal terms reported (single source); no club agreement.

Matheus Nunes: close, or not advanced?

Accounts of Matheus Nunes's new contract still differ. The Manchester Evening News said last week that City were close to a three-year deal worth about £150,000 a week (via Sports Mole). ESPN then reported talks were "not at an advanced stage" (via AllFootball).

Football Insider's Pete O'Rourke said on Sunday that City are "confident" and that Nunes wants to sign quickly (via Yahoo). Certainty: advanced talks at most; nothing signed.

Mateo Kovačić: heading for the exit in June?

Mateo Kovačić will leave at the end of the season, according to Matteo Moretto, who said the midfielder prefers Spain or Italy, with Juventus and Milan linked (Sports Mole). His contract expires in June 2027 and he can talk to foreign clubs from January. Certainty: rumour, single source.

Malo Gusto: a January door, but £75m

Chelsea's Malo Gusto has told the club he wants to explore a move, and City could revive their interest in January, says O'Rourke. He calls it "a hard deal to do" at Chelsea's £75m valuation (Football Insider). Certainty: concrete interest.

Donnarumma stays; Grealish's next move

Despite his errors, City have no plans to sell or loan Gianluigi Donnarumma, O'Rourke reports, though Lens goalkeeper Robin Risser has been linked (City Xtra via Yahoo).

Jack Grealish is back in Everton's squad after his club suspension and was expected on the bench at Brentford on Monday night (Goal). He will be a free agent in 2027; Aston Villa and Sunderland are said to be interested, and a City return is ruled out (TEAMtalk). Certainty: rumour.

Off the Pitch

A $2bn loan walks away, and the league closes ranks

The money side of the guilty verdict started to show this week, in the City Football Group's credit lines and in a vote at a league meeting.

The Financial Times reported on Thursday that Apollo pulled out of a $2bn (about £1.5bn) loan to City Football Group in the final stages of negotiation, after September's verdict. The money was meant to add liquidity and refinance existing credit (Football365; FourFourTwo).

CFG and Apollo declined to comment. The reports note Companies House filings showing Sheikh Mansour's Newton Investment and Development put in about £100m of new equity on October 14. It follows Bloomberg's report last week that banks are pulling back from financing City transfers.

The sponsor vote

On Thursday Premier League clubs voted to cover legal costs and damages for league directors if they are sued by City's sponsors Etihad or Etisalat. The motion passed 18-2, with City and Nottingham Forest reported to have voted against (City AM). Etisalat has called the findings "entirely false and defamatory".

The appeal itself is unchanged: the FT says it will be heard in mid-December, with a judgment in the new year, and sanctions only if it fails (Off The Pitch). Asked about the charges on Monday, Jérémy Doku said: "I am not the right person to speak about the charges" (PA via ESPN).

The Squad

Bouaddi in, Fernández out, and a squad being sorted

Maresca changed his midfield for Fulham and says he is still learning who suits which game. He has almost everyone to choose from.

The headline selection on Saturday was in midfield. Enzo Fernández dropped to the bench after international duty and Ayyoub Bouaddi, 19, started alongside Elliot Anderson (AFP via Yahoo). Anderson played higher up: "focusing on doing the best job for the team," he said (The False 9).

Enzo Maresca told reporters (AFP via Yahoo) he needs "to understand who is the ideal one for these kind of games, who is the ideal one for different kind of games". On rotation, he was blunt: "We need to not think about a sprint, we need to think about a marathon; 65 games" (City Xtra).

The back four

Marc Guéhi (hip) and Nico O'Reilly (back) both returned to start against Fulham, with Abdukodir Khusanov at right-back and Rúben Dias as captain. Joško Gvardiol came off the bench. With Matheus Nunes back from an ankle problem, Maresca has his full set of defenders for Napoli.

Donnarumma, Haaland and Foden

Gianluigi Donnarumma made three good saves and was booked for dissent. Maresca said Erling Haaland was "absolutely sure" his disallowed goal should have stood. Phil Foden started in the No 10 role and set up Haaland's best chance before going off on 70 minutes.

City Women

A record in Sweden, then a wake-up call at home

Khadija Shaw made Champions League history in the snow at Häcken. Four days later Tottenham ended a 16-game home winning run.

Manchester City Women won 2-0 at BK Häcken on Wednesday in falling snow. Lauren Hemp, on at half-time, saw a clearance deflect in off her early in the second half, and Khadija Shaw, also a half-time substitute, ran on to Hemp's long pass and beat Jennifer Falk on 77 minutes (Manchester City).

It was Shaw's 15th goal of the season and her 10th in the Champions League for City, making her the first City player to reach double figures in the competition. Falk saved Mary Fowler's stoppage-time penalty. City are unbeaten on nine points (W2 D3) and finish the league phase at home to Austria Wien on December 16.

City 1-3 Tottenham

Sunday was a different story. Shaw scored after 11 minutes at the Joie Stadium, but Tottenham levelled through Cathinka Tandberg on 40 minutes and won it with goals from Matilda Vinberg and Jess Naz, in her 100th WSL game (Rowdie; Sky Sports).

It ended a 16-game home winning run, was a second straight league defeat and Spurs' first WSL win over City since 2021. Sky's Millie Bright said City look disjointed without the injured Alex Greenwood, "a natural leader in the back line".

This is the wake-up call that we know we need to bring everything all the time.

Andrée Jeglertz, via Sky Sports

Where it leaves them

City stay second on 25 points from 11 games, two behind Chelsea, who drew 0-0 at Liverpool, with London City Lionesses third on 23 (ESPN). Andrée Jeglertz said: "We are second in the league and battling about winning the title, but we need to do better games than this."

There is now a pause. Greenwood (ankle) and Anna Moorhouse (foot) are expected back for the next WSL game, against Crystal Palace on December 13 (Manchester City). Hemp, Laura Blindkilde Brown, Beth Mead and Grace Clinton are with England for the World Cup play-off against Ukraine on December 1 and 5.

A club article headlined as Ayaka Yamashita's Chelsea red card being rescinded appeared this week; we could not open it to confirm the details.

Players Away

Haaland's late winner sends Norway through

The second half of the international window, November 16 to 17, brought two more Haaland goals and a first Brazil appearance for Allan.

Erling Haaland scored twice as Norway came from 2-0 down to win 3-2 in Denmark on November 17, the second a 96th-minute penalty that put Norway into the Nations League quarter-finals (TimesLIVE). Norway's manager Ståle Solbakken criticised reporters for questioning Haaland about City's charges (SportsCafe).

Enzo Fernández set up Nico Paz's opener in Argentina's 7-0 win over Costa Rica (A Bola). Ayyoub Bouaddi started in midfield as Morocco beat Niger 1-0 in Rabat (ESPN).

Allan came off the bench for about half an hour in Brazil's 7-0 win in Singapore; and scored the seventh, a deflected shot on 74 minutes (Al Jazeera). Rúben Dias did not appear in the line-up reported by ITV for Portugal's 3-2 win in Wales.

Rico Lewis, Stephen Mfuni and Max Alleyne started England under-21s' 3-2 win over France (see The Academy). No City Women internationals played in the window.
