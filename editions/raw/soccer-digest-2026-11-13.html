The Lead

Pochettino swaps the USMNT for a United rescue mission

Manchester United are eighteenth with eight points from ten games. They have paid roughly £12 million to change managers mid-break, hired the United States head coach, and given him four days between his first morning at Carrington and a trip to Anfield.

The Lead · November 13, 2026

There is a particular kind of panic that arrives only when a very large club looks down the table and sees the drop. Manchester United have spent a decade being bad in interesting ways. This season they have been bad in the plainest way there is: one win in ten league games, fourteen scored, nineteen conceded, eighteenth place, eight points.

On Thursday morning they sacked Michael Carrick. On Friday they confirmed Mauricio Pochettino as head coach on a two-and-a-half-year contract running to June 2029. He begins work on 18 November.

The sequence tells you how little of this was planned. Carrick took training at Carrington on Wednesday while the club finalised terms with his replacement at a meeting in Monaco. By the time the news broke on Thursday, a verbal agreement with Pochettino had already been reached.

A release clause, and a bill

Pochettino was under contract with U.S. Soccer until the 2030 World Cup. He signed that extension only three months ago. It carried a release clause of about $10 million, roughly £7.4 million, and United have triggered it.

Add Carrick's pay-off and the total outlay lands near £12 million. Both men were out of contract in the summer just gone. The same swap, made in June, would have cost nothing. That is the kind of detail that follows a club around.

United's statement leaned on what Pochettino has always been credited with: a high-intensity attacking style, cohesive teams, and a record of developing individual players. There were no quotes from the man himself, because for four more days he is still the head coach of the United States.

United say Pochettino was the only coach with whom they held direct talks about replacing Carrick. The Telegraph, November 13

Two games for one country, then another

Before Old Trafford, there is unfinished business. Pochettino leads the United States in both legs of their Concacaf Nations League quarter-final against Haiti: Saturday at Nu Stadium in Miami, Tuesday at TQL Stadium in Cincinnati. It is the first competitive series of the new cycle after a home World Cup that ended in the round of 16.

Then he flies. He starts on 18 November and his first match in charge is away to Liverpool on 22 November. Four days, one training week shared with returning internationals, and Anfield.

What he is actually inheriting

The squad is not the problem, at least not as United tell it. Their line this week was that the group is "far better than results and performances so far this season have indicated." There is some evidence for it. United beat Roma 3-2 in the Champions League on 3 November, Bruno Fernandes scoring a penalty and Matheus Cunha striking twice in the last seven minutes.

They also drew 4-4 at home with Aston Villa on 8 November, the highest-scoring Premier League match of the season. A team that can do both of those things inside a week does not have a talent problem. It has a shape problem and a nerve problem, and those are the two things Pochettino built his reputation on fixing.

He has six weeks before the January window opens, and he will be given it. Fabrizio Romano reported this week that Pochettino has already spoken with the hierarchy about January, and that specific meetings on recruitment are being arranged. United are expected to want a forward and a left-back at minimum.

The eighth man

Pochettino is the eighth permanent manager at Old Trafford since Alex Ferguson retired in 2013. Carrick had himself replaced Ruben Amorim in January, first as interim, then on a two-year deal in May, and he finished third last season. That is how quickly the ground moves under anyone who takes this job.

The relegation question is not yet a real one. Ten games is a quarter of a season, and United are four points off thirteenth. But the fact that the cost of going down is being modelled publicly is the measure of where the club currently sits.

Key facts: the United job

  • Carrick out: sacked 12 November, one win from ten league games, 18th in the table.
  • Pochettino in: two-and-a-half-year contract to June 2029, announced 13 November, starts 18 November.
  • The cost: a release clause of about $10 million (£7.4 million) to U.S. Soccer, and around £12 million in total with Carrick's exit.
  • Last USMNT duty: Haiti in Miami on 14 November, Haiti in Cincinnati on 17 November.
  • First United match: Liverpool away, Anfield, Sunday 22 November, 8:30 am Pacific.
  • Who decided: chief executive Omar Berrada, director of football Jason Wilcox, owner Jim Ratcliffe.

Madrid, Sunday, and a group nobody wants to lose

Spain have won all five. England have won four in a row and already have their quarter-final place. The Metropolitano on Sunday decides which of them tops Nations League Group A3.

Spain have taken 15 points from 15 in this Nations League, the latest a 2-0 win in Prague on Thursday. England are second on 12 with a goal difference of plus twelve, and beat Croatia 2-1 at Wembley on the same night to confirm their place in the last eight.

That Wembley night belonged to one man. Harry Kane won his 126th cap, passing Peter Shilton's 125 to become England's most-capped men's player, then scored the winner from the penalty spot in the 64th minute. It was his 92nd international goal. Morgan Rogers had opened the scoring on 24 minutes and Petar Sučić equalised just before the hour.

The matchup

England's problem on Sunday is not personnel, it is tempo. Thomas Tuchel's side were widely judged flat at Wembley despite the win, and Spain do not allow flat. The Spanish midfield has conceded very little across five matches and will look to make England defend long spells without the ball, which is where England have historically dropped deeper than their manager wants.

The key battle is in the first phase. If Declan Rice and Elliot Anderson can move England out of their own half cleanly, Anthony Gordon and Rogers have the pace to punish a high Spanish line. If they cannot, England spend the night chasing.

For Spain this is also about the record. Fifteen points from fifteen, with a plus-nine goal difference, is the kind of league phase that sets up both a seeding and a reputation before the March quarter-finals.

Elsewhere in League A, Group A1 turned on Thursday. Italy came from a goal down to beat France 3-1 at San Siro, their first home win over the French since 1962, and handed Zinedine Zidane his first defeat as France head coach. Francesco Pio Esposito scored twice and Davide Frattesi once.

France had led after ten minutes through Michael Olise, gifted the ball by a Gianluigi Donnarumma error. It was Olise's fifth goal in five Nations League games, a run only Kane has bettered. Italy, under Roberto Mancini in his second spell, go top of the group on ten points, level with France but ahead on head-to-head.

Transfer News

United's January list begins with a forward and a left-back

Romano says new arrivals are effectively guaranteed. The names in circulation are an out-of-favour Barcelona full-back and a striker nobody can yet stand up.

The clearest line on Manchester United's January comes from Fabrizio Romano, who said this week that the club "need to do something" in the window and that signings will happen. The summer left United short in the two places a relegation-zone position makes impossible to ignore: a left-back and a centre-forward.

The full-back thread leads to Alejandro Balde. Romano has called it a situation to watch, saying Balde could be open to considering an exit in January "if the situation continues like this in the next two months." The Spaniard is getting very limited minutes at Barcelona. Certainty: concrete interest, no agreement.

An earlier United approach was reported to have stalled on cost, with the club unwilling to meet a package around €40 million plus Balde's wages. Spain's Matteo Moretto, speaking on Radio Marca this week, said Balde "is thinking about it seriously" but poured cold water on the Roma version of the story: "Roma's priority is to sign forwards, not defenders."

The striker search is less advanced and more important. Romano expects United to move for a new forward in January without naming one he can stand up. Treat every name attached to it this week as speculation until a reporter puts a fee on it.

Real Madrid win the race for United's 16-year-old

JJ Gabriel has chosen the Bernabéu over Barcelona, Bayern and PSG. The fee being discussed for a player who cannot sign anything until 1 January is around €15 million.

Asked to choose between Barcelona and Real Madrid, the Manchester United academy forward JJ Gabriel did not pause: "Barcelona or Real Madrid? Obviously, Real Madrid." That quote, given last weekend, effectively closed a race that had also involved Bayern Munich and Paris Saint-Germain.

Madrid are reported to have reached a verbal agreement with the player, intending to complete the move in January. The Catalan newspaper Sport put the likely outlay close to €15 million for a 16-year-old. Barcelona withdrew, briefing that they would not "do anything foolish for the sake of young players," and have turned to the Mönchengladbach teenager Nico Ilicevic, who per Bild is being watched by Madrid as well.

Gabriel cannot sign or officially complete anything before 1 January. Certainty: verbal agreement reported, nothing signed, no official announcement.

Nico Paz, and the €80 million option hanging over Como's season

Como paid €60 million for the half of him they did not own. Real Madrid kept a buy-back for the summer of 2027. Everything the player does this season raises the stakes on both sides.

The most elegant piece of business in European football right now belongs to Real Madrid. They sold Nico Paz to Como for €6 million in 2024. Como bought the remaining 50 per cent of his rights this summer in a club-record deal worth €60 million. Madrid retained a unilateral buy-back option, valid only in the summer of 2027, at €80 million.

Romano has confirmed the clause and its value. Madrid's own statement at the time noted the arrangement was made "at the player's own request," and Paz said staying was "a unique opportunity to continue growing as a footballer."

Como's position, stated officially this week, is that "Nico Paz is 100% a Como player," while conceding a sale "would enhance our work and would also be important for the club." Paz had the chance to return to Madrid last summer and turned it down, fearing he would not get the minutes Cesc Fàbregas gives him.

The case for Madrid exercising the option grew considerably on 4 November, when Como won 9-1 at Lens, the heaviest result of the Champions League league phase so far. Spanish reports indicate Madrid intend to use it; AS has reported the club will not decide on buy-backs before the spring. Certainty: a contractual option and a reported intention, no decision taken.

The Rumour Mill

What is actually moving, who is only being talked about, and the gap between the two.

Antonio Nusa, and why January is unlikely

RB Leipzig's Norwegian winger is the most-linked attacker in Europe this month. Aston Villa are preparing a move, and Bayern showed interest in the summer.

Romano's read is cooling. A January transfer would be "difficult" unless a "crazy proposal" arrives, because Leipzig already generated significant money selling an attacker last summer.

Sky Germany's Philipp Hinze reports there is no release clause in Nusa's contract, and puts a realistic cost at €70 million to €90 million. Villa are also looking at Fenerbahçe's Oguz Aydin as an alternative. Certainty: concrete interest, no talks between clubs.

Endrick, and a Roma plan that depends on the player

Moretto said this week that despite José Mourinho publicly insisting Endrick will stay at Real Madrid, "I discovered that this would not be the case." Roma are among the clubs prioritising a forward in January. Moretto's framing is that the outcome depends more on what Endrick wants than on what Madrid want. Certainty: a rumour with one credible source.

Julián Álvarez and the valuation problem

Barcelona's search for a long-term successor to Robert Lewandowski keeps returning to Atlético Madrid's Argentine. Diego Simeone has admitted he cannot offer certainties about Álvarez's long-term future. Atlético's answer so far has been a valuation reported as record-breaking, which is a way of saying no. Certainty: a long-running link, no negotiation.

Bayern, Leverkusen and the German market

Bild's Christian Falk dismissed talk of Martin Ødegaard joining Bayern Munich, calling him "too old and far too good for it to be an option" given Bayern's midfield depth. Sky's Florian Plettenberg described the move as very unrealistic.

Bayern are instead linked with Club Brugge striker Nicolò Tresoldi, even after extending Harry Kane's contract to 2029.

Plettenberg also reports Bayer Leverkusen are the most concrete suitor for the 17-year-old Borussia Mönchengladbach attacking midfielder Wael Mohya, who has just received a first senior Germany call-up under Jürgen Klopp. Dortmund, Leipzig and Premier League clubs are monitoring. Certainty: shortlists and monitoring, nothing agreed.

Filed under unconfirmed

Gossip columns this week carried a link between Virgil van Dijk and Barcelona. No tier-one reporting sits behind it, and the Liverpool captain is 35 and under contract, so we list it as an unconfirmed rumour and nothing more. Separately, and confirmed, Eric Maxim Choupo-Moting will leave the New York Red Bulls on a free transfer at the end of the year and intends to keep playing, per Plettenberg.

Around the Leagues

Premier League

The table after ten matches reads: Arsenal 27 points, Brighton & Hove Albion 22, Manchester City 21, Liverpool 18, Chelsea and Everton 16. The most interesting number in it belongs to Brighton, who have scored 26 and conceded seven. Fabian Hürzeler's side have the division's best goal difference, plus nineteen, nine better than the leaders, and they are five points off the top having played the same ten games.

The season's defining story, though, is not on the pitch. On 29 September an independent commission found Manchester City guilty on 114 of 115 charges of breaching the league's financial rules, concluding the club used sham sponsorship arrangements to inflate earnings by roughly £900 million between 2009 and 2018. City have appealed, calling the verdict materially flawed in law, principle and fact.

On Friday the Financial Times reported the appeal will be heard in mid-December, with a verdict in the new year and any sanctions decided after that. In plain terms: nothing will be settled this season.

The commission's powers run from fines through points deductions to expulsion, and punishments can themselves be appealed, which is why legal observers think this runs for years. City are expected to argue the disputed sponsorship money came from the Abu Dhabi government as a sovereign body rather than a private company.

At the other end

Three clubs are in real trouble. Crystal Palace are bottom on seven points with a goal difference of minus fourteen, in their first season under Pierre Sage. Sunderland and Manchester United are level on eight. Coventry City, back in the top flight after twenty-five years, sit seventeenth on nine with a startling record of three wins, no draws and seven defeats.

Liverpool's autumn has lurched. Andoni Iraola, appointed on a two-year deal in June to succeed Arne Slot, lost at home to the league leaders and then 3-2 away at Fenerbahçe in the Champions League, before a 3-1 win at Palace steadied things. Alexander Isak and Florian Wirtz both scored in that game, which matters more than the three points did.

The injury list does not look steady at all. Virgil van Dijk withdrew from the Netherlands squad having admitted he had been playing through pain, Dominik Szoboszlai has had scans on a knee, and Ryan Gravenberch is recovering from a heel fracture. Iraola's stated wish for the break was simply to avoid new ones.

La Liga

Barcelona lead on 33 points from twelve, three clear of Real Madrid and five clear of Atlético Madrid. The gap was made on 9 November, when Barcelona beat Atlético 2-1. Their goal record is the season's outlier: 48 scored, 13 conceded, including a 7-2 win over Racing Santander and a 6-1 over Alavés.

Madrid's season is being conducted in a strange atmosphere. José Mourinho's side have won ten of twelve and conceded ten goals all season, and the story around them is one player's form. Vinícius Júnior has one goal and one assist in his last nine appearances and, per AS, one goal in twelve this season. He is also serving a four-match league ban for pulling the hair of Villarreal's Nathan Saliba.

El Debate reported this month that the club are studying his body structure, after concerns he no longer reaches his former top speed, and that Mourinho has given him a month, with the December fixtures as the deadline, to prove he should keep starting.

Mourinho has publicly backed him, telling TNT Sports Brazil that Vinícius "can work his physique with an individualised plan" while adding that "the most important thing is the team." Madrid also have a €125 million summer signing, Yan Diomande, who prefers the same flank.

Lower down, this is a promoted-clubs season. Deportivo La Coruña are ninth on 15 points and Racing Santander tenth on 14, while Málaga are bottom on five without a win. The club in genuine danger is Valencia, nineteenth on nine.

Bundesliga

Nine games in, neither of the top two has lost. Bayern Munich and Borussia Dortmund are level on 21 points, separated only by goal difference: Bayern have scored 29 and conceded nine, Dortmund 16 and seven. They drew 2-2 in Munich on 31 October in a Klassiker that settled nothing, and do not meet again until March.

SC Freiburg are the surprise of the autumn in third on 19, ahead of RB Leipzig on 18, who have won all five at home and lost three of four away. Bayer Leverkusen, in their first season under Carles Martínez Novell, are fifth on 16.

Bayern's week was defined off the pitch. Harry Kane, 33, extended his contract to 2029, a deal Joshua Kimmich publicly welcomed. At the bottom, Borussia Mönchengladbach have won once in nine and host Dortmund next Saturday. Promoted SV Elversberg, meanwhile, are seventh.

Serie A

The most compelling table in Europe. Lazio lead on 29 points from eleven and have not lost: nine wins, two draws, 25 scored and nine conceded, in Gennaro Gattuso's first season after Maurizio Sarri's departure. Inter are a point behind on 28 with the league's only perfect home record, and Roma third on 27, also unbeaten.

Matchday eleven on 8 November shifted things in small ways. Lazio drew 1-1 at Napoli, their first failure to win away this season, Davide Frattesi scoring before an own goal levelled. Inter beat Como 2-0 at San Siro. Milan won 2-1 at Genoa, Christian Pulisic scoring again, and sit fourth on 24 after their 3-1 derby win over Inter on 31 October in front of 77,691.

Como are fifth on 23 and have become genuinely frightening in Europe. The fallen giants are Napoli, tenth on 15, and Juventus, seventh on 17. Udinese are bottom on five, having sacked Kosta Runjaić on 2 November and appointed Paolo Zanetti the same day.

Ligue 1

Paris Saint-Germain lead on 23 points from ten, with Lille second on 21 and Paris FC, backed by the Arnault family and promoted last season, third on 20. Marseille are fourth on 18 after four straight wins, Lyon fifth on 17, Monaco on 16 and winless in four.

Lyon lost their manager in the middle of all that. Paulo Fonseca left on 4 November to join Sporting CP, a departure the club said it regretted the timing of, having kept faith with him through a nine-month touchline suspension earned in only his sixth game in charge. Carlos Vicens has been appointed as his successor.

PSG's bigger problem is medical: Khvicha Kvaratskhelia faces months out with a torn cruciate ligament. At the bottom, Le Havre have still not won. Didier Digard was sacked by the club's American ownership and president Jean-Michel Roussier then resigned.

Major League Soccer

The regular season finished on 7 November and belonged to Nashville SC, who won a first Supporters' Shield and set an MLS single-season points record of 77. Their defence conceded 25 goals; their designated-player trio of Hany Mukhtar, Sam Surridge and Cristian Espinoza combined for 42 goals and 25 assists under B.J. Callaghan.

Inter Miami, the defending champions, are the East's second seed. Lionel Messi won a second consecutive Golden Boot with 26 goals and 17 assists. In the West, Vancouver Whitecaps took the top seed on a goal difference of plus forty and 74 goals, Brian White scoring a career-best 20 alongside Thomas Müller.

The league's European arrivals have landed in different places. Robert Lewandowski has 9 goals and 5 assists in 19 games for Chicago Fire under Gregg Berhalter, and Antoine Griezmann has 14 and 8 in 18 for Orlando City.

Son Heung-Min and Denis Bouanga have 25 goals and 24 assists between them for LAFC, who have won one of their last nine. Timo Werner is at San Jose under Bruce Arena. St. Louis CITY won the US Open Cup, a first trophy, on the back of a 21-match unbeaten run in all competitions.
