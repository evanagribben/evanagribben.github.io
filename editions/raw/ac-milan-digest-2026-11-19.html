The Lead

Five points back, three games in nine days: Amorim's Milan restart the chase

Milan come out of the November break fourth, unbeaten away and on a club-record pace for a new coach, but 28th in the Europa League. Frosinone, Olympiacos and Cagliari in nine days will say which of those two seasons is the real one.

The international break gave Rúben Amorim something he had not had in more than a month: a week with no matches. He gave the players who stayed at Milanello seven days off, then called them back on Monday, November 16. Training resumed with Sunday's visit of Frosinone as the target.

The table he returns to is kind. Milan are fourth on 24 points from 11 games: seven wins, three draws and one defeat, with 23 goals scored and 13 conceded. Lazio lead on 29, Inter have 28 and Roma 27, per the Serie A table. Milan are also unbeaten away from home in the league, with three wins and three draws.

By one measure, nobody has started better in a Milan dugout. Amorim's 2.18 points per game over his first 11 Serie A matches is the best opening by a Milan coach in the three-points-for-a-win era, according to Milan Press figures reported by MilanReports. Corriere dello Sport wrote on November 15 that he has earned everyone's trust in five months.

Two seasons in one

Europe tells a different story. Milan have one win and three defeats in the Europa League league phase and sit 28th of 36 on three points, with a goal difference of minus three. Twenty-fourth place, the last that survives into the knockout play-offs, has four points, per the club's standings page.

The defeats were not narrow. Benfica won 2-0 at San Siro in September. Bournemouth won 3-0 after Mike Maignan was sent off in the 27th minute. Then Ferencváros won 2-0 in Milan on November 5, with Lenny Joseph scoring both. The only win was a 4-0 rout in Salzburg, built on a hat-trick from Alphadjo Cisse.

Four games remain: Olympiacos away on November 26, Sunderland at home on December 10, Levski Sofia away on January 21 and Ararat-Armenia at home on January 28, per Calcio e Finanza's calendar. Thursday in Piraeus is the hardest of them, and a defeat there would leave very little margin.

The system question

The surprise of Amorim's Milan is the shape. The coach famous for a back three has mostly used a back four, listed by the club as a 4-4-1-1. He returned to a 3-4-2-1 against Lecce and Atalanta and used a 4-4-2 in Salzburg and Genoa.

At Genoa on November 8 the side was Maignan; Mario Gila, De Winter, Sankhoun Diawara, Yunus Musah; Diego Moreira, Luka Modrić, Adrien Rabiot, Cisse; Christian Pulisic, Gonçalo Ramos, per Goal Italia. Cisse and Pulisic scored inside 19 minutes and Milan won 2-1.

Afterwards Amorim was candid about the limits. "Rotation is necessary for us," he said, and "we lack time in the final third," according to his post-match press conference. Three games in nine days will test both points.

Frosinone, and the Ramos problem

Sunday's opponent is no soft landing. Newly promoted Frosinone are sixth on 20 points and have taken 15 of a possible 18 at home. Away from home, though, they have only five, per ACMilanInside's preview. Their top scorer, Antonio Raimondo (eight goals), is ruled out with ankle ligament damage.

Milan's own striker question is louder. Gonçalo Ramos has gone 661 minutes without a goal for club and country since his penalty against Atalanta on October 18, and he had a poor break with Portugal, per Gazzetta via SempreMilan. Amorim's verdict after Genoa: "It's a process," per his DAZN interview.

The goals have come from elsewhere. Cisse, 19, has eight in all competitions. Rabiot has five, Pulisic and Moreira four each. Pulisic also scored three times for the United States during the break, and Pianeta Milan expects him to start on Sunday.

Selection depends on the medical room. Maignan picked up a knock with France but tests found no lesion, and he took part in Thursday's session, per MilanNews24. Strahinja Pavlović is back after a kidney problem but is being assessed daily. Fikayo Tomori is out.

The workload is the hidden factor. Milan's internationals logged 1,482 minutes in the window, more than any other Serie A squad, according to Transfermarkt figures cited by MilanPress. Expect Amorim's rotation to start on Sunday, not after it.

 
Off the Pitch

RedBird builds a bigger house around Milan

A new holding company, a Big 12 commissioner as its chief executive and a Frankfurt architect for the football side: the club's power map is being redrawn.

On November 13 RedBird launched Milan Sports & Entertainment, a holding company for AC Milan, its share of the new stadium, a possible NBA Europe team and arena, and hospitality interests. Big 12 commissioner Brett Yormark will be its chief executive, per Inside World Football. He stays at the Big 12 until the end of January.

Gerry Cardinale told the Financial Times a stock-market listing is "not a must-have" but that he is "architecting it with that in mind." Massimo Calvelli, chief executive since June, is expected to focus on the club itself.

Speaking at a Sports Business Journal conference on November 18, Cardinale called Milan "the hottest market in Europe," per SempreMilan. He told Gazzetta he already has planning permission for a 20,000-seat arena, Eurohoops reported.

Krösche agreed, not yet announced

On the football side, Eintracht Frankfurt's Markus Krösche has agreed terms to become head of football, overseeing the whole sporting area including the January window. Daniele Longo reported a three-year deal with an option at Calciomercato.com, with the announcement expected in December.

Milan are paying Eintracht about €3.5m in compensation, and Tuttomercatoweb says he becomes operational on January 1, possibly earlier. Hendrik Almstadt has been acting as operational sporting director since June. Krösche reportedly wants Eintracht's Timmo Hardung as his deputy; that is a rumour.

Money, a stadium and a kit

The 2025-26 accounts showed revenue of €464.6m and a net loss of about €24m, the first loss in four years, blamed largely on missing Europe, per Sky Sport Italia. Net debt rose to €145.3m, while sponsorship income passed €100m for the first time.

The new stadium with Inter, to be called the Generali Stadium (Foster + Partners and Manica, 71,500 seats), was unveiled on October 29 for the 2031-32 season. On November 9 the city council passed a non-binding motion, 22 votes to 6, asking for a health-impact study.

Opponents' appeal to the Council of State will be heard on May 4, 2027, per MilanPress. Separately, Adidas are reported to be returning as kit supplier from 2028-29 at about €500m over ten years, per Calcio e Finanza. That deal is not official.

 
Transfer News

Inácio for the defence, Endrick or Tresoldi for the attack

Cardinale wants a centre-back and a striker in January, and Krösche will arrive with a free hand. Here is where every thread stands.

Gonçalo Inácio leads the defensive search

A centre-back is the first priority. Fabrizio Romano said in October that the defender "remains the club's priority," and Gianluca Di Marzio agrees. Milan had a late-August bid for Sporting's Gonçalo Inácio rejected.

Gazzetta reported on November 16 that Inácio, 25, is the main target at about €40m to €45m, via MilanReports. Portugal's Record added on November 18 that Sporting will no longer block a January sale. His release clause is €60m, per SempreMilan. Status: concrete interest; no January bid confirmed.

Endrick and Tresoldi: two expensive answers

The striker search is for cover behind Ramos, not a partner, Di Marzio said on Sky, relayed by MilanNews24. TNT Sports Brasil reports Milan are preparing a €70m permanent offer for Real Madrid's Endrick (€60m plus €10m in bonuses), with Real wanting an €85m buy-back clause, per Goal.

The obstacles are real. Matteo Moretto says Roma are ahead, per Calciomercato.com, and after Brazil's win over Singapore on November 17 Endrick said he hopes to spend his life at Real, per SempreMilan. Status: concrete interest; offer figures single-sourced to TNT.

The other name is Club Brugge's Nicolò Tresoldi. Gazzetta says Brugge want €60m, though Cardinale has not committed, per Calciomercato.com. Di Marzio values him nearer €50m and calls a January deal unlikely. Status: concrete interest.

Rabiot close; Estupiñán, Camarda and Loftus-Cheek could go

Adrien Rabiot is "one step" from extending to June 2030, CalcioMercato.it reported on November 18, and Romano expects it "soon". It is not signed. Pulisic (to 2031, official October 30) and Pavlović (to 2031, official October 14) are already done.

Going the other way, Pervis Estupiñán has slipped down the order and is expected on the market in January, Longo reports. Francesco Camarda is expected to go on loan, with Torino, Udinese, Pisa and Sampdoria asking, per TMW.

Ruben Loftus-Cheek's contract ends in June 2027, so January is the last chance for a fee, Gazzetta noted. Tomori, Modrić and Pietro Terracciano also run to 2027. Omari Hutchinson's loan carries a €40m option, per TMW; no decision has been made.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Frosinone: a high press that travels badly

The promoted side have been the surprise of the autumn at home. Away from the Stirpe, they have been ordinary.

Frosinone arrive sixth, four points behind Milan, having won six of 11. Massimiliano Alvini, voted best coach in Serie B last season, plays a 4-2-3-1 with an aggressive high press. Fifteen of their 20 points have come at home, per Lega Serie A and ACMilanInside.

Their form shows both faces: a 4-4 draw at Atalanta, 5-1 and 4-2 home wins over Lecce and Sassuolo, a 2-0 win over Torino, then a 1-0 defeat at Cagliari in which Cichella hit the crossbar. They have scored 24, more than Milan, and conceded 15.

The big loss is Antonio Raimondo, their eight-goal top scorer, out with multi-ligament ankle damage. Daniel Bîrligea leads the line instead. The players to stop are the midfield pair of Calò and Cichella, who start every attack, and the wide runners behind Bîrligea.

Alvini called the trip to San Siro "rewarding" and said his side must "play our own game, aware of the difficulties," per MilanNews24. He also said 15 of his players were away on international duty, some returning only late this week. That is a single-source detail, but it cuts both ways.

The vulnerability is the space behind the press. If Modrić and Pulisic can turn out of the first line, Frosinone's back four has been exposed away from home. Milan have never lost to them: four wins and two draws, per TuttoFrosinone. Luca Pairetto referees.

The Squad

Pulisic's revival, Cisse's goals and a crowded treatment room

No match since November 8, so this week's squad file is about who is fit, who is firing and who wears the armband.

The headline news is that Mike Maignan should be fine. He was replaced at half-time of France's win over Turkey, but tests ruled out a lesion. Outlets describe the problem differently (adductor, hip), and Torriani is the alternative if he is not ready.

Strahinja Pavlović missed Ferencváros and Genoa with a kidney problem. Pianeta Milan says he trained with the group; Sky still calls him doubtful. Matteo Gabbia said on November 13 that he could rejoin the group in January after "an unlucky season," per TMW.

Form and roles

Christian Pulisic has three goals in his last four Serie A games and three more for the United States, and he signed a new contract to 2031 last month. He was due back in training on Thursday, per MilanReports.

Alphadjo Cisse, 19, is the team's top scorer with eight and came off the bench for Italy's senior side in Belgium. Rabiot has five league goals, joint top with Cisse in Serie A. Ramos has two in the league and is the one forward whose form is a concern.

Maignan is captain. Modrić wore the armband at Udinese, and Rabiot captained Milan for the first time against Ferencváros while Maignan was suspended, per TMW. Two probable shapes are reported for Sunday: a 4-2-3-1 (Radio Rossonera) and a 3-4-2-1 (TuttoFantacalcio).

The Academy

Futuro's winning run, a Primavera revival and a setback for La Mantia

Three straight wins in Serie D, ten goals in two Primavera games, and a young midfielder back under the knife.

Milan Futuro

Milan Futuro are in their second Serie D season (Girone B) after relegation from Serie C in 2025. They have won three in a row: 3-2 at Virtus Verona on November 1, 2-1 against Piacenza in a replayed match on November 4, and 3-0 against Virtus CiseranoBergamo on November 8, with two goals from Pandolfi.

That left them on 23 points, two behind joint leaders ChievoVerona and Nibbiano & Valtidone, per Notiziario Calcio. Last weekend's game at Leon was postponed to December 9. Tritium visit the Chinetti on Sunday at 5:30 am Pacific.

Primavera

Giovanni Renna's Primavera have come out of a one-win-in-five slump with two big wins: 5-0 at Monza on October 31 and 5-1 against AlbinoLeffe. They are seventh on 16 points, five behind leaders Parma, per the Primavera 1 table. They play at Lazio on Sunday at 2:00 am Pacific.

There was bad news on Thursday. The club said midfielder Simon La Mantia had surgery on November 18 to remove part of the meniscus in his left knee, per the official statement. It follows an ACL operation in December 2025. Rehab starts in the coming days.

At the top end, Camarda scored for Italy's Under-21s against France and looks set for a January loan. Akram Jadid, 15, has trained with the first team since August, per Il Fatto Quotidiano, though he has not made a competitive debut.

On loan

Nkunku has been the most productive loanee in Leipzig, though an adductor problem cost him three games. Athekame is a regular at Lyon. Odogu has not played at Toulouse. Options on Nkunku, Ricci and Giménez could bring in about €68m, per Pianeta Milan.

Milan Women

A hard November for Bakker's side

One point from three games, a heavy derby defeat and a Sassuolo test at the Vismara on Saturday.

AC Milan Women are eighth of 12 in Serie A Women with seven points from seven games (two wins, a draw, four defeats; goals 11-16). Roma lead on 21, per the league table. Suzanne Bakker, renewed to 2027 in June, needs a response.

November began with a 1-0 defeat at Como. Then came a 3-0 derby loss to Inter on November 7: Bugeja scored two penalties and Glionna the other, and Estévez was sent off at 65 minutes.

The response was spirited. In Naples on November 14, summer signing Sara Kanutte scored twice in the first half hour and Koivisto added a third, but Napoli drew 3-3. Kanutte, a Norwegian forward, and Spain's Nerea Nevado were the headline summer arrivals.

Sassuolo visit the Vismara on Saturday at 6:00 am Pacific (match centre). Angelica Soffia is in Italy's squad for the World Cup play-off final round against Finland, in Helsinki on December 1 and Taranto on December 5, per NapoliMagazine. The Coppa Italia resumes on December 19 to 20.

 
Players Away

Pulisic's bicycle, Modrić's record and a bad week for Ramos

Thirteen Milan players were called up, per Pianeta Milan. Most came back in form, and only Maignan came back hurt.

Christian Pulisic had the window of his life. He scored twice in the United States' 6-0 win over Haiti in Miami, per ESPN, then struck with an acrobatic volley, widely described as a bicycle kick, in the 2-0 return leg in Cincinnati on November 17, ESPN reported. That was Mauricio Pochettino's last match in charge.

Luka Modrić won his 208th cap against Czechia on November 15, drawing level with Lionel Messi and behind only Cristiano Ronaldo's 234, per Croatia Week. Diego Moreira scored his first Belgium goal in the 4-1 win over Italy, set up by Kevin De Bruyne.

For Gonçalo Ramos it went the other way: no shots in a 4-1 home defeat to Denmark, rated 4/10 by Portugoal, and substituted after 59 minutes in Wales. Ardon Jashari completed 75 of 79 passes in Switzerland's 4-0 win over Scotland.
