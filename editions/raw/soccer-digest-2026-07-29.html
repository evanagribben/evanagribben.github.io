The Lead

La Roja's second star, and the reckoning it left behind

Spain are champions of the world for the second time, but the fortnight since the final at MetLife has been about everything the tournament unsettled: a contested Golden Ball, Kylian Mbappé rewriting the record book, a farewell that will not quite come, and the largest coaching cull in World Cup history.

It took Spain 116 years of football and a fourteenth year of trying since their first world title to arrive here, and when it finally came it came the way Spain have always insisted the game should be won: with the ball, patiently, until the opposition's resistance simply wore through. On July 19 at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey, before a capacity crowd of 80,663, Spain beat Argentina 1–0 after extra time to win the first 48-team, 104-match World Cup — and to deny the holders the back-to-back that has eluded every nation since Brazil in 1958 and 1962.

The goal, when it came, was almost inevitable in its construction. Six minutes into extra time, Ferran Torres arrived at the end of a move that had pinned Argentina deep for the better part of two hours and finished with the calm of a man who had seen it coming. "I just saw the ball coming to me and I shot with the power of all the Spanish people," he said afterward. It was the only goal Spain needed against an Argentina side that mustered barely a threat all evening; Emiliano Martínez, magnificent to the last, kept the score respectable with a string of saves, but the tide only ran one way.

And yet the days since have belonged less to the champions than to the arguments the tournament left in its wake. The first was over the Golden Ball. FIFA's technical panel handed its player-of-the-tournament award to Rodri — the Manchester City and Spain midfielder who anchored La Roja's control and, in doing so, completed a remarkable comeback from the long-term knee injury that wiped out much of his previous club season. It is a defensible choice and, by the metrics Spain value most, arguably the correct one. It was also instantly contested, because the man many felt had lit up the summer, Lionel Messi, went home without it.

"We all dreamt of a World Cup in which we would make history. That didn't happen." — the tone of an entire continent of departing coaches, voiced here by the Netherlands' Ronald Koeman

The second argument was settled by the numbers, and it belonged to Kylian Mbappé. France went out before the final, but their captain finished as the tournament's leading scorer with ten goals, claiming a second consecutive Golden Boot after his haul in Qatar 2022 and, in the process, moving to the top of the all-time World Cup scoring chart. For a player still shy of thirty, now a season into his Real Madrid career, it is the kind of statistical marker that reframes a legacy in real time. Behind him the chase closed out with Messi on eight, Erling Haaland and Jude Bellingham on seven apiece and Harry Kane on six.

If the awards produced the noise, the aftermath produced the upheaval. According to a tally compiled by Pulse Sports and echoed across the wires, some sixteen or seventeen national-team managers left their posts in the days after the tournament, a coaching turnover without precedent at a single World Cup. France's Didier Deschamps stepped away after roughly fourteen years and two finals. Germany's Julian Nagelsmann resigned within days of landing home. Portugal's Roberto Martínez, the Netherlands' Koeman, Scotland's Steve Clarke and Belgium's Rudi Garcia all went, alongside a long tail of others from Croatia to Mexico to South Korea. Tunisia had already set the tone by sacking their coach after a single 5–1 defeat mid-tournament — an in-competition dismissal believed to be the first of its kind.

The contrast tells the story: the two men left standing are the two who reached the final. Luis de la Fuente, having delivered the trophy, is untouchable; Lionel Scaloni, whose Argentina fell one game short of immortality, retains the faith of a federation that knows what he has already built. Around them, half the international game is now hiring.

Which leaves the farewell that will not quite happen. In the immediate afterglow it was assumed Messi had played his last match in the pale blue and white. Instead, reporting out of Argentina in the week since — carried by beIN Sports on July 24 — indicates he has chosen to delay international retirement, that Scaloni intends to keep selecting him "over the next few months," and that the federation is planning a commemorative send-off on home soil rather than letting the story end on a defeat in New Jersey. "The number 10 will always be his," Scaloni said.

Two British records fall in seventy-two hours

The domestic game's answer to the World Cup was a spending spree — and a transfer ceiling that was broken twice in a single week.

While the champions paraded, England's clubs were busy setting a new benchmark for what a British footballer costs — and then breaking it again three days later. First, Manchester City agreed a fee reported at £116m to prise Elliot Anderson from Nottingham Forest, a British record that stood for barely forty-eight hours. Then, on July 22, Chelsea completed the £117m signing of Morgan Rogers from Aston Villa on a deal to 2033 — the most expensive British player of all time and a Chelsea club record, with Arsenal among the pursuers who declined to match the fee.

De Zerbi's Tottenham build the window's loudest project

No club has attacked this window like Tottenham. Under new head coach Roberto De Zerbi, appointed after Thomas Frank was dismissed in February, Spurs have assembled a rebuild north of £270m. The spine of it: Sandro Tonali for a club-record £92.5m from Newcastle, and Portugal midfielder Mateus Fernandes for £85m from the relegated West Ham, Spurs seeing off Manchester United. Add centre-back Jan Paul van Hecke (£52m from Brighton) and free transfers Andy Robertson and Marcos Senesi. They are not done: Fabrizio Romano reports Savinho's move from Manchester City is "absolutely on" at around £60m, gated only on City lining up a replacement.

Barça chase Álvarez as Madrid work the market by stealth

With Robert Lewandowski gone to Chicago on a free, Barcelona's summer hinges on a striker. Per Matteo Moretto, Barça have pushed hard for Julián Álvarez, but Atlético Madrid have rejected their bid and are holding firm on a valuation reported to have touched €150m. Across the city, Real Madrid — now run day-to-day by José Mourinho — have chased RB Leipzig winger Yan Diomandé, the summer's hottest contested asset, with PSG and Liverpool also in for nine-figure interest; Leipzig want north of €100m.

Serie A's giants reshape their front lines

With Dušan Vlahović having left Juventus on a free, Luciano Spalletti's side made official the arrivals of Randal Kolo Muani from PSG (€38m obligation plus €12m bonuses) and Leverkusen's Kerim Alajbegović (€33m plus add-ons). Vlahović is now the market's most intriguing free agent, with AC Milan reported to be closing on him (unconfirmed) and Barcelona lurking. Roma pushed Santiago Castro (€35m-plus from Bologna) through a medical, while Inter agreed roughly €40m for Tottenham's Cristian Romero — who, per Gianluigi Longari, is stalling in hope of a move to Barcelona.

Around the Leagues

Premier League

The English top flight returns in mid-August a very different place. The managerial map has been redrawn wholesale: Pep Guardiola's era at Manchester City has given way to Enzo Maresca; Arne Slot is gone from champions Liverpool, replaced by Andoni Iraola; Michael Carrick is permanent at Manchester United; Xabi Alonso has taken Chelsea; and Roberto De Zerbi is remaking Tottenham. West Ham, Wolves and Burnley went down; Coventry, Hull and Ipswich came up.

Pre-season offered early tea-leaves: Chelsea won a wild 6–4 over Western Sydney with João Pedro starring; Liverpool beat Sunderland 4–2 under Iraola; Manchester United thrashed Rosenborg 5–0 after an earlier loss to Wrexham; Crystal Palace, the Conference League holders now under Pierre Sage, slumped 3–0 at Bromley. United completed Andrey Santos (£50m) and Youri Tielemans (£35m) but saw a move for Atalanta's Éderson stall.

Off the pitch, the league's shift toward a Squad Cost Ratio model is the live governance question of the summer. Newcastle are reshaping under Eddie Howe without chasing a marquee No. 9, while Aston Villa must absorb an ACL rupture to Amadou Onana. In the women's game, Niamh Charles joined Manchester City from Chelsea.

La Liga

Real Madrid opened Mourinho's pre-season with a scratchy 1–0 win over Alcorcón before a goalless run-out with Leganés, Kylian Mbappé still on post-World Cup leave. Barcelona, breaking in Karim Adeyemi and Anthony Gordon, saw their build-up hit by an MCL tear to Frenkie de Jong. Atlético Madrid have been the busiest big club, adding Alejandro Grimaldo, Kang-in Lee and — pending final confirmation — Atalanta's Ademola Lookman for ~€40m.

Bundesliga

Florian Plettenberg reports Bayern Munich are "pleased with the current squad," prioritising departures to trim the wage bill. Borussia Dortmund have been the more active giant, closing on Genk's Konstantinos Karetsas (~€32m) with sporting director Ole Book seeking two attackers. Köln announced themselves in pre-season with an 8–0 rout of Bergisch Gladbach.

Serie A

Italy's summer is defined by a managerial merry-go-round: Spalletti at Juventus, Rúben Amorim at AC Milan, Massimiliano Allegri at Napoli and Gian Piero Gasperini at Roma. At champions Napoli, director Giovanni Manna confirmed Kevin De Bruyne "is part of the project," and the club are pressing to add a centre-back. Fiorentina tied Moise Kean to a new four-year deal.

Ligue 1

France returns first, on August 21. PSG reload again, their ledger featuring big outlays offset by the sale of Gonçalo Ramos to Milan and a pursuit of Monaco's Maghnes Akliouche. Monaco moved for Nantes striker Matthis Abline (€25m plus bonuses, medical booked); Lyon took Loïs Openda on a paid loan. Pre-season delivered a statement as Nice hammered Marseille 3–0.

Major League Soccer

Alone among our leagues, MLS is mid-season — and Inter Miami are surging on a six-game winning streak even with Lionel Messi and Rodrigo De Paul away on post-World Cup rest. Luis Suárez has carried the load, his late penalty settling a 1–0 win at CF Montréal that marked the debut of free-agent signing Casemiro. Out west, LAFC are white-hot with Son Heung-min scoring in three straight, and Orlando City welcomed Antoine Griezmann with a debut goal.
