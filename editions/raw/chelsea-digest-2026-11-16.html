The Lead

Ten games in, Alonso's Chelsea need Leeds to be a turning point

Fifth, two points off the top four, short of a fit striker and stung at Sunderland: what the international break changed, and what Saturday's visit of Leeds United will test.

Chelsea go into Saturday's 3pm visit of Leeds United at Stamford Bridge fifth in the Premier League with 16 points from ten games: two behind fourth-placed Liverpool and 11 behind the leaders, Arsenal. Xabi Alonso has had a fortnight to sit with the most frustrating result of his short tenure.

That was the 1-0 defeat at Sunderland on November 8, Sunderland's first league win since August. The numbers tell the story of the season in miniature. Chelsea had 69 per cent of the ball and completed 578 passes to Sunderland's 203, yet produced 0.53 expected goals to the hosts' 2.55, according to ESPN.

Enzo Le Fée's 36th-minute penalty, awarded after Levi Colwill brought down Brian Brobbey, decided it. Alonso did not hide. Brobbey was "stoppable", he told BBC Sport, and conceding penalties "is not good for us". Colwill was withdrawn at half-time and Moisés Caicedo, who had barely completed a full training session that week, finished at centre-back (via BBC Sport).

The missing No 9

The deeper problem is at the top of the pitch. João Pedro has not played since the win at Everton on October 17; the thigh problem (BBC Sport calls it a quad injury) surfaced on the eve of the Tottenham derby. Pedro Neto has been asked to play as a false nine in his absence.

The specialist alternatives are thin by design. Liam Delap, Nicolas Jackson and Marc Guiu all left in the summer (season record), leaving Emmanuel Emegha, signed from Strasbourg, and the 35-year-old Danny Welbeck as the out-and-out options. Neither started at Sunderland.

An injury list that shapes everything

Selection has rarely been settled. BBC Sport counted 24 changes to Chelsea's league line-ups in ten games, second only to Tottenham. Maxence Lacroix (hamstring) is expected out until around the turn of the year, Reece James is recovering from knee surgery, and Jorrel Hato and Jamie Gittens remain weeks away (Alonso, via Brit Brief).

There is some relief. Emiliano Martínez, who had surgery on a jaw broken in a collision with Everton's Jarrad Branthwaite, is expected back after the break wearing a mask, and Josh Acheampong's problem is described as minor. João Pedro's return is less certain: Roundtable reported on Saturday that it stays in doubt until he is back in full training.

What Leeds bring, and what is at stake

Leeds arrive ninth on 14 points (three wins, five draws, two defeats) and with a little momentum. Noah Okafor's 79th-minute winner against Tottenham on November 7, his first touch off the bench, ended a five-game league run without a win. Daniel Farke set up in a 3-5-2, and the goal came from a long throw, with Harry Wilson providing the assist (ESPN/PA).

That is the matchup in a sentence: Chelsea's possession against a Leeds back five that is happy without the ball and dangerous from set pieces. Chelsea have beaten Leeds twice in 2026 already, 1-0 in April's FA Cup semi-final under interim coach Calum McFarlane (record) and 6-3 in September's EFL Cup tie at Stamford Bridge.

The run that follows is unforgiving: Nottingham Forest away on November 27, then Crystal Palace, Liverpool and Manchester City in the first fortnight of December (ESPN fixtures). The hierarchy is prepared to give Alonso time, according to a single-sourced Football Insider report (via The Hard Tackle). A second straight league defeat would test that patience early.

Second feature

The Palmer question follows him back to Cobham

Thomas Tuchel left Cole Palmer out of England's squad. Morgan Rogers defended him, scored, and won a group. Chelsea get a rested No 10 for Leeds.

Cole Palmer spent the international break at Cobham. Announcing his squad on November 6, England head coach Thomas Tuchel said: "It counts when you are in camp and I just said last time that he misses too many opportunities to impress" (beIN Sports). Palmer has not played for England since a 1-0 friendly defeat to Japan in March.

His Chelsea team-mate Morgan Rogers pushed back. Speaking to BBC Radio 5 Live, Rogers said doubts about Palmer's commitment to England were "a lie", and that Palmer had been "in quite a low place with his injuries last year" (via The False 9). Alonso has said he is "very happy with his quality, with his attitude".

Rogers then made the case for himself. He scored England's opener in a 2-1 win over Croatia on November 12 and started the 3-2 win in Spain three days later, a result that won England their Nations League group (VAVEL). For Chelsea the upside is simple: Palmer arrives at the Leeds game rested, and Rogers arrives in form.

Transfer News

Gusto's price tag keeps Manchester City at arm's length

Chelsea want £75m for a right-back who has started three league games this season. That figure, not the player's wishes, is shaping January.

Chelsea have put a £75m valuation on Malo Gusto, and that number has so far deterred Manchester City, according to Football Insider's Pete O’Rourke (November 15). City explored a move in the summer and were put off by the same figure. Gusto's contract runs to 2030.

The context is minutes. Gusto has started only three Premier League games under Alonso, and came off the bench at Sunderland. A follow-up on The Hard Tackle suggests Gusto could appeal to Enzo Maresca, who coached him at Chelsea. Lower-tier outlets also name Manchester United as monitoring; we have not seen that from a primary source.

Certainty: concrete interest from City, single-sourced to Football Insider. No bid has been reported. Chelsea's stance reads as a price, not a refusal.

Koundé is the name in Spain if Gusto goes

The Barcelona defender is linked as Alonso's preferred right-back, but the reporting is thin and contradictory.

Spanish journalist Juan Jesús posted on October 30 that Chelsea had made Jules Koundé their "absolute priority" for the winter, adding that Barcelona would only listen if the player asked to leave (via TEAMtalk and The Pride of London).

A CaughtOffside report on November 15, relaying TEAMtalk, says Barcelona are now open to discussing a sale and that Chelsea would sanction Gusto's exit if the right replacement arrived. Those two positions do not sit easily together, and no fee or bid has been reported. Koundé is under contract until 2030.

Certainty: rumour. The chain runs through aggregators back to one journalist's social post. Treat it as the shape of Alonso's thinking rather than a live negotiation.

Alex Scott stays at Bournemouth, for now

The England midfielder remains Chelsea's most-discussed midfield target, but The Telegraph says January is off the table.

Bournemouth will not sell their star trio in January, The Telegraph reported on November 12 (The Telegraph, as relayed by Transfer News Live). That list includes Alex Scott, who has been linked with Chelsea since the summer. Aggregators repeat a claim of a rejected bid of more than £60m last summer; we could not trace that figure to a named reporter.

FootballTransfers puts his likely cost at €120m and says Chelsea intend to bid (via LiveScore), but its own copy stops short of saying an offer has been made. Accounts of Scott's fitness after England duty also conflict between outlets.

Certainty: concrete interest, blocked for January according to The Telegraph. That is a shift from earlier reports that Bournemouth might listen in January at the right price. Revisit in the summer.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Leeds United: hard to beat, dangerous from a dead ball

Daniel Farke's side are ninth, won their last game and are built to frustrate a team that has the ball.

Leeds United come to west London ninth with 14 points from ten games, and the shape of their season is in the record: three wins, five draws, two defeats and a goal difference of +2. They are hard to beat and rarely run away with games. Daniel Farke's side had gone five league matches without a win before beating Tottenham.

Against Spurs, Farke used a 3-5-2 with Ethan Ampadu, Ao Tanaka and Mateus Fernandes among the midfield and Dominic Calvert-Lewin leading the line (ESPN/PA). The PA report singled out goalkeeper James Trafford for key saves and criticised Leeds for wasting crossing positions.

The players to stop are Calvert-Lewin, Leeds' leading scorer this season, and whoever takes the set pieces. Okafor's winner came from a long throw, flicked on by Harry Wilson, and Chelsea's defeat at Sunderland turned on a conceded penalty. Defending the box, not the halfway line, is the brief.

The vulnerability is the flip side of the back five: Leeds cede territory. Sunderland showed that Chelsea's 69 per cent possession means little without runners, so the return of João Pedro, or Palmer and Rogers finding pockets between the wing-backs and centre-backs, may decide it. Leeds' team news had not been confirmed at the time of writing.

Recent meetings favour Chelsea: a 1-0 FA Cup semi-final win in April and a 6-3 EFL Cup win in September, both in 2026.

The Squad

Alonso's shifting XI, and who is back for Leeds

A settled side has been out of reach. The break offers a reset, and two or three returns.

Xabi Alonso's first ten league games have produced five wins, a draw and four defeats, and a team still changing shape week to week. Against Sunderland his side lined up as a 4-3-2-1 according to ESPN, with Jordan Henderson and Roméo Lavia beside Caicedo and Palmer and Rogers behind Neto.

In goal, Mike Penders has deputised for the injured Martínez, with the senior alternatives sent out on loan in the summer (Robert Sánchez to Como, Filip Jørgensen to Strasbourg). Martínez's expected return after the break, masked, gives Alonso a decision to make.

Caicedo's workload and the fantasy football leak

Moisés Caicedo is not on the injury list but reported discomfort in training and admitted to tiredness before the break (Roundtable). He then captained Ecuador and scored a penalty in a 2-0 win over Costa Rica, with Bolivia to come on Tuesday. Managing his minutes may be Alonso's quiet priority this month.

Off the pitch, Chelsea emailed staff reminding them of the consequences of using inside information on public games after Fantasy Premier League creators spotted players and staff removing João Pedro from their teams before the Tottenham game (Inside World Football). The club regards it as isolated. Alonso's response: "I am not into the fantasy team thing."

Cobham

Ezenwata's week, and the loan minutes that matter

A derby win for the Under-21s, a goal for England's Under-19s and a mixed picture out on loan.

The Under-21s went into the break with a 3-1 win at Tottenham in Premier League 2 on November 8, ending Spurs' six-game unbeaten start (Tottenham Hotspur). Harrison McMahon scored after seven minutes and Chizaram Ezenwata added two, the second in stoppage time. Kendry Páez and Reggie Watson both started.

Ezenwata carried that form into England duty, scoring a penalty as the Under-19s won a second straight European qualifier (Chelsea News). Sixteen-year-old Reggie Watson, who has already made his first-team debut, is with England's Under-17s; David Pleat called him a potential "generational talent", according to The Chelsea Chronicle (via LiveScore).

How the loanees are doing

Sports Mole counted every loanee's minutes up to the break. The busiest: Kaiden Wilson (Rochdale, 1,431 minutes in 18 games), Ollie Harrison (AFC Wimbledon, 1,095) and Reggie Walsh (Wigan, 923). At a higher level, Filip Jørgensen has 900 minutes at Strasbourg, Mamadou Sarr 778 at Real Sociedad and Axel Disasi 753 at Crystal Palace.

The concern is at the other end. Alejandro Garnacho has 95 minutes in seven matches at Aston Villa and Mykhailo Mudryk 16 minutes in one at Tottenham. Robert Sánchez has 360 at Como and Benoît Badiashile 332 at Napoli. Genesis Antwi, on loan at Strasbourg, earned a first Sweden call-up this month (Chelsea FC).

Chelsea Women

Baltimore's late strike sends Chelsea Women top

A 1-0 win over Manchester City ended City's unbeaten run. Now comes PSG in Europe.

Chelsea Women are top of the WSL. Sandy Baltimore's strike four minutes into first-half stoppage time, set up by Ellie Carpenter, beat Manchester City 1-0 at Stamford Bridge on November 14 and ended City's 17-game unbeaten run in all competitions (VAVEL).

City finished with ten after goalkeeper Ayaka Yamashita was sent off around the 75th minute for bringing down Alyssa Thompson outside the box (Everything Chelsea). The table now reads Chelsea 26 points, City 25, London City Lionesses 20 and Manchester United 18 after ten rounds (table).

Two early injuries, and a big week in Europe

The win came at a cost. Sonia Bompastor lost Kadeisha Buchanan after 12 minutes, saying she had taken a knock to the head in training and "was not herself from the start", and Melvine Malard, who had six league goals before kick-off, went off on 35 minutes with a hamstring problem (Hayters). No return dates have been given.

Chelsea's defence has been the foundation: 29 scored and five conceded in ten league games, eight wins and two draws. In the Champions League it has been tighter. Draws at Lyon and Häcken alongside home wins over Vienna and Roma leave Chelsea on eight points, outside the top four, per UEFA.

Wednesday's visit of Paris Saint-Germain (8pm UK, noon Pacific) is therefore significant. PSG are tenth after losing 2-1 at home to Bayern. Then it is back to the league at Liverpool on Saturday at 12:45pm UK (4:45am Pacific) (fixtures).
