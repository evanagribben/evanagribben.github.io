The Lead

Pochettino takes over a club in the bottom three

Michael Carrick lasted 15 games into a contract that ran to 2028. His successor has two and a half years, one free week at Carrington and a trip to Anfield to begin the repair.

On Thursday, November 12, Manchester United ended Michael Carrick's time as men's head coach. The club statement, as reported by Sky Sports, spoke of “a disappointing start to the season” and “the extremely difficult conclusion that a change is necessary”. A day later it named Mauricio Pochettino as his successor, effective from November 18 (Al Jazeera).

The numbers made the decision. United have one win, five draws and four defeats from ten league games: 8 points, 18th place, a point above bottom side Crystal Palace. Across all competitions they had won three of 15. Sky Sports called it the club's worst start to a season in 40 years, the poorest top-flight start since 1986 (Sky Sports).

How the decision was made

Chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox took the case for change to Monaco, Sports Illustrated reported, and Sky Sports says Sir Jim Ratcliffe ratified the decision. Carrick's last game was the 4-4 draw with Aston Villa on November 8, rescued by a 96th-minute Patrick Dorgu header.

Carrick had insisted he would not walk away. Asked after that draw whether he would resign, he said: “That's not going to happen” (PA via the Irish News). After his exit he wished the club “nothing but success” and said his love for United “never dies”, according to AFP.

His staff went with him. Sky reports that Jonny Evans, Jonathan Woodgate, Steve Holland, Travis Binnion and goalkeeping coach Craig Mawson have all left, with only assistant goalkeeping coach Connor King remaining. That detail is single-sourced. Overall Carrick won 15 of 32 games, including the run to third place last season that returned United to the Champions League.

“They know that not too many opportunities happen in your career like Manchester United.”

Mauricio Pochettino, on leaving the USA job (ESPN)

The deal, the fee and the farewell

Pochettino has signed a two-and-a-half-year contract that runs to the summer of 2029, according to Sky Sports, ESPN and AFP. The club statement itself did not state the length. United agreed to release him from the United States job after the Concacaf Nations League quarter-final against Haiti.

Reported compensation varies. Sky says a £7 million release clause, with £7.4 million paid; ESPN and David Ornstein of The Athletic put it at $10 million (about £7.5 million). US Soccer chief executive JT Batson said the money “will be used to invest in all the national team categories” (World Soccer Talk).

The USA beat Haiti 6-0 in Miami on November 14 in the first leg (Scarves and Spikes). The second leg is in Cincinnati on Tuesday, after which Pochettino flies to Manchester. The club says he starts on Wednesday; Sky expects his first session on Thursday. Leaving the USA was “one of the most difficult decisions of my career”, he said.

Why Pochettino, and what he inherits

TEAMtalk reports that Pochettino was chosen ahead of Eddie Howe, Julian Nagelsmann and Antonio Conte. David Ornstein said United interviewed him in 2024 and were “a little underwhelmed” (via TEAMtalk). A later Athletic report that Ratcliffe preferred Conte while the Glazers pushed for Pochettino was called “categorically false” by the club, as relayed by CaughtOffside.

The club's announcement, quoted by ESPN, describes him as “renowned for his high-intensity, attacking style of football”. The squad he inherits has conceded 30 goals in all competitions, the most in Europe's top five leagues (NBC Sports), and has not kept a league clean sheet all season. The Telegraph's Oliver Brown called the club a “circus” and doubted there would be money to spend in January (Telegraph via Yahoo).

His backroom team is not yet confirmed by the club. TEAMtalk reports that Jesús Pérez, Miguel D'Agostino, goalkeeping coach Toni Jiménez and his son Sebastiano Pochettino will follow him (TEAMtalk). He becomes United's eighth permanent manager since Sir Alex Ferguson retired in 2013.

The first task is brutal. Liverpool away on Sunday, then Sporting CP in Lisbon on Wednesday, then Brentford at Old Trafford. The fixture list allows no bedding-in period, and the bottom three allows no patience.

How the autumn slipped away

Carrick's last ten games tell the story in miniature. United scored 13 and conceded 23. Only once, against Roma, did they win, and the 4-4 with Villa showed both sides of the team: 26 shots and 3.55 expected goals, but four goals conceded from 12 Villa shots (ESPN).

The defensive record is the crux. Villa led 2-0 inside four minutes, the earliest United have ever trailed by two in a Premier League game (NBC Sports). No Premier League side had previously come from two goals down twice to draw a match (Opta Analyst).

Scouting the Next Opponent

Liverpool: strong on the road, oddly fragile at Anfield

Andoni Iraola's side are fourth, but they have collected only five points from five home games. Pochettino's debut lands on that weakness.

Liverpool sit fourth on 18 points from 10 games, nine behind Arsenal. Their split is striking: 13 points from 15 away from home, five from 15 at Anfield, where they have scored only four league goals and failed to score against Fulham, Manchester City and Arsenal (dave.sport).

They went into the break on a better note, winning 3-1 at Crystal Palace on November 8 through Alexis Mac Allister, Florian Wirtz and Alexander Isak, after league and Champions League defeats to Arsenal (0-1) and Fenerbahçe (2-3) (VAVEL). Andoni Iraola, appointed in June, said he was “very upset” with the penalty that cost his side a clean sheet (Liverpool FC).

The players to stop

Alexander Isak has seven goals this season. Florian Wirtz drifts between the lines, Mac Allister sets the tempo, and Virgil van Dijk organises the back line. Two caveats: van Dijk withdrew from the Netherlands squad without a stated reason, and Dominik Szoboszlai has a knee sprain, according to dave.sport. Both need checking nearer kickoff.

Where United can hurt them

Liverpool face Club Brugge in the Champions League on November 25 and Everton on November 29, so rotation is possible. United's best route is pressing the build-up and attacking early, before a home crowd that has seen little to cheer settles. Last May United won 3-2 at Old Trafford; they won 2-1 at Anfield in October 2025.

Transfer News

Centre-back first: the January list takes shape

Nothing senior is done, but the reporting has converged on a left-sided defender and, with Benjamin Šeško injured, a striker.

Chris Wheeler of the Daily Mail (via TEAMtalk) names three centre-backs as potential January targets: Calvin Bassey of Fulham, Nathan Collins of Brentford and Jarrad Branthwaite of Everton. A loan striker is called more likely than a permanent one. Status: concrete interest, no bids reported.

The Telegraph, via Sports Mole, reports that Pochettino sees Bassey as his first target. He is 26, left-footed, can cover left-back and captains Fulham this season. No fee has been reported in any primary article; figures in circulation come from aggregator headlines.

talkSPORT's Alex Crook says United want “a left-sided central defender” and expects them “to be busy” (via Football365). Pete O'Rourke of Football Insider says Collins “would tick a lot of boxes”. Branthwaite was valued at about £80 million by Everton in September, an old figure that would make him the hardest of the three.

Romano: “at least one signing” for Pochettino

Fabrizio Romano says United will hold conversations with the new head coach about signings and ideas. “I therefore expect at least one signing, but it could be even more,” he said, naming centre-back and striker as the two key areas (via TEAMtalk).

On a striker, Romano pointed to “Benjamin Sesko's physical issues. That is a serious possibility.” Šeško is reported to be out until 2027 with a shin problem. Crook mentioned “a little bit of talk” around Folarin Balogun of Monaco, who played under Pochettino for the USA. That remains speculation.

The review: 11 players under the microscope

Dean Jones of Flashscore reports that the planned overhaul goes ahead whoever the manager is, with “a substantial turnover of players” over six months. Eleven are under review: Shaw, Maguire, Martínez, Fernandes, Rashford, Dalot, Mount, Mazraoui, Zirkzee, De Ligt and Ugarte. Sales may be needed to fund summer spending.

Four of those deals expire in 2027. Romano reports an “overwhelming expectation” that Luke Shaw leaves next summer. Bruno Fernandes's contract talks have stalled, with the player wanting a deal to 2030 and the club reluctant; United hold an option to 2028 (TEAMtalk, via CaughtOffside). AS reported on November 1 that Lisandro Martínez could go in January for an acceptable fee (via CaughtOffside).

The Squad

A new regime at Carrington, and a thin back line

No match was played this week, so there are no ratings. The real questions are who is fit for Anfield and how Pochettino will use a squad in turmoil.

United played no first-team match between November 9 and 16 because of the international break, so this edition carries no player ratings. Last week's edition rated the 4-4 with Villa.

Pochettino's first rule

The Daily Mirror reports that Pochettino will end post-match days off: players will report to Carrington for recovery sessions the day after games, something Carrick seldom required. Football365 noted that several outlets called it a “ban”; it is better described as a routine (Football365).

The defence he inherits

The Telegraph judged that a back four of Dorgu, Mazraoui, Yoro and Heaven is not good enough to escape the bottom three. Availability makes it worse. Patrick Dorgu failed Denmark's medical tests with a hamstring problem and was sent home on November 13 (Sports Mole). Harry Maguire came off at half-time against Villa with a back problem.

Luke Shaw has a muscle problem and has not played since the Tottenham game; he is aiming for Liverpool. Lisandro Martínez has served his ban after the red card at Chelsea. Diogo Dalot was booed when substituted against Villa after conceding a penalty, and Carrick said: “We've got to be careful with that” (Football365).

Midfield, captaincy and the dressing room

Two subplots await Pochettino. AFP reports that captain Bruno Fernandes was dropped last month after criticising Carrick's tactics, and that Martínez criticised the club's leadership over investment. Fernandes still scored a penalty against Villa and set up Dorgu's equaliser. Kobbie Mainoo, dropped by England this month, will want to impress the new man.

Carrington

A first signing of the new era, and a youth call-up wave

A 16-year-old striker from Reading, a red card at Burnley and 15 Carrington players on age-group international duty.

The first signing of the Pochettino era is a teenager. Tyler Adjei, a 16-year-old centre-forward from Reading, was registered this week and joins the Under-18s, according to The Athletic (via Sports Mole).

Under-21s and Under-18s

Adam Lawrence's Under-21s drew 1-1 at Burnley on November 8. Bendito Mantato scored on 16 minutes, Zach Johnson equalised after an error by Albert Mills, and Dan Armer was sent off on 64 minutes. Jaydan Kamason stood out (The Peoples Person). They are ninth in Premier League 2 with 10 points from five games and host Crystal Palace on November 29.

The Under-18s had no fixture this week. They are second in the North division with 22 points from nine games, two behind Manchester City, who have a game in hand. They host Derby on Saturday at noon UK time, 4am Pacific (FotMob).

Called up

Harry Amass, Ayden Heaven, Jim Thwaites and Shea Lacey went with England Under-20s; Yuel Helafu, Albert Mills and Tynan Thompson with the Under-19s. Dan Armer and Tyler Fletcher joined Scotland Under-21s and Jacob Devaney the Republic of Ireland Under-21s (Yahoo Sports). Lacey, who scored on his first senior start against Brighton in September, came off the bench against Villa.

Out on loan

Altay Bayındır made his Celta Vigo debut in a 5-0 Copa del Rey win at Salamanca, keeping a clean sheet (The Peoples Person). André Onana kept his fifth clean sheet in seven games for Trabzonspor in a 2-0 win over Gaziantep, and the Turkish club reportedly want to keep him (Yahoo Sports; match date not confirmed).

Ethan Wheatley (Lincoln) had played only once by early November after injury, and Chido Obi (Willem II) was reported to have scored in a break friendly, which we could not verify. United have 15 players on loan this season (full list).

Manchester United Women

Le Tissier marks 150 games with a 25-yard goal

Two wins in seven days lift Eva Olid's side to fourth in the WSL, two points off the top three.

Manchester United Women beat Crystal Palace 2-0 at Leigh on Saturday. Captain Maya Le Tissier opened the scoring with a 25-yard strike on 18 minutes in her 150th appearance, the fifth player in the club's history to reach the mark. Elisabeth Terland added a second on 65 minutes from Jess Park's pass (Sky Sports).

Terland also had a goal ruled out for offside, and head coach Eva Olid was booked in the tenth minute. Six days earlier, Julia Zigiotti came off the bench to head a late winner in a 1-0 victory at Aston Villa, with Olid crediting the squad's depth (The Peoples Person).

The table and the week ahead

United are fourth on 18 points after ten games (W5 D3 L2, goal difference +1), two behind London City Lionesses. Chelsea went top with a 1-0 win over Manchester City, ending City's unbeaten start (Reuters via The Star; table).

Everton visit Leigh in the Subway Cup on Wednesday at 7pm UK (11am Pacific). On Sunday United travel to Birmingham City in the WSL, kickoff noon UK (4am Pacific), shown on Sky Sports in the UK (Yahoo Sports). The only transfer noise is a rumoured interest in Fiamma Benítez.

Players Away

Lammens the standout, a grim night in Braga

A penalty save against Italy for United's goalkeeper; a 4-1 home defeat for Portugal's two United men.

Senne Lammens had the best break of any United player. He made six saves as Belgium beat Turkey 2-1 on November 12, his long ball starting the winner (The Peoples Person). On Sunday he saved a stoppage-time penalty from Pio Esposito as Belgium beat Italy 4-1 to reach the Nations League quarter-finals (UEFA).

Portugal lost 4-1 at home to Denmark in Braga on November 14, Rasmus Højlund scoring twice. Bruno Fernandes came off on 79 minutes and Diogo Dalot played 90; A Bola gave both 3 out of 10 (centredevils; AFP).
