The Lead

Seventy-five million for a 28-year-old: inside the deal that tells you what Arsenal actually think went wrong in Budapest

Fabrizio Romano posted the two words on Wednesday morning. Arsenal have their man, Bruno Guimarães, for a flat £75 million from Newcastle United — and the champions of England have just spent the single biggest fee of the Mikel Arteta era on a player who turns 29 in November. That is not a contradiction. It is a diagnosis.

There is a particular kind of hangover available only to a club that wins its league by seven points and then loses a European Cup final on penalties, and Arsenal have spent the summer of 2026 nursing it. The Premier League title arrived in May after a twenty-two-year wait — 85 points, 26 wins, a goal difference of plus forty-four, Manchester City held off at last. Eight days later, in the Puskás Aréna, Kai Havertz put them ahead inside six minutes against Paris Saint-Germain and they could not hold it. Ousmane Dembélé equalised from the spot on the hour, the game died in extra time, and PSG won the shoot-out 4–3 to become the first club since Real Madrid in 2017 to retain the trophy.

You can tell a great deal about a club by what it buys after a night like that. Arsenal's answer, arrived at over six weeks of grinding negotiation and confirmed with Romano's "HERE WE GO" on Wednesday morning, is a 28-year-old Brazilian central midfielder with 176 Premier League appearances behind him and no obvious resale value. David Ornstein had trailed it for days — first that Arsenal were "nearing total agreement" with Newcastle, then that the fee was settled — and the shape of it says everything. This is not a squad-building signing. It is a win-now signing, and specifically a win-in-Europe signing.

Key Facts · The Guimarães Deal

Fee: £75m flat, no add-ons (Ornstein; Simon Collings)

Structure: Payable over 24 months (Martin Hardy, The Times)

Sell-on: Lyon due roughly £6–7m — 20% of Newcastle's profit

Personal terms: Agreed since July (Romano)

Medical: Permission granted; tests this week (Ben Jacobs)

Shirt: No. 39 — vacant, and the number he has worn at every senior club

Target date: Registered before the Emirates Cup, Sunday 9 August

Certainty tier: Medical stage / "here we go" — one step short of official. No club announcement at the time of writing.

Consider what Arsenal already had. Declan Rice and Martin Zubimendi are, on their day, as good a double pivot as exists in the league; Mikel Merino is a World Cup winner; Martin Ødegaard is the captain; Myles Lewis-Skelly finished last season playing some of his best football in central midfield rather than at left-back. On a whiteboard, midfield was not the problem. In Budapest it was. PSG's Vitinha took the man-of-the-match award for a performance built on exactly the quality Arsenal lacked once the game slowed down: the ability to receive under pressure, turn, and change the picture without needing a teammate to make the decision for him.

That is Guimarães' whole game. He is not a runner in the Rice sense, nor a pure positional pivot in the Zubimendi sense. He is a manipulator of tempo — the player who takes the ball off a centre-half with a defender on his shoulder and buys the team ten yards and two seconds. Four and a half seasons on Tyneside made him the most consistently press-resistant midfielder in the division, and he did it in a side that spent large stretches of last season in the bottom half. Newcastle finished twelfth, on 49 points, with a negative goal difference. He was, by some distance, too good for that.

"The number 39 gave me everything in life. It got me here to Newcastle. It fed me, clothed me, and paid for three-hour bus rides to pursue my dream."

— Bruno Guimarães, on the shirt number that will follow him to N5

The negotiation itself is worth dwelling on, because it is the clearest evidence yet of how sporting director Andrea Berta operates. Newcastle's opening position, per reporting on Tyneside, was that only a "ridiculous offer" would be entertained. Luke Edwards of the Telegraph reports that Arsenal eventually knocked something in the region of £25m off that asking price, with the agent Kia Joorabchian active in the middle. The lever was the player. Guimarães had agreed personal terms in July and, per Lee Ryder, told the new Newcastle head coach Matthias Jaissle directly that he wanted to go. Once that happened, the only genuine sticking point left was the payment schedule — and Arsenal solved it by agreeing to spread £75m across 24 months rather than fighting over the headline figure. It is a flat fee. No add-ons. Both clubs know exactly what this costs.

Two consequences follow immediately. The first is that Christian Nørgaard is gone — Everton agreed roughly £7m with Arsenal at the weekend and the Dane was undergoing his medical on Merseyside this week, again first reported by Ornstein. He leaves as a Premier League champion having started precisely one league game all season, the dead rubber against Crystal Palace after the title was already won, and having accumulated 101 league minutes across seven appearances. That was always the deal he signed up for, and at 32 he has taken the medal and moved on. The second is that Arsenal have now made themselves emphatic about Lewis-Skelly: Manchester United have been circling, and per Football Insider on Wednesday the club will not entertain it at any price. Signing a £75m midfielder and refusing to sell a 20-year-old one is a coherent position only if you intend to play both.

There is an argument against, and it deserves an airing. £75m for a player who will be 32 when the deal's instalments are still being paid is not obviously good business in a PSR world, and it is precisely the kind of purchase Arsenal under this ownership have historically avoided. By our arithmetic, the window's gross spend rises to roughly £143.5m once Guimarães lands, against recorded income of about £24.3m including Nørgaard — a net outlay north of £119m, before whatever the undisclosed Leandro Trossard fee turns out to be. The counter-argument is simply that amortisation over a long contract, the 24-month payment structure and the revenues of a title-winning, Champions-League-finalist season make it affordable, and that a club which has just discovered how narrow the last margin is does not get many summers in which the squad is one player away.

The tactical question that follows is the interesting one, and it will not be answered in Dublin or at the Emirates Cup. Does Arteta play Guimarães as the left-sided eight in a 4-3-3, pushing Rice deeper alongside Zubimendi? Does he pair the Brazilian with Rice in a two and ask Ødegaard to play higher still? Or is this, quietly, a succession plan for a captain who turns 28 in December and whose contract runs to 2028? What is not in doubt is the timing. Arsenal face Manchester City in the Community Shield in Cardiff on 16 August and open the defence of the title against newly-promoted Coventry City at the Emirates five days later. They want him registered and running by Sunday.

One last note on the certainty of it, because this newsletter tries to be precise about such things. As of noon Pacific on Wednesday there had been no announcement from Arsenal and none from Newcastle. What exists is a "here we go" from the most reliable aggregator of these things, a fee confirmed independently by the most reliable Arsenal reporter alive, and a medical that has been formally permitted. In August 2026 that is about as close to done as a transfer gets without a photograph of a man holding a shirt.

The hole where Saliba should be: how a World Cup semi-final wrecked Arsenal's defensive summer

A back injury sustained with France against Spain has taken the best defender in the league out of the opening months of the title defence — and turned a settled back line into the club's most urgent shopping list.

William Saliba came home from the World Cup with a back problem and a semi-final defeat, and Arsenal subsequently confirmed via an official medical update that the 25-year-old faces an extended spell out. He will not require surgery, which is the good news; several reports have put the absence at four to five months, which is emphatically not. Even at the optimistic end of that range, Arsenal will play the Community Shield, the opening block of the league season and the first Champions League matchday without the defender who anchored an 85-point title win.

The knock-on is already visible in the market. Arsenal made Piero Hincapié's loan from Bayer Leverkusen permanent for a reported £34.5m earlier in the window — a deal conceived as depth, now promoted to necessity — and Sky Sports report the club have registered interest in two more. John Stones is a free agent following the expiry of his Manchester City contract, which makes him cost-free and, given three years working under Arteta at the Etihad, tactically pre-installed. It also makes him 32 with a body that produced only 439 Premier League minutes last season across thigh, calf and muscular problems. Ezri Konsa is the other name: four years younger, the best ground-duel win rate in the division last season at 73 per cent, two years left on his deal, and a Aston Villa valuation around £60m that Sky describe as leaving a significant gap to Arsenal's number.

Nor is Saliba the only absentee. Jurrien Timber underwent groin surgery after picking up a problem in mid-March, during the run-in; he made a 54-minute cameo from the bench in the Champions League final, withdrew from the Netherlands World Cup squad on fitness grounds, and had not resumed grass training when Arsenal flew to Spain. He is in a genuine race for the Coventry opener. That leaves Gabriel Magalhães, Riccardo Calafiori, Cristhian Mosquera, Hincapié and Ben White to cover four defensive positions across four competitions. Berta's stated method — running several targets per position rather than betting on one — is about to be stress-tested in public.

Transfer News

Tzolis arrives with a goal in his first outing — and a job description written by Trossard's exit

£34m from Club Brugge. Status: OFFICIAL.

The signing that has quietly excited the training ground most this summer is not the Brazilian. Christos Tzolis joined from Club Brugge in a deal Sky Sports valued at £34m, and he has begun exactly as Arsenal would have drawn it: an assist for Reiss Nelson against MK Dons on 26 July, then a goal of his own at Girona on 1 August, cutting inside from the left and finishing.

He is, explicitly, the replacement for Leandro Trossard, who has departed for Beşiktaş for an undisclosed fee after three and a half seasons in which he was Arteta's most reliable solver of tight games. That is a considerable pair of boots. Arteta's early verdict is encouraging, praising a player who has "the goal between the eyes" and, pointedly, the work ethic to go with it. Tzolis for his part has said the pitch that convinced him was about his background and his style rather than a numbers target — which, from a 24-year-old Greek international arriving at the champions of England, reads as the right kind of confidence.

Nørgaard to Everton: a champion leaves after 101 league minutes

£7m to Everton. Status: Medical stage / agreement in principle (Ornstein).

Everton reached agreement with Arsenal for Christian Nørgaard at around £7m, with the Dane undergoing his medical this week. He arrived from Brentford a year ago as insurance in a squad chasing four fronts and finished with 20 appearances in all competitions, the overwhelming majority in cups, and a single Premier League start. He also finishes with a Premier League winner's medal, which is not nothing, and at 32 a move to a club that will play him is straightforwardly the right one for both parties. His exit is what clears the wage and squad space for Guimarães.

Vinícius: the summer's biggest story is also, for now, its biggest non-story

Status: Monitoring only — Real Madrid have no intention of selling (Sky Sports News, 28 July).

It has hung over the whole window, so let us be exact about where it stands. Vinícius Júnior has one year left on his Real Madrid contract and has not signed the extension. Arsenal have monitored the situation closely. Sky Sports News reported on 28 July that Madrid have no intention of selling, are confident of a new long-term deal, and that both the new head coach José Mourinho and president Florentino Pérez are personally invested in keeping him. Sky's own valuation of the player was around £137m.

What keeps it alive is that the contract talks have not concluded. Reporting this week indicates the gap is over image rights and the overall package rather than pure salary, with the player returned to pre-season training in Madrid and nothing signed. Arsenal's position, as best it can be established, is opportunistic rather than active: they are not negotiating, they are watching. Anyone telling you a fee has been agreed is ahead of the evidence. Treat every version of this story that does not carry an Ornstein, Romano or Sky byline with scepticism.

Barcola, and the £117m one that got away

Status: Concrete interest, no bid confirmed. PSG value him at £145m (Sky Sports).

The wide-attacking file did not begin with Bradley Barcola. It began with Morgan Rogers, and it ended badly. Sky Sports' Dharmesh Sheth reports that Arsenal worked the Aston Villa forward for weeks through intermediaries, baulked at an escalating valuation, and were then beaten to the punch by Chelsea, who moved decisively and closed at £117m within days of learning the price. That is the second consecutive window in which Arsenal's valuation discipline has cost them a first-choice attacking target, and it is the context for everything that has followed.

Barcola, 23, is the current name. Get French Football News, citing the Independent, reported in June that Arsenal were preparing a bid, with PSG thought open to a sale given Khvicha Kvaratskhelia's emergence as the preferred left winger for the biggest games. His contract runs to 2028 and extension talks stalled while the club chased Europe. Sky's paper-talk round-up has PSG valuing him at £145m, with Liverpool also interested. Realistically, a fee of that order does not coexist with £75m already committed to Guimarães and a centre-back still to buy — unless Arsenal sell more than they currently plan to.

The Rumour Mill

The smaller live threads, the sagas still simmering, and the ones we are watching before they break. Certainty labelled throughout.

Alex Scott is the name to watch. Sky Sports report that the Bournemouth midfielder has rejected a new contract on the south coast amid strong interest from Arsenal, Chelsea and Manchester United — the single most reliable pre-announcement tell there is. talkSPORT report that Berta made contact earlier in the window to understand the situation; Bournemouth want around £80m and rejected a £64m bid from Chelsea. The England international played 37 league games last season as Bournemouth qualified for the Europa League. Tier: concrete interest; no bid from Arsenal reported. With Guimarães arriving, this most likely becomes a 2027 story — unless it doesn't.

Myles Lewis-Skelly to Manchester United has been floated at around £60m. Football Insider reported on Wednesday that Arsenal have no intention of cashing in and will not entertain a sale, particularly to a domestic rival. Tier: link, actively denied.

Three senior players remain available. Fábio Vieira would like to return to Hamburger SV, where he produced 13 goal contributions on loan last season, but the clubs are roughly €10m apart and the deal is not currently live. Reiss Nelson is open to offers and has been linked with Benfica, Valencia and former loan club Fulham, without a concrete bid materialising. Gabriel Jesus is theoretically available but is reported to be determined to see out a contract that expires in 2027, which effectively settles it. All three came off the bench at Girona and were expected to feature against Real Betis. Tier: available, no advanced talks.

In goal, the arithmetic is settled. Illan Meslier's free transfer from Leeds — first reported by The Athletic and completed in July after his contract expired on 30 June — makes him third choice behind David Raya and Kepa Arrizabalaga, who is expected to stay despite a modest release clause. That, in turn, tees up a loan exit for 20-year-old Tommy Setford, whom the club want playing rather than warming a bench. Tier: expected; destination not confirmed.

What we are watching for. Two signals stand out. First, whether Arsenal move on Stones as a free agent — a signing with no fee attached, available at any time, and therefore invisible until it is announced. Second, the contract file: Bukayo Saka's deal expires in 2027 and talks have been progressing since the spring, while Gabriel Martinelli and Gabriel Jesus are also 2027 men. A champion club with eight core players out of contract in 2028 has renewals to do, and those tend to land in August.

The Squad

Eleven days to build a season: the World Cup bill comes due

Arsenal sent more players deep into a World Cup than almost anyone. The reward is seven medals. The cost is a pre-season that, for eight of them, barely exists.

The two friendlies played so far have been, by design, a mixture: senior players who were free early, then a wave of academy footballers. Arsenal beat MK Dons 3–0 at London Colney on 26 July through Reiss Nelson, Ethan Nwaneri and Ceadach O'Neill, with Meslier making an unofficial debut. Then came the first public outing, a 4–1 win at Estadi Montilivi against Girona on 1 August: Havertz on 15 minutes from a Max Dowman pass, Tzolis on the half-hour, Gabriel Jesus from a rebound, and Dowman himself after the break. Arnau Martínez got Girona's consolation from a corner. A 35-man travelling party tells you everything about the state of the senior squad.

The return schedule is the story. Havertz was back first, Germany having gone out in the round of 32. Hincapié and Viktor Gyökeres completed full training weeks before Girona; Gabriel and Martinelli returned midweek. Ødegaard, whose Norway lost to England in the quarter-finals on 11 July, has been available to train from this week but was not expected in Dublin. The players who went to the final weekend — Merino, Zubimendi and Raya among them — have around eleven days of preparation before the Coventry opener.

Two management calls sit inside that, and both are delicate. Declan Rice has been managing neural pain in his hamstring for more than six months. Bukayo Saka was playing through an Achilles problem by the end of the season. Both need assessment rather than a fixed date, and there is a credible case for holding one or both out of Cardiff entirely. Winning the Community Shield has never yet been a reason to spend a winger's autumn.

Tactically, the through-line of pre-season has been the promotion of youth into genuine senior positions rather than token cameos. Lewis-Skelly and Nwaneri were, per Arseblog's report, dominant as a first-half midfield pair at Girona — a picture that will be redrawn the moment Guimarães is registered. And the honest note from Spain is worth recording: Arsenal's intensity dropped noticeably in the second hour when the academy contingent came on, which is exactly what you would expect and exactly why the next three friendlies matter more than the first two.

Hale End

Two sixteen-year-olds who already move like men — and one twenty-year-old who needs an answer

A new academy director, nine new scholars, and a pre-season in which the kids were not there to make up the numbers.

Max Dowman set up Havertz for the opener at Girona and then scored one himself, running onto a through ball and finishing cleanly. He is sixteen. The most striking thing about him, on the evidence of a pre-season in which he has played against senior professionals, is not the technique — that has been obvious since he was fourteen — but the physicality. He holds people off. Marli Salmon, also sixteen and on his first professional contract since April, played a demanding defensive-midfield role in Spain with a composure that drew genuinely startled reactions. Both signed three-year deals, which is the maximum available to players of their age and tells you the club expects to renegotiate long before those expire.

Behind them, Ife Ibrahim gave a strong account of himself in defensive midfield and has begun drawing the sort of comparisons — long-limbed, deceptively quick, easy on the ball — that a young player is better off not reading. Ceadach O'Neill, eighteen and already a full Northern Ireland international, scored against MK Dons. Josh Ogunnaike and Louie Copley both filled in at full-back out of position and did not embarrass themselves, while Brayden Clarke, Andre Harriman-Annous, Ismeal Kabia, Demiane Agustien and Theo Julienne all got minutes.

All of this is happening under new management. Pascal De Maesschalck has begun his tenure as academy director, and a pre-season in which nine youth players travelled to Spain is a reasonable first statement of intent. Arsenal also confirmed nine new youth contract agreements last month and announced their 2026/27 first-year scholars, with 17-year-old Elijah Upson among the summer's academy recruits. Expect a wave of loan departures for the more experienced of the group over the next fortnight — the natural consequence of a squad in which two sixteen-year-olds are ahead of them.

Which brings us to the unresolved question of the summer. Ethan Nwaneri is back from a half-season at Marseille that produced nine Ligue 1 appearances, two goals and an assist — useful, but not the settled starting berth the loan was meant to deliver. He scored against MK Dons and was excellent in the first half at Girona. He is now twenty in a midfield about to absorb a £75m signing, and the reticence he expressed about his pathway when he signed his last contract has not been answered. Arsenal have to choose in the next three weeks: guaranteed minutes here, or a loan with guarantees written into it. Leaving him in limbo a second time is the one option that is clearly wrong.

Arsenal Women

Slegers tears it up and starts again: six out, five in, and a squad finally built for five competitions

Second in the league, out in the Champions League semi-finals, and visibly threadbare by April. The response has been the most aggressive rebuild Arsenal Women have attempted in years — and this week it claimed one more departure.

The diagnosis was never in doubt. Arsenal finished second in the WSL behind a Manchester City side that lost nothing and won 42 points from 16, and went out of the Champions League in the semi-finals on 2 May, beaten by OL Lyonnes after coming back from two down at half-time and running out of road. The verdict from those closest to the team was blunt: the squad was not equipped to compete on five fronts, the top five players by minutes were all national-team starters, and rotation never happened because there was nobody to rotate to.

Renée Slegers' answer has been ruthless. Out have gone Beth Mead (to Manchester City), Katie McCabe (to Chelsea), Laia Codina, Victoria Pelova and Manuela Zinsberger, with Rosa Kafaji joining London City Lionesses permanently in mid-July. Selling McCabe to Chelsea and Mead to the champions is not a decision a cautious club makes.

In have come five, four of them on free transfers, which is its own kind of statement about where Arsenal now sit in the market. Georgia Stanway arrives from Bayern Munich to fill the hole left by Lia Wälti a year ago; since 2022/23 she completed more passes in the opposition half than anyone in the Bundesliga. Ona Batlle returns to England from Barcelona having won more tackles than any other defender in recent Champions League play. Selina Cerci comes from Hoffenheim with 32 Bundesliga goals in 43 games and a 25.4 per cent conversion rate that comfortably outstrips Alessia Russo and Stina Blackstenius. Géraldine Reuteler, from Eintracht Frankfurt and given the No. 14 shirt, is the tactical wildcard — she has played across midfield, both wings and up front. And Lisa Baum, nineteen, arrives from RB Leipzig for a reported £510,000, the window's only confirmed fee.

This week's news is a departure. Jenna Nighswonger is joining Bay FC for a fee reported by ESPN at around £150,000. The American arrived from Gotham in January 2025 and never established herself, spending 2025/26 on loan at Aston Villa with an option that was not taken up. Her squad number went to Batlle this summer and she has not been part of pre-season — the transfer, in truth, was completed the moment those two things happened. With a World Cup on the horizon it is the right move for her.

Arsenal also confirmed their pre-season schedule this week: a training camp in Spain, then two public friendlies at Meadow Park — Paris FC on Sunday 23 August and Charlton Athletic on Wednesday 26 August — with behind-closed-doors games expected in Spain and possibly over the 29–30 August weekend. The WSL campaign begins away at Brighton on Sunday 6 September. Emirates Stadium hosts all WSL home games for a second consecutive season, with Meadow Park retaining the Champions League league phase and domestic cups; second place in the WSL earns direct entry to the league phase. The one gap Arseblog's Tim Stillman still identifies is a third goalkeeper, with a left-sided centre-back and one more wide attacker on the wish list. There is time.

Gunners Abroad

A blank week on international duty — and the receipts from the one that mattered

In the interests of accuracy: no Arsenal player, men's or women's, was in competitive international action in the seven days to this edition. The World Cup finished on 19 July and the next senior windows fall in September. Everything in this section is therefore a settling of accounts from the tournament that has shaped Arsenal's entire summer — because it is the reason the squad is where it is.

Three Arsenal players are world champions. Spain beat Argentina 1–0 after extra time on 19 July, Ferran Torres settling it in the 106th minute against ten men after Enzo Fernández was sent off late in normal time for a second booking. Mikel Merino came on in the second half and went closest of anyone to breaking it earlier, heading just wide in extra time. Martín Zubimendi replaced Rodri shortly before the goal, his first appearance of the tournament. David Raya was an unused substitute, having been back-up to Unai Simón throughout. All three now hold a Premier League title and a World Cup won in the same eight weeks.

The bill arrived elsewhere. William Saliba hurt his back in the semi-final France lost to those same Spain, and will miss the opening months of the title defence. Jurrien Timber withdrew from the Netherlands squad before a ball was kicked, still short of fitness after March's groin surgery. Martin Ødegaard's Norway went out to England in the quarter-finals on 11 July. Arsenal finished the tournament with seven medal winners across the podium places — a haul that reflects extremely well on the recruitment of the last three years and extremely badly on the calendar.

On the loan and European front, the notable Arsenal footballer abroad last season was Ethan Nwaneri at Marseille — nine Ligue 1 appearances, two goals, one assist — and he is now back at London Colney with his future to be resolved. We will carry a fuller loan ledger once this summer's departures are confirmed; at the time of writing, none of the 2026/27 loans out have been officially announced.

In Brief

THIRD KIT. Arsenal launched the 2026/27 adidas third shirt on 3 August: yellow, with a subtle lightning graphic across the body and the cannon in place of the full crest, echoing the 2021/22 away shirt. Replicas from £85, authentic versions £120, with a promotional film made with the French director FX Goby.

WENGER BREAKS SILENCE. Arsène Wenger, FIFA's chief of global football development since 2019, publicly distanced himself from Gianni Infantino's aborted "FIFA Forward Enterprise" plan to sell outside investors stakes in FIFA competitions. He said he was not involved and learned of it through the media, and called the withdrawal "absolutely necessary." The project collapsed within days after UEFA, CONCACAF and the AFC refused support, with senior officials resigning in protest.

THE EMIRATES. The stadium expansion is narrowing rather than growing. Gary Jacob of The Times reports the likeliest outcome is roughly 5,000 additional corporate seats rather than a major rebuild, with underground pipework making a 10,000-seat dig economically unviable and the club unwilling to decamp to Wembley. Arsenal have consulted the architects Populous; board member Otto Maly, who worked on SoFi Stadium, holds the brief. No formal announcement has been made.

CARDIFF CALLING. The 2026 Community Shield, the 104th, will be played at the Millennium Stadium in Cardiff on 16 August — the first time the fixture has been held there since 2006. Arsenal face Manchester City, who qualified as FA Cup winners after beating Chelsea in the final.

NO FAR EAST TOUR. For the first time in several years Arsenal have skipped a long-haul summer tour, preparing instead in Spain, Ireland and London. Given how many of the squad were still at a World Cup in mid-July, that looks less like a commercial decision than a medical one.
