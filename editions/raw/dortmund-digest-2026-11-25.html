The Lead

A point at the Emirates, top of the Bundesliga: Dortmund's week of statements

Niko Kovač's side absorbed an Arsenal storm, struck back through Maximilian Beier and head into Sunday's visit of Leverkusen as Bundesliga leaders. What the week proved, and what it did not.

Twenty minutes into Tuesday night at the Emirates Stadium, Borussia Dortmund looked like a side about to be overrun. Viktor Gyökeres had already driven a 25-yard strike past Gregor Kobel on ten minutes, Arsenal were camped in the Dortmund half, and Goal would later call the opening spell disastrous.

By full time the story had flipped. Maximilian Beier equalised on 55 minutes after a run of roughly 48 metres from 19-year-old Joane Gadou, and the 1-1 draw lifted Dortmund to 10 points and seventh place in the Champions League league phase, one point behind fifth-placed Arsenal (ESPN, Sky Sports).

How Dortmund survived

The numbers say Arsenal should have won. ESPN's data had the hosts on 63 per cent possession and 2.34 expected goals to Dortmund's 0.92. Arsenal hit the post twice late on, once when Jurriën Timber's cut-back was deflected against it and once through Declan Rice, and Kobel made three saves. Kepa Arrizabalaga, deputising for the injured David Raya, made three at the other end.

Niko Kovač answered with structure rather than panic. Dortmund kept the back three of Gadou, Waldemar Anton and Nico Schlotterbeck, with Julian Ryerson and Daniel Svensson as wing-backs. Kobel described the shape as a 5-4-1 that turned into a 5-3-2. It bent repeatedly and never broke a second time.

The equaliser came from the one Dortmund player Arsenal never solved. Gadou carried the ball out of defence and deep into Arsenal territory before Beier, who had wasted an earlier chance from Svensson's cross, finished. “Joane is just a beast, the way he sets that thing up,” Beier said afterwards (Yahoo Sports).

“It's a huge point for us, a huge moment for all of us.”

Niko Kovač after the 1-1 at Arsenal

The bench, and the handball row

Kovač went to his bench on 64 minutes, sending on Serhou Guirassy, back from the thigh strain he suffered against Real Betis on November 4, and Konstantinos Karetsas for Fábio Silva and Beier. Jobe Bellingham had a goal ruled out for offside on 68 minutes, and Marcel Sabitzer replaced him five minutes later.

Kovač also wanted a penalty when a corner struck Gyökeres's arm. “I thought VAR should have looked at the handball,” he said, before adding: “I am not crying about it. It is what it is” (AOL/PA). Schlotterbeck was more self-critical: “In the first half we played too sloppily at times.”

Top of the league, with a caveat

Four days earlier, Felix Nmecha's 80th-minute winner at Borussia-Park gave Dortmund a 1-0 win over Borussia Mönchengladbach and, with Bayern Munich held by Köln, top spot in the Bundesliga (Sportschau). After ten games they are unbeaten, with seven wins and three draws, on 24 points.

The caveat is goal difference. Dortmund's +10 is the worst of the top four; Bayern sit on +20 and RB Leipzig on +18 (Bundesliga table). Their last three league wins, over Frankfurt, Elversberg and Gladbach, have all come by a single goal. Kovač himself called Saturday's winner “obviously still a lucky goal.”

Off the Pitch

A new constitution, a growth plan and a loss: the boardroom week

Members backed sweeping governance reforms on Sunday, and the company set out how it wants to earn 130 million euros more a year by 2030/31.

Borussia Dortmund's members approved a new club constitution at Sunday's general meeting with more than 96 per cent support: 858 votes in favour, 34 against and 21 abstentions, well clear of the three-quarters majority required. All four counter-motions failed (bvb.de, schwatzgelb.de).

The changes make hybrid meetings with remote voting the norm, allow a secret ballot when 10 per cent of voting members request one, and give the Wirtschaftsrat, the club's financial oversight board, a bigger role alongside a new governance committee. Candidates for office now need minimum years of membership, 15 for the presidency, plus integrity and suitability checks.

A non-binding vote to lower the voting age to 16 passed 469 to 263 and goes back to the constitution commission. There were no elections. Membership of the e.V. has risen by about 14,000 to 251,200. The club also apologised to 32 people who have reported abuse in the 1990s; that investigation is continuing (Ruhr Nachrichten).

The numbers behind the ambition

At the company's annual general meeting, chief executive Carsten Cramer named five growth areas worth a potential extra 130 million euros by 2030/31, and said Signal Iduna Park could be used far more efficiently with targeted investment (bvb.de). “We must not rest on our laurels, but must think in new dimensions,” he said.

The backdrop is a 2025/26 net loss of 21.7 million euros, covered from reserves, with consolidated revenue down 65.6 million to 460.5 million. Finance director Thomas Treß called the share price “unsatisfactory”. Inside World Football framed the vote as the club preparing for a stadium overhaul.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Leverkusen: Schick's hunt for 100 and a defence that leaks

Fifth-placed Leverkusen come to Dortmund on a short week, with a prolific striker and a back line that has let in goals in bunches.

Bayer Leverkusen sit fifth on 19 points from ten games, with six wins, one draw and three defeats and a goal difference of +7 (Bundesliga table). Coach Carles Martínez Novell was under pressure in early November (Yahoo Sports), but his side have since won at Köln (2-1, November 8) and beaten Paderborn 4-0 last weekend (bundesliga.com).

They come in off a Europa League trip to Dinamo Zagreb on Thursday (fixture), three days before kickoff in Dortmund. Their last European outing was a 1-0 defeat by Marseille on November 5, decided by an Aleix García error (Bulinews). In the DFB-Pokal they came from 2-0 down to beat Nürnberg 5-2 (Bulinews).

The players to stop

Patrik Schick has nine league goals in ten games and 99 in the Bundesliga overall, so his 100th could come on Sunday. Three of his four goals against Dortmund have come at Signal Iduna Park (bundesliga.com). Behind him, Ibrahim Maza and García are the other key figures, though Maza sprained an ankle on international duty and was a doubt last week (Yahoo Sports).

Where Dortmund can hurt them

Leverkusen conceded nine goals across three league games in October. “We've had a lot of clean sheets but have conceded a lot of goals in other games,” Martínez admitted (SoccerNews). That fragility, plus legs tired by Zagreb, suits the second-half surges that won Dortmund the Frankfurt, Elversberg and Gladbach games.

History is a warning, though. Leverkusen won 1-0 at Signal Iduna Park in the league last season through Robert Andrich, and also knocked Dortmund out of the 2025-26 DFB-Pokal.

Transfer News

The winter centre-back hunt: Gechter leads a short list

Sporting director Ole Book wants more depth at the back, and the 2. Bundesliga is where Dortmund are looking.

Borussia Dortmund sporting director Ole Book wants another centre-back in January, and Bild's Dortmund podcast reported the club is willing to spend if the right player appears (Goal via Yardbarker). Right-back is an alternative priority, because Ryerson is the only natural option there since Yan Couto's loan to Como.

The name with most substance is Hertha Berlin's Linus Gechter, 22. Kicker reported concrete interest on November 19, with no negotiations yet; his contract runs to 2027, so a free transfer next summer is the fallback (90min, Goal). Kicker also named Nürnberg's Fynn Otto and Alexios Kalogeropoulos. Certainty: concrete interest.

An older thread involves Zagłębie Lubin's Marcel Reguła, 19, who Sky said in October met Book secretly and could be retrained at right-back; Lubin reportedly want 7 to 8 million euros (90min citing Sky). Certainty: concrete interest, single-sourced.

Reggiani could be the January exit

Teenage centre-back Luca Reggiani, 18 and under contract until 2029, may leave in January. Ruhr Nachrichten reports Dortmund are open to a sale at around 10 million euros, with AC Milan among the interested Serie A clubs, (90min, Bulinews). Sky has reported that his agent told the club he wants to leave (ran.de). Certainty: concrete interest, two outlets.

Who wants Dortmund's players

Barcelona's interest in Nico Schlotterbeck resurfaced this week. Mundo Deportivo, citing Bild and Sky, says his contract runs to 2031 with a release clause of roughly 50 to 60 million euros that cannot be triggered until summer 2027 and reportedly excludes Bayern (ysscores). Certainty: rumour.

Sky reports 17-year-old Mathis Albert is unhappy with his minutes. A winter exit is not on the table, and Manchester City have been linked; Book told Sky the club has a “clear plan” for him (Bulinews, 90min). Meanwhile Serhou Guirassy told Bild he is very happy, is under contract to 2028 and “can imagine” extending (bvbwld).

The Squad

Kovač's back three is now the default, and the bench is filling up

Niko Kovač, extended to 2029 in October (bvb.de), has settled on a back three for the big games. Gadou, Anton and Schlotterbeck started at Arsenal (bvbwld), after Kovač briefly switched to a back four against Elversberg on November 7. Gadou's half-time withdrawal at Gladbach was followed by his best game in a Dortmund shirt.

Up front, the returns of Serhou Guirassy and Carney Chukwuemeka from injury (both on the bench at Arsenal) give Kovač choices again (bvb.de). Fábio Silva has started the last two games; Guirassy's 64th-minute appearance suggests he could be back in the XI against Leverkusen.

Form and goals

Felix Nmecha leads Dortmund's Bundesliga scoring with five, ahead of Silva and Guirassy on three each; Beier leads for assists with three and Karetsas has two (FotMob). Nmecha has three in his last two league games, while the row over his goal celebration, which the club had asked him to tone down, carried into Sunday's meeting (dpa via bluewin, Ruhr Nachrichten ticker).

Set pieces are a curious gap: Dortmund have one corner goal in the league so far, against 14 from 181 corners last season. Kovač says goalkeepers are better protected now, so the blocking Nmecha used to do is more likely to be penalised (bvbwld).

The Academy

BVB II close the half level at the top; the U19s let Arsenal off the hook

Daniel Rios's BVB II finished the first half of the Regionalliga West season with a 2-0 win over Sportfreunde Lotte at Stadion Rote Erde on Friday. Finn Fuchs scored on 25 minutes and Kevin Mutove added his tenth of the season on 33. They are second on 38 points, level with leaders Rot-Weiß Oberhausen (Antenne NRW). Diego Ngambia made his first start, per bvbwld.

A painful night in London

Felix Hirschnagl's U19s led Arsenal 2-0 in the UEFA Youth League through Justin Hoy (34') and Tim Degener (43'), but goalkeeper Aaron Held's red card left them a man down from the 15th minute. Arsenal's 14-year-old substitute Luis Munoz equalised, and the hosts went on to win 3-2 (bvb.de). Dortmund must beat Inter at home on December 9 to reach the knockout round.

It capped a bad week: on Saturday the U19s lost 4-3 at Rot-Weiss Essen, their first league defeat of the season, despite two goals from Luke Fahrenhorst and one from Bjarki Gardarsson (Ruhr Nachrichten, single source). They still lead their DFB-Nachwuchsliga group on 24 points, one ahead of Duisburg.

Loan report

Yan Couto set up both goals in Como's 2-0 win over Cagliari on Saturday and was rated 7 by Tuttosport (Tuttosport). Diant Ramaj went off with a groin injury after about 18 minutes of Copenhagen's 5-0 defeat at Nordsjælland (fck.dk). Almugera Kabar's NEC lost 4-1 to PSV; we could not confirm whether he played (ESPN).

Women's Team

Twelve goals in the cup as the record-breakers roll on

Borussia Dortmund's women, coached by Markus Högner in the Regionalliga West (bvb.de), won 12-0 at Landesliga side BSV Heeren in the second round of the Westfalenpokal on Saturday, in front of about 700 fans. Marina Scholz scored four (bvb.de, Hellweger Anzeiger).

They top the league after winning all 12 league games before the November 15 derby, which they then won 4-0 against Schalke before 17,555 spectators, a Regionalliga attendance record (bvb.de). VfR SW Warbeyen are the nearest chasers. Next up is Bayer 04 Leverkusen II on Sunday, December 6, at 12:30 CET (3:30 AM Pacific); club reports differ on the venue.

Players Away

There was no international window this week; the year's last one ran from about November 11 to 18. Seventeen Dortmund men's players were called up for it (bvb.de): Schlotterbeck and Nmecha (Germany), Bellingham (England), Kobel (Switzerland), Ryerson (Norway), Svensson (Sweden), Veerman (Netherlands), Silva (Portugal), Karetsas (Greece), Enzo dos Santos (Luxembourg), Bensebaini (Algeria) and Albert (USA).

At youth level, Inacio and Mané went with Italy's U21s, Nwaneri with England's, Gadou with France's and Reggiani with Italy's U20s. Fifteen academy players featured in 20 youth internationals and scored three goals: Fadi Zarqelain and Ahmad Najdi for Germany U18 against Turkey, and Edon Qetinaj for Germany U16 (bvb.de). We found no national-team news for the women's squad.
