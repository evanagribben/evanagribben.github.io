The Lead

Arsenal Spend the Break Locking the Doors

While the rest of English football scattered across Europe, Arsenal quietly did the most consequential fortnight of business they will do all season. Four renewals, one spine, no transfer fee.

There is a certain kind of week that does not look like news. No match, no goals, no movement in the table. Just a run of short posts from reporters and a few polite sentences from players in mixed zones. And yet the fortnight Arsenal have just spent without kicking a ball may shape the next five years more than anything that happens at St James' Park on Saturday.

On Monday night David Ornstein of The Athletic reported that Arsenal had reached agreement with Declan Rice over a new five-year contract. Rice's existing deal ran to June 2028 with a further year in the club's favour. The fresh terms extend to the summer of 2031. Fabrizio Romano confirmed it within the hour.

Sky Sports News, which had the story in parallel, reported that Rice has verbally agreed terms, and that Arsenal want to bring him in line with Bukayo Saka, currently the club's top earner. Rice had all but trailed it himself on England duty last week, saying a new contract was hopefully on the horizon soon.

Four at once, which is the actual story

Rice alone would be a headline. Rice as the first of four is a strategy. On Tuesday afternoon Sky reported that David Raya, Jurrien Timber and Piero Hincapie are all close to finalising new deals of their own. BBC Sport has Arsenal in advanced talks with Hincapie over a twelve-month extension on improved terms.

Look at what that group has in common. Raya and Timber were both about to enter the final eighteen months of their contracts. Both arrived in the summer of 2023, Raya on loan from Brentford and Timber from Ajax, and both have since established themselves among the best in Europe in their positions. Hincapie joined on loan from Bayer Leverkusen in 2025 and only became a permanent Arsenal player on the first of July.

Timber, speaking to ESPN Netherlands after the Dutch lost 5-0 to Germany on Monday, was unusually direct. He called the reports good ones, said they were definitely on the right track, and added that he feels at home, is enjoying himself and expects the club to be competing for trophies in the coming years.

The days of Arsenal sweating on the futures of their best players, one summer at a time, are over. They are now buying certainty before anybody has to ask for it.

What this costs, and why it is still cheap

None of it is free. Arsenal spent £199.9m in the summer and recouped £123.5m, a net outlay of £76.4m, and they are now layering four significant pay rises onto a wage bill that already carries Martin Odegaard, Saka, Viktor Gyokeres and Bruno Guimaraes.

The club can carry it. Arsenal posted record annual revenue of £691m in their most recent accounts, with commercial income rising from £218m to £263m on the back of a new Emirates shirt deal reported at around £70m a year. In October the board also stepped back from a full stadium expansion in favour of a phased upgrade, which removes the largest capital commitment on the horizon.

Set against the market, the arithmetic is blunt. A goalkeeper of Raya's standard costs upwards of £40m. A defender who can play right back, left back and centre back, as Timber does, would be priced at twice that. Renewing is not thrift. It is the cheapest transfer business available.

Who is left on the list

The obvious name is the captain. Odegaard has just over eighteen months left and, speaking to TV2 on Norway duty on Saturday, admitted talks have not really started. He said he and the club have not talked about it that much, while making clear he wants to stay. German reporting has linked Bayern Munich, though the German journalist Christian Falk has said Bayern have no plans to move.

Behind Odegaard sit three more deals expiring in 2028: Kai Havertz, Ben White, whose contract carries a club option for an additional year, and Mikel Merino. Arsenal have form here. When the club does not see a long-term future, it lets a player run toward the end of his deal and leave, as happened this summer with Gabriel Martinelli, Leandro Trossard and Gabriel Jesus. Talks with Riccardo Calafiori are expected next year.

So the renewals tell you two things at once. They tell you who Arsenal think they are building around, and they tell you who they do not. On an international break, that is a great deal of information for nothing.

The Grind Is the Plan

Fifteen goals in ten league games should not be enough to lead the Premier League by five points. Arsenal have found a way to make it enough.

There is a number in the league table that ought to be an argument against Arsenal, and has instead become the clearest statement of what they are. They have scored fifteen goals in ten matches. Brighton, in second, have scored twenty-six. Arsenal's goal difference of plus ten is barely half Brighton's plus nineteen. And Arsenal are five points clear.

The explanation sits at the other end. Five goals conceded in ten league games. No other side in the top ten has conceded fewer than seven. Arsenal have won their last five in the league, and four of those finished 1-0 or 2-0.

It is not an accident and it is not new. Last season Arsenal kept a league-high nineteen clean sheets and conceded the fewest shots, the fewest shots on target, the fewest big chances and the lowest expected goals in the division, at an average of 0.7 conceded per game. They also scored twenty-five set-piece goals, the most in the league, including a record nineteen from corners.

The risk is obvious and worth naming. A team that wins by one goal is one injury and one bad afternoon from winning by none, and the forward line is currently without Saka and Christos Tzolis. That is precisely why the January shortlist, covered below, points almost entirely at the attack.

Transfer News

A Winger, a Striker and a Very Short Window

Kroupi Is the Name Arsenal Want, and the One They Probably Cannot Have

The Independent has Arsenal running a four-man attacking shortlist into January. The preferred target is the one with the firmest no attached to him.

Miguel Delaney of The Independent reported last week that Arsenal are looking at four attacking players for January: Alex Baena of Atletico Madrid, Endrick of Real Madrid, Antonio Nusa of RB Leipzig and Eli Junior Kroupi of Bournemouth.

Delaney's framing was unambiguous. The top target is Kroupi, and Bournemouth are resistant to selling in January. The 20-year-old has mostly played as a striker on the south coast but is comfortable from the left, which fits the specific thing Mikel Arteta is said to want most, which is pace.

The obstacles are stacking rather than clearing. A LiveScore round-up on Tuesday reported that Bournemouth have ruled out January talks altogether and want around €120m next summer, with Barcelona, Bayern Munich and Paris Saint-Germain all interested. A Mirror report relayed by the BBC's gossip column on 2 November had already flagged Bayern and PSG as rivals.

Certainty: concrete interest. Multiple national outlets, no bid, no talks, and a selling club publicly closed for business until the summer.

Nusa: Agents Spoken To, Scouts in the Stands, Eighty Million Euros in the Way

The Leipzig winger has moved from a name on a list to an active file. Romano is still telling people not to expect it in January.

The most developed of the four threads is Nusa. Graeme Bailey of TEAMtalk reported on Saturday that Arsenal have spoken to the Norwegian's representatives, that the club has had scouts at his last three matches, against Schalke, Manchester City and Augsburg, and that Leipzig value him at around €80m, roughly £66m.

Bailey added two details worth more than the fee. Arsenal want a left winger and a striker in January, and funds have been set aside. He also said any deal is expected to be difficult.

That matches what Romano said on his YouTube channel on 10 November, relayed by TEAMtalk, when he called a January sale unlikely and said there was still no guarantee at all, while adding that he would not be surprised to see something happen during 2027. German reporting via Bild placed Arsenal scouts at Leipzig's Champions League defeat by Manchester City, a game in which Nusa scored.

Certainty: concrete interest, moving toward talks. Representatives contacted and scouting confirmed by a named reporter. No bid. The most reliable insider on the beat is briefing against a January move.

Endrick, and the Loan Real Madrid Will Not Sanction

Arsenal have kept the line open. The reporting on whether anything sits behind it does not agree with itself.

Of the four names, Endrick is the one where available reporting actively conflicts, and readers should treat it accordingly. A LiveScore round-up published on Tuesday states plainly that Real Madrid will not allow the Brazilian to join Arsenal on loan in January, despite Arsenal maintaining contact, and that Arsenal must now look elsewhere.

Against that, a separate report circulating this week claims discussions with Endrick's camp have gone well. Those two things are not compatible, and neither has been stood up by a tier-one source. We are flagging it rather than picking a side.

Certainty: unconfirmed rumour, single-sourced on both sides.

Zubimendi: The Thread Arsenal Did Not Want in This Window

A £55.8m midfielder who cannot get into the league team is now the subject of exit reporting. The club's own briefing says otherwise.

The uncomfortable one. Martin Zubimendi arrived from Real Sociedad in July 2025 for £55.8m and has started none of Arsenal's ten Premier League matches this season. Declan Rice and Odegaard have started all ten.

Reporting this week has described the Spain midfielder's situation as critical and said he is open to everything, with Chelsea and clubs in Spain circling, and Metro reporting that Arsenal have set an asking price. Sky Sports' own squad analysis on Tuesday took the opposite view, saying Zubimendi looks set to stay and fight for his place.

The player himself has been blunt about it. Speaking to Marca on 10 November, Zubimendi said there was absolutely nothing to the rumours and that he was not close to anything, adding that he had not spent any energy on it, had not contacted anyone, and that the media should be more careful about what it publishes.

Sunday's hamstring injury in Madrid, covered in The Squad below, does not make any of this easier to resolve quickly.

Certainty: credible but contested, and denied on the record by the player. No club-to-club contact reported.

One note on the shape of all this. Sporting director Andrea Berta is working a market in which the club's stated need is a wide forward with pace and a striker, and in which the three names with the clearest paper trail all play for clubs with no reason to sell in midwinter. That is usually how a quiet January happens.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Newcastle United: A New Manager, an Old Problem

Matthias Jaissle replaced a club institution, inherited an expensively assembled squad and has won three league games in ten. Saturday is the sort of fixture that decides how long he gets.

Newcastle United sit twelfth with twelve points from ten matches: three wins, three draws, four defeats and a goal difference of minus three. That is fifteen points behind Arsenal after the same number of games.

The man in charge is Matthias Jaissle, appointed on 5 August on a four-year deal after Eddie Howe stepped down on 31 July following almost five years at St James' Park. Jaissle is 38, German, a former Hoffenheim centre-back forced to retire at 25. He coached Liefering, then RB Salzburg from 2021, where he won two Austrian titles, then Al-Ahli in Saudi Arabia, where he won back-to-back AFC Champions League titles. Through 7 November his Newcastle record read thirteen matches, five wins, three draws, five defeats.

Form, and where it has gone wrong

Newcastle arrive on a poor run. They were beaten 4-1 at Coventry City on 12 October, lost at home to Aston Villa, won 2-0 at Crystal Palace, then lost twice in four days to Everton, 3-1 in the EFL Cup and 1-0 at St James' Park. They drew 1-1 at Fulham on 7 November. One win in five going into the break.

Local analysis has been harsher than the table. The Newcastle Times, citing FourFourTwo's long-ball data, reported last week that Newcastle sit bottom of the Premier League's expected points table after ten games, and questioned whether Yoane Wissa and William Osula offer the aerial presence Jaissle's system asks for. That is one local outlet rather than a consensus, but it is the argument being made on Tyneside.

Who Arsenal must stop

Three names. Nick Woltemade, the Germany striker signed from VfB Stuttgart, is the focal point and the main aerial threat. Anthony Gordon carries the direct running from the left and is the one player likely to attack Ben White, or whoever starts at right back. Anthony Elanga is the pace in transition, and was sharp enough on international duty to set up Sweden's opener against Bosnia on Saturday.

Behind them, Dan Burn and Fabian Schar are a serious set-piece threat in both boxes. That matters against an Arsenal side that scores a quarter of its goals from corners and has Nicolas Jover on the staff.

Where they are vulnerable

Newcastle have conceded in each of their last four league matches. More to the point, they have scored once in their last three. A side that is not scoring and is giving up good chances is a poor matchup for a team that only needs one goal, which is precisely what Arsenal have been all autumn.

The Squad

Saliba Returns, Zubimendi Hobbles, Arteta Has a Choice

Arsenal played no match in the last seven days, so there are no player ratings this week. There is, however, an unusually eventful injury list.

Start with the good news, because there is some. William Saliba has not played a competitive senior match since the end of France's World Cup campaign because of a back problem, and has missed nine matches. During the break he played the first half of Arsenal's Under-21s' 5-2 win over Aston Villa on Sunday 8 November. He came off at half time, and Under-21 head coach David Horseman was quick to shut down any alarm, saying it was all pre-planned and nobody needs to panic. Saliba is expected to be available at Newcastle.

The Madrid problem

Martin Zubimendi was withdrawn after 72 minutes of Spain's 2-3 defeat by England on Sunday, and was then filmed on the bench holding the back of his leg. Spain head coach Luis de la Fuente confirmed afterwards that the midfielder had a muscle injury. He is a doubt for Saturday.

It caps a difficult few weeks. Zubimendi had been playing in a protective mask since breaking his nose against Everton last month, and he has admitted struggling physically at the end of last season, when Arsenal competed on four fronts into late May.

The sting is that Arsenal may not notice in the league. Declan Rice and Martin Odegaard have started all ten Premier League games, with Zubimendi a substitute in all of them. Europe is where rotation bites, and Arsenal play Borussia Dortmund three days after Newcastle.

Saka, and the front three without him

Bukayo Saka is still out. He injured his left quad around ten minutes into the 2-2 draw at Bayern Munich on 21 October and has now missed five matches. Arteta said on 6 November that Arsenal were talking in terms of weeks and would review after the international break. No return date has been given, and he will not feature at Newcastle.

With Saka out and Christos Tzolis still working back from the muscle injury he picked up on Greece duty in October, the right-hand side has gone to Noni Madueke, who has repaid it. He scored inside nine minutes in Prague and was central to the win over Hull. Sky's own coverage has taken to asking whether this is a different Noni.

The selection question for Saturday

The genuine choice is at centre forward. Viktor Gyokeres has scored in eight consecutive Nations League matches for Sweden and returns to London Colney with as much confidence as any striker in Europe. Kai Havertz has started seven of ten league games there and has two league goals.

Gyokeres's Arsenal numbers have not matched his international ones, which is the argument for Havertz. The counter is simply that a team scoring 1.5 goals a game has an in-form No. 9 sitting on the bench, and Newcastle's defence is the softest Arsenal will face for a month.

Hale End

Dowman Takes Another Record off Bellingham

A 16-year-old from Chelmsford spent the break rewriting the England Under-21 record book twice in four days.

Max Dowman was born on the last day of 2009. On Friday 13 November he came off the bench at Ashton Gate in a 1-0 defeat by Portugal and became the youngest player ever to represent the England Under-21 side, taking the record from Jude Bellingham.

On Monday, Lee Carsley gave him his first start, against France. England led 2-0 through Mikey Moore and Divin Mubama, conceded twice in five minutes after the 80th, and were heading for a draw when Harrison Armstrong found Dowman unmarked in the box in the 89th minute. He tapped in. At 16 years and 320 days he is now the youngest goalscorer in England Under-21 history, beating Bellingham's 17 years and 67 days, set against Kosovo in September 2020.

He already holds three Guinness World Records: youngest Champions League player, youngest Premier League goalscorer and youngest Premier League winner, the last at 16 years and 139 days when Arsenal took the title in May. He has two goals for Arsenal this season, both in the EFL Cup win at Ipswich Town.

Three Gunners in one England team

Dowman was not alone at Ashton Gate. Ethan Nwaneri set up Moore's opener with good work down the left, and Tommy Setford was in goal, beaten by a long-range strike from Giovani Versini for France's second.

The loan book

Ethan Nwaneri, Borussia Dortmund. A season-long loan agreed on deadline day, with no option or obligation to buy and Borussia Dortmund covering his wages in full. He wears No. 18 and has made seven appearances. The relevant detail for Arsenal supporters: under UEFA rules, loaned players are eligible to face their parent club, so Nwaneri can play at the Emirates on 24 November.

Tommy Setford, Stevenage. On loan in League One and now an England Under-21 goalkeeper, a meaningful step for a 20-year-old Arsenal signed from Ajax in 2024.

Ismeal Kabia, St Mirren. Season-long loan in the Scottish Premiership.

Khari Ranson, Billericay Town. The 19-year-old goalkeeper, an England youth international who has been at the club for close to a decade and has sat on the first-team bench, joined the National League South side on an initial 28-day loan confirmed on 10 November. He began with a clean sheet in a 7-0 Essex Senior Cup win over Little Oakley. His departure leaves Jack Porter as the Under-21s' first choice, with Lucas Nygaard as cover.

Elsewhere in the academy

Arsenal's Under-21s beat Aston Villa 5-2 on 8 November, the game in which Saliba logged his 45 minutes. Three academy players have already featured for the first team this season, all in the EFL Cup: Ife Ibrahim, Theo Julienne and Marli Salmon, with Salmon away with England's Under-18s this window. James Scanlon, signed from Manchester United in August, was on duty with Gibraltar.

Arsenal Women

Brilliant in Barcelona, Stuck at the Emirates

Renee Slegers' side took the first point anyone has taken off Barcelona in this season's Champions League, then drew at home in the league for the fifth time.

On Thursday night at the Spotify Camp Nou, Arsenal did something nobody else has managed this season. They took a point off Barcelona.

Frida Maanum put them ahead in the 22nd minute, following up after Cata Coll could only parry a Caitlin Foord effort. Barcelona had 65 per cent of the ball by the half-hour and still had not put a shot on target. Caroline Graham Hansen levelled just before the hour and the holders pressed hard without finding a winner. Irene Paredes and Patri Guijarro both went close.

It is the first time this season Barcelona have dropped a point in the Women's Champions League league phase, and it keeps Arsenal fourth with eight points from four matches, unbeaten, seven scored and three conceded. Head coach Renee Slegers called the performance resilient and a big boost. It was also the first meeting between the two since the 2025 final, which Arsenal won 1-0 for the club's first European title in eighteen years.

The league is the problem

Three days later the contrast was hard to miss. Arsenal drew 1-1 with London City Lionesses at the Emirates, with Mary Earps producing the sort of goalkeeping display that decides afternoons. Arsenal have now drawn all five of their home Women's Super League matches this season and won none.

That leaves them sixth on 14 points from ten games, eleven scored and eleven conceded. London City sit third, six points ahead, in the final Champions League qualification place. Slegers was blunt afterwards, saying they were happy with the one point but wanted and needed three.

The week was not all stalemate. Arsenal won 2-1 at Liverpool on 8 November, with Alessia Russo scoring twice to extend her run to three consecutive scoring league appearances. Arsenal's problem has not been the forwards so much as what happens when teams sit deep at the Emirates, something Slegers has acknowledged directly, talking about needing to be more varied and more consistent in the final third against a low block.

Next up is West Ham United at the Emirates on Sunday, which has become something close to a must-win if Arsenal are to climb back into the European places.

Gunners Abroad

Two Arsenal Defenders End Spain's 43-Game Run

Nineteen Arsenal players went away. Eight were involved in the same game in Madrid, and the ones in white won it.

Arsenal sent nineteen players on international duty in the final window of 2026, and the headline fixture delivered. In Madrid on Sunday, England beat Spain 3-2 at the Riyadh Air Metropolitano to end the world champions' 43-match unbeaten run.

Harry Kane's penalty on the stroke of half time put England ahead. Then came five minutes that belonged entirely to Arsenal. Ezri Konsa made it 2-0 in the 53rd minute from a Declan Rice assist. Ben White made it 3-0 in the 57th, set up by Jude Bellingham.

England then had Elliot Anderson sent off in the 63rd minute and spent the last half-hour with ten men. Mikel Oyarzabal converted a penalty, Nico Williams made it 3-2 with fifteen minutes left, and James Trafford produced a late double save to deny Fermin Lopez and Aymeric Laporte. Both sides finished on fifteen points, with England topping Group A3 on goal difference and advancing to the quarter-finals.

On the other side of it were three Arsenal players: David Raya, Mikel Merino and Zubimendi, the last of whom left the pitch injured. Konsa, for his part, had missed the Croatia game managing a knee knock picked up in Prague, then started and scored against the world champions. Alan Shearer has already named him the Premier League's player of the season so far.

Gyokeres makes history in Solna

The individual performance of the window belonged to Viktor Gyokeres. On Saturday at the Strawberry Arena in Solna he converted two first-half penalties as Sweden beat Bosnia and Herzegovina 3-2, sealing promotion back to Nations League A for the first time since 2020-21 under Graham Potter.

In doing so he became the first player in the competition's history to score in eight consecutive Nations League matches. The run dates back to October 2024 and has produced twelve goals. He now has sixteen Nations League goals in all, second only to Erling Haaland's twenty-three, and six in this campaign. For Sweden overall he has 27 goals in 42 caps. Daniel Svensson opened the scoring from an Anthony Elanga cross, which is a strange sentence to write four days before Elanga faces Arsenal.
