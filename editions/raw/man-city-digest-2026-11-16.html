The Lead

Eight days that will define Maresca's autumn

Fulham, Napoli and the leaders away, all inside eight days. Maresca has had a fortnight to fix a side that has stopped defending. He may have to do it without Guéhi.

The international break arrived at the right moment for Manchester City. It ends at an awkward one. Enzo Maresca's side resume on Saturday at home to Fulham, host Napoli in the Champions League on Tuesday, November 24, and then travel to Arsenal, six points clear at the top, on Sunday, November 29.

City went into the break third on 21 points after three straight league defeats: 1-2 at Aston Villa, 0-1 at home to Brighton and 1-3 at Nottingham Forest. A side that won its first seven league games under Maresca has not kept a clean sheet in eight matches since the 3-0 win at Anfield on October 11, and has conceded 17 goals in that run.

What the numbers say

The defeats were not lopsided on paper. An analysis for DAZN counted nearly five expected goals created by City across the three league losses. The difference was physical. Forest ran six kilometres more than City at the City Ground.

Maresca said as much that night. “When you don't match the intensity, when you lose all the duels, it's difficult,” he told reporters (Reuters via TimesLIVE). He also said the break would help “clarify what we can do better” (PA via the Irish News).

When you don't match the intensity, when you lose all the duels, it's difficult.

Enzo Maresca, after the 3-1 defeat at Forest

The midfield question

DAZN's diagnosis matches the one most analysts reached last week. With Rodri sold to Barcelona in August, Maresca has paired two creators, Enzo Fernández and Rayan Cherki, in front of Elliot Anderson. That leaves Anderson covering as a screen and a runner at once, and opponents have found the space either side of him.

One answer may already be in the squad. On Monday, Sky Sports' Transfer Talk panel argued that Ayyoub Bouaddi, the 19-year-old signed from Lille in the summer, should be playing more. He has started little, but he set up a goal in Morocco's 5-1 win over Niger on Friday.

A defence still in pieces

The back four is the bigger worry. Marc Guéhi went off at half-time at Forest with a hip problem and withdrew from the England squad; no return date has been given. Joško Gvardiol, who also went off at the break, is the good news: he played the full 90 minutes in both of Croatia's Nations League games.

Nico O'Reilly was left out by England to treat a back problem he has played through since September, and Antoine Semenyo (knee) is not expected back until December. Abdukodir Khusanov was not in Uzbekistan's squad either, which a local report put down to managing his recovery from an earlier problem.

Why these eight days matter

Fulham, 15th, should be the gentlest of the three. Napoli are 27th of 36 in the Champions League on three points, but a City win would keep City on course for the automatic top eight (NBC Sports). Arsenal is the one that will shape the title race.

The Arsenal game is confirmed for Sunday, November 29, at 4:30 p.m. UK, which is 8:30 a.m. Pacific, per NBC Sports. Lose to Fulham and Arsenal while the leaders win their other game, and the gap would be twelve points. Win both, and it can be no more than three. Either way, the Emirates will say whether this squad is a contender or a project.

Off the pitch

Appeal date emerges as the money men turn cautious

A December hearing would bring City's case to a head in the middle of the busiest month of the season.

The Financial Times reported on Friday that City's appeal against the commission's verdict will be heard in mid-December, over several days, with a judgment expected in the new year. Neither the league nor the club would comment. We have seen the report only as relayed by City Xtra on Yahoo.

That is earlier than the regulator's chair, David Kogan, suggested in October, when he expected a hearing in mid-to-late January (Al Jazeera). It fits the fast-track rules, which say the hearing should finish by December 24. Sanctions will be set at a separate hearing, and City can appeal those too (NBC Sports).

Two other reports added to the unease. Bloomberg ran a story on Friday headlined “Banks Pull Back From Manchester City Transfer Financing Over Sanctions Risk”; we could not read beyond the headline. And journalist Nick Harris reported that agents for several City players have begun legal groundwork on free-transfer exits should every appeal fail (FourFourTwo). That claim is single-sourced.

Erling Haaland, for his part, called the case a legal matter and said he is “incredibly happy” at the club, according to Football365 (via LiveScore).

Scouting the Next Opponent

Next up: Fulham (H), Saturday, November 21

Arbeloa's Fulham have stopped losing, but they still leave room behind

Unbeaten in four league games and comfortable on the ball, Fulham arrive with a plan City will recognise.

Fulham are 15th with 10 points from 10 games (W2 D4 L4), having scored 10 and conceded 12 (Sports Mole). Álvaro Arbeloa, appointed in July on a three-year deal, lost his first three league games and was under early pressure (Flashscore).

They have steadied since. Fulham beat Hull 1-0 at home through Alex Iwobi (Express & Star), won 1-0 at Coventry, drew 1-1 at Aston Villa thanks to a goalkeeping own goal, and drew 1-1 with Newcastle before the break, Rodrigo Muniz equalising (AfricaSoccer). In between, they went out of the League Cup to Crystal Palace.

System and the players to stop

Arbeloa has used a 4-2-3-1, with Sander Berge anchoring, Oscar Bobb, Josh King and Iwobi behind Gonzalo García, and Bernd Leno in goal. Fulham like the ball, which means they commit numbers forward and can be caught on the turn.

King is their top league scorer with two; six others have one each (ESPN). Iwobi takes the set pieces and drives the side forward, and Muniz has been the impact option from the bench. Before the Newcastle game Fulham reported no injury concerns, and Ryan Sessegnon was back from a three-match ban.

Where City can hurt them

This is a fixture City usually win. They have won 45 of 78 meetings, losing 17, and beat Fulham 3-0 at the Etihad on February 11 (Sports Mole). The lesson from Forest is the opposite one: City must win their duels first and their possession second.

Then Napoli

Napoli, under Massimiliano Allegri, are 10th in Serie A on 15 points after a 1-1 draw with leaders Lazio on November 8 (Reuters via The Star). They lost 4-1 at Porto in their last Champions League game (SoccerNews) and sit 27th of 36 on three points. Kevin De Bruyne returns to the Etihad with them.

Transfer News

Aït-Nouri's exit firms up as Gusto becomes the new name

Nothing is official six weeks before the window opens. But the outgoing story is hardening, and a right-back has joined the shortlist.

Rayan Aït-Nouri: Juventus lead the race

Gazzetta dello Sport reported on November 11 that Rayan Aït-Nouri is “certain” to leave City in January. Juventus want an initial loan with a permanent move possible in the summer, have made first enquiries, and are seen as ahead of Manchester United (via Sports Mole).

Sky Germany's Florian Plettenberg has said personal terms would not be a problem for United, but the relationship between the two Manchester clubs is (TEAMtalk). The left-back has not played a league minute this season. Certainty: concrete interest; no bid reported.

Malo Gusto: a new right-back link

talkSPORT reported that City and United are set to compete for Chelsea's Malo Gusto, a story picked up by the BBC's gossip column on November 12 (via The False 9). On Monday, Football Insider said Chelsea's £75m valuation could price City out (via The Hard Tackle).

The fit is obvious. Matheus Nunes and Khusanov have been sharing right-back, and Gusto is the only fresh defender link we found this week. Certainty: rumour; we have not seen the talkSPORT original.

Gvardiol: Barcelona told no

Spanish daily AS reported on Monday that Barcelona asked about Joško Gvardiol during the Rodri negotiations in the summer and were turned down, and that he is “unattainable” in January (via Sport Witness). His contract runs to 2031. Certainty: single-sourced; no live move.

Nunes close to a new deal

Matheus Nunes is reported to be close to a contract extension worth about £150,000 a week, up from about £130,000. The Manchester Evening News is cited as the origin (via Man City News); reports differ on the length. Nothing has been announced.

Grealish says sorry; Monga's loan in doubt

Jack Grealish posted a public apology to Everton fans on November 12 after his suspension for a breach of club discipline (The Independent via Yahoo). TEAMtalk, citing unnamed sources, says Everton have no plans to end the loan and that he is aiming to return at Brentford on November 23 (TEAMtalk).

Swansea are negotiating to end Jeremy Monga's loan in January, according to a local fan-site exclusive, with the £1m loan fee halved (Swansea Independent). He has not played for them. Certainty: talks reported by one local source.

Tresoldi becomes a summer story

Belgium's Het Laatste Nieuws reports that Liverpool have joined City and Chelsea in watching Nicolò Tresoldi, but that Club Brugge will not sell in January and want more than €50m (via Sport Witness). Certainty: concrete interest (scouting); now a 2027 thread.

The Squad

A fortnight to reset, and a squad that scattered

Most of the first team spent the break abroad. Maresca's selection calls start on Saturday, with the back four still undecided.

City have used a 4-2-3-1 all season. Rúben Dias wore the armband at Forest and Haaland at Leipzig. The open question is the centre of defence: if Guéhi misses Fulham, Dias and Gvardiol is the likeliest pairing, with Gvardiol now back to full fitness after 180 minutes for Croatia.

At full-back, Nunes and O'Reilly or Khusanov and Gvardiol have been Maresca's two combinations. With O'Reilly's back and Khusanov's fitness both being managed, that rotation is thin.

Foden left out, Cherki in a row

Phil Foden was not picked by England this time. Maresca said before the break: “We know that he can give much more” (via Yahoo). Foden scored a hat-trick against AEK Athens in October but started on the bench at Forest.

Rayan Cherki had a strong week on the pitch for France, then reportedly had a heated exchange with Bradley Barcola after the 3-1 defeat in Italy and left the dressing room furious. That comes from RMC Sport, relayed by City Xtra; it has no bearing on his City status.

Donnarumma under scrutiny

Gianluigi Donnarumma has been criticised for errors against PSG, Aston Villa and Leipzig, and conceded four for Italy in Belgium on Sunday. Joleon Lescott questioned both the structure and the goalkeeping this week (via Yahoo). Gerónimo Rulli, who won the League Cup shootout against Brighton, made two key saves for Argentina on Friday.

City Women

Four in Monza, then a first defeat at Stamford Bridge

City Women found their Champions League form in Italy, then lost top spot to Chelsea after Yamashita's red card.

Manchester City Women came from behind to win 4-1 at Inter in Monza on Tuesday, their first Champions League win after three draws. Elisa Bartoli headed Inter in front on 24 minutes before Aoba Fujino scored twice before half-time, Kerstin Casparij added a third and Grace Clinton, from Lauren Hemp's cross, scored her first Champions League goal (Sky Sports).

Head coach Andrée Jeglertz rested Khadija Shaw and started Hemp on the bench with Chelsea in mind. The win lifted City to seventh in the league phase, one point outside the top four (Sky Sports).

Chelsea 1-0 City

Saturday's top-of-the-table meeting turned on two moments. Chelsea's Sandy Baltimore scored in the fourth minute of first-half stoppage time, and on 75 minutes goalkeeper Ayaka Yamashita raced out of her area and brought down Alyssa Thompson. She was sent off, and Eartha Cumings came on in goal (Sky Sports).

City had chances. Hemp missed from a yard out on 64 minutes after Shaw chipped over Hannah Hampton, and Kadeisha Buchanan went off after 12 minutes having taken a knock in training. It was City's first defeat of the season, and Chelsea went top (Reuters via The Star).

I think we deserve more than going away from here with no points.

Andrée Jeglertz, after the defeat at Chelsea

Where it leaves them

Chelsea lead on 26 points from 10 games, City are second on 25. Shaw, held scoreless at Stamford Bridge, remains on 12 WSL goals. Alex Greenwood and Anna Moorhouse are still out with injuries picked up in training before the West Ham game.

A professional-foul red card normally brings a one-match WSL ban, which would rule Yamashita out of Sunday's home game with Tottenham; that has not been confirmed. First comes a trip to BK Häcken in the Champions League on Wednesday (5:45 p.m. UK, 9:45 a.m. PT).

Players Away

England's famous night, and a red card for Anderson

City's internationals produced goals, assists and one costly lunge. Nobody came home injured.

Elliot Anderson started both of England's Nations League games. He played 90 minutes in a 2-1 win over Croatia at Wembley, then 63 in Madrid on Sunday before a straight red card for a last-man foul on Lamine Yamal, with England 3-0 up. Spain scored the penalty, but England held on 3-2 to end Spain's 43-game unbeaten run and top their group (Sky Sports).

Thomas Tuchel questioned the “double punishment” of a red card and a penalty (GOAL). Anderson's ban applies to England's next competitive game, not to City.

Goals for Haaland and Doku

Erling Haaland set up one goal and scored a late penalty in Norway's 3-1 win over Wales, his 66th goal in 60 caps (AP via Fox Sports). Jérémy Doku assisted Kevin De Bruyne in Belgium's 2-1 win in Türkiye and scored in a 4-1 win over Italy (ESPN).

Rayan Cherki played in France's 3-1 defeat in Italy, then set up the 80th-minute winner against Türkiye (ESPN). Gianluigi Donnarumma captained Italy in both games and was beaten four times in Belgium. Joško Gvardiol and Mateo Kovačić started both of Croatia's games.

Further afield

Rúben Dias played 90 minutes in Portugal's 4-1 home defeat by Denmark. Enzo Fernández and Gerónimo Rulli both played the full game as Argentina lost 2-1 to Uruguay (La Nación). Ayyoub Bouaddi assisted Achraf Hakimi in Morocco's 5-1 win over Niger, and Iliman Ndiaye was with Senegal for their qualifier against Sudan. Vitor Reis and Allan were unused as Brazil lost 3-2 to Japan.
