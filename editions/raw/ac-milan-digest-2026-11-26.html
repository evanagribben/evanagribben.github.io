The Lead

Final call in Piraeus: can Amorim's Serie A machine finally travel?

Fourth in Serie A, 28th in Europe. Ruben Amorim's Milan have been two different teams this autumn, and Thursday in Piraeus is where the European version runs out of road.

The numbers tell the story of a split personality. In Serie A, Milan score 2.33 goals a game and convert 26.4 per cent of their shots. In the Europa League the figures fall to 1.00 goal a game and 10 per cent, according to a Gazzetta dello Sport breakdown. One competition has them in the title conversation. The other has them 28th of 36.

Four league-phase games have produced one win, three defeats and three points. The win was a 4-0 rout of Red Bull Salzburg on October 15, when Alphadjo Cisse scored a hat-trick. The defeats, to Benfica (0-2), Bournemouth (0-3) and Ferencvaros (0-2), came without a single Milan goal. Thursday's opponent, Olympiacos, sit sixth on 10 points.

The format is unforgiving. The top eight go straight to the last 16, and places nine to 24 enter a play-off round. Milan sit four places below that line with four games left: Olympiacos away, Sunderland at home on December 10, Levski Sofia away in January and Ararat-Armenia at San Siro on January 28. Corriere dello Sport's front page on Thursday called it a crossroads.

Why the two Milans exist

Rotation is the obvious suspect. Gazzetta notes that Mario Gila has never started at right-back in Europe, that Luka Modric had not started a Europa League game and that Adrien Rabiot started only two of the four. Ruben Amorim rejects the theory outright. “I can't believe we lost all these games in Europe because we rotated,” he said on Wednesday in a press conference relayed by MilanNews.

“I can't believe we lost all these games in Europe because we rotated.”

Ruben Amorim, Wednesday, on the eve of Olympiacos

His argument, as reported by Gazzetta via ACMilanInside, is that “we are 25, not 14”. The evidence cuts both ways. In Europe, Milan concede 1.92 expected goals a game against 1.28 in Serie A, and they need around ten shots per goal rather than four. Those are not only finishing problems; they point to a team that has looked less settled and easier to play through.

A thin squad for a must-win night

The timing is awkward. Milan travel with nine players unavailable: Davide Bartesaghi, Samuel Chukwueze, Matteo Gabbia, Ruben Loftus-Cheek, Alexis Saelemaekers, Strahinja Pavlovic, Luka Modric, Francesco Camarda and goalkeeper Pietro Terracciano, according to MilanNews and Pianeta Milan. Modric's absence is the big one, a strain of the right rectus femoris reported by Sky.

The good news is Mike Maignan, back after a European suspension, and Fikayo Tomori, who has returned to full training. Ramos is expected to shake off a minor calf complaint, per The Cult of Calcio. Ardon Jashari is the natural replacement for Modric as the deep playmaker, and the Swiss knows the opponent's engine room well: Remo Freuler is his international team-mate. “I can't wait to meet him and beat him,” Jashari said on Wednesday.

The matchup that decides it

Olympiacos under Imanol Alguacil play a 4-2-3-1 and press aggressively at home. They have won their last four home Europa League games and are unbeaten in nine, per the club's official preview. They also come in hot: a 6-2 comeback win over Iraklis on Sunday kept them second in Greece, behind Panathinaikos.

That suggests the template that broke Frosinone may suit Milan again. Against Frosinone's high line Milan sat in a mid-block, won the ball and ran, and all five goals came from transitions or quick combinations. Diego Moreira's carrying and Christian Pulisic's late runs are the weapons; the risk is that Olympiacos have the patience in midfield, through Freuler and Gustavo Sa, that Frosinone lacked.

Key battles: Jashari against Freuler for control of the centre; Moreira's pace against the Olympiacos left side; and Milan's centre-backs, likely Koni De Winter and Sankhoun Diawara, against Armando Gonzalez, the hosts' top European scorer with two goals. Before their 2018 defeat in Piraeus, Milan had never won a competitive game in Greece, according to UEFA.

What a result would mean

A win lifts Milan to six points and back into contention for the play-off places with two home games to come. A draw keeps the door ajar but leaves little margin. A defeat would make the play-off round, never mind the top eight, a long shot, and would sharpen the questions around how Amorim balances a congested calendar against a Serie A title push that the owner says he still expects.

Off the Pitch

Krösche close, Cardinale all-in

Milan's new head of football is reported to have agreed every key point. The owner, meanwhile, has promised to spend in January, at a cost the accounts will feel.

Markus Krösche is set to become Milan's head of football. On Thursday Bild reported that he has agreed all the key points of his contract and that a signature and official announcement are imminent, as relayed by Milan Reports. As of Thursday morning Pacific time, the club had made no announcement.

The compensation paid to Eintracht Frankfurt is reported at between four and five million euros (Bild), “in the region of €5m” (Football Italia, citing Sky Germany) or around €4m (Corriere dello Sport, via SempreMilan). Matteo Moretto reported a done deal on November 5, with Krösche expected to start work on January 1, 2027, according to Goal.

He will not be an all-powerful sporting director. Gazzetta reports that Gerry Cardinale keeps the final say after consulting an internal working group, with Krösche running transfer operations alongside Bobby Gardiner, head of football intelligence, and Tiago Estevão, head of recruitment (via SempreMilan). Bild says his January priorities with Amorim are a centre-back, a left-back and a striker.

The bill for ambition

Cardinale has been unusually public. At Milanello on November 21 he said, “We will spend money, but we will spend money smart and with a view to being consistent with the project” (SempreMilan, via Daniele Longo). After the Frosinone win he told TeleLombardia he still expects Milan “to compete for the Scudetto”.

Corriere dello Sport's analysis on Thursday puts numbers on that stance. Milan spent €160m on incoming players in the summer, and the paper says another €70m could follow in January. Renewals for Christian Pulisic (€3.5m to €5m net a season) and Pavlovic (€1.7m to €3m) have pushed squad costs up by about €40m, per Corriere dello Sport.

The same piece projects a 2026-27 loss of €140m to €150m against a €50m capital injection from RedBird, and warns that Champions League qualification is not guaranteed. These are the paper's estimates rather than club figures, but they frame the January window: big signings are possible, but every euro now raises the stakes on finishing in the top four.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Olympiacos: hot at home, short in attack

Imanol Alguacil's side have lost once in their last 18 games. They are also missing their best striker.

Olympiacos arrive in form. They have lost just once in 18 outings, with a 12-game unbeaten run under Imanol Alguacil (W10 D2), per The Cult of Calcio on Yahoo Sports. In Europe they have beaten Jagiellonia Bialystok, Marseille and Sparta Prague and drew 0-0 at Rennes. At home in Greece they are second, behind Panathinaikos.

Alguacil, the former Real Sociedad coach, sets up in a 4-2-3-1. Freuler and Gustavo Sa anchor the midfield, Kostas Fortounis floats behind the striker, and Jota Silva and Gelson Martins (or Leon Bailey, if fit) attack from wide. Lorenzo Pirola, formerly of Inter's academy, partners Panagiotis Retsos at centre-back in Pianeta Milan's predicted XI.

The players to stop

Armando Gonzalez is the leading European scorer with two goals. Jota Silva is the volume shooter: 17 shots in the league phase, only two on target, according to Cult of Calcio. Bailey picked up a knock against Iraklis and is a doubt. The hosts are without Ayoub El Kaabi, Yusuf Yazici, Gustavo Puerta, Santiago Hezze and Chiquinho.

The weakness is the one Milan exploited on Sunday. Olympiacos commit numbers when they press, and losing El Kaabi has left the attack less ruthless. A Milan team that defends compactly and breaks quickly through Moreira and Pulisic has a route through; a Milan team that concedes the kind of chances it has in Europe so far (1.92 expected goals a game) does not.

Transfer News

Endrick, a left-back and the January shopping list

Cardinale has made Real Madrid's Brazilian his public priority. Behind it sits a quieter, more urgent search on the left flank.

Endrick: concrete interest, a disputed bid

Milan's pursuit of Endrick is the loudest story of the week. Cardinale called the Real Madrid forward “one of the best players, if not the best player, in his position” on November 21. Fabrizio Romano said on Thursday that Milan are “very attentive” and that Cardinale is “directly involved” (SempreMilan).

Spanish journalist Anton Meana claimed on SER that Milan have made a formal offer of close to €70m to sign him in January (SempreMilan). That claim is single-sourced and unconfirmed by either club. Meana also said Jose Mourinho would prefer to keep the player, and Romano says Roma are interested too. Status: concrete interest; a bid is reported but not confirmed.

The left-back hunt

The more pressing need is at left-back. Calciomercato.com reports that Pervis Estupinan's exit is “very likely”, with interest from Spain (via SempreMilan). Corriere dello Sport adds that Amorim sees Davide Bartesaghi more as a winger. Bartesaghi's tendinitis now makes the gap more obvious.

Barcelona's Alejandro Balde is the biggest name. Tuttomercatoweb says Barcelona want €50m and that Juventus and Premier League clubs are circling (SempreMilan). Corriere dello Sport, via Pianeta Milan, says agent Jorge Mendes has discussed a January loan with an option that could become an obligation, valuing him at about €40m. Status: monitoring and early talks.

Manchester United's Noussair Mazraoui is also on Calciomercato.com's list. Bologna's Juan Miranda is not a live option: Matteo Moretto said Miranda once had a basic agreement with Milan before plans changed, and that he is not aware of Milan pursuing him now (Radio Rossonera).

Centre-back and midfield

Romano says the “doors are open” for Gonçalo Inácio to leave Sporting CP in January, with Milan in touch with his camp; Record puts Sporting's price north of €40m (SempreMilan). Amorim, his former coach, cooled things publicly by pointing to Pavlovic and Diawara as his options, per Gazzetta and Corriere dello Sport. Status: contacts, no bid.

Bruno Fernandes is a summer idea, not a January one. Tuttosport says he would welcome a return to Italy, with Milan and Juventus interested, but Manchester United hold an option to extend his deal by a year (SempreMilan). He shares an agency with Amorim. Status: early interest.

Contracts: Rabiot progressing, Tomori stalled

MilanNews reports that talks over Adrien Rabiot's renewal are progressing positively, with Milan willing to adjust his salary (SempreMilan). Cardinale's view is clear: “If it were up to me, he would be a franchise player.”

Fikayo Tomori is the opposite case. His contract expires in June 2027, and Moretto says renewal talks are “absolutely at a standstill”, leaving a sale for a fee or a free exit as the likely outcomes (SempreMilan). Modric's own future beyond this season remains open, per Gazzetta.

The Squad

Rabiot runs the show while the treatment room fills up

Five wins in six league games, a counter-attacking template that works, and a medical list that keeps growing.

Sunday's win showed what Milan look like when the plan clicks. Listed as a 4-4-1-1, they built from the back with three, Maignan stepping in as an extra centre-back, and Rabiot dropping between the lines. Out of possession they sat deep, invited a Frosinone high line that Gazzetta measured at 57 metres, and broke (SempreMilan). The result moved them back into the top four, five points behind Lazio.

Adrien Rabiot is the engine. He is the first Milan midfielder since Kaka in 2004 to score or assist in seven consecutive Serie A appearances, per the club. Amorim called him “one of the best midfielders in Europe right now” (SempreMilan). Rabiot, for his part, says Milan are “growing”.

Cisse, Ramos and the forward picture

Alphadjo Cisse has six Serie A goals in ten appearances and only turned 20 last month. When Ramos needed replacing at 51 minutes, Amorim chose Cisse over Camarda because of his one-on-one qualities. Goncalo Ramos, the €74m summer signing, scored his fourth of the season before coming off as a precaution: “We can't lose any more players,” Amorim said.

Christian Pulisic has scored in three straight league games, against Inter, Genoa and Frosinone, and was named the club's MVP for the game in its official awards (acmilan.com, November 23). Diego Moreira, signed from Strasbourg in August, is described by Corriere dello Sport as “irreplaceable” after another decisive display (SempreMilan).

The injury list

The list grew to nine this week. Modric's thigh strain is the headline: Sky says Cagliari on Monday is in doubt and Parma on December 6 is the target (SempreMilan). Pavlovic, who came on against Frosinone after a month out, was due to undergo further tests on a kidney problem on Wednesday (Gazzetta via SempreMilan).

The Academy

Primavera and Futuro both on winning runs

Four straight wins apiece for the under-20s and the Serie D side, a first pro deal for a 2009-born talent, and a decision looming on Camarda.

The Milan Primavera won 1-0 at Lazio on Sunday, Mattia Angelicchio heading in Luca Nolli's cross after eight minutes. It was a fourth straight win for Giovanni Renna's side, which moved into fourth place and the play-off spots (SempreMilan). Lecce visit on Saturday.

Milan Futuro beat Tritium 3-0 in Serie D, Levin Asanji scoring twice and Lorenzo Ossola adding a third (SempreMilan). They are fourth on 26 points from 12 games, three behind leaders Nibbiano & Valtidone, who have played one more (club standings). Real Calepina visit on Sunday.

On Wednesday the club announced a first professional contract for Ryan Eugen Rusu, born in 2009 (club statement).

Camarda's next step

Francesco Camarda, 18, has played 119 minutes this season. Di Marzio reported that Cagliari want him on loan and could include an option for Milan on Paul Mendy (SempreMilan). Moretto says the real exit door is abroad, with Cagliari having only asked about terms (SempreMilan). Italy U21 coach Silvio Baldini calls him “a conscientious boy who shouldn't become obsessed with the goal”.

Loan report

Youssouf Fofana has made ten LaLiga appearances for Sevilla, nine as a starter; Calciomercato.com says a Milan renewal looks unlikely (SempreMilan). Santiago Gimenez has made four league appearances for Porto with one goal, a penalty, and Porto are said to be highly unlikely to trigger their €18m option (SempreMilan).

AC Milan Women

Grimshaw's double lifts the Rossonere

A 3-2 win over Sassuolo ended a three-game run without a victory. The table still shows how much ground is left.

AC Milan Women beat Sassuolo 3-2 on Saturday, November 21. Christy Grimshaw scored after nine and 15 minutes, and Soffia made it 3-1 on 24 minutes. Clelland and Caiazzo scored for Sassuolo in a breathless first half, and Suzanne Bakker's side held on (club report).

It followed a 3-3 draw at Napoli on November 14, when Kanutte scored twice and Koivisto added a third, only for Napoli to equalise late (club report). Milan are sixth with 10 points from eight games (three wins, a draw and four defeats), 14 behind unbeaten leaders Roma, per Pianeta Milan's table.

Grimshaw is the team's leading scorer with five league goals. There is no league game in the next seven days. Fiorentina, third on 16 points, visit on the weekend of December 12 and 13, according to the published fixture list; the exact day and time have not been confirmed.
