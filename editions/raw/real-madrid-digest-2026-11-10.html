The Lead

Eight in a row, and Mourinho's Madrid have found a shape

Two away wins in four days sent Real Madrid into the international break as the form team in Spain. The questions now are about who plays, not whether it works.

Nobody at Valdebebas will say it out loud, but the week ended with José Mourinho's team looking like a finished product. A 3-2 win in Athens on Wednesday was chaotic and late. A 3-0 win at Mestalla on Saturday was controlled from the first whistle. Together they made it eight straight wins in all competitions.

 

The table explains why the run matters. Madrid sit second in La Liga on 30 points, three behind Barcelona and two clear of Atlético Madrid, per the standings after matchday 12. In the Champions League league phase they have 12 points from 12, second only to PSG on goal difference. The only league defeats came at Betis in September and at Atlético on September 20.

Athens: the game Madrid should have lost

For 20 minutes at AEK, Madrid were ragged. AEK Athens led through Koita after 12 minutes, and Sports Illustrated's verdict was that without Thibaut Courtois Madrid could have been three down early. Jude Bellingham back-heeled an equaliser from Marc Cucurella's cross on 32 minutes, after an earlier effort was ruled out for offside.

AEK went ahead again on 75 minutes when Pilios's shot squirmed through Courtois. Mbappé levelled three minutes later from Denzel Dumfries's cross, his sixth straight Champions League appearance with a goal. Then, in stoppage time, substitute Federico Valverde hit a 25-yard winner. Madrid finished with 70 per cent of the ball and an expected-goals tally of 3.35 to 0.86, per ESPN's match data.

“At 2-2, we did not accept the draw. We decided to take a risk to win.”

José Mourinho, after AEK Athens 2-3 Real Madrid

Mourinho's line afterwards, reported by AfricaSoccer, was that these are matches "only Real Madrid can win in the final minutes." It was also a warning. Antonio Rüdiger struggled with Luka Jović, and Dumfries was exposed on the right. Three days later, Mourinho changed the back line.

Mestalla: the version Mourinho wants

At Valencia, Valverde started at right-back with the captain's armband, Ibrahima Konaté and Dean Huijsen paired in the middle, and Arda Güler returned to the attacking line. Valverde opened the scoring from about 22 yards just before the half hour, Güler finished first-time on 36 minutes, and Mbappé ran onto Güler's ball over the top to round the goalkeeper on 75, per beIN Sports.

The numbers were lopsided: 68 per cent possession, 19 shots to four and an expected-goals count of 2.20 to 0.16, according to Sports Illustrated's match data. Valencia finished with ten men after José Luis Gayà's late red card. "This team smells good," Mourinho told reporters, before adding that his players were in "extreme fatigue" and that he hoped nobody came back injured from the break.

The shape that is emerging

Three selection calls now look settled. Valverde is the right-back, ahead of both Trent Alexander-Arnold and Dumfries. Güler and Bernardo Silva are the creators in midfield, with Valverde and Aurélien Tchouaméni no longer paired as the holding duo. And Yan Diomande, signed in the summer for a reported 140 million euros, has taken the left wing.

That last call is the one with consequences. Vinícius Júnior has one goal all season and no goal involvement in seven games, per Sports Illustrated. He was suspended for the Valencia win, the last of a four-match domestic ban for pulling a Villarreal player's hair in October. He is available against Celta Vigo on November 21, and Mourinho will have to choose.

Mourinho has managed the issue in public with care. He described his handling of Vinícius as "80 per cent soft hand, 20 per cent firm" and insisted the Brazilian "didn't provoke anything" when Diomande moved to the left. Valverde and Éder Militão have both backed their teammate. But Diomande has four goals to Vinícius's one, and the numbers are hard to argue with.

What the break means

The international window takes 18 first-team players away, per RPP and EFE, leaving Mourinho with a handful of fit seniors and academy players at Valdebebas. Madrid then play three home games in eight days: Celta on November 21, PSV Eindhoven in the Champions League on November 24, and Alavés on November 28. Barcelona and Atlético meet the same weekend Madrid face Celta, so the title picture could tighten quickly.

The Boardroom

Nadal joins the board as Pérez prepares a record budget

The tennis great's appointment and a reported 1.4 billion euro budget both go to a members' vote on November 21.

The board approved Rafael Nadal as a director at its meeting on November 3, and Real Madrid announced it the next day, according to Reuters, via Al Jazeera. The club cited his 92 singles titles and two Olympic golds, and said his values "form part of Real Madrid's identity." Nadal said he had accepted "with great enthusiasm, keen to learn and to contribute as much as I can."

Reuters noted that Nadal has been talked about as a possible successor to Florentino Pérez, a suggestion he has played down. Pérez, 79, won a new four-year term in June in the club's first contested election in two decades, beating Enrique Riquelme by roughly 65 per cent to 35 per cent of votes cast, per Wikipedia's election record.

The November 21 assembly

The club has called its ordinary and extraordinary general assemblies of member delegates for Saturday, November 21, the same day Madrid host Celta, per the official notice. The agenda covers the 2025/26 accounts, the 2026/27 budget, next season's membership fees and Nadal's ratification. Nothing on the agenda concerns statute changes or outside investment.

Marca's reported figures for the budget, relayed by Bernabéu Digital, are records across the board: revenue of about 1.405 billion euros, a sporting budget of 707 million euros covering wages and amortisation, and 154.8 million euros expected from player sales. The club's audited 2025/26 revenue was 1.221 billion euros excluding transfers, with a 26.3 million euro profit.

On Tuesday the club added Hotels.com as an official sponsor, with Emilio Butragueño signing at Valdebebas, per realmadrid.com. The club did not give a length; trade outlet 2Playbook reports the deal runs to 2029.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Celta Vigo: hard to beat, harder to watch score

Claudio Giráldez's side arrive at the Bernabéu on November 21 with seven draws and only 10 league goals.

Celta Vigo are 11th with 13 points from 12 matches: two wins, seven draws and three defeats, with a goal difference of zero, per FotMob. They have scored only 10 league goals. Their recent form is poor: a 2-1 Europa League win at Celtic, then a 0-0 with Betis, a 3-0 loss at Rayo, a 2-1 home defeat to Union Saint-Gilloise and a 0-0 with Levante on November 8.

Claudio Giráldez usually sets up with a back three and wing-backs, building patiently from the goalkeeper and pressing hard after losing the ball, according to Comuniate's season guide. He rotates heavily, which matters: Celta play a Copa del Rey tie at Salamanca on November 13 and carry Europa League commitments.

Who to stop

Miguel Román is their best performer by FotMob's ratings and takes their set pieces. Ferran Jutglà and Pablo Durán have two league goals each. Goalkeeper Ionuț Radu and veteran Marcos Alonso give the defence experience. One preview site lists Román among several absentees, but that is unconfirmed, so watch the club's own injury updates closer to the game.

The vulnerability is obvious: Celta do not score enough to punish a team that controls the ball. The last meeting, at Balaídos in March, was tight: Tchouaméni scored early, Borja Iglesias equalised, and Valverde won it in stoppage time, per SoccerNews.

Transfer News

JJ Gabriel: verbal agreement, but Manchester United are not letting go quietly

The 16-year-old forward wants Madrid. The paperwork, the age rules and a possible legal fight stand in the way.

Real Madrid have a verbal agreement with JJ Gabriel, the 16-year-old Manchester United academy forward who asked to leave Old Trafford, according to Sky Sports and ESPN's sources. Fabrizio Romano described the deal on November 5 as "99 per cent" and then "99.9 per cent" done, while stressing that nothing would be signed this week, per Football365.

The obstacle is Manchester United. The Manchester Evening News's Tyrone Marshall reported that United are weighing legal action, saying neither Madrid nor the player's camp has contacted them, and that FIFA training compensation would be far below his value, per Madrid Universal. Romano expects the clubs to look for a compromise. Any move also needs FIFA approval for an international transfer of a minor.

Matteo Moretto reported on November 4 that Barcelona are "no longer in the race," per Urgente24. Diario AS, relayed on November 9, frames the question as when rather than whether, projecting a January 2027 arrival with Real Madrid Castilla as the likely first stop, per Yahoo Sports. Certainty: verbal agreement and personal terms reported; nothing signed.

The buyback clauses: Nico Paz and Jacobo Ramón

Madrid hold a buyback option on Nico Paz at Como that, per Romano, can only be triggered in summer 2027, with "no chance" of a January move, per Sports Mole. Como sporting director Carlalberto Ludi said on November 9 that Paz is "100 per cent a Como player" until then, per SoccerNews. Certainty: contract option, no active talks.

Moretto also reported, on DAZN on October 29, that Madrid intend to use a buyback of under 9 million euros on centre-back Jacobo Ramón, also at Como, for a 2027 return, per Defensa Central. That is single-sourced. Certainty: concrete interest via a contract option.

January: no shopping planned

Diario AS reported on November 3 that the club plans no January signings and would only consider exits if a player asks to go, per Yahoo Sports. That sits awkwardly with The Independent's Miguel Delaney, who reported on November 10 that Madrid, Atlético and Chelsea are interested in Arsenal's Martín Zubimendi, with Arsenal wanting at least the 60 million pounds they paid, per Yahoo Sports. Certainty: rumour; no bid reported.

Endrick is staying. Mourinho said in October that "anyone interested in him will have to fight me hard" and that a loan was "impossible," per Madrid Universal. A low-reliability Spanish site claims he could now go, per Sports Illustrated; treat that as a rumour only. Meanwhile the club is said to be pleased with Franco Mastantuono's loan at Fiorentina, per SportsCafe citing Romano.

The Squad

Mestalla tunnel row, and who is left at Valdebebas

A half-time confrontation, a stripped-back training group and the recovery timelines for Rodrygo and Asencio.

Saturday's win had a sour half-time. Witnesses quoted by AS and ElDesmarque placed Antonio Rüdiger, Éder Militão and Jude Bellingham at the centre of a confrontation in the Mestalla tunnel, with Rüdiger reportedly clashing with a Valencia security guard, per Defensa Central. A claim that Bellingham struck a guard is uncorroborated.

The referee's report named only Valencia sporting director Braulio Vázquez, who confronted an assistant referee, per Tribuna. No disciplinary case has been reported against any Real Madrid player.

Availability

Rodrygo (serious knee injury) and Raúl Asencio (stress fracture of the right tibia) continued their individual recovery on Tuesday, per the club's training report. Sports Mole's November 5 update put Rodrygo's target as early 2027 and Asencio at about three months, with Ferland Mendy still out with a long-term thigh problem, per Sports Mole.

Thibaut Courtois is staying at Valdebebas: he stepped away from Belgium's Nations League campaign in September, per ESPN. Andriy Lunin and Álvaro Carreras were not called up, and Rüdiger stepped away from Germany after the World Cup, per RPP and EFE. Vinícius's ban is served, so he is available for Celta.

Form and numbers

Mbappé has 17 goals in all competitions, ahead of Bellingham on five and Diomande and Güler on four each, per the season record. Güler leads the squad with 42 chances created, per FotMob. Mbappé and Bellingham were both named in the 2026 FIFPRO World XI, per the LiveScore daily roundup.

La Fábrica

Juvenil A keep winning; Castilla rescue a point in a wild finish

The Youth League holders are four from four, and Mastantuono is turning heads in Florence.

Real Madrid's Juvenil A won 4-1 at AEK Athens in the UEFA Youth League last week, with goals from Lacosta, Enzo Alves, Daniel Yáñez and Bryan Bugarín, per the club's report. Álvaro López's defending champions have four wins from four. On Sunday they won 2-1 at Rayo Vallecano and lead their División de Honor group by six points, per the club's Juvenil A page.

Castilla

Julián López de Lerma's Castilla drew 2-2 with Hércules at the Alfredo Di Stéfano on Sunday, per the club's match report. Hércules led through Antoñín, Diego Aguado equalised on 59 minutes, Soldevilla's 86th-minute free kick restored the visitors' lead, and Jorge Cestero levelled on 90. Castilla are ninth in Primera Federación Group 2 with 16 points from 11 games.

Two academy graduates are with Spain's under-21s this week: Thiago Pitarch, who came through the academy and made his first-team debut in February, and Carlos Espí, signed from Levante in the summer, per Sports Illustrated.

On loan

Franco Mastantuono started Fiorentina's 2-2 draw with Juventus on Sunday and set up a goal that was ruled out late, per VAVEL. He scored in a 4-2 win at Sassuolo on November 2, and SportsCafe, citing Romano, reports the club is pleased with his four goals in Serie A so far, per SportsCafe.

 

Real Madrid Femenino

A late equaliser at Lezama, and a must-win night against Paris FC

Pau Quesada's side are second in Liga F but still searching for a first Champions League win.

Real Madrid Femenino let a win slip at Athletic Club on Saturday. Eva Navarro put them ahead on 85 minutes, but Ane Campos equalised in stoppage time for a 1-1 draw, per LaLiga. A week earlier they won 3-1 at Sevilla through Felicia Schröder, Caruso and Lineth Beerensteyn, per the club's report.

The draw leaves Madrid second in Liga F on 23 points after ten games, four behind Barcelona and four ahead of Atlético Madrid, per LaLiga's table. One concern, reported by a single fan outlet, is a run of ten games without a clean sheet, the worst in the club's history.

Champions League pressure

Madrid host Paris FC at the Alfredo Di Stéfano on Tuesday night (9:45 AM Pacific) in the Women's Champions League, per the club schedule. Three draws so far, against PSG, Manchester City and Bayern, leave them ninth with three points and four behind fourth, per Madrid Actual. Quesada said the aim is a top-four finish, per EFE via Infobae.

Linda Caicedo went off injured at Lezama but has trained since, according to a single fan-site report. Schröder is on the shortlist for the Ballon d'Or young player award. Next up in Liga F is FC Badalona at home on Saturday, then Inter in Europe on November 18.
