At a Glance

Festival season just ended and the city is better for it. Outside Lands packed up ten days ago and Stern Grove closed with Al Green yesterday afternoon. What is left is the good part: opera you can hear for free on a lawn, twelve pianos hidden in a botanical garden, and a Skinny Puppy side project that has played live maybe four times since 1985. These are the five weeks when San Francisco stops performing for visitors and goes back to entertaining itself.

Sport carries the load. The Valkyries play twice this week, vanish for a month, then return for a back-to-back that decides their seeding. The Giants have fourteen home dates across four series. USF quietly hosts seven soccer matches on Fulton Street that almost nobody outside the campus attends. And in the Presidio, a three-restaurant food hall built around one shared kitchen opened on Saturday with a Momofuku Ko alumnus consulting, which means the next fortnight of Bay Area food talk is already spoken for.

Pick of the Month

Opera in the Park

Sunday, September 13 · Robin Williams Meadow, Golden Gate Park · Free, no ticket, no RSVP

The day after San Francisco Opera opens its 104th season inside the War Memorial, the whole company walks across town and performs in a meadow. Principal singers, full orchestra, roughly fifteen thousand people on picnic blankets. No ticket, no dress code, no barrier of any kind.

Why this one Because it is the only event in five weeks where the most expensive art form in the city is handed over for nothing, on the same weekend those same singers command real money two miles east. Opening night at the opera house is the event that gets photographed. This is the one that matters, and a visitors' guide will either print the wrong date or miss it entirely.
