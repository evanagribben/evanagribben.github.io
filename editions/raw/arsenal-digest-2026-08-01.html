Vol. I  ·  The Pre-Season Edition

Saturday, August 1, 2026

A note on this edition: the scheduled Wednesday slot fired late this week, so we are anchored to Friday evening Pacific — Saturday morning in London. That is fortunate timing. The single biggest piece of Arsenal business this summer moved between 8pm and midnight UK time last night, and it is still moving as you read this. Everything below is stated at the certainty level the reporting actually supports, and where the evidence is thin we say so rather than smoothing it over.

The Lead

Arsenal Agree €90m for Bruno Guimarães — and Finally Say the Quiet Part Out Loud

Newcastle's captain has an agreement in principle to become the most expensive midfielder in Arsenal's history. It is the signing that turns a title defence into a statement of intent — and it lands in the same fortnight the champions lost their best defender for months.

There is a particular sound a transfer window makes when a club stops shopping and starts hunting. Arsenal made it on Friday evening. According to Hugo Guillemet of L'Équipe, whose reporting the English market then chased for the rest of the night, Arsenal and Newcastle United have reached an agreement in principle for Bruno Guimarães at a headline figure of €90m — roughly £77m — with the 28-year-old set to sign a contract running to 2031. A medical is being arranged. Agent fees, per the same reporting, are the last significant detail still to be settled.

This did not come from nowhere. On 8 July, David Ornstein of The Athletic reported that Guimarães had informed Newcastle of his wish to leave and join Arsenal — at a point when the Gunners' verbal position was somewhere around £60m. What followed was three weeks of the least subtle standoff of the window. Newcastle rejected €70m. They rejected €80m. They briefed, loudly, that €100m was the number. And then the ground gave way beneath them.

It gave way because Newcastle missed European qualification, because their captain wanted Champions League football and said so, and because — in the most extraordinary 48 hours any Premier League club has had this summer — Eddie Howe resigned. Guimarães asked to delay his departure for the club's La Manga pre-season camp; talkSPORT reported he had done exactly that, and by Friday night football.london was carrying his farewell message to Howe. A club without a manager, without European football, and without a captain who wants to stay is not a club that holds out for its asking price. Olympique Lyonnais, who inserted a 20 per cent sell-on when they sold him to Tyneside in January 2022, will bank something north of €8m for doing nothing at all.

What does Arsenal actually get? A 28-year-old Brazil international entering the last genuinely peak phase of his career, and — crucially — a midfielder who solves a problem the title-winning side did not know it had until May. Mikel Arteta's champions won the league on control: 26 goals conceded, 19 clean sheets in 37, and a double pivot of Declan Rice and Martin Zubimendi that strangled games rather than opening them. It was magnificent and, at moments, it was inert. In the Champions League final in Budapest, drawn 1–1 and lost on penalties to Paris Saint-Germain, Arsenal had the ball and no idea what to do with it in the final third.

Guimarães is the answer to that. He is not a destroyer and he is not a No. 10; he is the increasingly rare thing in between — a midfielder who receives under pressure on the half-turn, breaks a line with the pass rather than the dribble, and drags a team thirty yards up the pitch by force of personality. At Newcastle he was frequently the only player on the pitch willing to take the ball in the one place nobody wanted it. Put him alongside Rice and Zubimendi — and note that both of those are World Cup men returning late and jaded — and Arteta suddenly has three genuinely different ways to build a midfield rather than one very good one.

There is also a squad-management argument that nobody is making loudly enough. Arsenal are entering a season with the Community Shield, 38 league games, a 36-team Champions League league phase and two domestic cups, immediately after a World Cup summer in which fifteen of their players travelled and seven came home with medals. Zubimendi and Merino played to the final on 19 July. Rice and Saka played to the third-place match on the 18th. None of them is due back before 10 August. Signing a third top-tier central midfielder is not extravagance in that context. It is actuarial.

“From ownership to the board, the sporting director, myself — we know that we want to take this club to a different level.”

Mikel Arteta, speaking from Arsenal's training camp in Girona, 29 July

That quote deserves a moment. Arteta has spent six years choosing his words like a man defusing something, and he has spent most of them talking about process. On Wednesday in Spain he talked about money, and about who was authorising it. “A lot is happening,” he said. “We all know about the context of this transfer window.” Then, with unusual bluntness: “That is going to require a better squad, better individuals. We have identified where we have opportunities to grow… Hopefully very soon we can start to put things in a very concrete way.” Forty-eight hours later, £77m of concrete arrived.

Read the summer's ledger and the strategy is legible. Arsenal made Piero Hincapié's loan permanent for £34.5m on 25 June, took Illan Meslier on a free from Leeds United on 9 July, and paid Club Brugge a Belgian record £34m for Christos Tzolis on 23 July. Against that, they banked £17m from Beşiktaş for Leandro Trossard, £14.7m-plus from Porto for Jakub Kiwior, and £2.5m from Werder Bremen for Karl Hein. Add Guimarães and gross spend goes past £145m against roughly £34m recouped. That is not a champion consolidating. That is a champion pressing an advantage.

The discipline is real too, and it is worth crediting. Arsenal walked away from Jeremy Monga on price. They walked away from Morgan Rogers when Chelsea agreed a British-record £117m with Aston Villa to hijack a deal Arsenal believed was theirs — a genuinely painful loss, and one Andrea Berta's recruitment model absorbed by simply moving to the next name on a list of three or four per position. Dharmesh Sheth of Sky Sports News described that method plainly on 22 July: work several targets in every slot, then decide which one represents value. It explains why the club's attacking links this fortnight have read like a lottery draw.

One caution before anyone starts printing shirts. Nothing here is official. Arsenal have not announced Guimarães; Newcastle have not announced him; neither Ornstein nor Fabrizio Romano had declared the deal fully done at the time of writing. Agent fees have collapsed larger transfers than this one, and a medical is a medical. Call it what it is: a fee agreed, a medical booked, and a player who has already said his goodbyes.

Secondary Feature

The Saliba Hole, and a Title Defence That May Come Down to Physiotherapy

Arsenal rebuilt their entire medical department this summer. Then their best defender broke down at the World Cup and their right-back flew home from Spain.

On 22 July Arsenal issued the kind of statement clubs write when the news is worse than they would like to admit. William Saliba, they confirmed, had returned from World Cup duty with a back injury. “Extensive assessments have concluded that surgery is not recommended,” it read, “but William should now commence a managed recovery programme… he is expected to be out of action for an extended period.” L'Équipe put a number on the phrase the club would not: four to five months. That is a return somewhere between November and Christmas. Treat the timeline as reported rather than confirmed — the club has said only “extended period” — but note that Saliba has a documented history of chronic back trouble, and that he came off after thirty minutes of France's semi-final defeat to Spain on 14 July.

The irony is almost too neat. This is the summer Arsenal tore down and rebuilt their medical and performance department after what Daily Cannon described as an internal review led by the Spanish physiotherapist Joaquín Acedo. Out went the head of medicine Dr Zafar Iqbal after the Champions League final, along with Tom Allen, the head of sports science since 2017; Sam Wilson left for Juventus and Jordan Reece for Manchester United. In came Antonio Gómez from Braga as head of performance — confirmed on 29 July, and reportedly prised out only with difficulty — plus Arnaldo Abrantes from Aston Villa as head of medicine and Eneko Angulo from Real Betis as head of rehabilitation, with Simon Murphy promoted to head physio. It is the most thorough backroom overhaul of Arteta's tenure, and it was in place for approximately eleven days before the first catastrophe.

Which leaves a centre-back search running at speed and, so far, without a result. John Stones, a free agent after his Manchester City contract expired and a player Arteta has publicly called one he “really likes,” is reported to be joining Inter Milan instead. Ezri Konsa of Aston Villa — the best ground-duel win rate of any outfield player in the Premier League last season — is admired, but Villa want £60m and there is daylight between the valuations. Jacobo Ramón of Como, reported by James McNicholas of The Athletic on 28 July, is complicated by Real Madrid retaining half his economic rights and a buy-back. In the meantime Arteta has Gabriel Magalhães, Cristhian Mosquera, Piero Hincapié and Riccardo Calafiori, which is enough — until it isn't.

Then the calendar. Jurriën Timber had minor groin surgery over the summer to fix a longstanding problem and flew home from the Girona camp around 29 July for planned rehabilitation checks — sources stressed this was a scheduled stage rather than a setback, and there is no suggestion yet that he misses either the Community Shield or the opener. Ben White, back from the knee ligament injury that ended his season on 10 May, says simply: “Knee's all good.” But Saka, Rice, Raya, Zubimendi, Merino, Eze and Madueke are not due back before 10 August. Martin Ødegaard returns the week of the 3rd. That gives seven first-choice players roughly six days of training before Cardiff and a fortnight before Coventry. The Guardian's framing on Friday was the right one: this title defence may hinge less on who Arsenal sign than on whether they can keep a drained squad upright.

Transfer News

The Vinícius Júnior Saga: Real Interest, Real Wages, and a Lot of Noise

Ornstein stood the story up a week ago. What has happened since is a case study in how a legitimate exploratory link becomes an overnight “medical booked.” Read the sourcing carefully.

Start with what is solid. On 25 July, David Ornstein reported that Arsenal were working on a move for Vinícius Júnior should the Brazilian decline a contract extension at Real Madrid. Sky Sports independently described the interest as “real” but preliminary, with no club-to-club contact. Vinícius, 26 and the 2024 Ballon d'Or runner-up, has twelve months left on his deal. If Madrid cannot extend him, they face the choice every selling club dreads: bank a fee now or lose him for nothing in 2027.

The obstacle is not the transfer fee. It is the wage. Vinícius earns a reported €15–17m net a year. Madrid's offer has been around €20m; his camp has asked for €30m, or roughly €550,000 a week net. Translated into Arsenal terms, that is £450,000–£500,000 a week against a wage structure whose ceiling is Bukayo Saka at a little over £300,000. Reporting suggests Arsenal might stretch to £400,000. The Spanish analyst Terry Gibson summed the standoff up bleakly: “There's no budging on the contract from Real Madrid or Vinicius. This has been ongoing for 18 months.”

Now the noise. Between 19:53 on Friday and 06:00 on Saturday, the English tabloid and aggregator market produced, in order: “Vinicius Jr moves step closer,” “transfer edges closer after Real Madrid contract stance,” “Arsenal personal terms with Vinicius Jr agreed as medical set,” “Arsenal 'agree terms' with Vinicius Jr,” “told take it or leave it as Gunners 'agree £77million transfer',” and a “monstrous offer” from an unnamed insider. We would counsel considerable caution. The £77m figure appearing against Vinícius is, on the face of it, the Guimarães number migrating across headlines on the same night. No tier-one reporter had corroborated a medical. Sky's position as of this week is that a new Madrid contract remains the most likely outcome, and Madrid tabled a fresh offer on 28 July. José Mourinho, now managing at the Bernabéu, wants to keep him and has spoken to him about a clean slate.

Certainty tier: concrete interest, no club-to-club contact confirmed. Everything above that is single-sourced and, in several cases, sourced to outlets we would not stake a reputation on. What is genuinely notable is not the state of the deal but the fact that Arsenal are in the conversation at all. As Arseblog put it on 26 July, the link itself “ticks the ambitious box.” Five years ago this club were negotiating over Brighton's reserve left-back.

The Striker Question: Álvarez, Barcola, and the Ghost of Morgan Rogers

Arsenal have been linked with four different elite forwards in a fortnight. That is not indecision — it is Berta's shortlist doing exactly what it was designed to do.

The thread starts with Miguel Delaney of The Independent, who reported on 12 July that Arsenal wanted Julián Álvarez before pre-season began. The Argentine, 26, would prefer Barcelona; Atlético Madrid will not sell to a domestic rival. Mundo Deportivo reported on 27 July that Atleti see only two outcomes — Álvarez stays until 2030, or he goes to England or France — leaving Arsenal and Paris Saint-Germain as the credible suitors.

A word on the swap story, because it has been everywhere. Cadena SER's Miguel Martín Talavera reported on 25 July that Arsenal might offer £85m plus Viktor Gyökeres. Fabrizio Romano knocked it down flat on 28 July: no such package has been submitted, nothing is on track for Gyökeres to join Atlético, and Arteta is unwilling to sacrifice a striker who scored 21 goals in 55 games last season. Berta's message to enquiring clubs back in June, per Romano, was unambiguous: “We want to keep the player. We believe in the player, and Viktor Gyökeres is part of our project.” Certainty tier: rumour, publicly disputed by a tier-one source.

Bradley Barcola is the third name and looks the least likely. Dharmesh Sheth confirmed on 22 July that Arsenal retained interest after losing Rogers; PSG want £145–150m and, per late-July reporting, Liverpool have a verbal agreement. The fourth, Yan Diomande of RB Leipzig, should be struck off the list entirely: Sky have stated plainly that Arsenal are not in that race, and Real Madrid are in advanced talks at somewhere near £95m.

Which brings us back, as everything this month does, to Morgan Rogers. Arsenal had him as their primary attacking target. Ornstein broke the hijack; Romano confirmed it with a “here we go” — Chelsea reached a club-to-club agreement with Aston Villa at £117m, a British record, to get in front of an Arsenal bid, with personal terms to June 2032. It is the one genuinely painful defeat of the window, and it explains everything that has followed: Tzolis arriving as a Trossard replacement rather than a Rogers one, and the club then reaching for names — Vinícius, Álvarez — a tier above where it was shopping in June.

The Rumour Mill

Everything else simmering, with its certainty stated plainly. Arsenal have roughly five weeks to solve an outgoings problem they have barely touched.

Gabriel Jesus is the clearest sale target on the books. Ornstein reported in late May that Arsenal were quoting £18–20m and would not entertain a cheap sale despite the contract expiring in June 2027. Gianluca Di Marzio has AC Milan giving him “concrete consideration” with no formal talks; Juventus and Atlético Madrid are circling. Tim Stillman wrote on 29 July that the Brazilian returned to London Colney “with a year remaining on his contract and a FOR SALE sign firmly around his neck,” and did not rule out a contract termination. Concrete interest; no bid.

Reiss Nelson is expected to go. Sami Mokbel of BBC Sport reported in the week of 21 July that Arsenal are willing to sell and that the Hale End graduate is open to a move abroad, having turned down English clubs; Benfica — where Marco Silva had him on loan at Fulham — and Valencia are interested. A £10m valuation has been floated, single-sourced. Concrete interest; exit expected.

Fábio Vieira wants to return to Hamburg, where he contributed seven goals and six assists on loan. The loan carried a €20m option; HSV can reach about €10m and Arsenal value him near €18m. Sporting director Kathleen Krüger: “The price being quoted is not realistic for us.” Board member Eric Huwer, at HSV's Austria camp on 22 July: “We've fallen a little bit in love with Fábio. We're fighting for him every day and have also submitted figures to Arsenal… They're not even budging yet.” Formal offer submitted and rejected; talks stalled.

Ethan Nwaneri is the week's live one, and the reporting contradicts itself. Matteo Moretto, on Romano's channel on 30 July, said AC Milan have made enquiries and that Arsenal “would be willing to let him go… but they would like to sell him permanently without losing full control,” with RB Leipzig, Everton and Fulham also in. The Times put the asking price at £50m, with £35–40m up front acceptable; Corriere dello Sport the next day said €35m and that Arsenal will not sell. Borussia Dortmund want a loan. He has four years left, started against MK Dons and scored, and is in Spain. Enquiry only; no bid.

Elsewhere: Maxi Araújo, the Sporting CP left-back, has reportedly been offered to Arsenal and Liverpool by his agent against a €80m release clause — offered to club, exploratory. Isaan Khan of the Daily Mail reported on 28 July that Arsenal are “pushing hard” for Mylo Bernard, a 16-year-old Crystal Palace striker. Nicolò Schira has AS Roma monitoring Gabriel Martinelli, whose contract expires in 2027 and whose minutes look squeezed by the summer's wide signings — though Mokbel reports the Brazilian is content and there are no bids. Kepa Arrizabalaga is considering his future after the Meslier signing but no club has been named. Ben White's situation is genuinely unresolved: The Telegraph reported in early July that he is expected to leave, which other outlets flatly dispute. And Takehiro Tomiyasu, much missed in N5, has been training with Crystal Palace since 28 July after a Venezia move collapsed over non-EU registration slots.

One structural note that matters more than any individual name. Arsenal's estimated 2025/26 accounts show record revenue near £790m but a pre-tax loss, driven almost entirely by the collapse of player-trading profit from roughly £81m to nothing. Academy graduates carry no book value, which means every pound received for Nelson or Nwaneri is pure accounting profit. That is the cleanest lever the club has, and it is the real reason the outgoings column matters as much as the incoming one.

The Squad

Three Pre-Seasons at Once, and a Greek Winger Who Looks Like a Bargain

Arteta is running his squad in three staggered groups. Tonight in Girona, most of the team that won the league will not be on the pitch.

“It is all programmed,” Arteta said in Spain on Wednesday. “We have three different pre-seasons with different groups. But the most important thing is not the programme, it is the attitude behind the programme.” He also offered the line every manager offers and almost nobody means: “The shape that they have all come back, better than ever.” Given fifteen of his players went to a World Cup, one suspects he is grading generously.

Pre-season opened at London Colney on 20 July with the non-World Cup group — Myles Lewis-Skelly, Calafiori, Mosquera, Jesus, Nelson, White and Timber. The first fixture, a behind-closed-doors friendly against MK Dons on 25 July, finished 3–0. Reiss Nelson opened the scoring on ten minutes from a Tzolis assist; Ethan Nwaneri curled in a second from outside the box before half-time; Ceadach O'Neill, 18, made it three after the break, set up by Demiane Agustien. Seven players made their first senior appearances for the club. Note the shape of that first-half team — Kepa; Copley, Mosquera, Salmon, Calafiori; Lewis-Skelly, Nwaneri; Nelson, Dowman, Tzolis; Jesus — and consider that not one of those eleven is guaranteed to start against Coventry.

The squad then flew to Girona on 27 July for a camp, and the most encouraging early sign has been Christos Tzolis. Arsenal paid Club Brugge a Belgian record £34m for a 24-year-old who produced 17 goals and 23 assists in 36 league matches last season — a return that would be remarkable in any division. He is a left winger who also functions as a No. 10; his former assistant coach at Brugge, Hayk Milkon, describes a player who “runs a lot of kilometres in the game, but also at high speeds” and who “is always strongest the closer he is to the goal.” He has taken the No. 17 shirt, last made famous by Alexis Sánchez. Arseblog have already called him the potential smart signing of the summer, and on the evidence of one assist in a closed-doors friendly, they may be right for the wrong reasons — but the underlying numbers are not a fluke.

Two structural things worth holding onto for the season ahead. First, the set pieces: Arsenal scored 24 goals from dead balls last season, the most in the division, including 18 from corners — a Premier League record. Nicolas Jover remains at the club; the widely-circulated story about a set-piece coach departing referred to Chris Bradley of the women's team, not to Jover. Second, the shape: Arteta moved to a 4-2-3-1 in thirteen of thirty-seven league games last season, which the arrival of Zubimendi made possible and which Guimarães would make routine.

On the captaincy — and we flag this because it has been reported carelessly elsewhere — Martin Ødegaard wore the armband throughout 2025/26 and there has been no announcement of any change. A February report claiming Declan Rice would take over traces to a low-reliability aggregator and should be disregarded. What is genuine is that Ødegaard has under two and a half years left and is not, as far as we can establish, in extension talks. He does not return to training until the week of 3 August. That is a storyline for the autumn, not for today.

Hale End

New Regime, New Intake — and Eleven Kids on the Plane to Spain

Pascal De Maesschalck formally takes charge of the academy today. He inherits nine new scholars, a defender poached from Tottenham, and a 20-year-old winger auditioning for his own future in real time.

The date on this edition is Pascal De Maesschalck's first day as Arsenal's Academy Director. Note the title: Per Mertesacker was Academy Manager, and the change is deliberate. De Maesschalck arrives from Strasbourg, where he was technical director, and previously ran the academies at AS Monaco and Club Brugge. Andrea Berta: “He brings extensive experience from some of Europe's leading football clubs and has an outstanding track record in youth development.” The brief, as reported, is blunt — the first-team pathway, poor UEFA Youth League results, and the club's recent difficulty retaining its best teenagers.

Mertesacker, meanwhile, departs after eight years for one of the biggest jobs in European football administration. Announced on 24 July, he becomes the German FA's Managing Director of Sport, overseeing the men's and women's national teams at senior and youth level, succeeding Andreas Rettig from January 2027. “I am very much looking forward to this new task and the responsibility that comes with it,” he said. He is 41.

The recruitment has been busy. On 21 July Arsenal confirmed Elijah Upson, an 18-year-old England U18 centre-back — and the son of Matthew Upson, a 2001/02 title winner at Highbury — who came through Tottenham Hotspur's academy, made two U21 appearances and scored in the Youth League against Monaco. Spurs offered him a professional deal. He chose N5. He takes squad number 57, which had been held for the goalkeeper Alexei Rojas before Rojas declined a professional contract and joined FC Penafiel. Five days later, Romano declared “here we go” on Axel Donczew, a 16-year-old from Cardiff City who became the Welsh club's youngest-ever first-team player at 15 years and 234 days, breaking a record held by Aaron Ramsey. Too young for professional terms, so Arsenal agreed compensation instead. Sources differ on whether he is a midfielder or a winger; we will find out.

On 27 July the club announced nine first-year scholars: Hakeem Abubakar (85), Elyon Mbala (87), Daniel McCarron (88), Emerson Nwaneri (90), Jayden Oba (91), Theo Odaji-Ukueku (92), Angelinou Pedro (93), Charlie Purdy (94) and Joshua Sesay (95). Two names to underline. McCarron, 16, arrives from Dungannon Swifts having already made his senior debut in Northern Ireland, is a Northern Ireland youth international, and is exceptionally tall for his age; Arseblog tipped him on 24 July to partner the 17-year-old Poland youth international Patrick Stachow in the U18s after the pair played the last half-hour together against Boreham Wood and, in a passage where Arsenal conceded, “looked proper defenders.” Given how long this academy has struggled to produce centre-halves, that is not a small thing.

And Emerson Nwaneri, 15, younger brother of Ethan, has agreed both a scholarship and a pre-agreed professional deal that activates when he turns 17 in August 2027, running to 2030 — per Sam Dean of The Telegraph. Chelsea and Manchester City were interested. He already has Youth League and Premier League 2 debuts. In a week where his brother's asking price was being briefed to Italian newspapers, retaining the younger one is the more quietly significant piece of business.

Eleven academy players travelled to Girona: goalkeeper Khari Ranson; defenders Josh Ogunnaike, Marli Salmon and Brayden Clarke; midfielders Louie Copley, Ife Ibrahim, Demiane Agustien and Theo Julienne; and forwards Ceadach O'Neill, Ismeal Kabia and Andre Harriman-Annous. With Arsenal short at right-back, Copley and Julienne may get real minutes there. O'Neill — 18, signed from Linfield, and a senior Northern Ireland international since a friendly against Guinea on 4 June — is the one to watch.

The individual story, though, is Ismeal Kabia. Twenty years old, a right winger who also plays right-back, one senior appearance (the Carabao Cup against Bolton Wanderers in 2024), and a well-regarded loan season at Shrewsbury Town behind him. He was conspicuously absent from the U21 friendly at Boreham Wood, which triggered exit speculation; he is now training with the first team and has travelled to Spain while a decision on his future — stay, loan, or permanent sale — remains open. No destination has been reported anywhere. And one broader note worth stating plainly: with roughly five weeks until deadline day, Arsenal have not confirmed a single outgoing academy loan for 2026/27. West Ham United have enquired about Jaden Dixon. That is the entire list.

Arsenal Women

Seven In, Seven Out: This Is Renée Slegers' Team Now

A wholesale rebuild, mostly on free transfers, three departures to direct rivals, and a fixture list that throws Arsenal at the entire top four inside five games.

The fixtures landed on 30 July and they are unkind. Arsenal open the WSL season away at Brighton & Hove Albion on Sunday 6 September, 12:00 UK, at the Broadfield Stadium in Crawley. Then Crystal Palace at home on the 13th, Manchester United at home on the 19th, Chelsea away on the 27th and Manchester City away on 4 October. All four of last season's top four inside five matches. The North London derby at home falls on 13 March; the season closes at home to Charlton Athletic in late May 2027.

The structural change underneath that is significant: the WSL expands to fourteen clubs, meaning 26 league matches rather than 22, and with six Champions League league-phase games and the cups, a minimum of 33 competitive fixtures — potentially 42. All thirteen home league games will be played at the Emirates Stadium for a second consecutive season, with Meadow Park retaining the domestic cups and the Champions League league phase. Arsenal will take a women's team to Old Trafford and the Etihad for the first time.

The squad they take there is barely recognisable. Seven have arrived: Georgia Stanway from Bayern Munich (3 July, No. 4), Selina Cerci from Hoffenheim (6 July, free, No. 29), Géraldine Reuteler from Eintracht Frankfurt (8 July, free, four years, No. 14), Ona Batlle from Barcelona (10 July, free, No. 22), Lisa Baum, 19, from RB Leipzig (14 July, for a reported €500–600k — figures vary — in what was billed as a Bundesliga record for a female player, No. 19), the 18-year-old goalkeeper Isabella Damm from Brøndby (17 July, No. 40), and — the one inside our window — Misa Rodríguez from Real Madrid on 22 July, a free transfer after a club-record 215 appearances. Slegers on the Spain international: “Misa is a top goalkeeper who has performed consistently at a high level for several years. As well as her technical attributes, she has great presence and communication on the pitch.”

The goalkeeper stockpiling has a reason. Daphne van Domselaar is recovering from knee surgery on an undisclosed timeline and is unlikely to start the season; Manuela Zinsberger suffered an ACL last season and has since joined Borussia Dortmund. Rodríguez is expected to start, with Anneke Borbe competing.

Against that, the departures sting: Katie McCabe to Chelsea, Beth Mead to Manchester City, Victoria Pelova to Tottenham Hotspur — three to direct rivals — plus Laia Codina to West Ham United, Zinsberger to Dortmund and Rosa Kafaji permanently to London City Lionesses, where she posted, poignantly, “right club, wrong time.” Inside our window, on 28 July, the England U20 captain Laila Harbert joined San Diego Wave permanently — and here is the detail most outlets missed: Arsenal retained a matching right. As Tim Stillman put it, “if and when they sell her, we will be invited to match the offer they receive and, if we do, that gives us preference.” She joins the former Arsenal manager Jonas Eidevall in California. On 24 July, the 19-year-old striker Jessie Gale was reported set for a season-long loan at Newcastle United — reported rather than officially confirmed, so treat it as agreed-not-announced.

The most striking detail of the whole summer, though, is cultural. Per Stillman, Slegers has removed Katie McCabe from the leadership group and added Lotte Wubben-Moy and Alessia Russo; players now sit in squad-number order rather than by seniority. Kim Little remains captain having signed a one-year extension in March, with Leah Williamson as vice-captain. Eight established players have signed renewals under Slegers, and only three squad members were neither signed nor re-signed by her. It is, as Stillman wrote, her team now.

One in-window item to flag with a caveat: The Online Gooner reported exclusively on 31 July that Nikita Runnacles has been appointed set-piece coach and assistant goalkeeping coach, replacing Chris Bradley. We could not corroborate it elsewhere — treat as single-sourced. If accurate, it addresses a real problem: Arsenal conceded four set-piece goals in the WSL last season and finished four points behind champions Manchester City with only one league defeat and six draws.

Europe next. Arsenal reached the Champions League semi-final last season and lost it 4–3 on aggregate to OL Lyonnes, having beaten Chelsea 3–2 over two legs in the quarters; Alessia Russo was the competition's top scorer with seven. They won the inaugural FIFA Women's Champions Cup in February, beating Corinthians 3–2 after extra time at the Emirates with a 104th-minute Caitlin Foord winner. For 2026/27 they enter a restructured 18-team single league phase directly, provisionally in the top pot on a UEFA coefficient of 78.000. The draw is on 4 September; the final is in Warsaw next May.

Gunners Abroad

There were no senior international fixtures involving Arsenal players in the seven days to 1 August — the men's World Cup finished on 19 July and there is no major women's tournament this year. But the tournament's aftershocks are the story of this pre-season, so here is the ledger.

Fifteen Arsenal players travelled to North America. Seven came home with medals — more than any other Premier League club. David Raya, Mikel Merino and Martin Zubimendi are world champions, Spain having beaten Argentina 1–0 after extra time in the final on 19 July through Ferran Torres. For Raya, Merino and Zubimendi, that means a Premier League title and a World Cup inside three months — a combination achieved by a vanishingly small number of footballers. Bukayo Saka, Declan Rice, Eberechi Eze and Noni Madueke took bronze with England, who beat France 6–4 in an extraordinary third-place play-off on 18 July.

And William Saliba finished fourth with France, having limped out of the semi-final against Spain after half an hour with the back injury that will now cost him most of the autumn. Martin Ødegaard reached the quarter-finals with Norway. Kai Havertz went out with Germany in the last 32; Piero Hincapié (Ecuador), Viktor Gyökeres (Sweden), Gabriel Magalhães and Gabriel Martinelli (Brazil) have all now reported back.

One further note from further down the ladder: the 18-year-old Ceadach O'Neill made his senior debut for Northern Ireland on 4 June, coming off the bench in a 1–0 friendly win over Guinea — a reminder that Arsenal's international representation now runs several layers deeper than the first team. Looking ahead for the women: England begin their 2027 World Cup qualifying play-off against Greece away on 9 October and at home on 13 October, and the U20 World Cup in Poland in September is the reason Vivienne Lia was recalled early from her loan at Hammarby.

In Brief

Away kit — Arsenal launched the 2026/27 adidas away shirt on 24 July: an inverted "Bruised Banana," navy base with yellow zig-zag chevrons and red collar detail. £85 replica, £120 authentic. A new sleeve partner, Deel, appears alongside Emirates. The third kit is expected late August.

Stadium — Arsenal remain in discussions with Populous, the Emirates' original designers, about expansion toward 70,000 from the current 60,704. Chief executive Richard Garlick is leading it; cost estimates exceed £500m, and one scenario reportedly involves temporarily vacating the stadium. No planning application with Islington Council has been reported. Transport capacity at Arsenal, Finsbury Park and Drayton Park is the binding constraint.

Finances — Analyst estimates for 2025/26 put Arsenal's revenue near a Premier League record £790m, with commercial income above £300m, but a pre-tax loss driven by the near-total absence of player-trading profit. Independent modelling has the club comfortably inside both the Premier League's incoming squad-cost limit and UEFA's 70 per cent ratio. These are estimates, not audited accounts.

Ownership — No new KSE statement this week. The most recent substantive line remains Josh Kroenke's May promise that "we're going to look to strengthen" — which, on the evidence of the last fortnight, was not a platitude.

Laws of the game — The FA confirmed on 28 July that IFAB has approved three new anti-timewasting measures for 2026/27, including a trial protocol for goalkeeper injuries. Given how many Arsenal corners arrive in the 89th minute, this is not a neutral development.

U21s — David Horseman ran U21 pre-season and the friendly at Boreham Wood after Max Porter left for Stoke City's first-team staff in June, but no permanent U21 head coach has been announced. Adam Birchall continues with the U18s. Premier League 2 and U18 Premier League fixture lists for 2026/27 had not been published at the time of writing.

Cardiff calling — The Community Shield leaves Wembley because of pre-booked concerts, returning to Cardiff for the first time in twenty years. It is only the third time since 2007 the fixture has been staged elsewhere.
