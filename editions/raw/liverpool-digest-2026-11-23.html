The Lead

Wirtz and Ngumoha run riot as Liverpool take Pochettino's United apart

Ten men, ten chances created by one player and a teenager who outshone everyone: Liverpool's most complete Anfield performance of the season, with a warning about finishing.

Three training days. That was all Mauricio Pochettino had to prepare, and his side lasted eight minutes before it fell apart. Leny Yoro was sent off as the last man for bringing down Alexander Isak, and by 12 minutes and 40 seconds Liverpool led 2-0, their fastest two-goal lead against Manchester United in a league game, per beIN Sports.

It finished 4-0 at Anfield, and it could have been more. Dominik Szoboszlai curled the free kick into the bottom corner on 10 minutes. Three minutes later Florian Wirtz hooked in Rio Ngumoha's clipped cross, ESPN's report noted. Ngumoha added the third on 66 minutes and substitute Víctor Muñoz finished a one-two with Wirtz on 82.

How the game was won

The numbers describe a mismatch. Liverpool had 64 per cent of the ball, 28 shots to 10 and expected goals of 4.38 to 0.64, according to FotMob figures quoted by Sports Illustrated. They created nine big chances to United's none and won 15 corners. Only Alisson's two saves from Bruno Fernandes, the second in stoppage time, protected the clean sheet.

Yoro's red card shaped everything, and it began with Liverpool's own intent. Ronald Araujo played the ball over the top, Isak ran through and Yoro hauled him down. "It's a clear foul, he's the last player," Andoni Iraola said in his press conference. Pochettino saw it differently, arguing a minute earlier Jérémy Jacquet had fouled Bryan Mbeumo for a penalty.

This is still so early. This doesn't stop because we have a very difficult game against Club Brugge.

Andoni Iraola after Liverpool 4-0 Manchester United, via beIN Sports

Wirtz was the difference. He created ten chances from open play, the most by any player in a Premier League match since David Silva's 11 in March 2015, beIN reported. He also covered more ground than anyone. "He's in a lovely moment, and we want to keep him there as much as we can," Iraola said.

Ngumoha was the story. At 18 years and 85 days he became the youngest player to score or assist in a Premier League Liverpool v United game, SI noted. With Cody Gakpo injured on Dutch duty, he started wide and gave Diogo Dalot a miserable afternoon, leaving to a standing ovation on 77 minutes.

What it changes, and what it doesn't

Iraola refused the bait. "This is still so early. This doesn't stop because we have a very difficult game against Club Brugge," he said. He also wanted more ruthlessness: Isak missed several clear chances and Bradley Barcola missed a sitter, The Independent noted.

The table is kinder now. Liverpool are fourth on 21 points, seven behind Arsenal and one behind Brighton and Manchester City, per Sky Sports. Last week this Digest worried about five points from five home league games. It now reads eight from six, and the defeats to City and Arsenal feel less like a pattern.

For United it was a ninth league game without a win, ESPN noted. "It's so painful, we feel pain for that defeat, but we need to keep the head up," Pochettino said, per Yahoo Sports. For Liverpool, the test now is repeating it away from home: Brugge on Wednesday, then the Merseyside derby on Sunday, two away games in four days.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Club Brugge: high line, hot striker, and a team level with Liverpool

Second in Belgium and unbeaten in five, Ivan Leko's side beat Inter and PSV away this autumn. Their defence leaves space behind it.

Club Brugge arrive in form. They are second in the Belgian Pro League with 31 points from 12 games, one behind Union SG, per FotMob. Their last five read four wins and a draw, finishing with a 5-1 win at Mechelen on Saturday that Sporza called a good dress rehearsal for Liverpool.

In Europe they have nine points, exactly like Liverpool, who sit eighth to Brugge's ninth on goals scored. After an opening 3-2 home defeat to Aston Villa, Brugge won 4-3 at Inter, 3-0 against Lens and 2-1 at PSV, per FotMob.

The system and the danger men

Head coach Ivan Leko usually plays a 4-2-3-1 that becomes a 4-3-3 in possession. His side keeps the ball, presses high and attacks down the flanks, per Inter's opposition analysis. The striker to stop is Nicolò Tresoldi, a mobile runner with 10 league goals, per FotMob, and five in the Champions League, per Paramount+.

Behind him, captain Hans Vanaken is the creative hub and winger Carlos Forbs leads the team with five league assists. Winger Jan Virgili is doubtful with a groin injury from the PSV game, RotoWire reported, and missed Saturday's win. No suspensions were reported.

Where Liverpool can hurt them

The high line is the opening. Villa beat Brugge's press and scored three first-half goals in September, Inter's analysts noted, and Brugge concede about 1.3 expected goals a game. Belgian outlet Voetbalkrant singled out Liverpool's counter-attacks, Isak's runs and Szoboszlai's free kicks. Brugge's own set pieces are a strength, so the dead-ball battle cuts both ways.

Transfer News

Mac Allister talks begin, and the contract season arrives

Romano says conversations are open. The Athletic calls a new deal a priority. Nothing is close yet.

The biggest move this week was a negotiation, not a signing. On November 18 Fabrizio Romano said: "We can now confirm that conversations between #LFC and Mac Allister for a new contract have started. It's still early stages," as relayed by CaughtOffside. He added that Real Madrid explored a move in May and Liverpool were not interested in selling.

A day earlier, his father and agent Carlos Mac Allister said he had not yet been contacted "but we hope that will happen this week," per Yahoo Sports. The Athletic's Gregg Evans wrote that a new deal "now feels like a priority," as reported by Anfield Index. His contract runs to 2028. Status: talks opened, early stage.

Van Dijk: 'If not, then I'm going to say goodbyes in the summer'

The captain speaks on the record. The club, for now, does not.

Virgil van Dijk's contract ends in June 2027, and he gave Sky Sports his clearest answer yet on November 20: "If it's getting extended, we'll see then. If not, then I'm going to say goodbyes in the summer." He added that anyone claiming to know his plans was wrong.

Italian outlet Sport Italia claims he wants a two-year extension and Liverpool remain unconvinced, via Anfield Index; that is a single-sourced rumour. David Ornstein's view from last week stands: no clarity until the spring.

Camara: still the target, still no bid

The Athletic confirms the interest. Claims of agreed terms come from weaker sources.

James Pearce of The Athletic said on November 18 that "interest in Lamine Camara is still there," and that Liverpool would bring the deal forward to January if possible, though they see no "glaring hole" in midfield, per readliverpoolfc. Lamine Camara is Monaco's 22-year-old Senegal midfielder, under contract to 2029.

CaughtOffside claims Liverpool are "making progress" and close to personal terms, with Monaco wanting about €70m, as relayed by FootballTransfers; French outlet Topmercato repeated it without new detail. Status: concrete interest (Pearce); personal terms single-sourced and unconfirmed; no bid reported.

Alisson and Araujo

Transfer reporter Nicolò Schira says Liverpool have opened talks to extend Alisson's deal, which expires next summer, and that the goalkeeper wants a contract to 2029, per FootballTransfers. Single-sourced; the club has said nothing. Status: talks reported.

Barcelona sporting director Deco told Catalan radio that if Araujo stays at Liverpool and "the money comes in, that wouldn't be bad either," per GOAL. The loan includes a purchase option reported in Spain at €55m (£47m, per GOAL); Araujo would still need to agree terms.

The Squad

Gakpo hurt, Frimpong back, and a front four that picks itself

Iraola's 4-2-3-1 has found its shape. The question is how long the bodies hold up through four games in eleven days.

Liverpool lined up in a 4-2-3-1 on Sunday: Araujo, Virgil van Dijk, Jacquet and Milos Kerkez across the back, Szoboszlai and Alexis Mac Allister in midfield, and Barcola, Wirtz and Ngumoha behind Isak, per Sports Mole's line-ups. Van Dijk, who withdrew from the Netherlands squad, started and played the full game.

Gakpo is the new absentee. He picked up a calf (soleus) problem with the Netherlands and has not trained since; The Athletic's James Pearce says it is not serious, per Sports Mole. He is expected to miss Brugge, and Brit Brief suggests a return around the trip to Sunderland on December 2.

Midfield depth and the Gravenberch question

Ryan Gravenberch is still out with his heel. "Ryan has been training but still is managing the pain," Iraola said; he could return in Bruges. Until then the pairing is Szoboszlai and Mac Allister, with Trey Nyoni the first change. Nyoni replaced Mac Allister on 72 minutes on Sunday, days after starting England U21's 3-2 win over France.

Jeremie Frimpong's 13-minute cameo was his first appearance since mid-October. Conor Bradley and Giovanni Leoni are back in partial training but not in contention. "I hope they can play in some weeks," Iraola said. Hugo Ekitike is still recovering from his ruptured Achilles.

The scoring is spreading

Last week Isak had half of Liverpool's 14 league goals. Sunday's four came from Szoboszlai, Wirtz, Ngumoha and Muñoz, none of them Isak. Muñoz now has three league goals and Ngumoha his first. Isak still leads with seven, but the dependence has eased.

Kirkby

Inglethorpe's double wins a seven-goal thriller at Stoke

The U18s came through a scrappy afternoon, the U21s hit six in a friendly, and the Youth League side heads to Bruges.

Liverpool's U18s won 4-3 at Stoke City on Saturday. Finn Inglethorpe scored twice, the second a penalty, with Cam Williams and Erik Farkas also on target, liverpoolfc.com reported. Josh Abe beat three players to set up Williams, and goalkeeper Matty Wright made late saves after Stoke closed to 4-3. Everton visit the Academy next Saturday.

With no Premier League 2 game, the U21s beat Wigan 6-1 in a friendly on Friday. Will Wright scored twice, with Patrik Farkas, Jayden Danns (penalty), Josh Sonni-Lambie and Joe Bradshaw also scoring, per the club. They host Birmingham in PL2 on Saturday at 6am Pacific.

The UEFA Youth League side plays Club Brugge at The NEST on Wednesday at 2pm UK, 6am Pacific, the club confirmed. On youth international duty, Nyoni started England U21's 3-2 win over France, and Abe came on in England U17's 3-0 win over Ukraine.

Liverpool FC Women

Four goals at Forest, then a clean sheet against the leaders

Gareth Taylor's side are unbeaten in four and heading into the break in eighth.

Liverpool FC Women won 4-1 at Nottingham Forest in the Subway Players Cup on Wednesday. Mia Enderby scored twice, Beata Olsson added another and, after Forest goalkeeper Emily Batty was sent off on ten minutes, her replacement turned Enderby's cross into her own net, per liverpoolfc.com. It was four wins from five in the cup's league stage.

"This is a competition we want to win and we can win," head coach Gareth Taylor said, per the club, adding of Enderby: "there's a potential player in there."

Holding the leaders

On Saturday they held WSL leaders Chelsea 0-0 at St Helens. Goalkeeper Keating was player of the match, saving from Kaptein, Nüsken and Walsh, the club reported. It was a second straight league clean sheet and the fourth of the season.

Liverpool's chances were "probably better," Taylor said, per Yahoo Sports. He also singled out the centre-back: "We can't do that without Grace [Fisk]. She was incredible," per the club. The draw lifted Liverpool to eighth. By the Digest's count that is 12 points from 11 games.

Ceri Holland is training but worked away from the group on Saturday, and may still join Wales. Long-term absentees Josendal, Roman Haug and Csillag are not close, the club said. After the international break, Liverpool visit Birmingham City on Sunday, December 13, at 2pm UK (6am Pacific).
