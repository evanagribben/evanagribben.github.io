The Lead · World Cup Final

The Last Dance? Messi's Argentina Meet Spain's Machine

Sunday in New Jersey, the tournament's most relentless attack collides with its most impenetrable defence — and a 39-year-old chases immortality against the teenager tipped to succeed him.

There is a version of this final football has been quietly willing into existence for two years, and on Sunday it arrives. Argentina and Spain meet in the 2026 World Cup final on July 19 at MetLife Stadium (3 p.m. ET / 12 p.m. Pacific), pitting Lionel Messi, at 39 and chasing a second consecutive World Cup, against Lamine Yamal, the 19-year-old anointed as his successor. The bookmakers make Spain favourites at around -175 to Argentina's +125 — a nod to La Roja's suffocating control — but nobody who has watched Argentina these past three weeks would call it settled.

Argentina arrived the hard way, as they seem to prefer. Trailing England after Anthony Gordon's 55th-minute header, Lionel Scaloni's side turned the game late — Enzo Fernández levelling in the 85th and Lautaro Martínez heading a stoppage-time winner, both from Messi assists, for a 2-1 comeback. Spain, by contrast, dismantled France 2-0 through Mikel Oyarzabal's penalty and Pedro Porro's finish, extending Luis de la Fuente's team to 37 matches unbeaten with just one goal conceded across seven games — the meanest defence a single World Cup has ever seen.

"Spain were tactically all over the French team."

— Patrick Vieira, on the semi-final

The tactical puzzle is an immovable object against an unstoppable force. Spain will keep the ball — Rodri, the reigning Ballon d'Or holder, setting a rhythm few can disrupt — and dare Argentina to chase shadows. Argentina's answer is pressure and patience: half of their tournament-leading 18 goals have come after the 75th minute, and they trust Messi, now a right-sided creator, to conjure one moment. The stakes could hardly be larger: back-to-back World Cups for Messi (unmanaged since Brazil in 1962) against a coronation for Yamal, who at 19 would be only the fourth teenager to win a final.

Before the Final: France and England Play for Bronze

The two beaten semi-finalists meet first, in the third-place playoff on Saturday, July 18 (5 p.m. ET / 2 p.m. Pacific) in Miami, with Mbappé chasing the Golden Boot. France may be without William Saliba, who has been managing back trouble and sat out training — a concern that doubles as Arsenal news, given he is one of theirs. For Tuchel's England, beaten in a semi-final for the second tournament running, it is a chance to salvage a medal.

Transfer News

Barcelona Land Adeyemi and Chase Álvarez as Madrid Reload

A £100m bid for an Atlético striker headlines a frantic Spanish window.

Barcelona have confirmed the signing of Borussia Dortmund winger Karim Adeyemi — €22m plus €7m in add-ons, with a medical booked for Wednesday, per Fabrizio Romano — and are pushing hard for a bigger prize: a reported £100m (€118m) bid for Atlético Madrid's Julián Álvarez, who is said to prefer the Blaugrana despite Atlético's reluctance to sell and Arsenal's earlier interest. Real Madrid, meanwhile, have their own business done (Marc Cucurella for £52m, Bernardo Silva on a free) and continue to admire Bayern's Michael Olise as a future Galáctico — though Bayern "insist they want to keep him," making that one a long shot for now.

United Close On Tielemans — But the Ederson Deal Collapses

A medical scare undoes one Old Trafford deal even as another lands.

Manchester United are hours from completing Youri Tielemans from Aston Villa after triggering his £35m release clause; the Belgian has arrived in Manchester for a five-year deal that Romano expects "completed within the next 24 hours," with Andrey Santos already confirmed. But not everything has gone to plan: United's move for Atalanta midfielder Ederson is off, with the club citing medical concerns even as Atalanta insist the player is fit. It leaves United reshaping a midfield that has already waved off Rasmus Højlund, whose £43.2m sale to Napoli is done.

England's Big Spenders and a €39m Marseille Sale

City break their record; Spurs spend past £250m; Greenwood moves on.

Manchester City completed a British-record-scale £116m move for Nottingham Forest's Elliot Anderson, while Tottenham, under new boss Roberto De Zerbi, have blown past £250m — Sandro Tonali (£92.5m), Mateus Fernandes (£85m) and Jan Paul van Hecke (£52m) — even as Djed Spence pushes for a Spurs exit. Liverpool confirmed £55m Rennes centre-back Jérémy Jacquet, and in France, Marseille banked €39m selling Mason Greenwood to Fenerbahçe, with PSG's pursuit of Monaco's Maghnes Akliouche the market's dominant French storyline. Casemiro, a free agent, has agreed to join Inter Miami after the World Cup.

Arsenal

Tzolis Lands for €40m — and Arsenal Eye a £130m Statement

The champions' window shifts up a gear, with a marquee winger secured and a bigger one in their sights.

A year on from the Gyökeres and Zubimendi window that ended a 22-year title wait, Arsenal are moving from a position of strength — and today they moved decisively. The Gunners have agreed a €40m deal for Club Brugge's Christos Tzolis, confirmed by David Ornstein, landing a Greek international fresh off a stunning campaign: 22 goals and 29 assists in all competitions, the Belgian Pro League title, and the Belgian Footballer of the Year award. He arrives as Leandro Trossard heads the other way — a "here we go" €18m (plus €2m add-ons) move to Beşiktaş, with the forward flying to Istanbul for his medical and a contract to 2030.

Tzolis, tellingly, may not even be the headline. Arsenal's primary winger target is Aston Villa's Morgan Rogers, valued at a barely-believable ~£130m — a pursuit that would redefine the club's spending. One door looks to be closing, though: Julián Álvarez, long an Arsenal dream, now appears bound for Barcelona, who have lodged a £100m bid the Argentine is said to favour. Sporting director Andrea Berta, per Ornstein, is finalising the club's next moves regardless.

There is churn at the other end of the squad, too. Arsenal are open to offers of around £20m for Gabriel Jesus, with AC Milan leading a queue that also features Flamengo, Juventus and Atlético, while midfielder Christian Nørgaard is on Crystal Palace's wishlist after a season of limited minutes. Academy goalkeeper Remi Lupinski, 17, has signed his first professional deal.

Off the pitch, Arteta is reported to be weighing a "strategic decision" over Martin Ødegaard's role. And the World Cup has stretched his squad: Declan Rice, Bukayo Saka and Morgan Rogers all featured for England, while William Saliba — nursing the back problem noted above — is a doubt for France's bronze-medal match. Around the Leagues

Premier League

The selling clubs are shaping this window as much as the buyers. Newcastle, having cashed in on Gordon and Tonali, reinvested in Bazoumana Touré (£40.25m), Sean Steur (£20.5m) and Ewen Jaouen (£18m). Everton rebuilt busily (Röhl, Hackney, Tyrique George); Brighton keep trading (Vušković in, van Hecke out); Bournemouth added Alex Jiménez and Álvaro Rodríguez; and Chelsea are fielding interest in Garnacho. Off the pitch, De Zerbi's arrival at Spurs is the defining managerial move, and pre-season tours are underway ahead of the mid-August kickoff.

La Liga. Barcelona (Adeyemi in, £100m in for Álvarez) and Real Madrid (Cucurella, Bernardo Silva) dominate, with Atlético fighting to keep Álvarez and the rest of Spain bracing for the fallout.

Bundesliga. Bayern added Brown and Saibari and are eyeing Juventus striker Dušan Vlahović, while insisting Olise stays; Dortmund cashed in on Adeyemi; Leverkusen sold Hincapié to Arsenal; and Niklas Süle retired at 30.

Serie A. Napoli's Højlund headlines; Inter pivoted to Strasbourg's Guela Doué after the Khalaili medical collapse; Juventus added Kessié (and must fend off Bayern for Vlahović); Roma tied down Dybala to 2027 and are chasing Garnacho on loan.

Ligue 1. Marseille banked €39m for Greenwood; Monaco, Lille and Rennes strengthened; PSG's pursuit of Akliouche looms over the market.

MLS. Inter Miami sit atop ESPN's power rankings with Messi in imperious form, Casemiro's arrival stiffening a Supporters' Shield charge.
