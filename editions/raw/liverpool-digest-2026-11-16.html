The Lead

Pochettino's United arrive at Anfield as Liverpool count the cost of the break

A new manager with three training days, a Liverpool squad scattered across Europe and South America, and a home record that needs fixing. Sunday's game is closer than the table suggests.

The international break was supposed to be a pause. For Liverpool it has been a fortnight of anxious phone calls. Andoni Iraola sent more than a dozen senior players away after the 3-1 win at Crystal Palace, and the reports coming back have been mixed. Now Manchester United arrive at Anfield on Sunday with a new manager and nothing to lose.

United sacked Michael Carrick on November 12 with the club 18th, and appointed Mauricio Pochettino the next day on a deal to the summer of 2029, Sky Sports reported. United paid about £7.4m to release him from the USA job. He completes the USA's November games first, which leaves him roughly three training days before Anfield.

Iraola saw the risk coming. "The last thing we need now is to lose players in the international games," he said after Palace, per Sports Mole. Within two days the Dutch federation announced that Van Dijk "did not return fit" from the weekend and would leave the squad, as relayed by EPL Index. No diagnosis has been published.

The injury picture

The list is long for a side fourth in the table. Szoboszlai went off at Selhurst Park after 69 minutes with what Iraola called a knee he had "extended a little bit". He then played 90 minutes for Hungary in Georgia with the knee heavily strapped and set up a goal. "During the warm-up I was close to saying that I wouldn't be able to play after all," he admitted, per the Evening Standard.

Araujo, on loan from Barcelona and Iraola's first-choice right-back, was struggling to walk at the final whistle of Uruguay's 2-1 win over Argentina in Buenos Aires, according to Uruguayan paper El Observador, as reported by Stretty News. Neither club nor country has commented. Gravenberch (heel) is day to day, and Frimpong (muscle) and Bradley (knee) have not played since the autumn.

The last thing we need now is to lose players in the international games.

Andoni Iraola after Crystal Palace 1-3 Liverpool

Where the game will be won

If Araujo misses out, the right side becomes the key battle. United's predicted left-back is Patrick Dorgu, whose defending The Independent flags as a concern, and Barcola and Muñoz both run at full-backs. Liverpool's counter-attack, which produced Wirtz's goal at Palace, should find space against a side that lacks a natural holding midfielder with Carlos Baleba out.

The opposite risk is dead balls. All three goals Liverpool conceded at Fenerbahce on November 4 came from set pieces, as this Digest reported last week, and Bruno Fernandes delivers almost everything for United. Pochettino's sides traditionally press high, but analysts expect him to keep Carrick's 4-2-3-1 at first, Sky Sports' analysis suggests.

Then there is Anfield itself. Liverpool have 13 away points, the most in the league, but only five from five home league games, with defeats to Manchester City and Arsenal. United won both meetings last season, including 2-1 at Anfield in October 2025. A new-manager bounce against a nervous home crowd is exactly the trap Iraola needs to avoid.

The stakes are clear. Liverpool sit nine points behind Arsenal, four behind Brighton and three behind City. A win keeps them in touch with the top three before trips to Club Brugge and Everton; a defeat to the side 18th would turn a patchy autumn into a crisis of home form.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Manchester United: a new voice, the same leaky defence

Pochettino inherits a side that concedes early and often, but one with match-winners in Fernandes, Mbeumo and Cunha.

Manchester United are 18th on 8 points from 10 games (W1 D5 L4), their lowest total at this stage of a league season in 40 years, AFP reported. They have conceded 30 goals in all competitions and kept no Premier League clean sheet. Their last five: a 4-4 home draw with Aston Villa, a 3-2 win over Roma, and defeats to Chelsea (0-2), Bournemouth (0-3) and Como (1-4).

Mauricio Pochettino has mostly used a 4-2-3-1 at Southampton, Spurs and Chelsea, and a 4-3-3 at PSG and with the USA. With so little time, The Independent expects continuity: Lammens; Dalot, Yoro, Maguire, Dorgu; Tielemans, Mainoo; Diallo, Fernandes, Cunha; Mbeumo.

Three to stop, and where to hurt them

Bryan Mbeumo is United's top league scorer with four goals. Bruno Fernandes scored a penalty against Villa and takes almost every set piece. Matheus Cunha scored twice against Roma before being sent off, a red card that should only carry a European ban.

The weaknesses are obvious. Villa scored twice inside four minutes at Old Trafford, and Opta Analyst called United's starts to both halves "dreadful". They are 19th in the league for distance covered. Carlos Baleba (hamstring) is out, Harry Maguire is recovering from back spasms, and Luke Shaw, Benjamin Sesko and Marcus Rashford are injured.

Transfer News

Lamine Camara: Liverpool's January midfield target

The Monaco midfielder tops the shortlist. Claims of a done deal are thin, and Monaco's price is high.

Liverpool's clearest January target is Lamine Camara, Monaco's 22-year-old Senegal midfielder, under contract to 2029. Sky Germany's Florian Plettenberg named him and Bournemouth's Alex Scott as the two priorities on a three-man shortlist in late October, with Dortmund's Felix Nmecha "not a priority".

This week the noise grew. The Daily Mail's Lewis Steele said Chelsea sources believe Liverpool "may have a deal in place" with the player, via FourFourTwo; that is single-sourced and second-hand. The Athletic's James Pearce, speaking to The Redmen TV, raised a January move along the lines of the Luis Díaz deal, TEAMtalk reported.

Monaco are reported to want about €70m (£58m), and French outlet Topmercato says they are not inclined to sell mid-season. Camara almost joined Chelsea on deadline day. Status: concrete interest; personal terms reported but unconfirmed; no bid made public.

No rush for a centre-back, and no talks yet with Van Dijk

Pearce says the position is not a priority; Ornstein expects no clarity on the captain's future until spring.

Pearce reported on November 13 that centre-back is "not a priority" for January or next summer, via Yahoo Sports. The reasons: Jérémy Jacquet's impact, Giovanni Leoni's return from a cruciate ligament injury, and young options including Ifeanyi Ndukwe, who can be recalled from Levante. That effectively ends talk of Brighton's Luka Vuskovic for now.

It also frames the captain's future. Van Dijk's contract ends in June 2027, and The Athletic's David Ornstein said in a reader Q&A: "I wouldn't expect any clarity... until maybe the spring," as relayed by Yahoo Sports. A decision on Araujo's €55m purchase option is also expected in 2027.

Contract watch: Mac Allister, Nyoni and Alisson

Three negotiations that will shape Iraola's second season.

Alexis Mac Allister's father and agent, Carlos, told ESPN: "We haven't started renewal talks yet; if everything goes well, he'll renew... there may be news before the end of the year," per SoccerNews. The midfielder's deal runs to 2028. Real Madrid talk comes from a Madrid fan outlet and is rumour only.

Trey Nyoni's new long-term deal is in advanced talks and could be finalised during this break, GiveMeSport reported; nothing is official. Alisson, out of contract next summer, has said "my will is to stay".

The Squad

Iraola's selection puzzle: who plays right-back, and who holds midfield?

Fourteen league goals, seven of them Isak's, and a back line that depends on fitness news from three countries.

At Palace, Liverpool lined up as a back four with Araujo at right-back, Jacquet and Van Dijk in the middle and Kerkez on the left, Szoboszlai and Mac Allister in midfield, and Gakpo, Wirtz and Barcola behind Alexander Isak. If Araujo and Van Dijk are both missing, Iraola's options at the back thin out quickly: Joe Gomez came off the bench at Selhurst and Leoni is not yet back.

Midfield is the other worry. With Gravenberch's heel unresolved and Szoboszlai's knee strapped, 19-year-old Trey Nyoni is the obvious deputy beside Mac Allister. Iraola has trusted him: Nyoni started against Arsenal and scored in Istanbul.

Isak carries the scoring

Isak has seven of Liverpool's 14 league goals, half the total. Víctor Muñoz has two; Gakpo, Mac Allister, Szoboszlai and Wirtz one each, and Brighton's Pascal Gross scored an own goal, per LFC Click's season stats. Barcola, Gakpo and Wirtz have three league assists each.

That reliance is a strength while Isak is fit. He played 81 minutes of Sweden's 3-2 win over Bosnia on Saturday, so he returns sharp. Hugo Ekitike, his natural rival for the shirt, is still recovering from a ruptured Achilles, with a return reported for around Boxing Day.

Kirkby

A heavy night in Huddersfield, a big one for Josh Abe

The U21s' EFL Trophy run ended with a 4-0 defeat, while the U18s and England's youth sides kept the academy's week bright.

Liverpool's U21s lost 4-0 at Huddersfield Town in the EFL Trophy on Tuesday, a result that ended a group campaign already beyond saving. Joe Taylor scored twice in the first half hour, the club's report noted. Rob Page's side are sixth in Premier League 2 with 11 points from six games and next play Birmingham at Kirkby on Saturday November 28.

The U18s beat Salford City 6-1 in a friendly on Friday, Charlie Huth scoring twice, per liverpoolfc.com. They visit Stoke on Saturday morning. The Youth League side sit ninth of 36 after an unbeaten start and go to Club Brugge on November 25.

The best academy story came on international duty. Forward Josh Abe came off the bench and scored the winner in England U17s' 2-0 win over Poland, and Will Wright, scorer of a Youth League hat-trick in Istanbul, came on at half-time in England U19s' 5-0 win over Belarus, the club reported.

Loan watch

Keyrol Figueroa is flying at Port Vale: four goals and an assist in seven games, including a goal and an assist in the 4-2 FA Cup win over Scunthorpe, liverpoolfc.com reported. Harvey Elliott scored his first Valencia goal, a free-kick, in a 6-0 Copa del Rey win, per the club.

From The 4th Official's loan round-up: Calum Scanlon's cross won the penalty in Cardiff's comeback win over Burnley; Calvin Ramsay hit the woodwork for St Mirren against Rangers; Ármin Pécsi kept a clean sheet in Hartberg's 3-0 win over Altach; and Ifeanyi Ndukwe impressed for a half against Celta Vigo.

Liverpool FC Women

Olsson's double ends the WSL drought at Brighton

Gareth Taylor's side won 3-0 at the Amex for their first league win since September, after beating Aston Villa 4-1 in the cup.

Liverpool FC Women won 3-0 at Brighton on Saturday. Beata Olsson scored after four minutes, Cornelia Kapocs added a second early in the second half and Olsson finished it on 78, the club reported. It was a clean sheet and the first league win since beating Everton on September 27.

Midweek, they came from behind to beat Aston Villa 4-1 in the Subway Players Cup at St Helens, with Höbinger, Kapocs and Olsson twice on the scoresheet, per liverpoolfc.com. That makes five goals in three games for Olsson, counting her equaliser against Arsenal at Anfield on November 8.

"She is the only No.9 we have at the moment," head coach Gareth Taylor said, per the club, also praising defenders Jenna Clark and Grace Fisk. Liverpool now have 11 points from 10 WSL games (W3 D2 L5). Mia Enderby's X-ray after the Villa game was clear, and Ceri Holland is close to a return, the club said.

Next: Nottingham Forest away in the Players Cup on Wednesday (7pm UK, 11am Pacific), then Chelsea at St Helens on Saturday at 12:45pm UK (4:45am Pacific), live on Sky Sports in the UK.
