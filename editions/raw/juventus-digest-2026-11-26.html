The Lead

Serie A · Matchday 13 preview

Como, a striker debate and the Sunday that tells us what this Juve really is

After a 4-0 second-half demolition of Venezia, Spalletti's sixth-placed side head to the Sinigaglia five points behind Fabregas's Como, with the centre-forward question louder than ever.

For forty-five minutes on Sunday, Juventus looked like the side that had won one of four league games before the Napoli match: slow, stiff, predictable. Then Luciano Spalletti sent on Nick Woltemade and Pape Matar Sarr at half-time, and Venezia were buried by four goals in 18 minutes (Football Italia, beIN Sports).

Woltemade pounced on a deflected McKennie shot at 53'. Koopmeiners curled in the second at 59' after Woltemade's lay-off. Cabal headed in the third from a short-corner routine at 65', and Kolo Muani converted a penalty at 71' after Woltemade, ball in hand, gave him the kick. It was Spalletti's 600th Serie A match as a coach, according to beIN.

The win lifts Juve to sixth on 21 points from 12 games. That is solid rather than spectacular: 11 behind leaders Lazio, and five behind Como, the opponents on Sunday evening. In Europe the picture is brighter. Juve are fifth in the Europa League table on 10 points from four games, unbeaten, before tonight's visit of Omonia.

Why Como is the measuring stick

Cesc Fabregas's side are fifth on 26 points, with eight wins and two defeats from 12, and the second-meanest defence in the league (10 conceded, behind only Lazio's nine). LiveScore notes they are unbeaten in five home games this season, with four wins and a single goal conceded at the Sinigaglia (LiveScore).

They also won both league meetings 2-0 last season, per the same preview. Their one complication is the calendar: Como played AEK Athens in their first Champions League campaign on Wednesday, four days before Juve arrive, and are without Moise Kean, Jesús Rodríguez and Jayden Addai (DAZN). Trevoh Chalobah is back.

“We did well in terms of our mental strength, personality and character.”
Luciano Spalletti after Juventus 4-0 Venezia

The key battles

The first is in midfield. Without Manuel Locatelli and Khéphren Thuram, both long-term absentees, Juve lean on Koopmeiners, Douglas Luiz and McKennie. Douglas Luiz was hooked at half-time on Sunday. The second-half fix was energy: Sarr's bite, and a team that pressed "at the right moments", in Spalletti's words, which turned a 0.09 first-half xG into four goals.

The second is between Bremer and whoever leads Como's line. Bremer has been Juve's most reliable defender and a set-piece threat: he headed the equaliser at Fiorentina on November 8 (VAVEL). Set pieces are a genuine weapon. Cabal's goal on Sunday came from a corner routine, and he hit the woodwork from a corner against Napoli.

The third is the most interesting. Woltemade, a natural connector rather than a pure finisher, has changed the shape of the attack. Spalletti said he "needs to pick his own position and is good at linking up with his teammates" (club press room). Whether he starts at Como, after coming off the bench on Sunday, is the selection call of the week.

The striker debate

It hangs over everything. Dušan Vlahović left on a free in the summer, and the questions have not stopped. "How many times have you asked me about Vlahovic?" Spalletti snapped on Wednesday, insisting he had "spoke[n] about the need for certain characteristics, not a certain player" and adding: "That was not a desperate cry" (Football Italia, GOAL).

His solution for now is internal. He wants to try Kolo Muani through the middle, says Woltemade "can't play all the time", and confirmed that Jeff Ekhator, born in 2006, starts at centre-forward against Omonia "because we want an extra solution moving forwards". The January shortlist is covered in Transfer News below.

What is at stake

The arithmetic is simple. A win at Como would cut the gap to two points and drag Juve back into the conversation for the Champions League places. A defeat would leave them eight behind Como and, depending on Milan's Monday trip to Cagliari, potentially further adrift of fourth. Spalletti's heavy rotation for Omonia, with Neto expected in goal, tells you which game he rates higher.

Secondary feature: the loanee with no option

Woltemade's numbers tell the story of Juve's autumn. Football Italia counts five goals and four assists in 16 games, better output than any Newcastle attacker this season, and a goal or assist in all four Europa League appearances (Football Italia).

The catch is the paperwork. Football Italia reports that the loan from Newcastle carries no option to make the move permanent. If he keeps this up, the club that most needs a reliable attacker may have to negotiate from scratch next summer, which is exactly the kind of deal the club's UEFA settlement agreement makes awkward.

Transfer News

January window

The hunt for a No. 9 starts with Sørloth and Varela

Juve's shortlist is long; what they can afford, under a UEFA settlement agreement, is much shorter.

Alexander Sørloth is the name at the top of the list, according to Tuttosport's Lorenzo Aprile. The Atlético Madrid striker is what Spalletti has been describing all autumn: a finisher. Juve want a loan with an option to buy, because the outright price is "high and impractical". Stuttgart are also interested (Tuttosport, November 21).

There is a link worth watching: Jonathan David, on loan from Juve at Atlético, has three goals and two assists in 12 games, and Corriere dello Sport has floated a January swap involving Sørloth (All Football). That remains speculation.

Gustavo Varela is the hotter property. The Monza forward, born in 2005, has 11 league goals. Calciomercato.com reports that Juve have made contact, Inter lead the race and Napoli have met his agents several times (GOAL). Fabrizio Romano says an offer of €30 to 35 million could get a deal done in January.

Gianluca Di Marzio adds the catch: Juve and Napoli are "unlikely to be able to structure a deal involving the immediate payment". Benfica also hold a right to match any offer. Behind those two sit Raimondo, Scamacca and Balogun, all at the level of leads; Monaco would reportedly like Zhegrova in any Balogun deal.

Left flank

Udogie loan talk as Cambiaso's form dips

Tottenham are open to letting the Italy left-back go, and Spalletti likes his versatility.

Destiny Udogie has made five Premier League appearances this season and is out of the Tottenham coach's plans, Tuttosport reports. Juve are working on a loan with an option to buy, a six-month trial that would leave them free to return to Tottenham or pivot to free agents in the summer (Tuttosport).

Those free agents are already mapped: David Raum (Leipzig) and Tyrick Mitchell (Crystal Palace), both out of contract in June (Tuttosport, November 14). The urgency comes from within. Tuttosport describes Cambiaso as in poor form and says Cabal "does not offer sufficient guarantees" at left-back.

Outgoing

Ekhator's audition, and the sales Juve may need

A first start tonight could decide whether the teenager stays or goes on loan.

Jeff Ekhator, born in 2006 and signed from Genoa, makes his first start for Juve against Omonia. Genoa, Parma and Monza are evaluating a winter move, and Monza could fold him into a Varela package (Calcionews24). His future, the outlet writes, is decided by January.

The wider question is money. FootballTransfers cites "well-sourced reports" that Juve must sell in January to satisfy UEFA's rules, which remains unconfirmed (FootballTransfers). Corriere dello Sport warns that more than €70 million of summer loan buyouts are in doubt as several loanees lose form (All Football).

Scouting the Next Opponent

Europa League · Matchday 5 · Tonight

Omonia: old heads, low block, and a chance for Juve's kids

Berg's veterans have the oldest starting XI in the competition; Spalletti is expected to rotate heavily.

Omonia Nicosia arrive in Turin 18th in the league phase on six points, two wins and two defeats. One of the wins came against Celta Vigo, the side Juve beat 2-1 in October. Spalletti called their coach, the Norwegian Henning Berg, someone who "knows modern football very well" (Football Italia).

Juventus.com's preview notes a striking detail: Omonia have fielded the oldest average starting line-up in this Europa League (29 years and 347 days), with at least six players aged 30 or over in all four games (Juventus.com). Expect experience and compactness, not pace.

Shape and threats

Spalletti expects a 4-2-3-1 that drops into a back five out of possession. The man to watch is Loïs Diony, the French forward who leads their domestic scoring with six. Centre-back Lubomír Šatka has two league goals of his own, a reminder that Juve must defend set pieces with a rotated back line.

Football Italia's probable Omonia XI: Kaminski; Mayambela, Coulibaly, Šatka, Duverne; Andreou, Tănase, Marić; Ewandro, Diony, Montnor (LiveScore / Football Italia). Their form is patchy: a 3-1 defeat at NEC on November 5 and a 1-1 home draw with APOEL on November 21.

Where Juve can hurt them

A side built on experience can struggle with sustained width and runners in behind. Juve's probable 3-4-3, with Boga and Conceição either side of Ekhator, is designed to stretch exactly that. History is on Juve's side too: four wins from four against Omonia, and seven from seven against Cypriot opposition.

The Squad

Luciano Spalletti has spent the autumn managing absences. Yildiz has not played since the opening day; Spalletti said on Wednesday he has "started a specialised training session" and that "we have to wait for the right timing", calling him "a champion you can't simply just replace" (GOAL).

With captain Locatelli out, Bremer has worn the armband, and GOAL praised him for leading "by example" against Venezia (GOAL). Carnevali made a revealing point at the shareholders' meeting: on the UEFA list, Locatelli, as an Italian-trained player, could only be replaced by another, "so Ekhator, not Zhegrova".

Shape and selection

Spalletti has moved between a back three and a back four. Against Fiorentina and AZ he used a 3-4-2-1 (ESPN); against Venezia the line-up read as a four, with Celik, Bremer, Kelly and Cabal. For Omonia, Football Italia expects a 3-4-3: Neto; Gatti, Bremer, Lucumí; McKennie, Douglas Luiz, Koopmeiners, Cambiaso; Conceição, Ekhator, Boga.

Neto, 37, said he is "really excited" to play for the club again almost ten years after his last appearance (Football Italia via LiveScore). His likely start is a sign of how seriously Spalletti is protecting his first-choice XI for Como.

Form guide

Koopmeiners has found his range, scoring at AZ and against Venezia. Kolo Muani has goals in both of those games too and is now being asked to play as the central striker. Zhegrova set up the opener against Napoli off the bench and Cabal's goal against Venezia. Douglas Luiz is the concern: subbed at half-time on Sunday after a poor 45 minutes.

Next Gen

Massimo Brambilla's Juventus Next Gen have found some rhythm in Serie C Girone A. After a 4-0 thrashing at Lecco on November 8, they beat Treviso 3-0 on November 15 and won 3-1 at Giana Erminio on Saturday, November 21. They are 12th of 20 on 17 points from 15 games (4W 5D 6L) (Italian League, FotMob).

Arman Durmiši, 18, leads the scoring with four. Next up is Dolomiti Bellunesi at home on Sunday, November 29 (5:30 AM PT), then a trip to Union Brescia on December 6.

Graduates and loanees

The academy story of the week belongs to Jeff Ekhator, who starts at centre-forward against Omonia. Destiny Elimoghale, born in 2009, signed a new contract on November 3 running to June 2029. We could not confirm this week's Primavera results from a reliable source, so they are left out.

Juve's senior loanees are under scrutiny. Corriere dello Sport reports Jonathan David (Atlético) has three goals and two assists in 12 games; Fabio Miretti (Beşiktaş) missed five games with fitness and shoulder problems; Michele Di Gregorio (Bournemouth) is not a starter; João Mário has become a starter at Fiorentina; Vasilije Adžić scored against Juve for Sassuolo (All Football).

Juventus Women

Serie A Women · Matchday 8

Sarriegi hat-trick as Juventus Women hit Lazio for five

A 5-1 win in Formello leaves the Bianconere one point off the Champions League places.

Amaiur Sarriegi scored at 8', 12' and 72', Matilde Pavan added the third at 24' and Khelifi made it five at 83' in a 5-1 win at Lazio Women on Sunday, November 22; Eleonora Goldoni replied for Lazio at 26' (L Football, FIGC).

Isaac Guerrero's side are fourth on 15 points, one behind Fiorentina (16). Roma lead with eight wins from eight (24) ahead of Inter (19). Torino Top News notes the top three in Serie A qualify for next season's Women's Champions League (Torino Top News).

Europe

Four days earlier Juve beat OH Leuven 2-0 in Biella, their first home win of this season's Women's Champions League and their third league-phase victory, with Annamaria Serturini scoring her first goal (Juventus.com). Torino Top News says that result guaranteed at least a play-off place.

The league phase ends with a trip to Paris Saint-Germain on Wednesday, December 16 (12:00 PT) (Juventus.com). Serie A Women is on a two-week break for internationals and resumes with Juventus v Como Women on Saturday, December 12 (9:00 AM PT), per FIGC.

Players Away

There is no men's international window this week, so Juve's away-duty story is about the women and the loanees.

Eighteen Juventus Women players have been called up for the late-November and early-December window (Juventus.com). Italy have taken Lenzini, Rosucci, Salvai and Schatzer for Finland away on December 1 and Finland at home on December 5. Japan's Chiba faces Brazil on November 29 and December 5.

Spain's Bartel plays Chile on November 28 and Venezuela on December 4. Austria's Rusek (Switzerland, December 1), Portugal's Pinto (Iceland, December 1 and 5) and the Netherlands' De Jong (Slovenia, December 1 and 5) are also away. Noordman (Netherlands U23), Sylla and Graziani (France U23) and six Italy U19 players complete the list.

In European club football, the loanees Jonathan David (Atlético Madrid), Loïs Openda (Lyon), Fabio Miretti (Beşiktaş) and Michele Di Gregorio (Bournemouth) are all being watched with summer buyouts in mind; see Next Gen for their form.
