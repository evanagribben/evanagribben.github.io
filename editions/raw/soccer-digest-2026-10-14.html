The Lead

The champions of Europe walk into the one place nobody is losing

Paris Saint-Germain have won the last two Champions Leagues. Manchester City have won all eight games of Enzo Maresca’s reign. Wednesday night at the Etihad is the first evening this season that asks one of those sentences to give way.

There is a particular kind of fixture that the league phase was invented to produce, and this is it. Manchester City against Paris Saint-Germain, in the middle of October, with nothing eliminated and everything implied. The Etihad is sold out. The visitors are the holders of the European Cup, and the holders of the one before that. The hosts have not lost a match since the summer, under a manager who was appointed on the last Monday of June and has not yet found out what a bad week feels like.

Start with Enzo Maresca, because his start is the statistical story of the European autumn. Six Premier League games, six wins, sixteen goals scored and five conceded, eighteen points and a three-point cushion at the top. City’s own records department notes that this equals the best opening run by a new manager in Premier League history, matching Carlo Ancelotti at Chelsea in 2009/10 and Ole Gunnar Solskjaer’s caretaker spell at Manchester United in December 2018. Win at home to Ipswich Town on Saturday and it becomes a record outright. Add the Champions League and the domestic cup and the number is eight from eight, per the club.

The win that changed the temperature was Sunday’s. City went to Anfield and won 3-0, a scoreline that flatters nobody and settles nothing, because it arrived on the back of a red card. Liverpool were level and comfortable when Ryan Gravenberch collected a second yellow on 62 minutes; Erling Haaland scored two minutes and 37 seconds later, Jeremy Doku followed on 68 and Rayan Cherki finished it on 78. Jamie Carragher called the first booking “a shocking decision” and Gary Neville called the second “very soft”. Andoni Iraola, whose side had not lost all season, said the first foul “should never have been a yellow”. None of that changes the ladder. Liverpool are ninth and nine points back.

So City arrive in rude health. The interesting half of this fixture is the other one. PSG are not the team that beat Arsenal on penalties in Budapest at the end of May, because three of that night’s forwards have gone. Bradley Barcola is at Liverpool, and scored his first hat-trick for them in Austria on Wednesday afternoon. Randal Kolo Muani is at Juventus. Goncalo Ramos is at Milan, and Lee Kang-in is at Atletico Madrid. In their place Luis Enrique has Ferran Torres, bought from Barcelona for a reported fee around 41.5m pounds eleven days after he scored the goal that won Spain the World Cup, and Maghnes Akliouche from Monaco for around 43m, and Mika Godts from Ajax for around 39m.

That reconstruction has shown in the league. PSG are sixth in Ligue 1 on eleven points, three behind Monaco, having drawn their first two matches, lost at home to Monaco in early September and lost the Trophee des Champions to Lens in August. Three straight league wins have steadied it, the most recent a 3-1 over Le Mans in which Akliouche scored his first PSG goal and Desire Doue missed a late penalty. Luis Enrique called the afternoon “an almost perfect result” and then conceded the obvious qualifier: “We conceded a goal, and it was tough.”

“As a football fan, I like to watch City play.”

Luis Enrique, speaking before travelling to Manchester

In Europe, though, the machine still looks like the machine. PSG opened with a 6-1 dismantling of Slovan Bratislava in which Ousmane Dembele scored twice inside 22 minutes and Torres helped himself to a hat-trick. Dembele and Nuno Mendes were rested against Le Mans specifically to be available on Wednesday, and both trained through the week. Senny Mayulu is the absentee, though reports of the problem range from a thigh injury picked up with France’s under-21s to a dead leg, so treat the length of it as unsettled.

Tactically this is a collision of two high lines and two very fast front threes, which usually means it is decided in the five yards behind the full-backs. PSG’s great structural strength is the Vitinha and Joao Neves axis, which wins the second ball before anyone else arrives at it; their great structural risk is that Achraf Hakimi and Mendes push so high that the channels either side of Marquinhos and Willian Pacho become open country. City have spent six weeks showing they can run into exactly that. Doku and Cherki both scored at Anfield from positions nobody assigned them, and Haaland leads the Premier League scoring charts without having had a difficult afternoon yet.

There is also the matter that will not be in the team sheet. This is City’s first European night since an independent commission found the club guilty of almost all of the financial charges brought against it by the Premier League, covering nine seasons to 2017/18, including what the league’s own statement called “sham” sponsorship arrangements used to inflate revenue by more than 900m pounds. City lodged an appeal on 1 October, to be heard within twelve weeks. Sanctions have not yet been set. Maresca was asked about it in the international break and said simply: “I completely trust the club.” Asked whether past titles were tainted, he answered: “Absolutely not.”

And the last wrinkle is the sweetest. Pep Guardiola, who resigned in the summer after nine completed seasons, is expected in the stands. Maresca was asked whether that was awkward and declined the invitation: “He can come at any time he wants.” Nine years of City’s European identity were built by the man in the seat; the new one is unbeaten and has a European Cup holder to measure himself against. Kick-off was noon Pacific on the day this was written, which is to say that the edition and the football match were meant to reach the world at the same moment. Matchday three gives the loser no rest either: PSG host Barcelona next Tuesday, and City go again on the same night.

 

Mourinho goes back to Rome with his own October on the line

Four months into the Real Madrid job, Jose Mourinho returns to the club he managed for two and a half years, without Vinicius Junior and with four defenders in the treatment room.

The symmetry was always going to be too good to leave alone. Jose Mourinho was appointed Real Madrid manager in June on a three-year deal to 2029, his second spell at the club and his first since 2013. His first October in charge has handed him a trip to the Roma he managed from 2021 to 2024, and with whom he won the inaugural Conference League in 2022. He is still sung about in that stadium. He is also, four months in, being written about in Madrid in the way Madrid managers get written about.

The numbers behind the noise are not catastrophic and not comfortable. Madrid are fourth in La Liga on 18 points from eight, six wins and two defeats, beaten by Atletico Madrid and by Real Betis. Infobae noted this week that the tally is three points worse than Xabi Alonso’s side managed at the same stage, and framed a five-game October in fifteen days as the stretch that could shape Mourinho’s future. Saturday’s 1-0 over Villarreal was won by Diomande, the 140m euro summer signing, with his first Madrid goal, assisted by Fede Valverde. Kylian Mbappe hit a post, was twice denied, and was whistled by some of his own supporters.

The afternoon’s lasting damage was not the performance. Vinicius Junior, a substitute, had a goal disallowed and was then sent off after a VAR review showed him pulling the hair of Villarreal’s Nathan Saliba. On Wednesday the Spanish federation’s disciplinary committee handed down four matches: two for violent conduct away from the ball and two for protesting to the referee. Madrid’s claim of a “manifest material error” was rejected. He misses Sevilla on Sunday and, more to the point, he misses the Clasico at Barcelona on 25 October. His own public response was short: “The joy continues. My apologies to my team and the fans.”

Mourinho skipped his post-match press conference entirely after Villarreal, giving no reason, having himself been booked for protesting. That followed a September in which he produced a printed sheet of disputed refereeing decisions after the derby defeat to Atletico, prompting league president Javier Tebas to reject any suggestion of a conspiracy and Madrid to reply that Tebas is “not the owner of the competition”. Four defenders were unavailable for the flight to Rome: Ferland Mendy, Eder Militao, Rodrygo and Raul Asencio, whose prognosis the club lists as pending.

Roma, by contrast, have rarely looked more organised. Gian Piero Gasperini’s side are third in Serie A on 14 points with the best goal difference in the division and the best defence in it, three goals conceded in six games. They drew 1-1 at Fenerbahce on matchday one and held Como to the league’s first goalless draw on Sunday, a night that cost them Donyell Malen to the first red card of his career. Malen leads Serie A’s scoring charts with six and serves his domestic ban against Genoa on Saturday, which means he was free to play against Madrid. Mourinho, of all people, will have noted that.

Carvajal’s long wait ends in the unlikeliest place

A Champions League winner with Real Madrid, out of contract since June, has agreed terms with a club 14th in La Liga. The only remaining obstacle is a spreadsheet.

Dani Carvajal is 34 and has been a free agent since 30 June, when a Real Madrid contract that spanned six European Cups expired without renewal. This week it became clear where he is going, and it is not where anyone would have guessed: Getafe, fourteenth in La Liga on eight points, managed by Jose Bordalas, playing in a 17,000-seat stadium on the southern edge of Madrid.

The paper trail is unusually clean. Getafe’s interest was first reported by ABC. Marca reported on 6 October that an agreement had been reached, subject to La Liga approving the club’s squad-cost position. Getafe’s president Angel Torres confirmed as much on DAZN, saying Carvajal had passed a medical and that the move is a free transfer: “If I can reach an agreement with La Liga, he will most likely join us.” By 8 October the deal was still being described as at risk on exactly that point. Fabrizio Romano now reports the salary has been cleared and that only the official announcement remains.

Where it stands  Agreement reached, announcement pending. Reported by Fabrizio Romano, after Marca on 6 October and confirmation on camera from Getafe’s own president.

The lesson is less about Carvajal than about the mechanism. Getafe are operating under a La Liga squad-cost limit that is a rounding error next to the clubs Carvajal spent his career beating. Real Madrid’s 2026/27 limit was set at 832.78m euros, Barcelona’s at 582.76m, Atletico’s at 361.27m and Sevilla’s at 20.16m, the lowest in the division. In that world, a free transfer is not a bargain. It is a negotiation with a regulator.

 

Madrid quietly solve their biggest 2027 problem

Thibaut Courtois was on every list of players whose contracts expire next June. He will not be on the next one.

Thibaut Courtois has agreed a new Real Madrid contract to June 2028, according to Fabrizio Romano, who describes it as done with only the club’s statement outstanding. AS had reported on 13 September that the two sides were close to an extension on those exact terms. Madrid have not yet announced it.

Where it stands  Agreed, announcement pending. Romano, with AS reporting the same terms a month ago.

It matters more than a goalkeeper renewal usually does, because Madrid’s summer 2027 exposure is real. Antonio Rudiger and Brahim Diaz are also into the final year of their deals, and the club has already spent heavily at the other end of the pitch, including the 140m euros on Diomande. The one piece of long-term certainty they did buy themselves came in August, when Vinicius Junior signed through to June 2032, a deal that looks rather different in a week when he has been banned for four matches.

 

Kane leaves the door open, and takes a step back from England

Bayern’s captain says there have been no talks about a new contract. He also says the idea of going home has faded.

Harry Kane’s Bayern Munich contract runs to 2027, with a release clause reported at around 67m euros. Speaking to Reuters this month, he said he is open to extending beyond it, that no talks have taken place yet, and, most notably, that his desire to return to the Premier League has “decreased”.

Where it stands  No negotiation under way. The player is publicly receptive, and the release clause is reported rather than confirmed by the club.

The timing is interesting because Kane’s own form is not the issue and Bayern’s is. He became the fastest player to 100 Bundesliga goals, reaching the mark in 98 appearances in September’s 7-0 against Union Berlin. He has now scored in nine consecutive Champions League matches, including both of Bayern’s goals from open play and the penalty in Tuesday’s extraordinary 3-3 at Viking in Stavanger. He also provided both assists in the 2-2 at Augsburg on Saturday without scoring, after which he told DAZN it “feels more like a defeat”.

Bayern are third, eleven points from five, and have not won in four weeks. A club that has historically let contracts run until the player’s leverage has evaporated is dealing with a 33-year-old centre-forward who is the only reason several of those results were not worse. Expect this one to be settled quietly and in Bayern’s favour, and expect the Premier League clubs who have been waiting for the clause to become usable to read the word “decreased” several more times.

 

Manchester United keep answering a different question

The club insists Michael Carrick is safe. Everyone briefing about January is briefing about a left-back. Neither of those is the problem.

Six points from six league games is Manchester United’s worst start since 1986/87. On Tuesday they lost 2-0 at Atletico Madrid, conceding inside a minute to Pablo Barrios and finishing with ten men after Leny Yoro was dismissed. Fabrizio Romano reported on 13 October that the club is “still giving time and confidence” to Michael Carrick, that the players remain with him, and that the results are “not acceptable”. A day later he added that no last-minute change is expected and that United have not contacted any other manager.

Where it stands  Carrick is backed, for now. Two tier-one reporters arrive at the same answer from opposite directions, and no successor has been approached.

David Ornstein landed in the same place from the other direction: he knows of no desire to remove Carrick, but said the manager “needs to improve things” and pointed the rest of the blame at recruitment, executives and owners. He singled out Sunday’s trip to Leeds United as “massive”. Nicolo Schira has gone further, naming Leeds, Como and Bournemouth as the decisive run. Reports linking Eddie Howe and Fabian Hurzeler to the job trace back to unnamed accounts and should be treated as speculation. Antonio Conte is a different case: Romano says he would be open to a mid-season move to a historical club and would not want the Middle East, while explicitly refuting a claim that he was reaching an agreement with INEOS.

On the squad, Romano says January is “too early to say” and depends on conditions he did not specify, but that a left-back would not be “crazy money”. The name that keeps recurring is Adrien Truffert of Bournemouth, whom Ornstein valued at around 60m pounds last month while suggesting a deal was more likely in the summer of 2027. Bournemouth are expected to reject January offers. United wanted Newcastle United’s Lewis Hall in August; Hall has since signed a new contract there.

 

Milan and Inter spend the autumn protecting what they have

Two clubs with no money to spend in January are doing the next best thing, which is making sure nobody can take anything for free in June.

Inter have opened renewal talks with Federico Dimarco aimed at a contract to 2030, Gazzetta dello Sport reported on 12 October. The club holds a unilateral option to 2028, so there is no cliff edge, and no agreement has been reached.

At Milan, Gazzetta reported in late September that Adrien Rabiot is close to a renewal through 2030 with an option year. Nothing is official on either.

Where it stands  Talks opened at Inter, reported progress at Milan. Neither club has announced anything, and both reports originate with Gazzetta dello Sport.

Milan’s more pressing problem is structural. Matteo Gabbia tore a left hamstring with partial tendon involvement at Sassuolo on Sunday and is out for months, with a specialist deciding between surgery and conservative treatment. A club that drew 1-1 that afternoon and sits fifth now has a January window in which a centre-back is close to compulsory. Links to specific names have circulated without attribution and are not worth printing yet.

Juventus, meanwhile, are reported by Tuttosport to be considering a striker in January to support Randal Kolo Muani, whose 81st-minute header at Cagliari rescued a point and was his first Serie A goal of the season. They have the balance sheet to think about it: the board approved accounts carrying a forecast 66m euro loss on 29 September and proposed a delegation for a capital increase of up to 250m euros, with Exor confirming an advance.

 

The Rumour Mill

Everything else that is moving, and how much of it is real

Everything simmering, with the reliability stated plainly. Several of this week’s loudest stories are the thinnest.

Advanced, or close to it

Marc Bernal’s Barcelona extension, from 2029 to 2031 with a 500m euro clause, was reported by El Gol Digital on 12 October as closed but unsigned. One non-tier-one source, no club confirmation, and worth watching rather than believing. Barcelona’s last official announcement on the midfielder was a renewal to 2029 a year ago. Certainty: single-sourced report of an agreement.

Concrete interest, with a club saying no

Alex Scott is the window’s clearest example of a selling club getting its answer in first. Bournemouth have told Chelsea and Real Madrid that the midfielder is not leaving in January, per talkSPORT as relayed on 7 October. Chelsea bid more than 60m pounds in the summer and were refused. Reports of a nine-figure valuation are the writers’ estimates rather than anyone’s quoted number, and the two best accounts of his contract length contradict each other, one saying a single year remains and one saying 2028. Certainty: concrete interest, no sale sanctioned.

Rumour, and treat it that way

A 70m euro January bid from Liverpool for Barcelona’s Jules Kounde was reported on 14 October by TheHardTackle, attributing it to the Spanish site Fichajes, with the admission that no formal talks have happened. That is a low-reliability chain and nothing more. The same applies to Pedro Porro to Real Madrid and to the various claims about Florian Wirtz’s future, both of which currently live on aggregator sites rather than with any reporter who would put a name to them.

The loudest of the lot deserves a flat warning. The widely circulated story that Erling Haaland will leave Manchester City for Barcelona in 2027, with a figure around 130m pounds attached, traces back to an unverified social-media account and has been repeated by outlets that did not check it. There is no statement from either club or from the player, and Spanish reporting indicates there is no relegation release clause of the kind the story depends on. Certainty: noise. It is included here only because readers will have seen it everywhere else.

Stated needs, which is where real deals start

Paris Saint-Germain were reported by L’Equipe in early September to want two things in January: a right-footed centre-back to understudy Marquinhos, and an attacker. Juventus want a striker. Milan need a centre-back. Manchester United want a left-back. Those four sentences are more predictive of what actually happens on 1 January than any name currently trending, and three of the four needs were created by injuries or exits that happened in the last six weeks.

The 2027 Bosman class

The list of players whose contracts are reported to expire next summer is, as ever, the quietest and most consequential document in football. It currently includes Bruno Fernandes, Lisandro Martinez and Luke Shaw at Manchester United; Alisson and Virgil van Dijk at Liverpool; Mateo Kovacic at Manchester City; Richarlison at Tottenham; Kane at Bayern Munich; Rudiger and Brahim Diaz at Real Madrid; Koke at Atletico Madrid; Hakan Calhanoglu at Inter; Christian Pulisic and Fikayo Tomori at Milan; Kevin De Bruyne, Frank Anguissa and Stanislav Lobotka at Napoli; Paulo Dybala and Lorenzo Pellegrini at Roma; and Marcel Sabitzer at Borussia Dortmund.

The compilation is a secondary aggregator’s rather than any club’s, and renewal status varies from name to name. Courtois is the obvious one already resolved.

The managers’ market

Venezia sacked Giovanni Stroppa on Tuesday after six defeats in six, five goals scored and sixteen conceded. Gianluca Di Marzio reported on Wednesday that Davide Nicola has agreed a deal to 2028, and Cremonese confirmed he had terminated his contract there by mutual consent. Certainty: agreed, Venezia announcement pending. In MLS, LAFC dismissed Marc Dos Santos on 27 September and appointed Xavi Tamarit as interim. Borussia Monchengladbach are still winless under Alexander Blessin, who arrived on 22 September.

 

Around the Leagues

Premier League

Leaders: Manchester City, 18 points from 6. Most goals: Brighton. Most goal involvements: Joao Pedro, 8.

One team has turned the Premier League into an arithmetic problem. Manchester City have won all six, and the gap at the top is three points over Arsenal with Brighton a further two back. The chasing has been done in near silence: Enzo Maresca inherited a squad that was supposed to need rebuilding and instead has 16 goals and five conceded, and the 3-0 at Anfield on Sunday was his first genuinely hostile away afternoon. Joao Pedro of Chelsea leads the division for goal involvements with eight, five goals and three assists, per BBC Sport, which tells you how open the rest of it is.

Behind City, the most convincing football is being played in two places nobody is shouting about. Brighton are third, are the division’s top scorers, have won three successive league games without conceding and have had ten different scorers; Fabian Hurzeler told Sky “it is not about having one good goalscorer”, which is both a boast and a recruitment strategy. They won 2-0 at Sunderland with ten men after Pascal Struijk was dismissed on a VAR review, with Maxim De Cuyper and a debutant, Rayan Hadjam, scoring.

Chelsea answered a 3-0 defeat at Brentford with a 5-1 against Bournemouth in which Jordan Henderson scored after 65 seconds and again from 25 yards, Joao Pedro scored twice, and Xabi Alonso switched to a back three after conceding a league-high twelve goals in his first five games. Brentford and Everton are both still unbeaten.

The bottom half is where the season is actually happening. Manchester United drew 1-1 at home to Tottenham despite Rodrigo Bentancur being sent off for a headbutting motion at Luke Shaw, a dismissal that triggered a touchline brawl and still was not enough; Dominic Solanke equalised in stoppage time for a Spurs side who have not won a league match since May despite spending over 300m pounds in the summer, and whose manager Roberto De Zerbi said last month he had “never seen a team like us, so fragile and inconsistent”.

Fulham are bottom on two points, Alvaro Arbeloa insisting “I don’t feel fear” after a 2-1 loss at Ipswich Town built on two goals in the last three minutes.

Coventry City beat Newcastle United 4-1 on Monday for their first top-flight home win in more than 25 years, Jayden Mfuni becoming their youngest Premier League scorer, after which Matthias Jaissle apologised to the travelling support and said conceding eight in two away Mondays “is not good enough for the Premier League”. And at Crystal Palace, Oliver Glasner returned with Nottingham Forest, led after three minutes, and lost 3-1.

In the Women’s Super League, Manchester City lead on 15 points from Chelsea on 13 and London City Lionesses on 12, the Lionesses having won 6-1 at Tottenham with Alexia Putellas and Kadidiatou Diani scoring twice each.

La Liga

Leaders: Barcelona, 24 points from 8, still perfect. Pichichi: Raphinha, 12.

Eight matches, eight wins, 34 goals. Barcelona have not been this ruthless this early in a very long time, and Saturday’s 3-0 over Getafe was their 23rd consecutive home league win. Anthony Gordon scored his first Barcelona goal inside two minutes, Gabriel Jesus and Jules Kounde added the others, and Hansi Flick used the afternoon to make a point about workload, praising the squad and saying Lamine Yamal’s minutes must be managed. They did it without Raphinha, who leads the Pichichi race with twelve and is nursing a hamstring edema picked up with Brazil. Flick’s line of the week, relayed from AS: “We want trophies, not records.”

Everything else in Spain this week was chaos of one kind or another. Rayo Vallecano’s first home match of the season, against Athletic Club, was abandoned after supporters protesting against president Raul Martin Presa threw paper onto the pitch; the referee restarted it once, stopped it again and told both coaches it could not continue. No official resumption date has been set, Spanish reporting points towards early November behind closed doors, and the regulations do not simply award Athletic the points. Athletic, who also had a September fixture at Levante postponed by a flooded pitch, have two games in hand.

Elsewhere Levante drew 3-3 with Sevilla on Monday in a match that produced three goals after the 90th minute and a red card for Isaac Romero for his celebration; Real Betis beat Osasuna 5-2 with two from Isco to go third on 19 points; and Javier Aguirre began at Valencia with a 2-2 at Racing Santander in which the 18-year-old Otorbi scored his first senior goal.

Bundesliga

Leaders: Freiburg and Dortmund, 13 points from 5. Top scorer: Schick, 5. Record pace: 172 goals in five matchdays.

Germany is in the middle of the highest-scoring start in its history: 172 goals in the first five matchdays, beating the 170 of 1984/85, at 3.82 a game. On Saturday it produced a record that may never be beaten. Augsburg’s Robin Fellhauer scored after 8.4 seconds, the fastest goal in Bundesliga history, when Manuel Neuer chipped the kick-off straight to him. Bayern Munich drew 2-2, Harry Kane assisting both goals without scoring, and Vincent Kompany defended his goalkeeper on the grounds that Neuer “has played football long enough”.

The table has rearranged itself around that. SC Freiburg are top, level on 13 points with Borussia Dortmund and ahead on goal difference, after a 2-1 over Schalke 04 won with ten men, Ritsu Doan’s compatriot Goto heading in a Vincenzo Grifo cross on 85. Dortmund dropped their first points of the season in the strangest fashion, leading Werder Bremen 2-0 with two minutes left and drawing 2-2, all four goals arriving in the final ten minutes and Niclas Fullkrug scoring against his former club. Bayern are third on eleven and have not won in four weeks.

RB Leipzig beat Eintracht Frankfurt 6-0, Christopher Nkunku scoring the first pure hat-trick by a Leipzig player in the Bundesliga and reaching 50 league goals with the second, and Patrik Schick leads the scoring charts with five in five for Bayer Leverkusen.

Two sides are in open crisis. Frankfurt have conceded sixteen in five, and Adi Hutter substituted his captain Robin Koch at half-time of the Leipzig game, saying he expects more from a captain and that a signal was needed; Koch told Kicker that running away “is not an option” and will start and captain the side against Koln on Friday. Borussia Monchengladbach are bottom with no points from five, with six players out including captain Tim Kleindienst, Ko Itakura and Nicolas Kuhn.

Bayern have a European problem of their own making. Michael Olise was sent off in Stavanger for a second booking after throwing the ball into the face of Viking captain Zlatko Tripic. Kompany was blunt. He called it “uberblod”, said the dismissal was “100 per cent his mistake”, and noted that Tripic had been the smarter man. Olise misses the home game against Arsenal on 21 October.

Serie A

Leaders: Inter and Lazio, 16 points from 6. Best defence: Roma, 3 conceded. Capocannoniere: Malen, 6.

Serie A has produced the most unlikely leader in Europe, and it is not even top on its own. Lazio are level with Inter on 16 points after five wins from six, the best start of the three-points era for the club, with the division’s second-best defence. Gennaro Gattuso beat Monza 3-1 on Sunday with goals from Nuno Tavares, who had not scored in three years, Gustav Isaksen and Andrea Pinamonti. Roma are third with three goals conceded in six games. Cagliari are fourth. Juventus are sixth and Napoli, the reigning point of reference, eighth.

Inter are the story, because Inter are two clubs at once. At home in Serie A they beat Parma 3-0 with Hakan Calhanoglu back from injury. In Europe they have no points and no answers: beaten 2-1 at Real Madrid on matchday one and then 4-3 at the San Siro by Club Brugge, who were given a hat-trick by Nicolo Tresoldi despite Inter having 31 shots. They have now lost seven of their last eight Champions League matches. Cristian Chivu took it on himself, telling Sky Sport Italia that two big chances went begging, “so a little frustration set in, we got frenetic, lost our balance”, and adding: “I take responsibility.” Gazzetta’s verdict was six words long: dominant in Italy, disastrous in Europe.

Napoli are in the same hole and are being louder about it. Beaten 3-1 at Villarreal after Amir Rrahmani had put them ahead, they also have nothing from two matchdays, and Massimiliano Allegri used the aftermath to deliver the week’s most Allegri sentence: in Italy, knowing how to defend “has become almost a disgrace”. He also warned that if his players travel to Venezia on Saturday without humility, “we will come out with broken bones”.

Venezia, for their part, sacked Giovanni Stroppa on Tuesday. Como lost their first ever Champions League match on Wednesday, 3-2 at Feyenoord, having led through Assane Diao, who then limped off on 23 minutes.

The week’s administrative news was substantial. The Milan administrative court rejected five challenges to the sale of San Siro to Inter and Milan, clearing the way for the new stadium, while Serie A’s twenty clubs meet in Milan on Friday to consider a JPMorgan plan to create a media company and lift international television income from around 250m euros towards 500m.

Two dismissals defined the weekend’s temper. At Cagliari, where Randal Kolo Muani rescued a 1-1 for Juventus, referee Marcenaro sent off both managers, Luciano Spalletti and Fabio Pisacane, after a post-match confrontation. Both were banned for a match, Spalletti missing Sunday’s visit of Lazio. Donyell Malen, Serie A’s leading scorer on six, serves a one-game ban for the red card he collected at Como.

Ligue 1

Leaders: Monaco, 14 points from 6, unbeaten. Top scorers: nine players on 4.

France has the most genuinely open table of the six, and the club at the top of it is the one that sold its best young attacker to the champions. Monaco are unbeaten on 14 points under Filipe Luis, the former Brazil and Atletico left-back appointed in July, having drawn 2-2 at home with Toulouse after leading 2-0 at half-time. Lille are second on 13 under Davide Ancelotti, Rennes third on the same points, Lyon fourth, Paris FC fifth and Paris Saint-Germain sixth.

Nine players share the top of the scoring charts on four goals apiece, among them Ferran Torres, Esteban Lepaul and Amine Gouiri.

The week’s real subject, though, was tackling, and the Ligue 1 clubs are no longer being polite about it. Lyon lost Lois Openda, on loan from Juventus, to a fractured fibula in Friday’s 2-1 defeat at Lens, and issued a statement criticising referee Jeremy Stinat for the number of fouls he left unpunished. Openda is out until at least the November international window.

Two days earlier Lille had gone public over Tiago Santos, whose knee was caught by Le Havre’s Amir Richardson in a tackle that drew no card from referee Bastien Dechepy and no VAR intervention. Santos has a complete cruciate rupture and a meniscal lesion and will be out more than six months. Lille’s statement noted that “no sanction was pronounced, not even a warning”. Le Havre replied that both referee and VAR judged no sanction necessary and that a retroactive punishment would fall outside the rules.

The rest: Marseille ended a run of five consecutive defeats in all competitions by winning 2-0 at Troyes through Amine Harit and a 98th-minute penalty from Himad Abdelli, which lifts Bruno Genesio’s side to thirteenth and no higher. Lens beat Lyon for Yannick Cahuzac’s first win since he replaced Dino Toppmoller, who was dismissed in September after six matches. And Lille settled 26.6m euros of stadium-related debt, which in the current French financial climate counts as a result.

Major League Soccer

Leaders: Nashville SC, 63 points. Golden Boot: Messi, 23 goals. Decision Day: 7 November.

The American season is five matchdays from its end and has one runaway and one record in play. Nashville SC lead the overall table on 63 points from 28 games, thirteen clear in the East, at a club-record 2.22 points a game; the league’s single-season record is the 74 Inter Miami posted in 2024, and as of the start of October Nashville needed fifteen points from their last seven to beat it. They won 4-1 at Austin FC on Saturday. Vancouver Whitecaps lead the West, and are the only side so far confirmed through.

Decision Day is 7 November. Nine clubs qualify from each conference, the eighth and ninth seeds meeting in a single-game Wild Card on 18 November, with a best-of-three first round from 20 November and MLS Cup on Friday 18 December.

Lionel Messi leads the Golden Boot with 23 goals and 13 assists, four clear of FC Dallas’s Petar Musa on 19, with Rafael Navarro, Milan Iloski and Brian White on 17.

Four days after his international farewell, a 3-0 win over Benin in Buenos Aires in which he scored his 126th Argentina goal from the penalty spot and cried at the end of it, he scored twice in Miami’s 4-0 against DC United, with Luis Suarez also scoring twice. He is contracted at Miami through 2028.

Around that: Philadelphia Union beat Real Salt Lake 2-1 to extend a club-record unbeaten run to thirteen games, Iloski scoring his 17th. St. Louis City beat LA Galaxy 6-2 with a Magnus Holse hat-trick inside 21 minutes. LAFC, who dismissed Marc Dos Santos on 27 September and are being run on an interim basis by Xavi Tamarit, drew 1-1 with Vancouver despite the visitors losing a man in the seventh minute; Son Heung-min has 13 assists and six goals for them this season and Hugo Lloris has twelve clean sheets.

And the structural news that will outlast all of it: this is the last full season of the spring-to-autumn calendar, with the league planning a shortened 14-match transitional season in early 2027 before moving to a summer-to-spring schedule.

In Brief

Luis Suarez’s goal at Lens was his seventh in the Champions League and made him Sporting CP’s outright leading scorer in the modern competition. Sporting won 2-0 and have a maximum six points.

Sabah of Azerbaijan won their first ever league-phase Champions League match, 2-0 against Slavia Praha, through Kaheem Parris and a late Joy-Lance Mickels breakaway.

Brighton’s Pascal Gross became the oldest player to assist in three successive English top-flight games, at 35 years and 117 days.

Venezia sacked Giovanni Stroppa after six defeats in six. Davide Nicola has agreed terms to 2028, with Cremonese confirming he terminated his contract there by mutual consent.

Rayo Vallecano’s first home fixture of the season was abandoned amid supporter protests against the club president. The resumption date, venue and conditions are all still unresolved, and the rules do not award Athletic Club the points by default.

Liverpool went to Austria on Wednesday without Virgil van Dijk in the starting eleven and won 6-3, Bradley Barcola scoring his first Liverpool goals and Ronald Araujo and Lewis Koumas also opening their accounts.
