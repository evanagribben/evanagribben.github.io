The Lead

Zidane Answers France's Call

Fourteen years of Didier Deschamps end, and the one man France always wanted finally takes the seat. Zinedine Zidane inherits a beaten finalist-that-never-was, a generational talent in Kylian Mbappé, and a nation that expects nothing short of everything.

For the better part of a decade the question was never whether Zinedine Zidane would one day coach France, but when the door would finally swing open. On Tuesday it did. The Fédération Française de Football confirmed Zidane as national-team head coach on a four-year contract, closing the 14-year tenure of Didier Deschamps and installing the most decorated Frenchman in the game's modern memory as the custodian of its national side. "I turned down every offer because I only wanted this," Zidane said on his unveiling — a line that will echo through a qualifying cycle that begins almost immediately.

The timing is both poetic and pointed. France arrived at a home-continent World Cup as one of the favourites and left it fourth, undone by Spain in a semi-final that exposed the seams of an ageing spine, then beaten in a delirious 6-4 third-place shoot-out with England. Deschamps, a World Cup winner as both captain and coach, departs as the most successful manager in the country's history and, simultaneously, as a man whose methods had visibly run their course. He leaves Zidane a squad still blessed at the top end and thinning underneath.

"I turned down every offer because I only wanted this." — Zinedine Zidane, on taking the France job

The central relationship, inevitably, is with Kylian Mbappé. The captain has just won a second consecutive World Cup Golden Boot — ten goals across the tournament, a feat no player had managed at back-to-back editions — and now plays under the compatriot he grew up idolising. The two carry a shared Real Madrid inheritance and a shared appetite for control; how Zidane frames a team around Mbappé without becoming subservient to him is the first tactical riddle of his reign. The early hint is that he will not simply preserve Deschamps' cautious, counter-punching blueprint. Zidane's Madrid, for all its pragmatism in the biggest moments, was built on trusting elite players to improvise; a France side with that licence would look very different from the risk-averse machine of the past two cycles.

The in-tray is not empty. The defensive core that carried Deschamps to two finals is fraying, the midfield needs reimagining around the next generation, and the goalkeeping succession behind an ageing group is unresolved. Zidane also inherits the delicate diplomacy of managing egos he once shared a dressing room's worth of silverware alongside as a player-turned-icon. His managerial CV — three straight Champions Leagues at Madrid, a LaLiga title, an almost supernatural knack for winning knockout football — answers most doubts about the biggest nights. The open question is whether a man who has only ever coached at club level, and who walked away from Madrid twice citing fatigue with the grind, has the patience for the slow architecture of international management, where you see your players for a fortnight at a time.

He will not have long to acclimatise. France's autumn schedule opens with a UEFA Nations League assignment away to Turkey before a marquee home date with Italy in early October — the sort of fixture that will tell us quickly whether the Zidane effect is real or merely romantic. A World Cup cycle now resets toward 2030, and France remain, on paper, among the two or three most talented nations on earth. The margin between that talent and another near-miss is precisely the space Zidane has been hired to close.

There is a broader story his appointment crowns. This has been the most turbulent post-tournament managerial market in living memory: a wave of national-team departures unusually large even by World Cup standards, with federations from the Balkans to South America changing course at once. Zidane-to-France is the marquee hire inside that churn, the appointment every other move will be measured against. For a federation that has spent years fielding polite questions about its succession plan, the answer, when it came, needed no explanation. France did not hire a coach this week. It hired an idea of itself.

Messi Won't Say Goodbye Just Yet — and the Bench Bosses Reshuffle

The tournament's final whistle triggered an emotional reckoning and a managerial gold rush at once.

The image of Lionel Messi, 39, sitting on the MetLife turf after defeat in what many assumed was his international farewell has given way to something gentler. Reports out of Argentina, relayed through beIN Sports, indicate Messi will not retire abruptly; instead the AFA is planning a dedicated farewell match on home soil, announced far enough in advance that a stadium can be filled to say goodbye, with manager Lionel Scaloni expected to keep the door open until then. It is, for now, reported rather than officially confirmed — but the sentiment is unmistakable: Argentina wants a send-off, not a shrug.

Around that human-interest thread, the coaching market has gone into overdrive. Zidane's move to France is the headline, but it sits inside a broader exodus of national-team managers — one of the largest post-tournament turnovers in memory. The reshuffle has bled into the club game too, where José Mourinho at Real Madrid, Massimiliano Allegri at Napoli and Rúben Amorim at AC Milan have all begun new eras. The pre-season, in other words, is being coached by a very different cast than the one that started last term.

Transfer News

Mourinho's Madrid Goes Shopping — and Lands a €100m Teenager

A new manager, a "win-now" mandate, and a headline capture that stunned the market: Yan Diomande, 18, from Leipzig.

The most eye-catching deal of the week belongs to Real Madrid. Fabrizio Romano declared his "here we go" on Yan Diomande, the 18-year-old Ivorian winger, with agreement reached with RB Leipzig for a fee reported over €100m (ESPN pitched it around €135m), a medical booked and a contract to June 2031. It is a staggering outlay on a teenager, and a statement of intent from a club that has handed the dugout to José Mourinho for a second spell with an explicit brief to build a team that wins now. Notably, PSG pulled out of the race, citing what they called disproportionate fee and salary demands.

Diomande lands atop a rebuild already well advanced. Real Madrid have brought in Marc Cucurella from Chelsea (~€55m), Denzel Dumfries from Inter (~€20m, officially confirmed), and secured Bernardo Silva and Ibrahima Konaté on free transfers from Manchester City and Liverpool respectively. The through-line is a squad being reshaped at pace and with real money, with Mourinho reportedly pulling strings on recruitment. The one domino still to fall — and the season's biggest tug-of-war — is in midfield.

The Álvarez Standoff: Barcelona Sets a Clock, Atlético Won't Blink

A ~€100m offer with an expiry date, a president who won't name the deadline, and a striker caught in the middle.

No saga better captures the window's brinkmanship than Julián Álvarez. Barcelona have a formal offer on the table — reported around €100m, structured — with an explicit shelf life. President Joan Laporta confirmed as much publicly: the bid "isn't unlimited," and the club "won't wait forever," though he declined to attach a figure or a date. By late in the week the mood music had cooled, with Catalan outlets reporting Barça "won't go crazy" to raise the offer and won't include players, while Atlético Madrid, who value the Argentine World Cup finalist far higher and are loath to strengthen a domestic rival, wait it out.

The subplots multiply. Real Madrid saw an earlier approach — read by most as a bluff — rebuffed, and other suitors have circled before shifting focus elsewhere. Barcelona, for their part, engineered a substantial financing move against future television rights to give the front office room to operate. The certainty tier here sits at offer live but stalled: everything hinges on whether Álvarez himself forces the issue.

Life After Salah: Liverpool Chase Barcola as Mo Eyes Saudi Arabia

Anfield's post-Salah reset runs through Paris — but the fee gap is a chasm.

Liverpool, now under Andoni Iraola, have identified Bradley Barcola as the marquee replacement for Mohamed Salah, whose contract expired at the end of June. Romano reports Barcola has decided he will not renew at PSG and sits atop Liverpool's list, with personal terms broadly agreed. The obstacle is the fee: PSG's valuation has been quoted anywhere from €120m to a British-record-shattering €170m, against a Liverpool number closer to €120m. Advanced on the player, distant on the price — the classic mid-window impasse.

As for Salah himself, the 34-year-old is reported to have a framework agreement to join the Saudi Pro League, weighing offers understood to involve Al-Ittihad and Al-Ahli, with a move to MLS floated as a fallback. It would draw a line under one of the most productive eras any Premier League forward has enjoyed.

London's Big Spenders: Chelsea and Spurs Break Their Own Records

Xabi Alonso's Chelsea blend youth with experience; De Zerbi's Tottenham rebuild the spine at nine figures.

Chelsea, now managed by Xabi Alonso, headline the English window. The club's British-record £117m capture of Morgan Rogers from Aston Villa reset the market before the week began; since then Chelsea have agreed a ~£55m deal for France defender Maxence Lacroix from Crystal Palace, and — in a shrewd nod to dressing-room ballast around a very young squad — added the experience of Danny Welbeck from Brighton (Romano's "here we go") and veteran Jordan Henderson on a free from Brentford.

Across town, Roberto De Zerbi's Tottenham have spent to match ambition. The £100m club-record signing of Sandro Tonali from Newcastle and the £52m arrival of centre-back Jan Paul van Hecke from Brighton are done; the live pursuit is Savinho from Manchester City, with personal terms reported agreed and the clubs negotiating around a £60m-plus fee.

Serie A Reloads: Juventus Rebuild, Inter Hunt a Free-Agent Stones

Turin overhauls its attack while Milan and Naples set the pace in the dugout.

Juventus have been Italy's busiest club. Randal Kolo Muani's protracted move from PSG is completing — reported around €45m, though the structure (permanent versus an earlier loan framework) has bounced between outlets — alongside the ~€33m capture of Kerim Alajbegović from Bayer Leverkusen. In the other direction, Loïs Openda has joined Lyon on a season-long loan.

Inter, under Cristian Chivu, have reached a verbal agreement to sign the free-agent John Stones, and are pushing for Tottenham's Cristian Romero, though personal terms are not agreed and Barcelona interest complicates matters. Roma, meanwhile, have Santiago Castro through a medical after a ~€35m deal with Bologna, with Artem Dovbyk heading the other way on loan.

Around The Leagues

Premier League

The English top flight enters 2026-27 almost unrecognisable in the dugout. Liverpool replaced Arne Slot with Andoni Iraola; Manchester City moved on from Pep Guardiola to Enzo Maresca; Manchester United handed Michael Carrick the job permanently; Chelsea turned to Xabi Alonso; and Tottenham appointed Roberto De Zerbi. With the season pushed back a week to accommodate the home-soil World Cup, clubs are only now reassembling — and the pre-season results have carried the usual health warnings, none louder than Manchester United's chastening friendly defeat to Wrexham.

The tours have criss-crossed the globe: Liverpool across North America, Chelsea and Tottenham in Australia ahead of a mouthwatering pre-season clash, and Manchester City through South Korea. Injury lists are the pre-season subplot — Liverpool reported a lengthy absentee list including Joe Gomez, while at Everton the news was mixed, with James Garner undergoing groin surgery but Jarrad Branthwaite back in action for the first time since April.

Off the pitch, the financial framework has shifted: the Premier League has moved from Profit & Sustainability Rules to a new Squad Cost Ratio system this cycle, with a proposed salary cap voted down. The change is widely read as the backdrop to Aston Villa's remarkable summer of sales — north of £200m raised, headlined by Morgan Rogers' British-record move. The competition kicks off on August 22.

La Liga

Spain's summer belongs, so far, to Real Madrid and the return of José Mourinho, who has taken his first sessions at Valdebebas while juggling a squad still trickling back from World Cup duty — Kylian Mbappé was held out of an early friendly. The mood at Barcelona was rattled by news that president Joan Laporta was hospitalised with an arrhythmia, forcing the cancellation of a planned pre-season trip to England. Elsewhere, Real Betis made Antony's loan from Manchester United permanent at ~€25m under Manuel Pellegrini, and Sevilla and Atlético Madrid did quiet, tidy business at the margins.

Bundesliga

Germany's headline is a sale, not a signing: RB Leipzig cashing in on Yan Diomande for a nine-figure fee is the kind of trading that keeps the club's model humming. Borussia Dortmund have been active on both ledgers — losing Karim Adeyemi toward Barcelona while adding young defensive cover — and Bayern Munich have kept their powder relatively dry. The curtain-raiser is set: the Franz Beckenbauer Supercup pits Bayern Munich against Borussia Dortmund at Signal Iduna Park on August 22.

Serie A

Italy's off-season has been defined by its benches. Napoli replaced Antonio Conte with Massimiliano Allegri, inheriting Scott McTominay and Kevin De Bruyne; AC Milan turned to Rúben Amorim; and Inter have handed Cristian Chivu the reins and a new contract. The market has followed the managers, pointing to a division bracing for one of its most open title races in years.

Ligue 1

The national story — Zidane's ascent — overshadows the club game, but PSG, fresh from their European triumph, are running an exit-heavy window: Kang-in Lee gone to Atlético Madrid for €40m, Randal Kolo Muani departing for Juventus, and Bradley Barcola increasingly likely to leave, while they test Monaco's resolve over Maghnes Akliouche. Lyon landed Loïs Openda on loan, and Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang is reported set to leave Marseille for Deportivo La Coruña. The season opens with the Trophée des Champions — RC Lens versus PSG — on August 16.

Major League Soccer

MLS is the one league in full flow, back from its World Cup pause and building toward the Leagues Cup. Inter Miami have won six straight, with Luis Suárez in vintage form (nine goals) and Casemiro making his debut after a headline-grabbing, and now MLS-investigated, arrival from Manchester United. Lionel Messi, freshly back in training, sat out the All-Star fixture. Out west, LAFC have been irresistible, with Denis Bouanga and Son Heung-min combining for goals, while Orlando City's Antoine Griezmann marked his debut with a goal. Seattle Sounders have lost five straight. In the market, LA Galaxy sold Gabriel Pec to Cruzeiro for a club-record fee up to $13.5m and brought in Kyōgo Furuhashi.

MLS snapshot (approx. late July): East — Nashville SC 39, Inter Miami 37, New England 29, Chicago Fire 26, NYCFC 25. West — Vancouver 33, LAFC 33, San Jose 33, Real Salt Lake 26, Houston 26. Nashville lead the Supporters' Shield race.
