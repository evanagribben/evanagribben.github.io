The Lead

Two tight wins, 21 points and the closest title race in years

Dortmund beat Real Betis and Elversberg by a goal apiece with tired legs and a striker in the treatment room. The table calls them Bayern’s equals; the performances say the break has come at the right time.

The scoreline was the same twice, and so was the feeling afterwards. Borussia Dortmund beat Real Betis 2-1 in the Champions League on Wednesday and SV Elversberg 2-1 in the Bundesliga on Saturday. Neither was comfortable. Both were enough. Nine league games in, Dortmund are unbeaten on 21 points, level with Bayern Munich, who stay top only on goal difference after winning 2-1 at Mainz.

Bundesliga.com calls it the tightest title race in years. Dortmund have not had 21 points after nine games since 2013-14, and they own the league’s best defence: seven conceded. The catch is the gap in goal difference, +20 to +9, which says Bayern have been more emphatic. Dortmund have been efficient.

Wednesday: Betis lose their perfect record

Betis arrived with nine points from nine. They left with their first Champions League defeat of the season. After a goalless first half, Fábio Silva followed in when Álvaro Vallés parried Jobe Bellingham’s shot from five metres, 1-0 on 49 minutes, per the club’s match report.

Aitor Ruibal equalised on 67 minutes from an Abde Ezzalzouli cross, after a mistake by Waldemar Anton. Five minutes later Konstantinos Karetsas, on for the injured Serhou Guirassy since the 34th minute, took Silva’s pass and drove a low 17-metre shot into the near corner. All Football says he was 18 years and 350 days old, the youngest Dortmund scorer of a Champions League winner.

The win took Dortmund to nine points and 12th place in the league phase, per NBC Sports, before trips to Arsenal and Aston Villa. Niko Kovac reportedly put the target for the top eight at 17 or 18 points, so Dortmund probably need about three wins from their last four.

“That’s something no one can take away from us.”

Saturday: Nmecha carries a weary side

Three days later Kovac changed three players and the shape. With Guirassy out with a thigh strain, Dortmund lined up in what the club called a 4-3-3: Joane Gadou at right-back, Carney Chukwuemeka and Karetsas wide, Silva through the middle. Chukwuemeka lasted 34 minutes before a left-thigh problem ended his day, and Maximilian Beier came on.

Felix Nmecha decided it. On 55 minutes he curled a 19-metre shot into the bottom corner from Silva’s pass. On 71 he hit a loose ball from 23 metres at 124 km/h, per the club report. Substitute Noel Futkeu’s overhead kick from a short corner made the last nine minutes tense, but Gregor Kobel was not beaten again.

What the two games told us

The honest reading came from inside the club. “Our fatigue showed in certain situations,” sporting director Ole Book told Sky. “After going up two-zero, we stopped attacking and didn’t have enough possession.” Kovac called it “very much a hard-fought victory,” adding that Dortmund came under pressure “because we gave the ball away too easily,” per the post-match reactions.

Context matters. Elversberg was Dortmund’s ninth competitive game in 29 days. Kovac has used a back three against Betis, a back four in Munich and against Elversberg, and switched back to three at 67 minutes on Saturday when Julian Ryerson and Ramy Bensebaini came on. The flexibility is a strength. The cost is that few combinations have had time to settle.

Then there is the striker question. Silva has stepped in well: a goal and an assist against Betis, the assist for the opener against Elversberg. Kovac said Guirassy’s absence costs height at set pieces and that the chance now falls to players such as Silva and Beier. The club hopes Guirassy is fit again after the break.

Secondary feature: the crown, the club and Felix Nmecha

His goals kept Dortmund level with Bayern. His celebration has drawn in the president, the fans and the national team.

Nmecha’s two goals against Elversberg were followed by a row that has reached the club president and the national team coach. After each goal, Nmecha mimes taking off a crown and laying it on the grass. ZDF links the gesture to “The King’s Return”, a campaign of the Christian players’ network Ballers in God, which posted about him after Saturday’s game.

The club’s own fan and supporters’ department criticised the celebration, citing ARD research that accuses the network of calling homosexuality a sin, per the AP. The club told Bild it expects all players’ conduct to align with its values and said it would speak to Nmecha.

On Monday, according to WDR, president Hans-Joachim Watzke told a meeting of that department the club would ask Nmecha to stop. Bild, relayed by BVBWLD, reports Dortmund want the network to stop using him in its promotion and are weighing legal steps if it does not.

Nmecha wrote on Instagram that he had been told his celebration caused “some misunderstandings” and that he did not want to put himself or any organisation in the foreground. The network said on Tuesday it meets all people “with love and respect regardless of their faith.” Germany coach Jürgen Klopp has spoken with Nmecha at the national team camp; no details were released.

DFB sporting director Rudi Völler stressed that freedom of religion applies and said the federation would talk to Nmecha again. The issue could surface at the club’s members’ meeting on November 22. For now, Dortmund’s best player of the week is also its most delicate conversation.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Gladbach: bottom, rebuilding, and dangerous on a good day

Alexander Blessin has had seven weeks to fix the league’s worst start. A 17-year-old Germany debutant is the player to watch.

Borussia Mönchengladbach are bottom of the Bundesliga with four points from nine games: one win, one draw, seven defeats, goals 12:25. They have already changed coach. Eugen Polanski left on September 13 after three straight defeats, and Alexander Blessin, formerly of St. Pauli, was appointed on September 22, per ZDF.

There have been signs of life. Gladbach won 3-2 at Stuttgart on October 23 for their first points, then drew 0-0 with Paderborn. Last Friday they lost 3-1 at Hamburg, where Joe Scally equalised on 44 minutes before Ko Itakura was sent off for a second yellow card early in the second half, per Bulinews.

Shape and the players to stop

Blessin played a 3-5-2 in Hamburg, per the DFB data centre, with Scally and Ullrich as wing-backs and Shuto Machino alongside Wael Mohya up front. Mohya, 17, is the story: he scored and assisted in Stuttgart and has just been called up by Klopp for Germany, per dpa. Scally has scored in both of the last two away games.

Itakura’s red card should rule the centre-back out, since a second yellow carries an automatic one-match ban. Goalkeeper Moritz Nicolas had a muscle problem at the end of October and Daniel Batz has been in goal. Tim Kleindienst and Franck Honorat were among nine absentees in late October; their status for the 21st is not yet confirmed.

Where Dortmund can hurt them

Twenty-five goals conceded in nine games is the worst defensive record in the league, and a three-man defence without Itakura will be short of experience. The warning is history: Gladbach beat Dortmund 1-0 at Borussia-Park last May, per statz.ai, and the game falls straight after an international break that has scattered 17 Dortmund players across Europe and beyond.

Transfer News

Anton has a concrete offer, but no deal yet

The centre-back wants a pay rise closer to the top of the squad; Bild says English clubs are circling.

Dortmund have made Waldemar Anton a concrete offer to extend a contract that runs to 2028, but there is no agreement. Sport Bild reported last week that he rejected a first proposal. This week, per BVBWLD citing Sky, Dortmund have put a concrete offer to him, and both sides are said to be optimistic.

Money is the gap. Sport Bild puts Anton’s current salary at €5.5m to €6m and says he wants “greater appreciation”. Bild reporter Jörg Weiler said on the club’s insider podcast that Anton “of course also has offers from England”; no clubs were named. According to Sport Bild via 90min, a deal to 2030 would suit him.

Status: advanced contract talks, no agreement; English interest single-sourced to Bild.

Kobel, Ryerson and Guirassy: the next three contracts

All three run to 2028. Sport Bild sets out where each one stands.

Anton is not the only key player tied to 2028. Sport Bild reports that Gregor Kobel has had no talks yet, but that the club will open them in the coming weeks, with Paris Saint-Germain said to have enquired repeatedly. Bulinews, via Yahoo Sports, carries the same line.

Julian Ryerson has had first contact through his agents, Sport Bild says, but is also weighing a move abroad. For Serhou Guirassy, the internal plan is an offer to 2030, with talks around the turn of the year; his decision reportedly depends on whether Dortmund can compete for titles. The current table helps that pitch.

Status: Kobel, planned talks with PSG interest; Ryerson, early talks; Guirassy, planned offer. All from Sport Bild.

Book names the gap: full-backs

The sporting director all but confirms where January money would go.

Speaking to DAZN on the night of the Betis game, Ole Book hinted at January business on the flanks: “Perhaps it is no great secret that we do not have too many full-backs,” he said, adding “we will see what is possible,” per BVBWLD.

Marcel Regula remains the name. The Zaglebie Lubin attacker is a player Dortmund want to retrain as a wide man. New this week: Polish outlet Kanal Sportowy says Lubin now want at least €10m, up from €7m to €8m, and fussballdaten.de claims Real Madrid are also interested, per BVBWLD. No bid has been reported.

Status: stated need from the club; Regula, concrete interest, price rising; Real Madrid link single-sourced.

Reggiani wants out

The Italian youth international has had only two brief first-team outings.

Centre-back Luca Reggiani, 18, has told Dortmund through his agent that he wants to leave in January because of a lack of minutes, Sky reports, via BVBWLD and GOAL. Dortmund reportedly want about €20m, possibly structured with bonuses and a sell-on share. AC Milan, who had a bid turned down in the summer, are named as likely buyers.

Status: exit request reported; no bid. Single-sourced to Sky.

The Squad

Guirassy and Chukwuemeka join the treatment room

The break arrives at the right moment for a squad that played nine games in 29 days.

Two more names joined the injury list this week. Serhou Guirassy hurt his thigh against Betis; Kovac confirmed it was “only” a strain and said “we hope he will be fit again after the international break,” per 90min. He has stayed in Dortmund rather than join Guinea.

Carney Chukwuemeka went off against Elversberg with a left-thigh problem; Bild reports a muscle fibre tear and an absence of several weeks, per Neue Westfälische. The club has not given a timescale. Waldemar Anton, who had played 1,417 of a possible 1,440 minutes, withdrew from Germany duty with muscle problems, the club said.

The Svensson let-off

Daniel Svensson was fortunate on Saturday. Around the 20th minute he caught Elversberg captain Lukas Pinckert above the ankle; there was no card and no VAR review, and Pinckert later went off injured. Referee Harm Osmers told Sky it was “at least yellow” and that red could be debated. “We were lucky there,” Kovac admitted, per Flashscore, adding it was a split-second action.

Form and rotation

Felix Nmecha is in the form of his Dortmund career, and Joey Veerman looked at home at the base of the 4-3-3. Silva has a goal and two assists in the week. Konstantinos Karetsas, whose minutes Kovac has managed since his ankle problem, scored his first goal for the club. Bellingham, now an England player, said he owes much of his development to Kovac, per Bulinews.

One positive note on the long-term list: Giannis Konstantelias is said to be ahead of schedule after his cruciate ligament tear, with Bild and Greek media now pointing to February or March, per GOAL. The club has set no date.

The Academy

Gardarsson keeps scoring as the U19s stay top

Three goals in two games for the Icelandic forward; the U23s hold the leaders.

Felix Hirschnagl’s U19s drew 2-2 with Real Betis in the UEFA Youth League on Wednesday. Justin Hoy scored after five minutes and Bjarki Gardarsson restored the lead after Ruben De Sa’s quick reply, before Fran Batan equalised on 36 minutes. Dortmund are 10th with seven points, per the club report, and go to Arsenal on November 24.

On Saturday Gardarsson scored twice more in a 3-2 league win over VfL Osnabrück, and substitute Levin Vogt struck the winner in stoppage time, per bvb.de. The U19s went into the weekend top of their DFB youth league group with 21 points and unbeaten, per the club’s weekend preview.

BVB II and the loanees

Daniel Rios’s U23s lost their unbeaten record 1-0 to FC Gütersloh on October 31, then drew 0-0 at leaders Rot-Weiß Oberhausen in front of a season-high 6,116 on Saturday. They stay second in the Regionalliga West, three points behind, per the club. Sportfreunde Lotte visit on Friday, November 20.

On loan, Yan Couto played the full 90 minutes of Como’s 9-1 Champions League win at Lens and 74 minutes of a 2-0 defeat at Inter, per FotMob. The Digest could not confirm any minutes this week for goalkeeper Diant Ramaj at FC Copenhagen.

Women's Team

Twelve from twelve, and a derby with a crowd target

The first goal conceded all season barely dents a perfect start.

Markus Högner’s side won 6-1 at SC Fortuna Köln on Sunday to make it 12 wins from 12 in the Regionalliga West. Annika Boden’s 74th-minute goal for Köln was the first Dortmund have conceded all season. Ronja Leubner and Rita Schumacher scored twice each, with Alexandra Popp and Jacqueline Meißner also on target, per the club report.

The previous weekend’s result, unconfirmed in our last edition, was a 5-0 win at Recklinghausen with a Popp hat-trick, per bvb.de. Dortmund lead on 36 points with a goal difference of +58. Next is the Revierderby against Schalke at Stadion Rote Erde on Sunday, where the club is chasing a league-record crowd under the banner “Mission 17.555”.

Players Away

Bellingham joins Jude in the England squad

Seventeen players travel; Guirassy, Chukwuemeka and Anton stay home.

Seventeen Dortmund players are away this break, per the club. The headline is Jobe Bellingham’s first senior England call-up, alongside his brother Jude, for the Nations League games against Croatia at Wembley on Thursday and in Spain on Sunday. “Playing alongside my brother Jude for England is a huge dream,” he said, per Bulinews.

For Germany, Nico Schlotterbeck and Felix Nmecha are in Klopp’s squad for Serbia away on Friday and the Netherlands in Berlin on Monday. Anton withdrew injured and Ridle Baku was called up instead, per t-online. Joey Veerman and the Netherlands meet Karetsas’s Greece on Friday before Veerman faces his Dortmund teammates in Berlin.

Kobel (Switzerland), Ryerson (Norway), Svensson (Sweden) and Silva (Portugal) also have two Nations League games each. Enzo dos Santos is with Luxembourg, Ramy Bensebaini with Algeria for Africa Cup of Nations qualifiers and Mathis Albert with the United States. Nwaneri (England U21), Gadou (France U21), Samuele Inácio and Filippo Mane (Italy U21) and Reggiani (Italy U20) play youth friendlies.

Old faces

Cole Campbell, sold to Elversberg in the summer, came off the bench at Signal Iduna Park on Saturday and had a chance two minutes later. Ethan Nwaneri, on loan from Arsenal, had cameos in both games and will face his parent club on November 24.
