The Lead

The clock started at 7pm on Thursday

Manchester City have appealed. The verdict stands until a new panel says otherwise, the punishment has not been set, and the timetable for both is now the most important document in English football.

The deadline was Friday. Manchester City did not wait for it. The club confirmed on Thursday 1 October that it had lodged its appeal at 7pm that evening, against the independent commission that found it guilty of serious breaches of Premier League financial rules across nine seasons, from 2009/10 to 2017/18.

The appeal went to the Chair of the Premier League’s Judicial Panel, ITV News reported. The league says the Appeal Board hearing will stay private and confidential until its outcome can be published.

City’s statement repeated the line it has held since the verdict. The commission’s opinion, it said, contains “clear material errors of law, principle and fact, and is unsafe.” It added, without qualification: “The Club is innocent of the accusations made by the Premier League.”

“This disciplinary case, and this decision, are the most significant in Premier League history.”

Richard Masters, Premier League chief executive

What the appeal is actually about

The commission found that sponsorship income booked by the club was, in large part, not paid by the sponsors at all. NBC Sports’ summary of the findings puts it at around 87 per cent of the reported commercial income over the period, supplied instead through the owners, the Abu Dhabi United Group.

City’s central argument, as ITV describes it, is that government funding through the Abu Dhabi Crown Prince Court helped sponsors meet their obligations. The commission rejected that account as a narrative built after the fact. That is the ground the appeal will be fought on: not whether money moved, but whose money it was.

The timetable, and why it matters

The Premier League handbook says an appeal hearing must finish within twelve weeks of being lodged. Counted from 1 October, that lands on Christmas Eve. The appeal panel then has 30 days to rule, which points to a decision in January.

There are two catches. Sports Mole noted that it is unclear whether these fast-track rules apply to a case that began before they were introduced. And City’s chief executive Ferran Soriano has warned that the process could take considerably longer.

Route and key facts

The Premier League v Manchester City

The charges
Serious breaches of financial rules over nine seasons, 2009/10 to 2017/18, plus charges of failing to co-operate with the investigation.
The verdict
Guilty of all the serious-breach charges and most of the co-operation charges. Communicated on 24 September, redacted decision published on 29 September.
The appeal
Lodged at 7pm on Thursday 1 October, a day inside the deadline. Heard in private by a fresh panel.
The clock
Hearing to finish within twelve weeks, around 24 December. Ruling within 30 days of the hearing, so likely January 2027.
The sanction
A separate hearing, with no date set. City can appeal any sanction too.
Waiting in the wings
Arsenal, Liverpool, Manchester United and Tottenham, who reserved the right to claim compensation in 2024. The Football Association is reviewing the findings.

The punishment nobody has priced yet

The sanction is a separate process, and nobody has said when it will be heard. Several Premier League clubs want it settled before the end of this season, according to The Sun as relayed by Sports Mole, and believe the new fast-track rules should apply.

The precedents are small next to the scale of this case. Everton lost eight points across two profitability cases in 2023/24. Nottingham Forest lost four. Those were single-season accounting breaches; this is nine seasons and a finding of deliberate concealment.

The Football Association has added another layer. It said it is reviewing the commission’s findings, that the decision has “significant implications for the integrity of the game”, and that it may act where appropriate. It will say nothing more while proceedings continue.

The second front: money

Four clubs preserved the right to sue back in 2024: Arsenal, Manchester United, Liverpool and Tottenham. The Independent has reported they are planning to act, with a combined claim of around 200m pounds discussed. The Times put the potential exposure to rival claims at more than 100m pounds.

None of that can start yet. Legal action can only begin once the case and its appeals are finished, which is another reason the January date matters so much.

And the football carries on

Through all of this, City are top. Enzo Maresca’s side have won all five league games and lead on fifteen points. Their first match back from the break is at Anfield on Sunday, against Liverpool.

That is the strange shape of the autumn. The club playing the best football in England is also the club whose place in the league depends on a hearing held in private before Christmas. Every point City win between now and January is a point that may or may not count for anything.

 

The bill for the longest break in memory

Club football in Europe’s big leagues has been dark since 20 September. As it ends, the clubs are counting what four internationals in one window cost them.

FIFA’s new calendar folded two international windows into one this autumn. National teams played up to four matches between 24 September and 6 October, and the big European leagues paused for three weekends. It returns this Friday.

Niko Kovac spoke for a lot of coaches on Wednesday. “Those four games were definitely too many for me. The break was too long,” the Borussia Dortmund coach said, in comments carried by dpa.

His point was not only about injuries. “We also had two and a half weeks with no rhythm at all,” he said. That matters for a side that won its first four league games and leads the Bundesliga.

“When you have to play four games, there’s a lot of travel, and the squads are relatively small.”

Niko Kovac, Borussia Dortmund coach

Dortmund’s own list shows why. Konstantinos Karetsas and Daniel Svensson came back with colds. Nico Schlotterbeck hurt an ankle in Germany training and is doubtful for Friday. Julian Ryerson is nursing a knock from a collision.

In Barcelona the worry is Raphinha. He left the Brazil squad early with muscular swelling in his right thigh after the win over Australia. Early signs were not serious, but Marca reported that Hansi Flick will not rush him back.

The reason is last season. Raphinha hurt the same thigh on Brazil duty in September 2025, his return was accelerated, and he broke down again. He then missed 58 days and 13 matches, Sports Mole recalled.

Napoli had problems before the break and have more after it. Tuttomercatoweb reported on Wednesday that Antonio Vergara has a strain of the right soleus and will miss at least four or five matches.

Mati Olivera came back from Uruguay with a groin problem. Costantino Favasuli, hurt with Italy’s under-21s, is probably out until after the November break. They join a list that already included Alex Meret, Alessandro Buongiorno, Scott McTominay and Frank Anguissa.

That leaves Massimiliano Allegri with nine matches between 10 October and 4 November, and a squad that is thinner than it has been all season.

Elsewhere the damage had been done earlier in the window. Real Madrid diagnosed Kylian Mbappe with a hyperextension of the back of his left knee, picked up while scoring for France against Turkiye, and said he would be out for two weeks or more. Bayern Munich lost Jamal Musiala to a torn muscle fibre in his right thigh in the warm-up for Germany’s game with the Netherlands.

The sixteen-year-old who wants to leave Manchester United

JJ Gabriel turned 16 on Tuesday. That was the day he was due to sign scholarship terms at Carrington. Instead, the talk is of Barcelona and Madrid.

JJ Gabriel is the most talked-about academy player in England. He has 29 goals in 32 games for Manchester United’s under-18s and has already scored twice in two Premier League 2 matches. He made his senior debut in a pre-season friendly against Atletico Madrid this summer.

Last month he told the club he wanted to leave. He turned 16 on Tuesday 6 October, and Sports Mole reported that day that his time at United is “seemingly coming to an end”.

The German journalist Florian Plettenberg names Real Madrid and Barcelona as favourites. Both are said to be ready to offer a large salary, bonuses and a signing-on fee. Bayern Munich are described as interested but behind.

The player reportedly prefers Barcelona. He has trained there since announcing he wanted to go, and his family are regular visitors.

The route matters as much as the destination. A move to a club in the European Union at 16 depends on holding the right passport. Plettenberg reports that Gabriel is set to receive a Cypriot passport through his mother, which would make a free move possible in January.

There is friction too. His representatives were said to be angry that he was left out of United’s Champions League squad against Sabah. They had wanted him to get at least 20 minutes in that game and in matches against Brighton and Bournemouth.

Where it stands  Player wants out and clubs are circling. No agreement with any club has been reported, and United have not released him. The passport route is single-sourced.
 

Carvajal picks Getafe, if La Liga allows it

A six-time European champion with Real Madrid has said yes to a club on the southern edge of the city. The last word belongs to the league’s accountants.

Dani Carvajal is 34 and has been a free agent since his Real Madrid contract ended on 30 June. He has not played an official match since. This week he chose his next club, and it is Getafe.

This week, Getafe president Angel Torres confirmed that Carvajal had passed a medical and that the agreement with the player was closed. Fabrizio Romano reported a verbal agreement too, citing ABC.

Coach Jose Bordalas called the player himself to explain his role, and the response was positive. The terms of the contract, including its length, have not been published.

Where it stands  Agreed with the player, medical passed. Getafe still need La Liga to approve the registration under the club’s squad-cost limit, and Torres said it cannot be called final until then.

The obstacle is the spending cap. Getafe operate with far less room than the clubs Carvajal spent his career with, and Bordalas admitted as much in September. Even a free transfer has to fit under the limit, and that is now a conversation between the club and the league.

 

Bournemouth put up the closed sign for Scott

Chelsea are expected to come back for Alex Scott. Bournemouth have already given their answer.

Bournemouth will not sell Alex Scott in January, talkSPORT reported, as relayed by SoccerNews on 7 October. Club sources say he stays until at least the end of the season.

Chelsea had a bid of around 64m pounds turned down in the summer and are said to be preparing another. Real Madrid are also watching. Scott, 23, has not agreed a new contract, which will keep the story alive.

A week earlier, the journalist Dean Jones had reported the same stance for Flashscore and put Bournemouth’s valuation at around 110m euros. Treat that number as an estimate, not an asking price.

Where it stands  Concrete interest, no sale sanctioned. Two separate reports agree on Bournemouth’s position.
 

The Rumour Mill

What else is moving, and how much of it is real

Everything simmering, with the reliability stated plainly. The windows are shut, so almost all of this is about January or next summer.

Preliminary contact

Bayern Munich have been in touch with the representatives of Nick Woltemade, Tuttosport reported via Sport Witness on 29 September. The forward is on loan at Juventus from Newcastle United.

Newcastle would reportedly want about 60m euros to sell next summer. The report itself stresses that nothing is imminent. Certainty: early contact only.

Contract work in progress

Real Madrid were reported by AS in mid-September to be preparing a new deal for Thibaut Courtois to June 2028. His current contract runs to 2027. Nothing has been announced. Certainty: talks reported, no agreement confirmed.

Noise, and treat it that way

The story that Erling Haaland will join Barcelona in 2027 came from a transfer “insider” on X, citing unnamed Barcelona sources. No club or player has said anything.

The Spanish paper Sport reported that Haaland’s contract, which runs to 2034, has no release clause for relegation or anything else. That is the useful fact here. Certainty: noise.

Stated needs

The most reliable guide to January is not names but gaps. Napoli’s injury list is long and getting longer. Manchester United may be about to lose their best academy prospect for little more than training compensation. Getafe are trying to fit a free agent under a spending cap. Watch those three before you watch the gossip.

 

Around the Leagues

No top-flight league in Europe played this week. This is a round of standings, injuries and what comes next. MLS kept going in midweek.

Premier League

Leaders: Manchester City, 15 points from 5. Most goals: Brighton, 16. Back: Saturday 10 October.

The table at the break has one clean line through it. Manchester City have won all five and lead on fifteen points. Arsenal are second on twelve after losing 3-0 at Brighton in their last match.

The most interesting team below them is Brighton. They have scored sixteen league goals, the most in the division, and they beat the champions 3-0 on 19 September.

Leeds United are fifth and unbeaten, with two wins and three draws. Only Brighton have conceded fewer than their three goals. They go to the Emirates first on Saturday morning.

The pressure is at the other end. Tottenham and Fulham have two points each. Roberto De Zerbi’s Spurs have not won a league game since May, and they go to Manchester United on Saturday evening.

United are in their own trouble, with five points from five. Michael Carrick also has the JJ Gabriel situation to manage, which is not the kind of distraction a struggling side needs.

Chelsea have seven points and have conceded more than anyone. Xabi Alonso’s first home game back is against Bournemouth.

The weekend’s biggest game is on Sunday. Liverpool host Manchester City at Anfield, the first chance for anyone to test the leaders since the verdict and the appeal. Then Coventry City host Newcastle United on Monday night.

La Liga

Leaders: Barcelona, 21 points from 7, still perfect. Back: Friday 9 October.

Barcelona have won all seven and are five points clear. Atletico Madrid and Real Betis are on sixteen, with Real Madrid on fifteen after defeats to both.

The question for Hansi Flick is whether to play Raphinha against Getafe on Saturday. The early reading is that he will be careful, because October is brutal: Getafe, Galatasaray away, Real Betis away, Paris Saint-Germain away, then the Clasico.

Jose Mourinho’s Madrid are waiting on Kylian Mbappe, who was given two weeks or more at the end of September. They host Villarreal on Saturday night. Mourinho has five games in fifteen days ahead of him, including a return to Roma in the Champions League.

Javier Aguirre takes charge of his first league match for Valencia, away at Racing Santander on Sunday. He was appointed on 23 September after a poor start under Carlos Corberan, and has had the whole break to work with the squad.

And Getafe, who go to the Camp Nou on Saturday, are still waiting for La Liga to clear the Dani Carvajal signing.

Bundesliga

Leaders: Borussia Dortmund, 12 points from 4, perfect. Back: Friday 9 October.

Borussia Dortmund lead the Bundesliga with a perfect record, four wins from four. SC Freiburg and Bayern Munich are both on ten.

Dortmund open the weekend at home to Werder Bremen on Friday night. Kovac may be without Schlotterbeck, and two midfielders are only just over colds.

Bayern go to Augsburg on Saturday without Musiala. Vincent Kompany’s side have dropped points already and cannot afford to let Dortmund get further away.

At the bottom, Borussia Monchengladbach have no points from four. Alexander Blessin arrived on 22 September and has had the whole break to find a first win. His first game is the Rhine derby at Koln on Sunday.

The pick of Saturday is the late game, RB Leipzig against Eintracht Frankfurt.

Serie A

Leaders: Inter, Lazio and Roma, 13 points from 5. Capocannoniere: Malen, 6. Back: Saturday 10 October.

Italy has a three-way tie at the top. Inter, Lazio and Roma all have thirteen points from five games.

Roma have the best defence in the league, three goals conceded. They also have its top scorer, Donyell Malen, with six. Gian Piero Gasperini’s side go to Como on Sunday lunchtime.

Lazio under Gennaro Gattuso are the surprise. They host Monza on Sunday, while Inter host Parma on Saturday evening.

Napoli’s problem is bodies, not points. Allegri faces nine games in under four weeks with at least ten players unavailable or doubtful. They host Frosinone on Saturday night, then go to Villarreal in the Champions League.

Venezia are bottom with no points from five, and Giovanni Stroppa is under real pressure. Juventus go to Cagliari on Sunday night and Milan visit Sassuolo.

Ligue 1

Leaders: Monaco, 13 points from 5, unbeaten. Back: Friday 9 October.

Monaco lead Ligue 1 at the break, unbeaten under Filipe Luis, the former Brazil and Atletico Madrid left-back who took over in the summer. Lyon are second on eleven. Lille and Rennes have ten each.

The champions are not in the top four. Paris Saint-Germain have eight points from five after rebuilding their attack in the summer. Luis Enrique’s side host Le Mans on Saturday.

Lower down, Marseille have three points and Bruno Genesio needs a result at Troyes on Sunday. Le Havre are bottom on two.

The weekend opens on Friday with Lens against Lyon. Lens have been under Yannick Cahuzac since Dino Toppmoller left in September, and are still waiting for a first win under him.

Major League Soccer

Leaders: Nashville SC, 60 points from 27. Supporters’ Shield lead: 10 points. Decision Day: 7 November.

Nashville SC came out of the break where they went in: far clear. They are 18 wins, 6 draws and 3 defeats, on sixty points, ten ahead of Vancouver Whitecaps in the Supporters’ Shield race.

The record is now realistic. Inter Miami set the single-season mark of 74 points in 2024. Nashville need fifteen from their last seven to pass it, according to a Nashville SC match preview.

Wins at Austin FC on Saturday and at home to Sporting Kansas City next Wednesday could clinch the Shield.

In midweek, Seattle Sounders beat Sporting Kansas City 2-1 on 1 October. Andre Luiz put Kansas City ahead, Sebastian Gomez came off the bench to equalise, and Cristian Roldan won it in the 90th minute.

It was Seattle’s third straight win and eighth match unbeaten. It lifted them to seventh in the West on 38 points, according to the match report.

The emotional moment of the week happened in Buenos Aires. Lionel Messi played his last game for Argentina on Tuesday, a 3-0 friendly win over Benin. He set up Nicolas Otamendi’s opener and scored a penalty, his 126th international goal.

“Not coming anymore is going to be deeply painful,” he said. He remains under contract at Inter Miami until 2028, but AFP reported he also said that, after his father’s death two months ago, he may stop playing altogether sooner than expected.

Miami host DC United on Saturday. Decision Day is 7 November.
