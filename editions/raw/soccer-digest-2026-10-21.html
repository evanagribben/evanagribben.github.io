The Lead

Mourinho Walks Back Into the Camp Nou

Thirteen years after he left Spain, Jose Mourinho returns to Barcelona as Real Madrid's manager, six points adrift of a side that had won eleven straight until Tuesday, and without the two wingers who were supposed to win him the game.

There is no gentle way to walk into the Spotify Camp Nou, and Jose Mourinho has never looked for one. On Sunday evening, at 1 p.m. Pacific, he takes Real Madrid back to the ground where he spent three seasons in open warfare with the institution he is about to visit, and he does it as the underdog. Barcelona have won nine from nine in La Liga. They have 27 points. Real Madrid have 21, and are third, behind Atletico Madrid as well. A Barcelona win on Sunday makes the gap nine points before Halloween.

What makes the fixture genuinely hard to call is that both sides arrive bruised in different places. Barcelona's winning run, eleven matches across all competitions, ended on Tuesday night at the Parc des Princes, where Paris Saint-Germain beat them 4-2 in the best game of the Champions League season so far. It was not a collapse.

It was something more worrying for Hansi Flick: two individual errors, one from Lamine Yamal inside his own half for Desire Doue's opener, one from Warren Zaire-Emery's opponent under a high ball, and a midfield that could not hold the ball when Vitinha decided to take the game. Barcelona equalised twice, through Xavi Espart and then Yamal himself, and still lost by two.

Real Madrid, meanwhile, will play the biggest match of their season so far without Vinicius Junior, who is suspended, and without Rodrygo, who has a cruciate ligament injury. That is a Clasico without either of the wingers who have carried Madrid's transitions for the better part of five years. Kylian Mbappe has nine La Liga goals and will almost certainly start centrally.

Beyond him, Mourinho is short of the specific thing his teams have always been built to do, which is run at a high line with pace in the wide areas. Ferland Mendy is back in training. Raul Asencio is not available. Madrid at least arrive in form: they beat RB Leipzig 4-1 at the Bernabeu on Wednesday night, Mbappe and Arda Guler among the scorers, a third straight Champions League win.

Which leaves the tactical question of the week: does Mourinho try to win this game, or does he try to stop Barcelona winning it? His record at the Camp Nou in his first spell suggests he is perfectly willing to do the latter and absorb the criticism, and there is a reasonable argument that 21 points from nine games is not a platform from which to gamble.

Barcelona's shape under Flick is still the most aggressive in Europe's top five leagues, a high line that invites exactly the ball in behind that Mbappe has spent his career running onto. PSG hurt Barcelona by playing through them rather than over them. Madrid, without Vinicius and Rodrygo, may not have that option.

Barcelona's own absentee list is longer. Dani Olmo came off injured in Paris. Andreas Christensen and Eric Garcia have thigh problems, Jules Kounde a sprained ankle. Roony Bardghji ruptured a cruciate ligament in training in August and Frenkie de Jong has been out since tearing the medial collateral ligament in his right knee at the World Cup.

The absence that matters most tactically is at centre back: with Christensen out, Ronald Araujo on loan at Liverpool and Kounde injured, Gerard Martin is Flick's only natural left-sided option. Catalan reporting this month names a left-footed centre back as Barcelona's clearest squad need, and the club cannot address it in January without selling first. More on that below.

The two squads were rebuilt around each other over the summer, which gives Sunday an extra edge. Barcelona spent roughly 174 million euros on seven players, headlined by Anthony Gordon from Newcastle United for 70 million plus up to 10 million in variables and Rodri from Manchester City for 60 million plus up to 15 million, with Karim Adeyemi arriving from Borussia Dortmund and Joao Cancelo back on a free.

Robert Lewandowski left at the end of his contract. Ferran Torres was sold to PSG for 48.5 million euros, which is why he was on the pitch in Paris on Tuesday in the other shirt, and why he hit the post against the club that made him.

 

"I don't care about [the favourites tag] because I don't see PSG as the favourite. This match isn't decisive. The decisive month will be in April."

The individual duel to watch is Raphinha against whichever full back Mourinho trusts. Raphinha leads the Pichichi race with twelve goals, three clear of Mbappe, and is the one Barcelona attacker whose form has not wobbled. Yamal has eight in La Liga and nine goals with ten assists across eleven appearances in all competitions, and Joan Laporta chose the week of the PSG tie to tell L'Equipe that he is going nowhere.

"He's a Barca fan and came through La Masia," Laporta said, in beIN Sports' English rendering of the French interview. "All of that helps when it comes to renewing a contract. We're not worried."

The head-to-head record in the fixture's modern run reads 29 wins to Barcelona, 21 to Real Madrid, ten draws. None of that will matter much by Sunday evening. What will matter is whether Mourinho, who built his reputation on exactly this kind of night, can produce one more of them with half his attack missing, and whether a Barcelona side that has not lost a league game all season can absorb the first real test of its nerve.

The tie is at 1 p.m. Pacific, 9 p.m. in Barcelona. Spain's clocks go back that morning, so the local time reads the same as it did in September while the Pacific conversion shifts.

 
 

Como's Night

Como 4, Manchester United 1

A club playing its first Champions League campaign took Manchester United apart on the shore of Lake Como, and the questions about Michael Carrick are now the loudest in English football.

Nico Paz scored inside two minutes. By the time Anastasios Douvikas made it two early in the second half, Manchester United had managed no shots on target at all in the first half, had committed nine fouls to Como's two, and had seen 62 per cent of the ball go the other way.

Michael Carrick made two substitutions at the interval, sending on Amad Diallo and Carlos Baleba for Matheus Cunha and Andrey Santos. It did not help. Como finished 4-1 winners, with Paz scoring twice and assisting another, and Douvikas and Ramon also on the scoresheet, according to Corriere dello Sport.

It was Como's first home match in the competition. Como are fifth in Serie A on 14 points, have won four of seven, and beat Fiorentina 3-1 away on Sunday. Cesc Fabregas, whose contract runs to June 2028, has built something that now has to be taken seriously rather than enjoyed as a curiosity. His line after the match, to Gianluca Di Marzio, was about the next one rather than this one: "Crediamo tanto in Baturina. Vogliamo diventare una realta solida." We believe a great deal in Baturina. We want to become a solid reality.

United's position is harder to describe charitably. They are 13th in the Premier League on seven points from seven games, with one win, and drew 1-1 at Leeds United on Sunday. They have now lost to Atletico Madrid and Como in successive Champions League matchdays after opening with a 4-0 win over Sabah. Carrick used his press conference last weekend to talk about January, telling Sky Sports: "When the next transfer window comes, you want to plan and you want to make the best of it. Whatever that looks like at this stage, we don't quite know. But without doubt, there's opportunity because the transfer window is open."

Sky reported centre back and left back as the priorities. With Luke Shaw injured, United currently have no senior left back.

The commercial irony is sharp. In late September the club reported record revenue of 677 million pounds achieved without European football. It now has European football and a 4-1 defeat to a club that was in Serie B three years ago.

Across the rest of matchday 3 on Wednesday night, nine matches produced 34 goals and exactly one draw. Real Madrid beat RB Leipzig 4-1, Aston Villa made it three wins from three by seeing off Viking 3-1, Club Brugge beat Lens 3-0 and Inter Milan edged Shakhtar Donetsk 1-0 with ten men after Alessandro Bastoni was sent off.

Real Betis beat Feyenoord 1-0 through an Isco penalty, Galatasaray came from 2-1 down to win 4-2 at Lille with Victor Osimhen scoring, and Bayern Munich were held 2-2 at home by Arsenal. The shock was in Lisbon, where LASK, pointless after two matchdays, won 3-2 at Sporting CP.

 
 

Transfer News

Europe's summer window closed on September 1, so nothing below is a completed transfer. What is moving instead is the thing that always moves in late October: the January pre-contract clock, which starts on New Year's Day and turns every player whose deal expires in June 2027 into either an asset to be sold now or a free agent to be lost later. Certainty tiers are stated for every item.

The January 1 Clock Is Already Driving the Market

Every contract expiring in June 2027 becomes a pre-contract target on New Year's Day. That single date explains most of this week's reporting.

Under FIFA rules a player inside the final six months of a contract can agree a pre-contract with a club in another country. From January 1 that applies to the whole June 2027 cohort, and clubs with sellable players and no signatures are suddenly motivated. The cleanest illustration is at Bournemouth, where Rob Dorsett and Mark McAdam of Sky Sports News reported on October 16 that the club will consider offers in excess of 80 million pounds plus add-ons in January for Alex Scott, who has refused a new contract reported to be the best the club has ever offered.

Status: concrete interest with a club valuation set, no bid on the table.

Fabrizio Romano corroborated it four days later, saying Chelsea are "definitely there" and have had several contacts with Scott's representatives after a rejected summer bid, and that Liverpool made calls in late June and early July without ever bidding. Scott is 23, an England international, currently injured, and has a little over 18 months left. Marco Rose was blunt when asked: "I told you that Alex Scott was going to stay in the summer. He's a player of our club and now he's injured. If he is back, he's playing for Bournemouth."

The same mechanism is producing stories everywhere. Jean-Philippe Mateta is out of contract in June 2027 at Crystal Palace and, per talkSPORT material carried in Sky's Paper Talk on October 20, is showing no inclination to extend, with Manchester United and Tottenham Hotspur both linked. Graeme Bailey of TEAMtalk reported in late September that United's director of football Jason Wilcox rates his Premier League goals record. Status: concrete interest plus reported player willingness, no talks between clubs. Mateta has made two appearances this season with a hamstring problem, which is the obvious complication.

In Spain the same clock is ticking loudest at Sevilla. Javier Merino of JaviSFC reported on October 21 that centre back Andres Castrin, out of contract on June 30 2027, agreed terms in early October on a three-year extension with a significant rise and a 50 million euro release clause, and that the club is still waiting for a formal response from his agency.

From January 1 he can sign elsewhere for nothing. Bayer Leverkusen are following closely; Valencia, Real Sociedad, Leeds United and Espanyol are named as interested. This is a Sevilla-specialist outlet rather than a national one, so treat the detail as single-sourced, though the structural facts are not in dispute. Garcia Plaza said in late September: "Ojala renueve, ojala."

 

Liverpool Have Three Midfielders on One List

Alex Scott, Lamine Camara and Adam Wharton are all live Liverpool links inside ten days. Romano says the intent is explicit.

The most useful kind of transfer signal is not a single report but a cluster, and Liverpool have produced one. Fabrizio Romano said on October 20 that "Liverpool are planning to do something important in midfield in 2027," and inside the same ten-day window three separate midfielders have been reported as targets.

Lamine Camara is the one with the most history. Romano reported, in material written up by James Marshment at TEAMtalk, that Chelsea had agreed a 47 million pound fee for the 22-year-old Senegal international with Monaco on deadline day before the deal collapsed, that Chelsea were "furious" and are "no longer in talks," and that Liverpool are now frontrunners at a price closer to 59 million pounds. Status: concrete interest and long-term tracking, no bid. Single-origin to Romano, carried by two outlets.

There is a separate Liverpool thread worth tracking. Romano said on October 21 that there are "active conversations ongoing between Rio Ngumoha and Liverpool over a new, long-term contract," that the club "absolutely want to keep Rio," and then hedged carefully: "Let's see what's gonna happen in the future, but his contract talks are taking place." Rio Ngumoha is 18 and under contract to 2028. Status: active talks, no agreement. Separately, The Telegraph reported on October 21 that Liverpool have not yet opened renewal talks with Alexis Mac Allister, whose deal runs to 2028, and that Chelsea are watching. Single-sourced.

 

Kane Agrees Terms to 2029, and Dortmund Tie Down Kovac

Bayern got the shorter deal they wanted. Dortmund extended their manager a week before the doubts came back.

Michael Bridge of Sky Sports News reported on October 16 that Harry Kane has agreed a new Bayern Munich contract in principle through the summer of 2029. Sky Sport Germany and Bild had the same line the previous day. Kane reportedly wanted 2030, so Bayern got their preferred term.

The Times put the terms at roughly 474,000 euros a week, just under 25 million a year, which is his existing salary unchanged. Status: personal terms agreed, not signed and not announced. Bridge: "There are just a few things to iron out before confirmation." President Herbert Hainer called the talks "hervorragend." No release clause has been reported.

At Borussia Dortmund, the extension is done and official. Niko Kovac signed to 2029 on October 13, along with assistants Robert Kovac and Filip Tapalovic, per the club and Get German Football News. Lars Ricken, managing director for sport, said: "With Niko Kovac, we've been on a very good path since he took office a little over a year and a half ago." Kovac inherited an 11th-placed team in February 2025 and finished second on 73 points last season.

 

Barcelona Have About One Million Euros of Room

A detailed look at the club's fair-play position concludes that any January signing must be preceded by a sale. The renewals are eating the margin.

Artur Lopez published the single hardest number in this week's European reporting. Writing for Cronica Global on October 11, he set out Barcelona's position under La Liga's salary rules: the club's limit has risen from 432 million euros to 582 million, and its sporting costs have risen 115 million to 581 million. That is a margin of roughly one million euros. His conclusion is unambiguous: "Todo fichaje en enero de 2027 tendra que pasar primero por ventas." Every January signing will have to come after a sale.

At Real Madrid the renewal board is busier and the money less constrained. Jose Felix Diaz of Diario AS reported on October 16 that Jude Bellingham has agreed an extension to June 2031, two years beyond his current deal, with a rise and no figure published. Status: agreement reached, official announcement still expected, and effectively single-sourced to AS. Thibaut Courtois has verbally agreed a one-year extension to 2028, per both Romano and MARCA on October 13, unsigned.

Arda Guler's extension to 2031 is reported closed and awaiting announcement. Antonio Rudiger, out of contract in 2027, has no deal and no talks, which makes him Madrid's only senior expiry inside twelve months. Eduardo Camavinga, to 2029, has no extension conversations at all. Vinicius Junior's file is closed: he signed to June 2032 in the summer, and his terms set the ceiling every other negotiation at the club is measured against.

 

Nico Paz, Chelsea, and Real Madrid's 80 Million Euro Button

Como own him outright. Madrid can buy him back next summer for a fixed price. Chelsea have been watching, and Como's number starts with a one.

The most interesting contractual object in European football right now sits on the shore of Lake Como. Marco Conterio of TUTTOmercatoWEB reported on October 21 that Chelsea staff have watched Nico Paz in recent Serie A and Champions League matches and are not deterred by the price. Como own 100 per cent of his registration after paying around 60 million euros in June 2026. Real Madrid hold an exclusive buy-back at 80 million euros, exercisable in the summer of 2027, and take a percentage of any onward sale if they decline it.

Guido Lorenzelli of CalcioComo added the same day that Como would only entertain 100 to 120 million euros, and attributed the Chelsea line to unnamed market whispers. On the Spanish side, Diego Vargas at Defensa Central reported the identical 60 and 80 million structure and said Madrid have not decided. Status: concrete interest at scouting level, no bid. The sequencing is the story: if Madrid press the button at 80 million, they buy a 22-year-old playmaker for less than Como could get on the open market, and Como's upside evaporates.

Around the Leagues

Premier League

Seven from seven, a regulator, and a reckoning

Manchester City have won all seven of their Premier League matches under Enzo Maresca, who was appointed on June 29 on a three-year deal after Chelsea received more than 17 million pounds in compensation and after Pep Guardiola stepped down in May following a decade at the club. City beat Ipswich Town 3-2 on Saturday, lead by three points on 21, and have a goal difference of plus 12.

Erling Haaland leads the division with eight goals. All of which is happening against a backdrop that nobody at the club can control: an independent Premier League commission upheld 114 of the 115 charges relating to financial rule breaches across 2009-10 to 2017-18, with the decision issued on September 29 and first reported by David Ornstein of The Athletic on September 25.

The case moved again this week, and in a direction nobody had flagged. On October 21 Insider Sport reported that the UK Financial Reporting Council has opened inquiries into City's accounting, disclosed in a letter to the House of Commons. It is not yet a formal investigation, and the FRC can sanction individuals rather than the club. The commission's findings said City inflated sponsorship revenue by up to 830 million pounds using undisclosed owner funding, while also concluding that the auditors were unaware. Two days earlier the same outlet set out City's appeal grounds.

Chief executive Ferran Soriano said the League's case rests on "a single false accusation, that the owner's personal money was somehow and secretly put into the club," that "this is just not true," and that the club has supplied "bank statements, money transfers, witnesses, and everything necessary." City appealed on October 1, and a ruling is not expected before late January. Unlike the 2020 UEFA case City won, the Court of Arbitration for Sport is not available: Premier League disputes run as private arbitration and an appeal board's decision is final. No sanction has been decided, and no credible outlet has reported a specific one. The points-deduction figures circulating trace to tabloid sourcing.

Underneath City, the division is stranger than the table suggests. Brighton are third on 16 points with a goal difference of plus 18, the best in the league, after dismantling Crystal Palace 5-0 on Sunday; Fabian Hurzeler was September's manager of the month. Chelsea under Xabi Alonso are fourth on 13 after winning 2-0 at Everton, and Liverpool, in Andoni Iraola's first season, fifth on 12 after a 2-1 win at Brentford, with Alexander Isak on five goals. Then the oddity: Manchester United are 13th on seven points after a 1-1 draw at Leeds United and Wednesday's 4-1 defeat at Como, with Michael Carrick under more pressure than any manager in the league.

Newcastle United, now managed by Matthias Jaissle after Eddie Howe resigned in August, are 11th and lost 1-0 at home to Aston Villa. Tottenham Hotspur beat Coventry City 3-1 on Monday. Sunderland are bottom on four. And at Everton, David Moyes said on October 16 that his own contract talks have hit "a bit of a lull," linking it to the Friedkin Group putting the club up for sale: "It's not the most important thing to me. I just want to keep driving Everton on."

 

La Liga

Nine from nine, and a Clasico without wingers

Barcelona have won all nine, scored 30 more than they have conceded and sit on 27 points. Atletico Madrid are second on 22 after a 1-0 win at Espanyol, and Real Madrid third on 21 after beating Sevilla 4-1 at the Bernabeu on Sunday. Barcelona won 5-2 at Real Betis the day before.

Raphinha leads the Pichichi on twelve, three ahead of Kylian Mbappe. The two Real Madrid league defeats came at Atletico Madrid, 1-2, and the pattern of their season is a side that wins handsomely and then loses the big one, which is precisely the pattern Jose Mourinho was hired to break.

Elsewhere Villarreal drew 0-0 with Elche and Valencia drew 0-0 with Athletic Club, which tells you most of what you need to know about the division's middle. Real Betis are fourth on 19, Real Sociedad fifth on 16. At the bottom, Malaga are the only side yet to win, on four points from nine with four draws, and Valencia are 19th on six. Promoted Racing Santander and Deportivo de A Coruna are both still in the fight, with Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang on five goals for Depor.

 

Bundesliga

Four points cover the top four, and Gladbach have none

Six matchdays in, Bayern Munich and Borussia Dortmund lead on 14 points, with SC Freiburg and Bayer Leverkusen on 13. Bayern announced themselves on Saturday with a 6-1 destruction of RB Leipzig, which remains the result of the German season. Leverkusen beat Freiburg 2-1 to join the group. Dortmund have not won in three weeks, drawing 0-0 at Union Berlin after a 2-2 with Werder Bremen, which is why Niko Kovac spent his press conference pointing at the table rather than the performances. Patrik Schick of Leverkusen leads the scoring charts on five.

The defining story, though, is at the other end. Borussia Monchengladbach have lost all six and have zero points, with eight goals scored and twenty conceded. Per Felix Kunkel of SPORT1, losing the first six is only the fourth time it has happened in Bundesliga history, after Fortuna Dusseldorf in 1991-92, Mainz 05 in 2020-21 and Bochum in 2022-23, and only Dusseldorf were relegated. A seventh straight defeat, at VfB Stuttgart on Friday, would make them the first club ever to be pointless after seven matchdays. The previous floor is one point.

Sporting director Rouven Schroder is not pretending. "Bei aller Kritik und allem, was wir haben, werden wir den Laden morgen nicht zusperren," he told 90min. For all the criticism, we are not shutting up shop tomorrow. He added that seven or eight players planned for the first eleven are unavailable "aber das ist kein Alibi," but that is no alibi, and that the remedy is psychological rather than financial: "Man muss in die Kopfe reinkommen und Selbstvertrauen einflossen."

Alexander Blessin, appointed on September 22, was franker still after the 1-2 home defeat by TSG Hoffenheim: "Wo soll ich anfangen? Wir haben teilweise Angsthasen-Fussball gespielt." Where should I start? We played scared-rabbit football at times. Sport Bild has reported a broken dressing room, cliques and senior players doubting teammates. Notably, reporting this week says a Stuttgart defeat is not expected to cost Blessin his job, and that sacking the sporting leadership is ruled out for now.

 

Serie A

Lazio level at the top, Juventus in open revolt

Inter Milan and Lazio lead on 19 points apiece after seven games, both unbeaten, with Roma on 17 and AC Milan on 15. Inter won 3-1 at Bologna; Roma beat Genoa 2-0; Milan edged Atalanta 3-2; Como won 3-1 at Fiorentina and are fifth on 14. The two results that mattered most, though, went against the league's biggest names. Venezia beat Napoli 1-0, and Lazio won 3-0 at Juventus through Oliver Provstgaard Nielsen, Gustav Isaksen and a late Fisayo Dele-Bashiru goal.

Juventus are eighth on eleven points, and the fallout has been extraordinary. Asked afterwards whether his squad still follows him, Luciano Spalletti said: "Se la squadra mi segue ancora? Fino a ieri avrei detto di si, oggi non lo so." Until yesterday I would have said yes. Today I don't know. His captain Bremer had already told reporters "non punto il dito, ma c'e qualcuno che davanti ai fischi ha paura," I'm not pointing the finger, but there is someone who is afraid when the whistles come.

Spalletti's reply: "Le sconfitte non salvano nessuno, c'e dentro fino al collo anche lui." Defeats save nobody, he is in it up to his neck as well. Reporting says Bremer will be fined over the row. By Wednesday Spalletti had changed tack, telling his pre-Rennes press conference: "Questa volta li ho fatti parlare tutti, io son stato zitto." This time I let them all speak and I kept quiet.

Maurizio De Santis of Fanpage reported on October 20 that Juventus have put Spalletti on pause with a short-term vote of confidence covering four league games and two Europa League ties before the November break, with Stefano Pioli as stopgap and Antonio Conte a future rather than immediate option. La Gazzetta dello Sport added Simone Inzaghi, now at Al-Hilal.

Cost is the brake: Spalletti's contract runs to 2028 at around 5 million euros net a year. But the best-sourced line of the week knocks all of it down. Gianluca Di Marzio wrote on October 20: "Cambia tutto? No. Dalle societa filtra fiducia. Ne la Juventus ne il Napoli hanno pensato o lavorato su soluzioni alternative." Neither Juventus nor Napoli has thought about or worked on alternatives.

Napoli are ninth on ten points, a fifth defeat in nine matches, and the financial arithmetic is as much a shield for Massimiliano Allegri as the reporting is. Marco Conterio of TUTTOmercatoWEB calculated on October 19 that Allegri's deal to June 2029 at 4.5 million euros net leaves roughly 25 million gross outstanding with no cheap termination route, and ANSA reported Aurelio De Laurentiis has ruled out a change, describing one as close to lethal financially.

Allegri's own diagnosis, to Sky: "De Laurentiis l'ho sentito anche stamattina, le parole servono a poco. Non possiamo pensare di giocare una stagione con undici calciatori. Il problema e che prendiamo gol troppo facilmente." The injury list supports him. Scott McTominay missed six weeks after Napoli announced on September 1 that he would undergo a pulsed-field ablation to correct a mild benign arrhythmia, and regained his sporting certification on October 7. Alessandro Buongiorno is not expected back before January. Napoli meet Roma on Saturday.

 

Ligue 1

Five clubs, fourteen points, and a torn knee in Paris

Something has happened in France that has never happened before. After seven matchdays Olympique Lyonnais, Paris Saint-Germain, Paris FC, Lille and Monaco all have 14 points from four wins, two draws and one defeat, separated only by goal difference, with Lyon top on plus eight. Per beIN Sports, five clubs have never been level at the top this deep into a Ligue 1 season. PSG have won twelve of the last fourteen titles and have built no lead at all. Rennes are sixth on 13. Marseille are ninth on nine.

Then came Tuesday night. PSG beat Barcelona 4-2 at the Parc des Princes through Desire Doue, Lucas Hernandez, Vitinha and Fabian Ruiz, a third straight Champions League win, and left the pitch with their season reshaped. PSG confirmed on Wednesday that Khvicha Kvaratskhelia has ruptured the anterior cruciate ligament in his right knee, sustained early in the second half as he sprinted back to tackle, and that he will undergo surgery in the coming days. Romano's read is that his season is over. Ousmane Dembele, who pulled up with a left thigh injury in the 19th minute, and captain Marquinhos, who came off on the hour with a right thigh problem, are both out for several weeks per the club's own bulletin.

Nuno Mendes was already absent, confirmed by AP on October 18 as facing weeks out. Luis Enrique afterwards: "I have the best players in the world. They are the best for me." He now has rather fewer of them. Foot Mercato noted on Wednesday that PSG's only natural left winger remaining is Mika Godts, signed for 40 million euros, who has one assist in seven appearances and 203 minutes. No external January target has been reported.

Off the pitch, Olympique Lyonnais published annual results on Wednesday for the year ended June 30: revenue down 19 per cent to 222.3 million euros, a net loss attributable to shareholders of 87.3 million, financial debt of 409.8 million and equity of minus 248.3 million. That is a sharp improvement on the prior year's 201.2 million loss, driven by a 36 per cent cut in personnel costs and 242.2 million of debt extinguishment, offset by a 205.1 million write-down on receivables from Botafogo and the Eagle Football entities.

The DNCG cleared Lyon for this season on June 26 but capped the wage bill, which is the context for Paulo Fonseca's unresolved extension: sporting director Matthieu Louis-Jean said after the 2-1 win over Nice that "on discute toujours avec le coach" and "on a toujours dit qu'on prenait notre temps." Fonseca's deal expires in June 2027. John Textor and Eagle Football Holdings served a 20.9 million euro claim in the Lyon commercial court on July 30.

At Paris FC, Pierre Ferracci announced on the evening of October 19 that he is stepping down as president after fourteen years, handing over to Antoine Arnault subject to board ratification on November 9. He told L'Equipe, quoted by Foot Mercato: "C'est le bon moment pour moi de passer la main. Antoine Arnault est pret." And: "Le club est entre de bonnes mains et je suis content de faire cette annonce quand il est en haut du classement."

His final match as president is expected to be against Monaco on November 1. Separately the LFP has opened the tender for Ligue 1's domestic television rights for 2027 to 2032, bidding running from November 3 to December 7, with a recent legislative change allowing a single exclusive bundle. Vincent Labrune cited "a certain number of registered concrete interests from different actors."

 

MLS

Nashville chase a record, Messi chases history, and the playoffs are new

Nashville SC are one win or draw from the Supporters' Shield with four matches left. They have 67 points and twenty wins, are unbeaten in six, and missed their chance to clinch on October 17 when they drew 1-1 at San Jose Earthquakes. Per MLSsoccer.com, they need eight points from those four games to set the league's all-time points record. Their next match is Saturday at CF Montreal.

Inter Miami are second in the East on 55 points and can no longer catch them: even a perfect finish would tie Nashville on points, and total wins is the first tiebreaker, where Nashville's twenty is out of reach. Lionel Messi scored twice in the 3-1 win at Atlanta United to reach 25 goals for the year and, at 39, a career total of 936.

He has scored or assisted in each of his last twelve MLS matches and has 40 goal contributions this season. Miami's likely first-round opponent is seventh-placed Orlando City, who have Antoine Griezmann and beat D.C. United 4-1.

The playoff format has changed, and it matters. The top nine in each conference qualify, seeds one to seven go straight into Round One, and seeds eight and nine meet in a single Wild Card match. Round One is a best-of-three series with no aggregate score, ties going straight to a shoot-out, and later rounds are single elimination. Decision Day is November 7, the Wild Card matches are on November 18, and MLS Cup is December 18.

Projected seeding as of Monday morning had Nashville, Inter Miami, New England Revolution, Chicago Fire, Charlotte FC, Philadelphia Union, Orlando City, New York Red Bulls and FC Cincinnati in the East, and Vancouver Whitecaps, St. Louis CITY SC, FC Dallas, San Jose Earthquakes, Houston Dynamo, LAFC, Seattle Sounders, LA Galaxy and Colorado Rapids in the West.
