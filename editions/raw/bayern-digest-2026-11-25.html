The Lead

Champions League preview

Without Kane and Olise, Bayern begin a winter of improvisation in Lille

Harry Kane is out until late winter, Michael Olise is hurt and six senior players miss tonight's trip to northern France. Vincent Kompany says the result cannot depend on one returning winger. What Bayern can still be, and what they cannot, starts to become clear at 12:00 PT.

For a club built to beat opponents with a striker who scores every week, Bayern Munich now have to find another way. Harry Kane tore the syndesmosis ligament in his left ankle during the 1-1 home draw with 1. FC Köln last Friday, caught by Joel Schmied in the 15th minute and forced off five minutes later. He was operated on that night, and sporting director Max Eberl has since put the absence at three to three and a half months, according to Ligainsider and Sportschau.

The same evening took Michael Olise off at half-time with a bruised forefoot. He left the Allianz Arena on crutches and, like Kane, did not travel to Lille, the club confirmed in its official preview. Manuel Neuer (foot tendon), Serge Gnabry (thigh), Ismael Saibari (ankle surgery) and Alphonso Davies (hamstring) also stayed in Munich.

Kompany put the scale of it in a single number on Tuesday. "I think we scored around 180 goals last year. About 130 of those goals are now gone," he said, as reported by Yahoo Sports. His answer to that gap was not a formation but a temperament: "What we do have is players who can run and fight."

The match: who leads the line?

The selection question is obvious and unresolved. Against Köln, Tom Bischof replaced Kane and later Lennart Karl was pushed forward; neither looked like a natural No. 9. Luis Díaz is fit again after a minor hamstring tear, but Kompany said he and Jamal Musiala are not ready to start. "I'm delighted that Lucho is back, but tomorrow's result cannot depend on Lucho," he said.

That suggests a front line built on movement rather than a target. Nathaniel Brown, signed as a left-back, has been playing on the left wing out of necessity and scored the equaliser against Köln. Tim Binder, 19, made his Bundesliga debut on the right that night. Expect Kompany to ask his midfield, Joshua Kimmich and Aleksandar Pavlović, to supply shots from deep as well as possession.

I'm not a fan of panic football; we want to keep things calm for now.

Vincent Kompany after the Köln draw, via fcbayern.com

The warning sign from Friday is efficiency. Bayern had 65 percent of the ball and 16 shots against Köln but only two on target, and the Bundesliga data gave the visitors 2.07 expected goals to Bayern's 1.10. Jonas Urbig's saves kept the point. Against a Lille side that concedes freely in Europe, a repeat of that profile could still be enough, but not by much.

The stakes and the context

Bayern went into matchday 5 on 8 points from four games and, after Tuesday's results, around 14th in the 36-team table per Goal. The top eight go straight to the last 16. With Slavia Prague at home on December 8 still to come, three points tonight would put Bayern back in range of that group.

Lille arrive in the opposite position: 35th of 36 with one point, yet third in Ligue 1. Their coach, Davide Ancelotti, knows this club from the inside, having worked at Säbener Straße on his father Carlo's staff in 2016-17. He said his side "must prepare for Bayern as a team and not focus on individual players they have" (fcbayern.com preview). With Kane gone, there is less for anyone to focus on.

There is also the man on the touchline. Kompany returned against Köln after missing five matches through illness, his arm still in a sling from a shoulder injury; assistant Aaron Danks went unbeaten in his place. Kompany's goal for the run to Christmas is plain, as he told fcbayern.com: "It's a chance to secure wins in a different way. That's what I'd like to achieve before Christmas."

Off the pitch

The AGM where the Musiala row went public

Record merchandising, thinner profit and an unscheduled defence of Jamal Musiala from the floor: Saturday's annual general meeting showed a club arguing with itself.

The numbers FC Bayern presented at Saturday's Jahreshauptversammlung at BMW Park were solid rather than spectacular. Group revenue for 2025-26 reached 988.2 million euros, up 1.0 percent, but pre-tax profit fell 16.9 percent to 35.3 million and net profit 15.9 percent to 22.8 million, chief executive Jan-Christian Dreesen reported on fcbayern.com. Merchandising hit a record 152.8 million.

Dreesen also restated the club's transfer stance: "Wir sind nach wie vor kein Verkäuferclub" (we are still not a selling club). He said the market had lost its bearings and Bayern would not chase it, leaning on youth instead. Members elected Marko Pešić second vice-president, replacing Walter Mennekes, and discharged the presidium by a large majority, according to the fcbinside live ticker.

The Musiala intervention

The moment people will remember came from a member. A neurologist, Jonatan Hoffmann, criticised club leaders for publicly writing off Jamal Musiala and drew loud applause, the Abendzeitung reported. The context: honorary president Uli Hoeneß had said on television that what Musiala can deliver now is no longer what his contract describes, and Karl-Heinz Rummenigge questioned the sums involved.

Eberl, Christoph Freund and Kompany have all backed the player, who made a neurological condition public at the start of the season. On Tuesday his advisers met Herbert Hainer, Dreesen and Eberl for about an hour, and on Wednesday the club issued a statement saying the player's health comes first for both sides and that he has its full support, the Abendzeitung reported. Eberl's summary: talk with Jamal, not about him.

The players' bodies have weighed in too. ZDF reported that FIFPro sees a coordinated strategy to pressure the player and that the German players' union VDV insists contracts must be honoured. Musiala, under contract to 2030, returned for his first league minutes since September against Köln. The pitch, as Kompany put it, will decide the rest.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Lille: bright in France, fragile in Europe

Davide Ancelotti's young side are third in Ligue 1 but have one point from four Champions League games.

LOSC Lille come into tonight in two different moods. Domestically they are third in Ligue 1 with 21 points from 11 games, behind Paris Saint-Germain and Paris FC, per fcbayern.com. In Europe they have lost to Real Betis, Arsenal and Galatasaray and drew 0-0 at Bodø/Glimt, and they lost 1-0 at home to Lyon on Saturday, their first home league defeat of the season.

Davide Ancelotti, appointed in the summer after years on his father's staffs, including at Bayern, sets up in a 4-3-3. The likely XI from Khel Now: Ozer; Ngoy, Nianzou, Alexsandro, Perraud; Mukau, Bentaleb, André; Bakwa, Haraldsson, Diaoune. Nianzou's fitness is disputed between sources.

The players to stop

Soriba Diaoune, 19, has two goals in three Champions League games and is the obvious outlet in transition. Hákon Haraldsson is the creator between the lines and had Lille's best chance against Lyon. From the bench, the veteran Olivier Giroud scored the late winner against Lens on October 30, and Ayase Ueda is back after more than a month out.

The vulnerabilities are in the numbers: ten goals conceded in four European games and no win in their last six European matches, per the BFW preview on Yahoo Sports. Tiago Santos and Hamza Igamane are out with cruciate ligament injuries. The only previous meetings came in the 2012-13 group stage, when Bayern won 1-0 in Lille and 6-1 in Munich.

Transfer News

Eberl's answer to Kane's injury: no blind activism

Bayern will look at the January market but say a signing is far from certain, and their real striker search points to next summer.

The first instinct after a long-term injury to a striker of Kane's output is to buy one. Bayern are resisting it. "Wir werden keinen blinden Aktionismus betreiben" (we will not act blindly), Max Eberl said, adding that whether Bayern sign anyone at all "has not been decided" and asking: "Which club gives up its best player in winter?" The quotes were reported by fcbinside and footy.de.

Herbert Hainer said winter signings are not the club's wish but that Bayern can react if they must, and Dreesen wants to see how the injured players return first, according to Sky Sport. Kompany declined to discuss a window two months away.

The names for later

The strikers attached to Bayern this week are longer-term targets. Sport Bild reports Bayern are watching Eintracht Frankfurt's Younes Ebnoutalib, 23, whose price is put at 30 to 40 million euros with a contract to 2031; a summer move is far likelier, and Dortmund are also keen (via SPOX). Status: monitoring, single-sourced.

Italian outlets link Bayern with Club Brugge's Nicolò Tresoldi, 22, at around 60 million, with Milan competing (Football Italia via Yahoo). Gazzetta also says interest in Nick Woltemade remains, but he has already played for two clubs this season and could not join before summer 2027 (fcbinside). All three sit at the rumour stage.

The goalkeeping question

With Neuer out until after Christmas and both he and Sven Ulreich contracted only to June 2027, goalkeeping is the other live file. Sky names Hoffenheim's Oliver Baumann, 36, as the preferred candidate to back up Jonas Urbig, with Fulham's Bernd Leno and Schalke's Loris Karius also mentioned. Kicker reports Bayern will talk to Neuer and Ulreich before Christmas (GFFN summary).

Not for sale

Hainer told t-online that Michael Olise, under contract to 2029, would not be sold even for up to 300 million euros, and said he had discussed the winger with Real Madrid president Florentino Pérez in the summer (via the ran news ticker). Jonathan Tah, linked with Barcelona, is also contracted to 2029 and not available.

The Squad

Kompany's patched-up XI and the Urbig era

Six senior players are out, a teenager is starting on the wing and Bayern's No. 1 shirt has a new occupant for now.

Bayern lined up against Köln in what the listed positions suggest was a 4-2-3-1: Urbig; Laimer, Upamecano, Tah, Stanišić; Kimmich, Pavlović; Olise, Karl, Brown; Kane. It was unchanged from the win at Mainz, and Joshua Kimmich wore the armband in Neuer's absence. With Kane and Olise gone, two of the four attacking places now need new owners.

Vincent Kompany is back on the bench after missing five matches with illness in late October and November; Aaron Danks won four and drew one in his place, including 2-0 at Atlético Madrid and 6-1 against Magdeburg in the DFB-Pokal (theScore).

The goalkeeper debate

Jonas Urbig has been No. 1 since Neuer partially tore a tendon in his foot at Mainz on November 7, and Friday was his best game yet. Didi Hamann has already asked whether Neuer, who turns 41 in March and is contracted to 2027, gets the shirt back on his return, as reported by Volksstimme.

Form and the attack

Before his injury Kane led the squad in assists, with ESPN counting eight across the season, but SPOX noted Friday was his fifth straight game without a goal. Olise had been Bayern's top Bundesliga scorer with six. Brown's equaliser was a reminder that the summer signing, bought as a full-back, is now one of the more dangerous players in the side. Upamecano, Kompany said, has "been delivering consistently over the past few months."

The Campus

Two debuts on a bad night, and a Youth League trip today

Injuries are opening first-team doors for Bayern's academy, while Bayern II and the U19s carry on below.

The Köln draw handed Tim Binder, 19, his Bundesliga debut at half-time, a month after his first competitive appearance in the DFB-Pokal win over Magdeburg, and gave 19-year-old Codjo-Evora his professional debut on 87 minutes. In that cup game, David Santos Daiber scored his first senior goal (fcbayern.com). Sixteen-year-old Bastian Assomo, with four goals for Bayern II, is tipped as next.

Bayern II are 11th in the Regionalliga Bayern with 24 points from 16 games, with up to three games in hand on most rivals; leaders FC Memmingen have 42. Saturday's trip to Eltersdorf was called off. They host Burghausen on November 29 (table).

The U19s top their DFB-Nachwuchsliga preliminary group with 24 points from 10 games. In the UEFA Youth League they have four points from four games (6-1 against Bodø/Glimt, 3-3 with Arsenal, defeats at Viking and Atlético) and play Lille today at 15:00 local time, 06:00 PT.

FC Bayern Women

Eleven from eleven, and a record within reach

FC Bayern Women beat Hoffenheim 1-0 on Monday to stay perfect in the league and equal the men's Bundesliga unbeaten run.

FC Bayern Women needed only an eighth-minute goal from forward Imade, scored from close range after Hoffenheim failed to clear a cross, to beat their visitors 1-0 at the Campus on Monday. Goalkeeper Mahmutovic made two important saves and Hoffenheim keeper Ann-Katrin Dick cleared a Klara Bühl shot off the line (Sportschau).

It was an 11th league win from 11 and restored a six-point lead over Wolfsburg. The team is now unbeaten in 53 league games, equalling the men's Bundesliga record from November 2012 to March 2014; a home win over Nürnberg on December 11 would break it. Lena Oberdorf returned from a knee problem as a late substitute: "It feels great to be back on the pitch."

Europe and a contract to watch

Last Thursday's 4-0 win at Austria Wien, with goals from Bühl, Pernille Harder, Alara Şehitler and Proost, came in front of 9,124 fans, a record for an Austrian women's club match. Harder's goal was her 70th for Bayern (fcbayern.com). Coach José Barcala's side are third in the league phase with 11 points and host Barcelona on December 16.

Off the pitch, Klara Bühl, the team's top scorer with ten goals in all competitions as of mid-November, told Sport Bild she has not decided on a contract that ends in 2027 and will decide over the winter break, as reported by t-online. Barcelona have repeatedly been linked.

Players Away

Eighteen Bayern women head for international duty

No Bayern men are on international duty this week; the women's window opens on Friday, and the men's November games left one lasting cost.

Bayern's women have 18 players called up, per Mia san rot. Germany, with Giulia Gwinn as captain plus Mahmutovic, Kett, Bühl, Dallmann and newly called-up Alara, play Argentina in La Plata (Sunday, 15:15 PT) and Peru in Rio de Janeiro (Friday, December 4, 13:15 PT). Alara replaces Wolfsburg's injured Sophia Kleinherne, her first senior call-up in about a year.

Elsewhere, Proost's Netherlands, Viggósdóttir's Iceland and Austria's Bayern trio of Zadrazil, Dunst and Naschenweng play two-legged World Cup play-offs: the Netherlands on December 1 and 4, Iceland and Austria on December 1 and 5. Imade is with Spain for friendlies against Chile and Venezuela, Harder and Ballisager with Denmark, Tanikawa with Japan against Brazil and Amani with Ivory Coast against Senegal.

The men's window, in brief

Last week's round-up covered the men's November games in full. The headline items: Harry Kane became England's most-capped men's player with his 126th appearance against Croatia (Al Jazeera), and Germany beat the Netherlands 5-0 with Kimmich, Tah, Pavlović and Brown starting (fcbayern.com).

The cost remains Alphonso Davies, who tore a muscle fibre in his left hamstring after six minutes for Canada in Guatemala and had feared surgery, though Volksstimme reports he can avoid an operation. Kompany would not put a timeline on his return.
