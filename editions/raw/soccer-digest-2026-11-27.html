The Lead

Nineteenth, and Out of Excuses: Pochettino's Real Work Starts at Old Trafford

Manchester United have eight points from eleven league games and the worst defence in Europe's big five leagues. Brentford arrive on Saturday for the first home match of the Pochettino era. January will not come in time to fix this.

There is a version of Manchester United's season that can still be rescued, and it does not begin with a signing. It begins at three o'clock on Saturday afternoon in Manchester, against a Brentford side that put four past Everton last weekend and sit sixth.

United are 19th. They have eight points from eleven matches, one win, and a goal difference of minus nine. One point separates them from safety. It is the first time since November 1986 that the club have been in the bottom two having played at least ten league games.

Mauricio Pochettino has had five days of actual coaching time with them. His first match was the 4-0 defeat at Anfield on November 22, the heaviest loss by a United manager in his first game since 1926. His second was a 2-2 draw at Sporting on Wednesday that he described himself as a source of pride.

What Saturday actually asks of him

Brentford are the sort of opponent that exposes exactly what is wrong with this United team. They are direct, they are ruthless from set pieces, and they do not need possession to hurt you. United have conceded 34 goals in all competitions, more than any side in the top five European leagues.

Pochettino's trademark is a high, man-oriented press. That is also precisely the thing his own captain said the team was not being asked to do under the previous regime. After the 4-1 defeat at Como in October, Bruno Fernandes went on the record about it.

It's a decision from the staff to not press man to man. That's on the manager and staff to see it, not me. Bruno Fernandes, to TNT Sports after Como 4-1 Manchester United, October 21

Whatever one makes of a captain saying that in public, it tells you what Pochettino inherited: a squad that had stopped believing in its instructions. Installing a press takes weeks. Installing the fitness to sustain one takes longer. He has neither.

One piece of good news

Leny Yoro is available. The Football Association overturned his eighth-minute dismissal at Anfield on Tuesday, November 24, accepting United's claim of wrongful dismissal. The FA's statement was brief: his "one-match suspension has been removed following a successful claim of wrongful dismissal."

That matters more than it sounds. United's defensive record is catastrophic partly because they keep playing without their best defenders. Yoro's red card turned the Anfield game inside of ten minutes, and Szoboszlai scored from the free kick that followed.

The structural problem no press can solve

At Sporting, goalkeeper Senne Lammens saved a Luis Suárez penalty, then conceded another one 75 seconds later by fouling Araujo, then spilled a ball in his own box for Suárez to equalise at 80 minutes. Bruno Fernandes had scored twice in three minutes after the break. United still did not win.

Pochettino's response was telling. He talked about "responsibility," and then immediately added that the defensive line needed to cover the goalkeeper better. It was a manager declining to hang a 23-year-old out to dry while making it clear the problem is collective.

United sit 19th of 36 in the Champions League league phase on seven points, with Leipzig at Old Trafford on December 8 still a real chance to reach the knockout play-offs. But the league is the job. Six points from the next two would take them to fourteen, which is more than every side currently 13th or below. That is the arithmetic of the thing. It starts Saturday.

Maresca's City Have Stopped Winning, and Sunday Will Not Be Kind

An eight-game winning start has given way to one win in seven in regulation time. Manchester City go to the league leaders on Sunday six points adrift.

Enzo Maresca began his Manchester City tenure with eight straight wins. According to the Associated Press, City have now won once in their last seven matches in regulation time across all competitions, and that run includes a penalty shootout victory over Brighton in the League Cup.

Four Premier League games have passed without a win. The 2-2 draw with Napoli in the Champions League on Tuesday was a tenth consecutive game without a clean sheet. City are third on 22 points, level with Brighton but behind on goal difference, and six behind the leaders.

Erling Haaland remains the division's leading scorer on nine goals, two clear of Alexander Isak. He has also been unusually blunt, saying the team is not performing and that it hurts, and calling Sunday's trip "the most difficult game of the season."

Maresca's own framing has been that the two clubs are at different stages of their projects and cannot be compared, and that he is "not a magician." Both things can be true and neither will help much at 8:30am Pacific on Sunday. City beat Manchester United and Liverpool away earlier this season. They have not looked like that team for a month.

Transfer News

The Window Opens in Five Weeks, and Three Clubs Have Already Been Forced Into It

United's January Is Already Written, and It Starts With a Number Nine

Fabrizio Romano says Pochettino will be in the room for the planning. Two or three signings, striker first.

Romano reported this week that United began their January planning conversations with Pochettino involved from the moment he arrived, despite his title being head coach rather than manager. His summary of the brief was direct: "Mauricio Pochettino needs and wants a new striker in the January transfer window."

The reasoning is unavoidable. Benjamin Šeško is out until January after his shin problem returned, Joshua Zirkzee is not trusted to fill in, and Bryan Mbeumo has been operating as the number nine. Romano expects "around two to three signings," with a centre-back and a left-back also under consideration "based on opportunities."

On the names, Romano is cooler than the headlines. Of Folarin Balogun he said on November 16 that "my understanding is that, at the moment, there is no active conversation taking place," while adding that he "would not be surprised if Balogun gets offered to Man Utd because the relationship with his agents is a good relationship." On Bournemouth's Evanilson he was blunter still, saying a mid-season sale is not realistic and that the summer "could be different." Both are links rather than deals.

Two Ruptured Knees Have Rewritten Paris Saint-Germain's Winter

Desire Doué has ruptured an anterior cruciate ligament. Khvicha Kvaratskhelia is already out with the same injury.

Doué scored in PSG's 3-2 win over Nice and was taken off before half-time after twisting his right knee in a challenge with Sofiane Diop. The club confirmed a ruptured anterior cruciate ligament in the right knee, with surgery before the end of November. Luis Enrique called it a "serious injury."

No official timeline has been set. Reports suggesting around six months would put him out for the rest of PSG's season and France's Nations League quarter-final against Spain in March. With Kvaratskhelia also a long-term absentee with an ACL injury, PSG are down two first-choice wide forwards at once, with Ousmane Dembélé only now nearing a return.

Romano's read: "Bayern and PSG can now seriously consider attacking the market in the January transfer window." For a club that top Ligue 1 and sit second in the Champions League league phase on thirteen points, this is not a rebuild. It is an emergency winger search, and every selling club in Europe knows it.

Bayern's Winter Is About One Player, and It Is Not a Signing

Jamal Musiala's health, his contract, and a players' union accusing the club of applying pressure.

Musiala collapsed during pre-season games in August. He later said publicly that he had been diagnosed with "absence seizures caused by a neurological dysfunction," that it posed no health risk, and that he intended to keep playing. He has barely featured since, with a muscle injury compounding matters, though he travelled to Lille and had a late effort there.

Bayern officials met his agents on Tuesday, November 24. Max Eberl, the board member for sport, told dpa the talks were "very, very constructive" and said the discussion centred on health and recovery rather than wages, with the club's priority to "give Jamal this trust."

That framing is contested. On the Saturday before the meeting, the global players' union FIFPRO accused Bayern of "a coordinated strategy to put pressure on Musiala," adding that "Contracts are there to be respected" and that injury or a dip in form gives a club no grounds to reopen agreed terms. Reporting suggests some senior figures at the club have questioned whether last year's extension still reflects the player.

Separately, Romano says Bayern's previous reluctance to buy in January has softened under the weight of injuries, and that a decision on targets will come "close to Christmas" rather than in November. Bayern lost for the first time all season on Wednesday, 2-1 at Lille.

RB Leipzig Are the Window's Most Important Selling Club

Antonio Nusa is priced out of a January move. The teammate behind him is not.

Romano's position on Nusa, as of November 16, is that Leipzig "are not pushing to sell," that a January move is "not particularly concrete," and that the club "will not offer any discount." The starting price is around €70m to €75m. His conclusion: "Either there is a completely crazy bid or, for Nusa, it's going to be the summer."

Set against that, FootballTransfers reported on November 26 that Chelsea had reached a January agreement for the Norwegian. That is a single outlet, it contradicts the best-sourced account, and it should be read as a claim rather than a development until someone of Romano's or David Ornstein's standing repeats it.

The gettable one is Johan Bakayoko. Romano says the winger "is no longer untouchable" and could cost under €20m in January. Leipzig, fourth in the Bundesliga on 21 points and fresh from a 5-1 win at Union Berlin and a 3-1 defeat of Lens in Europe, can afford to sell one and keep the other.

The Rumour Mill

Everything still simmering, with its certainty labelled. Read the tier, not the headline.

Agreed, and officially so

Sidy Barhama Ndiaye to Liverpool. Romano gave this his "here we go" on November 25: "Liverpool have verbally agreed deal to sign 16 year old winger Sidy Barhama Ndiaye." The Senegalese joins in January 2028, when he turns 18. Worth noting the nuance: The Athletic's James Pearce described it as Liverpool having "secured an option" rather than a completed agreement. Ndiaye was on trial at Liverpool in 2025 and scored and assisted twice in Senegal's 9-0 win over Cuba at the Under-17 World Cup.

Mohamed Zongo to Chelsea. Romano reports the deal for the Burkina Faso midfielder as fully agreed, having previously been expected to go to Strasbourg. No fee reported. Single-sourced on the detail.

Concrete interest, no agreement

Endrick, away from Real Madrid. Romano says AC Milan are "in love" with the Brazilian and are waiting to see whether Real Madrid will sanction a January loan. Liverpool and Aston Villa have also been linked. Nothing agreed anywhere.

Alex Scott to Chelsea. talkSPORT reports Chelsea as "clear frontrunners" for the Bournemouth midfielder in January, with Liverpool the main rival, and says Bournemouth want around £100m for a mid-season sale. Scott has hinted he would rather stay for Bournemouth's European campaign, and given that they now top their Europa League league phase, that is not a throwaway line.

Rumour and link

Antonio Rüdiger back to Chelsea. Football Insider reports Chelsea are weighing a return for the defender, who is out of contract in July 2027. Rumour tier.

Alejandro Garnacho's loan at Aston Villa. The Chelsea-owned winger was left out of a Champions League squad, and the BBC has suggested the arrangement "already looks to be over" despite Villa holding a conditional agreement to buy at the end of the loan.

Joshua Zirkzee out of Old Trafford. The domino. Romano has been explicit that United want a striker in January "regardless" of what happens with Zirkzee, but an exit would accelerate everything.

Around the Leagues

Around the Leagues

Premier League

The team nobody predicted is second

Brighton are second. Arturo Ruiz's side have 26 league goals, more than anyone in the division, and a goal difference of plus seventeen that puts them ahead of Manchester City on the same 22 points. They also lost 2-0 at Hull City last weekend, which is the kind of result that tells you this is a very good attacking team rather than a finished one.

Behind them, Liverpool are fourth on 21 and look like a club that has stopped arguing with itself. Andoni Iraola's 4-0 win over United was their most complete performance of the season, built on 28 shots and a Florian Wirtz display that German media seized on. They then won 3-0 at Club Brugge to reach twelve points in the Champions League. Romano reports the Anfield hierarchy are "delighted" with Iraola and already discussing transfer plans for 2027 and beyond.

The bottom is a genuine fight

Only four points covered 13th to 20th going into this round. Crystal Palace are last on seven, though Thomas Frank, appointed November 16, got his first win on Thursday, a 2-1 Europa League defeat of ten-man Real Sociedad. Nottingham Forest under Oliver Glasner and Sunderland sit on nine, one above United. Promoted Coventry City and Hull City have both already taken scalps.

Elsewhere: Brentford's 4-0 dismantling of Everton lifted them to sixth, Tottenham beat Ipswich 2-0, and Chelsea under Xabi Alonso open this round at the City Ground on Friday night. Newcastle, now managed by Matthias Jaissle, drew 2-2 at home with the leaders and are 13th, which is its own small story.

La Liga

Barcelona have twelve wins from thirteen matches and no draws at all. They also had to come from 2-0 down at home to Villarreal on Sunday to keep it that way. Tajon Buchanan and Tani Oluwaseyi scored inside seven minutes; Gerard Martín, Fermín López and a Pau Navarro own goal answered inside eleven after the break. Lamine Yamal hit the post from the penalty spot and had a hand in two of the three.

Hansi Flick's side lead on 36 points with a goal difference of plus thirty-six. Raphinha is the division's leading scorer on fifteen. José Mourinho's Real Madrid are second on 33, also without a draw all season, and thrashed PSV 5-1 in Europe on Tuesday; Kylian Mbappé has thirteen league goals. Diego Simeone's Atlético are third on 31 after winning 3-0 at Getafe.

The fixture to note is not this weekend's. Barcelona visit Deportivo A Coruña on Sunday, then host Atlético on Wednesday December 2 in a rearranged match that is the first real test of whether this is a title race at all.

Bundesliga

Two clubs are unbeaten and only one of them is Bayern. Borussia Dortmund lead on 24 points from ten games after Felix Nmecha's 80th-minute header beat Mönchengladbach 1-0, his third goal in the final fifteen minutes this season. Niko Kovaç was honest about it: "Based on our second-half performance, I think we deserved to win, but of course, it was still a lucky goal."

Dortmund have conceded seven goals in ten matches, fewest in the division, with six clean sheets. Bayern are second on 22, unbeaten domestically but held 1-1 at home by Cologne and then beaten 2-1 at Lille in the Champions League, their first defeat in any competition this season. Freiburg are also on 22, Leipzig on 21 after that 5-1 at Union Berlin. Niclas Füllkrug and Patrik Schick share the scoring lead on nine. Bayern go to Hamburg on Saturday.

Serie A

Both Rome clubs are unbeaten after twelve matches. Lazio lead on 32 points under Gennaro Gattuso with ten wins and two draws, a record built on a defence that has conceded nine. Gian Piero Gasperini's Roma are second on 30, unbeaten but with three draws, and Donyell Malen's twelve goals lead the league.

Inter are third on 29 and threw away a game they had won twice over. At Atalanta they led 2-0, then 3-1, then 4-2, and drew 4-4. Pio Esposito scored his first Serie A hat-trick and it counted for nothing. Cristian Chivu did not soften it: Inter "failed the exam," were "too soft," and needed to be "more determined in the duels." Hakan Calhanoglu went off injured in the first half.

Rúben Amorim's Milan are fourth on 27 after beating Frosinone 5-1 and winning 4-2 at Olympiacos. Cesc Fàbregas's Como are fifth on 26 and third in the Champions League. Luciano Spalletti's Juventus are sixth on 21 and beat Omonia 4-1 in the Europa League. Lazio's unbeaten run goes to Torino on Saturday.

Ligue 1

Paris Saint-Germain top the table on 26 points from eleven, and the two ACL injuries above are the whole story of their winter. Behind them, promoted Paris FC are second on 23, with Lassine Sinayoko the league's leading scorer on nine and a 4-0 win over Angers to their name. Lille are third on 21 and spent Wednesday beating Bayern Munich, Ethan Mbappé striking the post in the move that led to the winning own goal.

Lyon are fourth on 20 after winning 1-0 at Lille and drawing 0-0 with Lillestrøm in Europe. Marseille are fifth, and had the weekend's most embarrassing result anywhere: 2-0 up at home at half-time through Amine Gouiri and Igor Paixão, beaten 3-2 by promoted Le Mans, with Jean Vercruysse scoring in stoppage time. It ended a five-match winning run and Roberto De Zerbi's side dropped out of the European places.

Major League Soccer

The best-of-three first round has produced one genuine shock and several near misses. Columbus Crew, the seventh seed in the East, won 3-0 at Inter Miami in Game 1, with Gonzalo Tapia scoring twice and Taha Habroune once after Gonzalo Luján's 36th-minute red card. The reigning MLS Cup champions and Lionel Messi can be eliminated tonight in Ohio.

Nashville, the East's top seed, lost 1-0 at home to ninth-seeded FC Cincinnati. Charlotte won 2-1 at Chicago. New England beat Philadelphia 2-0, Vancouver beat Real Salt Lake 2-0, FC Dallas beat Portland 3-0, San Jose beat Houston 2-1, and St. Louis beat LAFC 3-2 despite a Denis Bouanga penalty and a late Son Heung-Min free kick. Every Game 2 is played tonight and Saturday. Conference semifinals are December 5 and 6; MLS Cup is December 18. This is the last full season before the league moves to a summer-spring calendar.

In Brief

In Brief

Bournemouth top their Europa League group. Marco Rose's side won 2-1 at Celta Vigo with ten men to go five from five in the league phase, Álvaro Rodríguez and Rayan scoring after Iago Aspas had opened.

Benfica humiliated in Plzen. Viktoria Plzen led 3-0 at half-time and won 4-0, one of the heaviest European defeats of Benfica's modern era.

Real Madrid 5-1 PSV. Mourinho's side lead the Champions League league phase on fifteen points, the only club with a perfect record after five matches.

Slovan Bratislava remain on zero. Beaten 2-0 at home by Real Betis, they are 36th of 36 with a goal difference of minus eighteen.

US Soccer want Guardiola. talkSPORT reports the federation has made Pep Guardiola its top target to replace Pochettino, with initial talks mooted and Roberto Martínez also under consideration. Pochettino has publicly encouraged Guardiola to take it. Nothing is agreed.

Milan's Amorim keeps winning in Europe. The 4-2 win at Olympiacos followed a 5-1 league defeat of Frosinone, eleven goals in two matches.

The WSL has expanded to fourteen clubs. Birmingham City and Crystal Palace came up, and Charlton Athletic made their top-flight debut via a penalty shootout play-off win over Leicester. Chelsea and Manchester City have set the pace, and Manchester City's Khadija Shaw led the scoring charts with nine goals at the start of November.

Pochettino's compensation. United's deal to release him from US Soccer is understood to have cost around £7m in release-clause payments. He had extended his federation contract to 2030 only three months earlier.
