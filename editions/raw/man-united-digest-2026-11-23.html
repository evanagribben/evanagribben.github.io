The Lead

Eight minutes into the Pochettino era, United's season found a new low

A ten-day coaching change, a first match at Anfield and a red card before the ink was dry. The 4-0 defeat showed how deep the repair job runs, and how little time there is to start it.

Nine days after Manchester United named Mauricio Pochettino head coach, his first match was effectively over before his players had settled. Leny Yoro was sent off after eight minutes at Anfield, Liverpool led 2-0 inside 13, and the final score was 4-0. It was, as Sky Sports recorded, equalling the heaviest defeat in a United manager's first game, set in 1926.

The sequence that decided the afternoon took seconds. A long clearance beat Matthijs de Ligt, making his first Premier League start in 357 days, and Yoro, the last man, brought down Alexander Isak just outside the box. VAR confirmed the red card. Dominik Szoboszlai drove the free kick through the wall and past Senne Lammens at his near post.

Florian Wirtz made it 2-0 three minutes later from Rio Ngumoha's cross. Pochettino moved first: Bryan Mbeumo was withdrawn after 22 minutes for Harry Maguire, and United dropped into a back five that held until the hour. Ngumoha, who became the youngest scorer in the history of this fixture, added the third on 66 minutes, and substitute Victor Munoz the fourth on 82.

What the numbers say

The gap was not only about the man advantage. ESPN's match data shows Liverpool with 64 per cent possession, 28 shots to United's 10, and an expected-goals total of 4.38 against 0.63. Lammens made seven saves, which is why the scoreline was not worse, and the visitors put only three shots on target all afternoon.

The wider context is starker still. United are 19th with eight points from 11 games, their lowest return at this stage since 1972-73 if three points are awarded for a win, and their first spell in the bottom two this deep into a season since November 1986. They have conceded 34 goals in all competitions, the most of any side in Europe's major leagues.

"It's so painful, we feel pain for that defeat ... It's difficult to accept that because it was a foul under my view and for me was a penalty."

Mauricio Pochettino, to BBC Match of the Day

Pochettino's grievance, and his gamble

Pochettino's anger centred on a challenge by Liverpool's Jacquet on Mbeumo that went unpunished. "If that was a red card then that was a penalty," he told the BBC, as reported by Yahoo Sports. United have since told the FA they will appeal Yoro's dismissal, according to Sky Sports, although former referee Dermot Gallagher backed the decision.

His own selection drew criticism too. He made three changes from the 4-4 draw with Aston Villa, all in defence, recalling De Ligt and Lisandro Martinez and starting Patrick Dorgu. Sports Illustrated argued that a high line and an aggressive press played into the hands of a quick Liverpool attack. ESPN's ratings faulted the choice of De Ligt.

Captain Bruno Fernandes chose to look for positives. "The result won't show that but I am very proud of everyone in the dressing room," he said, in comments carried by Yahoo Sports. Pochettino asked his players to "translate that anger to an energy" before Wednesday's trip to Lisbon.

Why the next ten days matter

The fixture list offers no breathing room: Sporting CP away in the Champions League, then Brentford at Old Trafford on Saturday without the suspended Yoro, then Newcastle away on December 2. United sit 17th in the 36-team Champions League table with six points from four games, so Lisbon is also about staying inside the knockout play-off places.

How United ended up here: Carrick out, Pochettino in, and the bill

Two permanent head coaches sacked in one calendar year, a release clause paid to US Soccer, and quarterly accounts that show the strain.

Manchester United dismissed Michael Carrick on Thursday, November 12, Sky Sports reported, with the club calling it "the extremely difficult conclusion that a change is necessary". Carrick, who finished third last season after arriving as interim in January, had eight points from ten league games at the time.

Pochettino was confirmed the next day on a deal to the summer of 2029, per Sky Sports. He oversaw the United States in a 6-0 win over Haiti on November 14 and a 2-0 return leg on November 17, an 8-0 aggregate farewell, before taking his first session at Carrington on Thursday, November 19.

He brings his long-time staff: Jesus Perez, Miguel D'Agostino, Toni Jimenez, Alec Scott and his son Sebastiano Pochettino, ESPN reported.

The cost

The club's first-quarter results, published November 19, show revenue of £146.1m and a net loss of £18.1m for the three months to September 30, with £200m drawn on the revolving credit facility, according to the earnings release. The False 9, citing the filing, put the payment to US Soccer for Pochettino at £7.58m.

Chief executive Omar Berrada told Sky Sports that Carrick's results were "not good enough, not acceptable" and acknowledged "three permanent managers in less than three seasons". He insisted the shared ownership between the Glazers and INEOS is "working very well".

Scouting the Next Opponent

Sporting CP: a reshaped side with a striker in form

Paulo Fonseca has been in charge for less than three weeks, but Sporting arrive on four straight clean sheets and above United in the Champions League table.

Sporting CP sacked Rui Borges and appointed Paulo Fonseca on November 4. They were third in the Primeira Liga on 24 points from 11 games in early November, behind leaders Porto, and tenth in the Champions League table with nine points from four matches. Manchester United are seven places lower, on six.

Form is good. According to ESPN's results, Sporting have won their last four without conceding: 2-0 against Casa Pia, 1-0 against Shakhtar in Europe, 1-0 against Moreirense and 4-0 in the Taça de Portugal on Saturday. Their only league defeat came 2-1 to Académico de Viseu on October 25.

The players to stop

Striker Luis Suárez has 12 goals in 14 games this season, four of them in the Champions League, according to DAZN. Winger Geny Catamo has four league assists, and midfielder Sergi Altimira has been the side's steadiest performer. Previews disagree on Fonseca's shape, with a back three and a 4-3-3 both predicted.

The vulnerability is novelty. Fonseca is still installing his ideas, and the two games Sporting have lost this season came when they conceded twice. United will be without Matheus Cunha, who serves a one-match UEFA ban after his late dismissal against Roma, Sports Illustrated reported. Yoro's domestic ban does not apply in Europe.

Transfer News

United's January shopping list: a left-footed centre-back, a left-back and a No. 9

No official business this week, but the outline of Pochettino's first window is clear. Every thread below is at the interest stage at most.

Calvin Bassey: the centre-back priority (concrete interest)

Manchester United have monitored Fulham's Calvin Bassey, 26, "for a few months", Fabrizio Romano said on November 16, valuing his physicality and left foot as cover for Lisandro Martinez, whose contract runs to 2027. The Daily Mail named Bassey and Brentford's Nathan Collins as the main targets.

The obstacle is Fulham. The Athletic reported on November 19 that the club want a long-term extension and have "no intention" of selling in January, as relayed by Sports Mole. Bassey himself told TNT Sports on November 22: "We're still negotiating, we're still talking," per the Evening Standard. Status: concrete interest, no bid reported.

Left-back: Balde and Robinson (interest)

Barcelona's Alejandro Balde, 23, is the bigger name. Spanish daily Sport reported that Barcelona are open to moving him on after a meeting with agent Jorge Mendes, via FootballTransfers, and TEAMtalk's Graeme Bailey says United have spoken to Mendes at around €40m. Barcelona's Deco said publicly that Balde "hasn't told us he's not happy". Status: concrete interest.

The other name has a Pochettino connection. Antonee Robinson, 29, won caps under him with the USMNT, and Football Insider reports he is "high on the shortlist", per ESPN's rumour round-up. Newcastle, meanwhile, will not sell Lewis Hall mid-season, the Telegraph reported. Status: interest, single-sourced.

A stopgap No. 9, and an exit for Zirkzee

Benjamin Sesko is out with a shin injury until at least January, and Sky Sports says the club plan to back Pochettino in January with a forward among the targets. Monaco's Folarin Balogun is the most-mentioned option, though Romano reported "no active conversation" on November 16 (FootballTransfers). Status: link.

Going the other way, Joshua Zirkzee "can leave" in January because he wants minutes, Romano said on November 19, via Football365. Italian outlet TuttoMercatoWeb reports Napoli want him on a permanent deal. Romano was clear that Sesko will not be sold. Status: exit permitted, no bid reported.

The Squad

Pochettino's first week: captain confirmed, day off scrapped, a drone overhead

Three training sessions before Anfield, a public vote of confidence in Fernandes and a long injury list.

Bruno Fernandes stays captain. "Bruno is our captain and a very important player," Pochettino said at his first press conference on November 20, adding that he wants to be "involved in all the main decisions that will happen on the squad". He has also ended the day off after matches; players now report to Carrington for recovery, the Mirror reported, citing The Sun.

His first session was filmed by a drone from outside the training ground. The operator said he flew in unrestricted airspace with the right licences, BBC Sport reported. Pochettino asked: "What is the point of doing something that can be very dangerous?" The footage, Sky Sports said, passed eight million views.

Shape and selection

Accounts of the Anfield shape differ, with Sports Illustrated reading a 3-4-2-1 and others a 4-2-3-1, but the intent was plain: a high line and an aggressive press. After the red card, United went to a back five with Maguire on, which left Matheus Cunha isolated until he was replaced at half-time. Mainoo and Tielemans were outnumbered in central midfield.

Carrington

Camacho trains with the first team as the U18s fall to Derby

A Spanish teenager in prolific form, a painful defeat at Carrington and an unhappy exit to Real Madrid.

The U18s lost 3-2 to Derby County at Carrington on Saturday. Derby scored through Troy Ogedengbe, an own goal and Charlie Smith; Kai Rooney, back from suspension, converted a penalty in first-half stoppage time and Jaume Camacho curled in on 58 minutes, Derby's report shows.

Camacho, 17, has eight goals in his last four league games, The Peoples Person reported, and trained in Pochettino's first full session on November 19. A Carrington insider named eight more academy players in that session, including Jack Fletcher, Tyler Fletcher and Harry Amass; that list rests on a single social-media post (The Peoples Person).

The U21s had no game this week and host Crystal Palace on November 29. Less happily, 16-year-old JJ Gabriel confirmed his move to Real Madrid in a video on November 17, Sports Illustrated reported, with Ben Jacobs saying United learned of it on social media. Reported compensation figures conflict.

Women's Team

Awujo's stoppage-time header extends a 14-game unbeaten run

Eva Olid's side came from behind at Birmingham and reached the League Cup quarter-finals with a perfect group record.

Manchester United Women trailed Birmingham City from the fourth minute at St Andrew's on Sunday. Elisabeth Terland equalised on 80 minutes and Simi Awujo headed the winner deep into stoppage time for a 2-1 victory, ESPN's match page shows. United had 66 per cent of the ball and Jess Park had eight shots.

Anna Sandberg, back from injury, came off the bench and crossed for both goals, The Peoples Person reported, adding that the run is now 14 games unbeaten in all competitions since a September loss to Chelsea (single source).

On Wednesday, a second-string side beat Everton 2-1 in the League Cup through Mared Griffiths and Emma Watson to make it five wins from five in the group, Yahoo Sports reported. United are fourth in the WSL on 21 points, six behind leaders Chelsea (ESPN).

Next up is a pause: United's next WSL game is Brighton on December 13 (GOAL fixtures). Before that, Maya Le Tissier, Ella Toone and Jess Park are with England for the World Cup play-off against Ukraine.

Fernandes scores for Portugal as the Lionesses call up three

The men's window closed on November 17; the women's opens this week.

Bruno Fernandes captained Portugal and curled in the opening goal after 11 minutes of a 3-2 Nations League win in Wales on November 17, ESPN reported. Diogo Dalot started at right-back and was withdrawn on 71 minutes; Karl Darlow was an unused substitute for Wales.

Three United women are in England's squad for the World Cup play-off against Ukraine: Maya Le Tissier, Ella Toone and Jess Park (England Football). The first leg is in Murcia on Tuesday, December 1, at 9:00am Pacific; the second is at Wembley on December 5.
