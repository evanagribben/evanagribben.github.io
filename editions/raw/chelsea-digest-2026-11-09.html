The Lead

No striker, no spark: Chelsea stall at the Stadium of Light

Enzo Le Fée's penalty sent Xabi Alonso's side into the international break fifth and 11 points off the top. The absence of João Pedro, and a habit of alternating wins with defeats, now defines their autumn.

There are defeats that tell you nothing and defeats that tell you everything. Chelsea's 1-0 loss at Sunderland on Sunday belonged to the second group. A single penalty from Enzo Le Fée in the 36th minute was enough, and Xabi Alonso did not pretend otherwise. "I think that we didn't deserve more than probably one point," he said (DAZN).

The penalty came from a familiar weakness. Levi Colwill, already booked, dragged down Brian Brobbey, and the Dutch forward had been getting the better of the centre-back for half an hour. Alonso withdrew Colwill at half-time to avoid a second yellow and moved Moisés Caicedo into the back line, where he handled Brobbey far more comfortably (FotMob; Evening Standard).

Chelsea had the ball, and very little else. They held about two-thirds of the ball and attempted 648 passes, yet their first shot on target arrived in the 45th minute. NBC's live coverage put their first-half expected goals at 0.19 against Sunderland's 1.71.

"We missed a bit of patience and a bit of quality, creativity in the last third to create more chances."
Xabi Alonso after the defeat at Sunderland

The missing No 9

The root of the problem is not hard to find. João Pedro, Chelsea's joint top league scorer with five goals, is out with a thigh injury and has no firm return date. Without him Alonso asked Pedro Neto to play as a makeshift striker, a role for which, as the Standard put it, he lacks the physical presence.

Chelsea have now scored once from open play in four matches: Morgan Rogers' strike against Manchester United is the only one, alongside penalties at Anfield and against United (Sports Mole). Two of the three league games since his injury have been lost.

Alonso was candid about the squad's thinness. "We are shorter in options and we need to go through this time," he said. The injury list explains why: João Pedro, Reece James, Maxence Lacroix, Jorrel Hato and Jamie Gittens were all unavailable, Josh Acheampong felt a minor problem, and Emiliano Martínez was left out as he returns from jaw surgery (Evening Standard).

A pattern, not a blip

Zoom out and the shape of the season is clear. Since back-to-back wins over Bournemouth and Everton in mid-October, Chelsea have gone loss, draw (lost on penalties), win, loss. Away from Stamford Bridge they have lost three of their five league games, at Arsenal, Brentford and now Sunderland (results via VAVEL).

At 16 points from 10 games Chelsea sit fifth, level with Everton, two points behind fourth-placed Liverpool and 11 behind Arsenal (NBC Sports; BeSoccer). Bournemouth are one point further back. The margin for another alternating month is thin.

There is some reassurance. Alonso's side have scored 19 league goals, the attack is far better with João Pedro and Palmer both fit, and Leeds, ninth, arrive at Stamford Bridge on November 21 having won once in five away league games. But Alonso's own diagnosis, a lack of "patience" and "creativity in the last third", is a fair summary of a team still finding its identity in his first autumn.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Leeds United: stubborn at home, short of goals, and back at the Bridge

Daniel Farke's side arrive ninth and freshly buoyed by a late win over Tottenham.

Leeds United come to Stamford Bridge on Saturday November 21 sitting ninth on 14 points, with three wins, five draws and two defeats (BeSoccer). Their defining number is 10: goals scored and goals conceded across 10 games add up to just 18, the profile of a side that keeps games tight.

Their last outing was their best in weeks. Substitute Noah Okafor scored with his third touch, on 79 minutes, to beat Tottenham 1-0 at Elland Road on November 7, Leeds' first win since mid-September (Leeds United).

How they play

Daniel Farke switched to a back three with wing-backs partway through last season, and analysts describe Leeds as an aerial and set-piece threat with a goalkeeper, James Trafford, comfortable with the ball at his feet (VAVEL). That matters: one analyst counts Chelsea as having conceded six set-piece goals in nine games, the most in the league (single source, Substack analysis).

The men to stop are Dominic Calvert-Lewin, Leeds' top league scorer with four, and left-sided defender Tarik Muharemović, who has two assists and scored in the cup tie at Stamford Bridge (FotMob). Gabriel Gudmundsson went off at half-time against Spurs with a hamstring problem and Farke only "hopes" he will be fit; Anton Stach has been a doubt with a broken toe (LiveScore; Sports Mole).

The vulnerability is away form and finishing: Leeds have won once in five away league games, and Farke admitted after Spurs that the "finishing pass wasn't there" for long spells. Chelsea also have recent history on their side, winning 6-3 in the EFL Cup in September and 1-0 in last season's FA Cup semi-final (Leeds United; AP via KSL).

Transfer News

Alex Scott remains the January priority, at a price

Bournemouth will listen, but the reported figure has climbed towards £100m.

Chelsea's pursuit of Bournemouth midfielder Alex Scott is the thread that will define the January window. A £64m bid was rejected in the summer, and BBC Sport's Nizaar Kinsella reported in October that Bournemouth are open to a January sale at around £100m or more, depending on their league position (BBC Sport via Flashscore).

This week The Sun (relayed by CaughtOffside, November 5) described Scott as Chelsea's top January target. A claim that personal terms are already agreed comes from journalist Simon Phillips and is unconfirmed (Football365). Certainty: concrete interest; no fee agreed.

Colwill and Gusto: two defenders, two different futures

Chelsea want to tie one down and may let the other go.

Levi Colwill, whose afternoon at Sunderland ended at half-time, is under contract to 2029, and Chelsea are reported to want to open talks on improved terms amid interest from Barcelona and Liverpool (FootballTransfers, November 7). Certainty: link from the Barcelona side; no bid.

Malo Gusto looks headed the other way. TEAMtalk's Graeme Bailey reported on November 9 that the France full-back is ready to leave and Chelsea will listen to offers, with Liverpool, Manchester City, Tottenham, Atlético, Milan, Como and Leipzig named (TEAMtalk). RMC's Fabrice Hawkins had reported that a Liverpool deadline-day loan approach failed (Football365). Certainty: club open to a sale.

The defensive shopping list: Koundé and Camara

A right-back with Alonso's seal of approval and a midfielder who nearly arrived.

Radio Marca's Matteo Moretto reported in October that Chelsea asked Barcelona about the conditions for Jules Koundé, a deal likely above €50m (Sport Witness); a later report called him an "absolute priority" for the winter (TEAMtalk). Certainty: concrete interest.

Monaco midfielder Lamine Camara was the deadline-day deal that collapsed: Monaco accepted about £47m, then pulled out. talkSPORT's Ben Jacobs says Monaco now want around €70m (Sports Mole). Certainty: concrete interest after a collapsed deal.

The Squad

Alonso's selection puzzle: thin up front, shuffled at the back

Seven absentees, a rotating system and a captain who will not return before January.

Xabi Alonso's preferred shape has been a 3-4-2-1 that drops to a back five without the ball, but he has also tried a 4-2-3-1 and lined up with four at the back at Sunderland (single source, Substack analysis). The same analysis counts 19 league goals from about 13.3 expected goals: Chelsea have been finishing above the chances they create.

Estêvão was dropped to the bench after a lively display against United, and Martínez was left out because Alonso felt playing him would be "too rash, too risky" (Evening Standard via AOL). Henderson returned from illness to start (Evening Standard).

Captaincy and leaders

Reece James is captain, confirmed by Alonso in August (Evening Standard via Yahoo), but knee surgery in October will keep him out for about three months (Evening Standard via AOL). In his absence Alonso has said "we have other leaders", with Caicedo and Colwill among the senior figures.

Four Chelsea players were booked at Sunderland: Colwill, Neto, Fofana and Caicedo.

Cobham

U21s go second as UEFA Youth League run ends in shock defeat to Flora Tallinn

A brilliant week for Ezenwata and McMahon, a painful one in Europe.

Harry Hudson's U21s won 3-1 at Tottenham in Premier League 2 on Sunday. McMahon scored on seven minutes from a long ball by Reggie Watson, and Ezenwata added two, taking his season tally to seven. Chelsea are second in PL2 with 16 points from seven games (Chelsea FC).

Europe was a different story. Chelsea lost the second leg of their UEFA Youth League tie 3-2 at home to Flora Tallinn on November 4 and went out 5-4 on aggregate, after a 2-2 draw in Estonia. Mathis Eboué and Ibrahim Rabbaj scored (ERR; West London Sport).

Dan Hogan's U18s beat Charlton 3-1 in the U18 Premier League Cup on Saturday, with two penalties from Jashayde Greenwood and a goal from Andrew Pennie, to reach the quarter-finals (Chelsea FC). They remain top of U18 Premier League South despite a 5-3 defeat at West Ham (Chelsea FC).

Loan watch

At Strasbourg, Dário Essugo scored in a 5-1 win over Auxerre on November 1 (GidStats), while Filip Jørgensen kept goal in both games of the week. Mamadou Sarr (Real Sociedad) suffered a calf injury against Lyon and withdrew from the Senegal squad (Africa Soccer).

Alejandro Garnacho played three minutes for Aston Villa against Fulham and was unused at Barcelona; he has yet to start a league game. Axel Disasi played 90 minutes twice for Crystal Palace. The club's October loan report highlighted Essugo, Murray-Campbell and Samuels-Smith. Loan figures are mostly single-sourced from FotMob player pages.

 
Chelsea Women

Six at The Valley, then a frustrating point at the Bridge

Everton's draw ends a seven-game WSL winning run and hands Manchester City top spot before Saturday's showdown.

Chelsea Women began the week with a 6-0 win at Charlton on November 1. Nathalie Björn, captaining the side in her first WSL start in a year, headed the opener, Malard scored twice, and Ellie Carpenter, Aggie Beever-Jones and Dragoni added the rest (Chelsea FC). The cost was Lauren James, who went off with a hamstring injury.

Saturday's 1-1 draw with Everton at Stamford Bridge was more frustrating. Baltimore scored on 28 minutes, but Lucy Bronze turned a Momiki cross into her own net minutes later, and Chelsea could not find a winner despite around 20 shots (Chelsea FC; Everton's own report credits Malard with the goal).

"The result is really frustrating for us," said Sonia Bompastor. "We were probably too slow... not clinical enough, not ruthless enough" (Chelsea News). Chelsea are still unbeaten in all competitions this season.

The title race and the week ahead

City's 5-0 win over West Ham took them top: 25 points to Chelsea's 23, though Chelsea's goal difference (+23) is better (WSL table). The sides meet at Stamford Bridge on Saturday, 1:30pm UK, on BBC One (Chelsea FC).

First comes Tuesday's Women's Champions League trip to BK Häcken. Chelsea have seven points from three games after wins over Austria Wien and Roma and a draw in Lyon (Flashscore). Lauren James will miss at least five games, according to the BBC.

Players Away

Rogers flies the flag for England; Anselmino gets his first Argentina call

Thirteen Chelsea players head off for the November window, which runs from November 11 to 17.

Morgan Rogers is the only Chelsea player in Thomas Tuchel's England squad for Nations League games against Croatia and Spain. Cole Palmer was left out again; Tuchel said he "misses too many opportunities to impress" (beIN Sports).

Aaron Anselmino has a first senior call-up for Argentina, alongside Martínez and Barco, and Geovany Quenda could make his Portugal debut beside Pedro Neto (Chelsea FC). Also away: Gusto (France), Palestra (Italy), Penders and Lavia (Belgium), Estêvão (Brazil), and Caicedo and Kendry Páez (Ecuador). Loanees Jørgensen (Denmark) and Genesis Antwi, a first-time Sweden pick, are also called up.

No internationals had been played at the time of writing; the full schedule with Pacific kickoff times is in What to Watch below.

In Brief
  • Rosenior speaks: former head coach Liam Rosenior told So Foot his roughly 100-day spell "has nothing to do with football" and promised to say more later (Sports Mole).
  • Ownership: Clearlake has held full control since Todd Boehly and Mark Walter sold their stakes in September, a deal that valued the club at about £5bn (ESPN). No new developments this week.
  • Stadium: the choice still lies between rebuilding Stamford Bridge and a new 60,000-seat stadium at Earl's Court; no decision this week (Football Ground Guide, citing the Daily Mail).
  • FA case closed: the July settlement of the Abramovich-era charges (a £10m fine and a suspended two-window transfer ban) means the transfer ban remains suspended rather than live (Sky Sports).
  • EFL Cup: Chelsea's exit at Anfield on October 28, 6-5 on penalties after a 1-1 draw, leaves the FA Cup third round in January as their only cup (Sky Sports).
  • Debutants: 16-year-olds Reggie Watson and Mahdi Nicoll-Jazuli have both made first-team debuts this season; Nicoll-Jazuli was on the bench at Sunderland (Talk Chelsea).
