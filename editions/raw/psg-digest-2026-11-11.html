The Lead

Eleven straight wins, and PSG did it without their stars

Since losing Kvaratskhelia, Dembélé and Marquinhos on one October night, with Nuno Mendes already out, Luis Enrique's side have not dropped a point. The break arrives at the right moment, and the cavalry is close.

On the night of October 20, Paris Saint-Germain beat Barcelona 4-2 and lost half a team. Khvicha Kvaratskhelia tore the anterior cruciate ligament in his right knee; AFP reports he will be out for more than six months. Ousmane Dembélé and Marquinhos went off with thigh injuries (Sud Radio/AFP), and Nuno Mendes was already missing after a thigh problem at Strasbourg three days earlier (Reuters via The Star).

What followed is the most convincing argument yet for the squad Luis Enrique has built. Lyon 2-0. Le Havre 5-1. Villarreal 0-6. Troyes 1-0. Four games, four wins, 14 goals for and one against. Count the seven wins up to and including the Barcelona night and PSG reach the international break on an 11-match winning run in all competitions, the club's longest since 2015-16 (AllFootball).

Luis Enrique put it in his own terms after Troyes. "Eleven wins in a row, that's good," he said, adding that what stood out was winning every match between two international breaks for the first time under him (SoccerNews). PSG lead Ligue 1 on 23 points, two clear of Lille, and sit first in the Champions League league phase with 12 points from 12 and a +15 goal difference (NBC Sports).

A record in Spain

The headline act was Tuesday, November 3, at the Estadio de la Cerámica. PSG led Villarreal 4-0 after 16 minutes and two seconds, the fastest four goals in Champions League history, through João Neves, Désiré Doué, Senny Mayulu and Fabián Ruiz. Ruiz added a second after the break and 17-year-old David Boly scored on his Champions League debut (psg.fr).

It was PSG's biggest away win in the competition, built on 2.69 expected goals to 0.66 (SoccerNews). Luis Enrique refused to celebrate it. "This doesn't mean anything," he said. "In February and March, we'll have to prove everything" (SoccerNews). Days later he called the scoreline "almost bizarre" and "an accident".

Grit against Troyes

Troyes on Saturday was the opposite kind of test. With Hakimi rested and Marquinhos, Dembélé, Mendes and Kvaratskhelia out, PSG had 73% of the ball and 15 shots, but needed Illia Zabarnyi's header from Mayulu's cross on 39 minutes to break a stubborn defence (psg.fr). Troyes thought they had equalised late, but the goal was ruled out for offside (Flashscore).

Grinding out a 1-0 is what title sides do between the statement nights. Vitinha completed 166 passes. Ferran Torres hit the target four times without scoring, extending his drought to nine games, and Luis Enrique shrugged it off: "That's how it is: a striker scores and misses goals" (Sports Mole).

“If you think you're unplayable or invincible, you'll clearly lose the next match.”

Luis Enrique, after Troyes (translated)

The understudies

The deeper story is who has stepped in. Mayulu has scored against Le Havre and Villarreal, assisted the winner against Troyes, made L'Équipe's Ligue 1 team of the week and, on Wednesday, earned a first senior France call-up (Ouest-France). Mika Godts, the summer signing from Ajax, scored at Le Havre and set up Mayulu against Lyon. Warren Zaïre-Emery filled in at right-back.

Rotation has been relentless rather than reckless. At Le Havre the XI was heavily changed, with Vitinha, Neves and Doué on the bench, and Achraf Hakimi still scored a hat-trick from right-back (psg.fr). The coach has kept his 4-3-3 shape but shared the load, and his warning to the squad was blunt: "If you think you're unplayable or invincible, you'll clearly lose the next match" (PlanètePSG, translated from French).

The cavalry returns

The two weeks off could not be better timed. The club's medical update on November 6 said Marquinhos, Dembélé and Mendes were back in individual training, and French media are targeting the trip to Nice on November 21 for their return (Allez Paris, Kick Football). Those dates are media estimates, not club ones. Maghnes Akliouche (calf) is being treated.

Then comes the run that will define the autumn: Nice away, Roma at the Parc on November 25, Lorient three days later. PSG have won every game since September 9. Nobody inside the club pretends that streak survives forever. The point is that it has survived the worst injury week of Luis Enrique's tenure.

The Parc

The €2bn idea: rebuilding the Parc instead of leaving it

L'Équipe says the owners are studying a 70,000-seat "Porte des Princes". Massy and Poissy are still in the race, and a decision on a priority site is due before the end of the year.

The stadium saga took another turn on November 4. According to L'Équipe, PSG's Qatari owners would put €1.5bn to €2bn into buying the Parc des Princes and rebuilding it, lifting capacity from about 48,000 to 70,000, with standing areas considered (Kick Football, VIPSG, both relaying L'Équipe).

The plan would take in the land around the ground, including car parks, a petrol station and the Stade Géo-André, which Stade Français wants to keep. PSG would decamp to the Stade de France during the works. L'Équipe was explicit: nothing is signed and there is no timetable.

Three sites, one deadline

In an October 17 statement, the club said it would pick a priority site in autumn 2026, while stressing that this would not be a final decision (VIPSG). Le Parisien reported on October 23 that no project was out of the race and that the QSI board would name its priority site before the end of the year (Kick Football).

Massy's mayor, Nicolas Samsoen, told Le Parisien he had "no doubt the project works technically" (PlanètePSG). The gap on price is old and wide: PSG offered about €40m for the Parc in 2022, while valuations have ranged from about €200m to about €350m (VIPSG). Mayor Emmanuel Grégoire has had a mandate to negotiate since April.

Transfer News

No replacement for Kvaratskhelia, and the club means it

Three reporters and the coach himself line up behind the same message: Godts gets the left flank, and January will be quiet unless something else breaks.

The clearest transfer story of the week is a decision not to act. On November 4, Canal+ reporter Olivier Tallaron wrote that, barring another major injury to a key player, PSG will not go after anyone to replace Kvaratskhelia this winter (Les Titis du PSG). Certainty: club position, reported by Canal+.

That matches what RMC Sport's Fabrice Hawkins reported in late October: the club is counting on Mika Godts and is not necessarily targeting a direct replacement, though a young, high-potential player at a reasonable price is not ruled out (Yahoo Sports). Luis Enrique's own line on October 24 was that no injury means you have to sign, but the club stays open to opportunities (VIPSG).

Dembélé's new deal is close, not done

AFP describes talks as very constructive. Some outlets say it is signed; the reliable ones do not.

Ousmane Dembélé, under contract to 2028, is negotiating an extension to June 2030. On November 6, AFP quoted a source close to the talks calling them "very constructive and positive", with the aim of reaching agreement quickly and "a few points to settle" (Orange/AFP). Mundo Deportivo also reports the sides moving closer.

Certainty: advanced talks. Several French outlets have gone further and called it done pending an announcement; that is not confirmed by any tier-one source, and PSG have announced nothing. Reports also say talks with Kvaratskhelia on a longer deal continue despite his injury; that is single-sourced.

Boly's choice: PSG or Frankfurt

The 17-year-old who scored at Villarreal is the most urgent contract on the desk.

David Boly's aspirant contract runs to June 2027. RMC Sport reports PSG have offered a five-year professional deal, which he can sign once he turns 18 on January 22 (Kick Football). L'Équipe calls the offer attractive. Sky Germany's Florian Plettenberg reported on November 8 that Eintracht Frankfurt want him in January and would pay a fee (VIPSG).

Certainty: offer made, no agreement announced. Paris Team reports the player wants to sign only with PSG; that is single-sourced. RMC also reports PSG want to extend forward Quentin Ndjantou, possibly to 2033.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Nice: Wahi's hot streak and a wing-back gamble

Pantaloni's side are sixth and winning, but they have conceded 12 in 10 and are missing their best centre-back.

OGC Nice host PSG at the Allianz Riviera on Saturday, November 21, the first game back after the break (ligue1.com). They come in sixth on 17 points after winning four of their last five, most recently 2-0 at Angers with goals from Sofiane Diop in the second minute and Jonathan Clauss late on (Sports Mole).

Olivier Pantaloni, hired in the summer after Nice survived a relegation play-off, plays a 3-4-3 in which the wing-backs carry the side at both ends (ligue1.com, Nice-Premium). A typical XI has Clauss and Nkounkou wide, Axel Witsel as captain in midfield, and Wahi between Gauthier Hein and Mohamed Amoura.

The players to stop

Elye Wahi is the threat: six league goals and two assists in nine games, including a hat-trick, two from the penalty spot, in a 5-2 win over Rennes on October 30 (Flashscore, ESPN). Clauss has three goals from right wing-back; Diop has two goals and three assists; Hein has three assists.

Where Nice can be hurt

Nice are without Moïse Bombito after leg surgery, and Antoine Mendy and Laurent Abergel are out with ACL injuries (Get French Football News). Pantaloni has admitted his squad is not at full fitness and that the Nkounkou and Amoura left side is still finding its feet. A back three with advancing wing-backs leaves room behind for Hakimi and Doué to attack.

The recent history is mixed. PSG won 4-0 in Nice in March (psg.fr) and 1-0 at home in November 2025 with a stoppage-time header (psg.fr), but it was Nice who ended PSG's unbeaten league season with a 3-1 win at the Parc in April 2025 (ligue1.com).

The Squad

A 4-3-3 that keeps changing faces

Luis Enrique has spread the minutes, moved Zaïre-Emery to right-back and backed a misfiring striker.

Luis Enrique has stuck with his 4-3-3 throughout the streak but rarely with the same eleven. The trio of Vitinha, Fabián Ruiz and João Neves remains the spine when fit and available, while Mayulu, Godts, Dro Fernández, Zaïre-Emery and Ferran Torres have all had regular starts (psg.fr). With Marquinhos out, Achraf Hakimi has worn the armband.

Against Troyes, Warren Zaïre-Emery played right-back as Hakimi was rested, not injured. Zabarnyi and Willian Pacho have formed the central pairing, with Lucas Hernández at left-back in Mendes's absence. Matvey Safonov has started the recent games in goal, with Lucas Chevalier on the bench (ESPN).

Form and roles

Ferran Torres, signed from Barcelona in the summer, has gone nine games without a goal and was benched at Villarreal. Luis Enrique has publicly backed him: "Do not worry about any PSG player" (Sports Mole). Hakimi, by contrast, has been prolific, with a hat-trick and two assists at Le Havre (Al Jazeera).

Two milestones on Saturday: Lucas Beraldo made his 100th PSG appearance, and 18-year-old Younes Idder, a left-footed attacking midfielder, was named in a first-team squad for the first time (psg.fr, Allez Paris).

The Academy

Boly's breakthrough, and a first Youth League defeat

The Campus produced a record-setting scorer in the senior side, while the U19s were beaten in Spain.

David Boly, a 17-year-old right-back, made his Ligue 1 debut at Le Havre on October 30 and scored four days later at Villarreal, aged 17 years and 285 days. That makes him the second-youngest PSG scorer in the Champions League, behind Zaïre-Emery (psg.fr). His contract situation is covered in Transfer News.

On the same night, the U19s lost 3-0 to Villarreal in the UEFA Youth League, their first defeat of the campaign; they have seven points from four games. Coach Jean-François Vulliez's side conceded inside a minute (Les Titis du PSG). Roma visit the Campus on November 25. In the Championnat National U19, PSG came back to draw 3-3 at Amiens on November 8 (psg.fr).

PSG Féminine

A derby draw, a rout, and a Parc des Princes setback

Paulo César's side are second in the Arkema Première Ligue but lost ground in Europe against Bayern.

Paris Saint-Germain Féminine had a busy fortnight. They drew 1-1 at Manchester City in the Women's Champions League on October 29, Tara Elimbi Gilbert equalising two minutes after Khadija Shaw's late penalty (Sky Sports). On November 1 they drew the derby 1-1 at Paris FC, with a stoppage-time winner ruled out for offside (Paris FC).

On November 6 they beat Le Havre 4-0 at the Campus through Merveille Kanjinga, Naomie Feller on her 25th birthday, Griedge Mbock and Elimbi Gilbert (psg.fr). Then on Tuesday at the Parc des Princes they lost 2-1 to Bayern Munich: an own goal and a Klara Bühl strike before half-time, with Paulina Dudek's penalty only a consolation (psg.fr).

Where they stand

In the league PSG are second on 19 points after eight games (six wins, a draw and a defeat), behind Lyon, who won all seven of their first games and beat PSG 4-0 in Paris. In Europe, a draw at Real Madrid, a 5-0 win over Louvain, the draw in Manchester and Tuesday's defeat leave them on five points by our count, with Chelsea away next and Juventus at home on December 16.

Next up: Marseille away on Saturday, November 14, then Chelsea at Kingsmeadow on Wednesday, November 18 (psg.fr fixtures). The women's U19s remain perfect, beating Rennes 4-1 for a seventh straight win (psg.fr).
