The Lead

Seven up, three down: what has gone wrong for Maresca's City

A record-breaking start has curdled into the club's worst league run since 2004. The problems are structural, the injuries are mounting, and the fixtures after the break are brutal.

Six weeks ago Manchester City looked unstoppable. Enzo Maresca had won his first seven Premier League games, a record for a new manager in the competition. On Saturday his side lost 3-1 at Nottingham Forest, a third straight league defeat, and they go into the international break third, six points behind Arsenal.

The run reads Aston Villa away (1-2), Brighton at home (0-1) and now Forest away (1-3). City have scored two goals in those three games and conceded six. According to The National, it is the club's worst league sequence since the autumn of 2004.

The City Ground defeat was the bleakest of the three. City were 2-0 down inside 18 minutes, their earliest two-goal deficit in a league game since Liverpool in November 2019, according to GOAL. They then had 78 per cent of the ball, 665 accurate passes and 1.59 expected goals to Forest's 1.00 (ESPN), and still lost by two.

How Forest pulled it apart

The opener, after six minutes, came from the way City want to play. Forest pressed high, Jair Cunha took the ball off Joško Gvardiol in City's half, and finished. Liam Delap added the second on 18 minutes after a mix-up between Iliman Ndiaye and Elliot Anderson, then sealed it on the counter with 17 minutes left.

Rayan Cherki's half-volley into the top corner on 52 minutes briefly offered a way back. Fernández had already headed wide from six yards and Jair cleared a Haaland effort off the line before the break (ESPN). ESPN's match data counts three big chances for City and none for Forest.

We need to have balance. We won seven in a row. Now we have lost three in a row, so we need balance on that.

Enzo Maresca, after the Forest defeat

Maresca's own diagnosis was candid. "First one is a consequence of the way we want to play," he said. "Second and third goals quite similar: we lost the duel then concede on transition." He added: "We are conceding too many goals unfortunately," while insisting there was "no panic" (BBC Sport via Yahoo).

The structural problem

The sharpest reading came from Jonathan Wilson in The Guardian on Monday. His case: a high back four is being exposed in transition, and with Rodri sold to Barcelona the midfield lacks a natural screen. Enzo Fernández and Cherki are light defensively, so Anderson is doing the physical work of two players.

The numbers support him. City have not kept a clean sheet in the eight games since the 3-0 win at Anfield on October 11, conceding 17 goals in that time. Gianluigi Donnarumma has also made costly errors, including at his near post in Leipzig on Wednesday.

Wilson also raised a harder question: whether the guilty verdict on 114 of the Premier League's 115 charges, delivered in late September and now under appeal, is weighing on the dressing room. "The issue is tactical," he wrote, "but it may not only be tactical."

Injuries arrive at the worst time

The defensive reshuffle will be forced, not chosen. Marc Guéhi went off at half-time with a hip problem, had a scan and has withdrawn from the England squad, with Dan Burn called up. Gvardiol also came off at the break; Maresca said he "had some issue already during the week."

Both "asked for a change," Maresca said. Antoine Semenyo has been out since mid-October with a knee injury expected to keep him out until December, and Nico O'Reilly was left out of the England squad because he is not fully fit.

What comes next

The break could hardly arrive at a better moment, but what follows it is unforgiving. Fulham visit the Etihad on Saturday, November 21, Napoli follow on the Tuesday in the Champions League, and then comes the trip to Arsenal, the leaders, the following weekend.

Lose at the Emirates and the gap could be nine points before December. Win the three and the narrative changes overnight. Maresca called his side "a work in progress" in Leipzig and "a learning journey" in Nottingham. The next fortnight will show how quickly it is learning.

Haaland wears the armband and finds Leipzig again

Wednesday's 3-2 win at RB Leipzig showed both sides of this team. City led through Castello Lukeba's own goal from O'Reilly's cross, were pegged back by Ridle Baku, went ahead through Fernández's header and were caught again by Antonio Nusa within three minutes (Bundesliga.com).

Erling Haaland, captain for the first time this season, settled it on 78 minutes from Anderson's low cut-back. It was his 13th goal in nine games against Leipzig, the most he has scored against any club (Sky Sports). Leipzig had won all five home games before kick-off without conceding (AFP).

Off the pitch: the appeal clock is ticking

City lodged their appeal on October 1 against the commission's verdict that they breached 114 of the 115 charges, saying the decision contains "clear material errors, of law, principle and fact" (Sky Sports). No sanction has yet been set, and no hearing date has been announced (ESPN).

The appeal rules allow 12 weeks for a hearing, which points to late December, though City may contest the timetable. The BBC's Dan Roan explained on November 5 that the league board argues for a sanction and a three-person commission decides it (BBC via Yahoo). Gary Neville spoke of an "obvious cloud" on Sky this week (Sky Sports).

Elsewhere, Sheikh Mansour put a further £100m into City Football Group in October, lifting his stake to about 81 per cent (Inside World Football). On Saturday the club released a charity shirt with Manchester band Blossoms to mark 40 years of City in the Community (Yahoo).

Scouting the Next Opponent

Next up: Fulham (H), Saturday, November 21

Arbeloa's Fulham like the ball, and that may suit City

Fifteenth and still finding their feet under a new coach, Fulham have tightened up lately. But their open style leaves space behind.

Fulham sit 15th with 10 points from 10 games (W2 D4 L4), with 10 goals scored and 12 conceded. Álvaro Arbeloa, appointed in July to replace Marco Silva, failed to win any of his first six league games but is unbeaten in the four since (W2 D2).

They drew 1-1 with Newcastle on Saturday, Rodrigo Muniz equalising three minutes after Jacob Murphy's opener (theScore). Before that came a 1-0 win over Hull, a 1-0 win at Coventry and a 1-1 draw at Aston Villa.

System and style

Arbeloa sets up in a 4-2-3-1 with Sander Berge anchoring a double pivot and Oscar Bobb, Josh King and Alex Iwobi behind Gonzalo García. Fulham average 58 per cent possession, fifth-highest in the league (FotMob). "We're not the best team in open games," Arbeloa admitted in late October (beIN Sports).

The players to stop are King, the top scorer with two league goals; Iwobi, who takes the set pieces and leads the side in line-breaking passes; and García, whose 4.1 expected goals have not yet brought a league goal. Muniz is a dangerous option from the bench.

Where City can hurt them

Fulham often leave only three players behind the ball, and counter-attacks have punished them (Fulhamish). After five games they had allowed the most big chances in the league (Fantasy Football Scout). Set pieces are not a weakness, though: they have conceded only once from a dead ball.

Tom Cairney has a knee problem but is expected back. The history favours City heavily: they have won the last five meetings, most recently 3-0 at the Etihad in February (Ahram Online). By 11v11's count City had won 19 straight meetings going into last December's 5-4 at Craven Cottage.

Transfer News

Aït-Nouri heads for the exit as City weigh up January

Nothing is official, but the outgoing thread is now firm, and City's defensive troubles are shaping the shortlist.

Rayan Aït-Nouri: expected to leave

The clearest story at City is an exit. The Athletic's Sam Lee reported on November 3 that Rayan Aït-Nouri is expected to leave in January if a suitable offer arrives (as cited by City Xtra). The left-back, signed for £31m in 2025, has not played a league minute this season, behind Gvardiol and O'Reilly.

Sky Germany's Florian Plettenberg reported that personal terms would not be an issue for Manchester United, with the relationship between the two clubs the main obstacle (Football365). Juventus are preparing a loan with an option to buy, per The Athletic, and Sports Mole judges them the likelier destination.

Certainty: concrete interest from other clubs, no bid reported. Aït-Nouri was also left out of Algeria's squad this month after a dispute over playing time in September (Africa Soccer).

Who replaces him? The left-back question

If Aït-Nouri goes, City may look for cover. Spanish daily Sport reported that agent Jorge Mendes has contacted City about Barcelona's Alejandro Balde, with Barça open to around €40m and Mendes preferring a loan with an option (via City Xtra). Certainty: rumour, single-sourced, and dependent on Aït-Nouri leaving.

A striker for the future

Italian daily La Stampa reported that City and Chelsea have asked Club Brugge about striker Nicolò Tresoldi, with a €50m starting price and Brugge reluctant to sell mid-season (via Sport Witness). Graeme Bailey also reported City tracking him (Yahoo). Certainty: concrete interest, two mid-tier sources.

Grealish and Monga: loans under strain

Jack Grealish has missed four games for Everton, including two after a breach of club discipline in late October. Reports split on what comes next: Football Insider says Everton could end the loan in January (Yahoo), while TEAMtalk says there are no such plans and he is targeting the Brentford game on November 23 (TEAMtalk). His City contract expires in June 2027.

Swansea and City are negotiating to end Jeremy Monga's loan early, according to local reports relayed by The72. The winger, signed from Leicester in the summer, has not played for Swansea. Certainty: advanced talks, local sources only.

The Squad

A defence in flux, and Anderson asked to do everything

Maresca's rotation of full-backs is about to become a necessity, and the midfield balance is the debate of the break.

City manager Enzo Maresca has used a 4-2-3-1 all season, rotating between two full-back pairings: Nunes and O'Reilly, or Khusanov and Gvardiol. At Forest, Rúben Dias wore the armband; at Leipzig, Haaland did. Phil Foden, scorer of a hat-trick against AEK Athens, started on the bench on Saturday.

The midfield is the hot topic. With Fernández and Cherki both attack-minded, Elliot Anderson is being asked to cover as a holding midfielder and a box-to-box runner at once. He was City's best player at Leipzig and, by most ratings, their best midfielder at Forest.

The Academy

A first Youth League win, and Samba's debut still echoing

City's under-19s won 2-1 at RB Leipzig in the UEFA Youth League on Wednesday, their first win of this season's campaign, with goals from Teddie Lamb on 53 minutes and a Dexter Oliver penalty on 57 (Foot Mercato). Ryan McAidoo and Braithwaite, both registered with the first-team squad, started.

The under-18s were knocked out of the U18 Premier League Cup by Burnley on Saturday, a week after beating the same opponents 2-1 in the league. The under-21s had no game this week and return to Premier League 2 against West Brom on November 27.

The breakout of the season so far remains 17-year-old Floyd Samba, who scored twice on his debut in the 5-0 League Cup win over Norwich in September (Irish News). He was on the bench at Leipzig.

City Women

Shaw's perfect hat-trick puts City back on top

A 5-0 rout of West Ham and a Chelsea slip return City to first place, just in time for the trip to Stamford Bridge.

City Women beat West Ham 5-0 at the Joie Stadium on Saturday to return to the top of the WSL.

Khadija Shaw scored a perfect hat-trick (header, left foot, right foot), taking her to 12 WSL goals in nine games (ESPN).

Aoba Fujino and substitute Iman Beney scored their first league goals of the season. Lauren Hemp, who set up one of Shaw's goals, leads the WSL with six assists (Opta Analyst). It was City's biggest league win since February.

With Chelsea held 1-1 by Everton, City return to the top on 25 points from nine games, two ahead of Chelsea and six ahead of London City Lionesses (Dave). They are unbeaten in the league, with eight wins and a draw.

Injuries and a huge week

The blow came before kick-off: Alex Greenwood (ankle) and goalkeeper Anna Moorhouse (foot) were hurt in training and are out until after the international break. "They will be out for a while," said head coach Andrée Jeglertz (Hayters).

City are at Inter in the Women's Champions League on Tuesday, sitting 10th in the league phase after three draws (2-2 at Bayern, 1-1 with Real Madrid and PSG) (Inter). Then, on Saturday, it is first against second at Stamford Bridge, live on BBC One.

Players Away

Thirteen City men away, with Guéhi home and Gvardiol a doubt

The November window runs from November 9 to 17 (Premier League). Elliot Anderson is City's only England player after Guéhi's withdrawal; England host Croatia, with Gvardiol and Kovačić, at Wembley on Thursday before visiting Spain on Sunday (England Football).

Haaland's Norway host Wales on Saturday. Cherki's France visit Donnarumma's Italy on Thursday. Doku is back in Belgium's squad after a calf injury (beIN Sports), and Dias joins Portugal for Denmark on Saturday.
