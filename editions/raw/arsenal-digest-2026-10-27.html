The Lead
Anfield, Without Him
Arsenal arrive on Merseyside on Sunday level on points with Manchester City and shorn of the player who has started almost every important afternoon of the Arteta era. What follows is a test of whether the champions are a system or a supporting cast.
There were about ten minutes on the clock at the Allianz Arena last Wednesday when Bukayo Saka played a simple pass, reached for the back of his upper leg and stopped running. He did not go down theatrically. He did not wave at the bench. He simply walked, slowly, towards the touchline with the expression of a man who already knew. Eberechi Eze came on. Twenty-five minutes later Eze put Arsenal in front in Munich, and by the end Declan Rice had rescued a 2-2 from a game Bayern Munich had begun to control. The night was a credit to the squad. It was also the moment this season changed shape.
Mikel Arteta was immediately blunt. "It doesn't look good at all but we have to wait and see," he said in Germany, and by Friday he had hardened it: "It will be weeks." A second scan was ordered because of fluid in the area, and the club expects to rescan again a week to ten days from now before fixing a return date.
Several national outlets have put the window at six to eight weeks, which would take the vice-captain past the Champions League league phase and into the back half of December. That figure is not Arteta's, and the Digest treats it as unconfirmed; what is confirmed is that Saka will not play at Anfield, and that Arsenal are now asking a reshaped front line to go to the venue where their season most often curdles.

The Iraola problem
Begin with the tactical shape of it, because this is not the Liverpool anyone scouted a year ago. Andoni Iraola replaced Arne Slot on a two-year contract in June and has spent four months dismantling the idea that Anfield must be a possession fortress. Against Brighton on Sunday his side won 2-0 with 35.6 per cent of the ball, which Opta logged as Liverpool's second-lowest possession share in a Premier League home win since 2003-04. Brighton had 64 per cent and one shot on target. That is the Iraola signature transplanted from Bournemouth: a high, aggressive, man-oriented press, an invitation to play out, and then four players arriving in the box within six seconds of the turnover.
Which sets up the key battle, and it is not the one the television build-up will choose. Liverpool's press is designed to force the ball into the full-back channels and then trap it there. Arsenal's build-up this season has leaned harder than ever on Myles Lewis-Skelly stepping inside from left-back and on Ben White holding width on the right, because William Saliba has not played a minute all season and Jurriën Timber has been in and out. With Riccardo Calafiori only just cleared and Piero Hincapié freshly back from an ankle problem, the back four that walks out at Anfield may be the fourth or fifth distinct combination Arteta has used in thirteen games. Against a side that hunts exactly those players, composure in the first fifteen minutes will decide more than any substitution.

Who plays wide, and who scores
The selection questions multiply from there. Noni Madueke has taken the right wing and done it well, scoring the only goal at Nottingham Forest and starting on the left in Munich before drifting back to his natural side. He is not Saka and Arteta has sensibly stopped asking him to be: where Saka manufactured chances by holding the touchline and inverting late, Madueke is at his best attacking a defender who is already moving. That suits a transition game, which is precisely what Sunday will be. Eze, meanwhile, has two goals in his last three appearances and looks liberated by a role that lets him begin wide and finish central.
The uncomfortable question is the centre forward. Viktor Gyökeres has not scored for Arsenal this season. Not in the Premier League, not in Europe, not in the Carabao Cup. He assisted Ezri Konsa's opener against Everton and played the one-two that set up Rice in Munich, and Arteta's defence of him has been consistent rather than grudging: "He was unlucky two or three moments. He's in front of goal, he's won the position to both centre-backs," the manager said on Monday, adding that the ball simply does not arrive.
The data broadly agrees that Arsenal's open-play threat is up on last season. It does not change the fact that a title race in which your striker has nothing is a title race fought with a hand behind your back, and that Kai Havertz, three goals already, is the man Arteta has turned to when the game needs a goal rather than a structure.

What is actually at stake
The stakes are plainer than usual, because the table has closed up in Arsenal's favour at exactly the moment their squad has thinned. Manchester City went to Villa Park on Saturday and lost 2-1; Arsenal beat Everton and drew level on 21 points, second only on goal difference. In other words the champions have been handed the initiative in the week they lost their most reliable attacker. Win at Anfield and they go top with the hardest away day of the autumn behind them. Lose and the narrative writes itself: a title defence that survives on set-piece margins and a goalkeeper, with no striker and no Saka.
Which is unfair, but not entirely. The record at least is suggestive. Arsenal have not beaten Liverpool in any competition since February 2024, a run of three draws and one defeat that spans the whole of their rise from nearly-men to champions. They drew 0-0 at the Emirates last January and lost 1-0 at Anfield the previous August, both games in which they controlled possession and found no way through. Iraola's version of Liverpool will offer them far more of the ball and far less space. On the evidence of Munich, where Arsenal were second best for forty minutes and still left with a point, that may suit them better than anyone expects.
Before any of that there is tonight: League Two Fleetwood Town, in the last sixteen of the Carabao Cup for the first time in their history, with no VAR and penalties straight after ninety minutes. Arteta has made nine changes and handed debuts to Illan Meslier and the 18-year-old Ceadach O'Neill. "We know the risk," he said. "Show them the Arsenal shirt and what we represent and that's it." It is the right instinct. It is also, this week, the second story.
The Set-Piece Machine Went Quiet for 153 Days
Arsenal scored nineteen Premier League goals from corners last season, a competition record. Before Ezri Konsa's header on Saturday they had scored none at all this season. The numbers say this is mostly not a conspiracy.
Konsa's seventh-minute finish from Martin Ødegaard's corner against Everton ended a run of thirteen competitive games and 153 days without a goal from a corner or free kick. The previous one came from Madueke's delivery against Crystal Palace on the final day of last season, in May. For a team whose set plays accounted for 26.8 per cent of their goals in a title-winning campaign, the highest share any Premier League champion has recorded, that is a genuine structural absence rather than a quirk.
The Opta Analyst breakdown published on Saturday is worth reading in full, because it argues against the simplest explanations. Arsenal are still whipping in almost as many crosses from corners (84.9 per cent this season against 86.6 per cent last) and every single crossed corner has been an inswinger. They are actually winning first contact far more often: 42.9 per cent, second highest in the league, against 28.3 per cent last season.
What has fallen is the quality of what follows. Shots from corners are down from 2.8 a game to 2.1, expected goals from corners down from 0.36 a game to 0.22, and the conversion rate from a league-best 8.8 per cent of corners to nothing at all. Arsenal are 1.55 goals below their corner expected goals, the second-largest shortfall in the division behind Liverpool.

Three explanations have real weight. The first is personnel: Saliba has missed the entire season and Timber, one of last year's most dangerous near-post runners, has been intermittent. Havertz now leads Arsenal for first contacts from attacking corners with four; Gabriel, the totem of the whole operation, has one.
The second explanation is placement. Arsenal have cut central six-yard-box deliveries by nearly eight percentage points and added almost exactly that volume to the near post, chasing flick-on routines that die on the first defender when the delivery is a fraction flat.
The third explanation is simply the league: corners have produced 9.8 per cent of Premier League goals this season against 18.0 per cent last, and referees are awarding defending teams free kicks from four per cent of corners, the highest rate in seven seasons. Grappling is being policed. Nicolas Jover's choreography depended on it being tolerated.
The consoling counterpoint is that Arsenal have not needed it. Open-play expected goals are up roughly seventeen per cent, from 1.06 a game to 1.24, and the team is second in the table having conceded five league goals. A side that has swapped a dead-ball edge for a live-ball one has traded sideways, not down. But dead-ball goals are the ones you can still manufacture at Anfield when a press has pinned you in your own half for an hour, and that is the context in which Konsa's header mattered more than its timing suggested.
Transfer News
Brugge Draw a Line in the Sand Over Tresoldi
Arsenal's interest in the Italian striker is real and reported by Sky. The selling club's answer, delivered through the Belgian press this week, was about as flat a no as a board can give.
The most substantial live thread of Arsenal's autumn runs through Belgium. Nicolò Tresoldi, 22, has twelve goals in fourteen games for Club Brugge this season, four of them in three Champions League matches including one against Aston Villa, and sixteen in twenty-seven for the Germany under-21s.
Sky Sports Germany reported this week that Arsenal are seriously interested and had him on their radar over the summer, while judging a January move unlikely and a summer approach the likelier route. The Belgian outlet Nieuwsblad went further, reporting that Arsenal are weighing a January bid with Barcelona also interested. Status: concrete interest, no bid, two independent reports.
Brugge's response was unusually direct. A board member told Nieuwsblad that "there is no price for Nicolò, and there never will be," adding that any January offer would be "completely irrelevant" to them. The club rejected €35m from AS Roma in the summer, Tresoldi is contracted to 2029, and the stated position is that they expect to sell him eventually but want a marquee exit rather than a mid-season one. Read that as a price being set by refusing to set one.
The strategic logic is obvious, and it is also why the Digest urges patience with it. Arsenal's centre forward has not scored this season. Havertz is 27 and has never been a volume striker.
On the Here We Go podcast in September Fabrizio Romano described Arsenal's striker search as neither "imminent or urgent," said the club were "very happy with the strikers they have," and pointed to summer 2027 rather than January, with Eli Junior Kroupi of Bournemouth named as a player they may revisit. Romano also flagged right-back as the position to watch. Nothing reported since has contradicted that framing. A Tresoldi deal in January would be a reaction to Saka's injury as much as to Gyökeres's drought, and Arsenal's recruitment under Andrea Berta has not been a reactive operation.
Calafiori, and the Price of Being Indispensable
Chelsea have not gone away. Real Madrid have been mentioned. The eye-catching valuation attached to all of it comes from an aggregator, not a tier-one source.
Riccardo Calafiori scored twenty-three seconds into the Community Shield and equalised against Leeds United on October 10, and has become the kind of left-sided defender who starts conversations. Chelsea retain interest after a bid Arsenal rejected, Real Madrid have been linked, and the figure circulating is a €100m asking price that would be a world record for a defender. That number originates with CaughtOffside and has not been stood up by a tier-one reporter, so treat it as mood music rather than a valuation. Status: rumour, single-sourced on the fee.
What is harder to dismiss is the squad arithmetic. With Saliba absent all season and Timber and Hincapié both carrying knocks, Calafiori is simultaneously Arsenal's most valuable asset in that part of the pitch and their least dispensable one. Any serious January bid would land in the middle of an injury crisis. It is not a coincidence that reporting on an Arsenal interest in Xavi Espart, the 19-year-old Barcelona full-back, has been framed as insurance against losing him.

Scouting the Next Opponent
Liverpool, Rebuilt From the Press Up
Andoni Iraola has spent four months turning Anfield into a counterattacking ground. Arsenal visit a side that no longer wants the ball and is suddenly very good at not having it.
Liverpool sacked Arne Slot and appointed Andoni Iraola on a two-year contract on June 4, then spent more than £200m rebuilding around him: Bradley Barcola from Paris Saint-Germain for a reported £106m, Jérémy Jacquet from Rennes for £55m, Víctor Muñoz from Osasuna for £34.5m and Ronald Araújo on loan from Barcelona with a purchase option.
On the other side of the ledger, Mohamed Salah, Andy Robertson and Ibrahima Konaté all left on free transfers and Curtis Jones went to Inter Milan. It is a different club, and for six weeks it looked like a confused one.
It is not confused now. After a 0-3 home humiliation by Manchester City on October 11, Liverpool have won four in a row in all competitions, including 2-1 at Brentford and 2-0 at home to Brighton. They sit fourth on fifteen points from eight, one behind Brighton, and third in the Champions League league phase on nine points.
Alexander Isak has six league goals, three in his last three league games, and Barcola has assisted him in each of the past two. Cody Gakpo has been impressive from the left. Florian Wirtz manufactured the opener against Brighton with the shot Pascal Groß turned into his own net.
The three Arsenal must stop are therefore clear. Isak is the finisher and the first press trigger, dropping into the left channel to drag a centre-back out. Barcola is the width and the cut-back, switched to the right against Brighton and immediately decisive from there, which puts him directly into whichever of Lewis-Skelly or Calafiori plays left-back.
Wirtz is the connector, and his availability is genuinely unclear: he twisted an ankle on the hour against Brighton, with Iraola saying it "doesn't look very big" while conceding the schedule forces caution. Dominik Szoboszlai is the other doubt, having lasted 32 minutes at Brentford and missed Brighton with a hamstring problem; Iraola says he is "close." Jérémie Frimpong, Hugo Ekitike and Víctor Muñoz are all out, with Conor Bradley, Giovanni Leoni and Vitezslav Jaros also absent with knee problems.
Where they are vulnerable
Where are they vulnerable? In exactly the place their new identity creates. A side that concedes 64 per cent of the ball at home and generates its chances from transition has to defend long spells in a mid-block, and Brighton proved the block can be pulled around: they simply could not finish, managing one shot on target from all that possession. Liverpool have also drawn three of eight and conceded in each of their first four league games of the season, including two-goal leads surrendered against Newcastle United and Nottingham Forest. If Arsenal can survive the opening twenty minutes of the press, a patient 2-0-style afternoon is available. The dead ball is the other lever, and the reason the Konsa goal mattered.

The Squad
A Depth Chart Being Tested to Its Design Limits
Arsenal spent £200m in the summer to be able to absorb exactly this. The absorption is working. It is also not free.
The single most striking fact about Arsenal's season is one that almost never gets said out loud: William Saliba has not played a minute of it. The back problem he aggravated at the World Cup has been managed without surgery, he is understood to be back working on grass, and the current target is December with another scan to come. Arsenal are second in the Premier League with five goals conceded in eight games and a first-choice centre-back who has not kicked a ball. That is the clearest possible vindication of the Ezri Konsa signing, and of Cristhian Mosquera, about whom Arteta was pointed this week: "We have won some big games playing with him and we need him."
The rest of the ward is less reassuring. Saka is out for weeks with the quad. Christos Tzolis, the £34m summer arrival, tore a hamstring on international duty with Greece and has barely been seen. Calafiori missed the Everton start with a small issue and came off the bench. Hincapié has returned early from an ankle injury.
Gabriel hurt himself clearing a cross on 56 minutes on Saturday, carried on and scored twelve minutes later. Rice went off on 80 with what the club has not called an injury. The reporting from now.arsenal on Monday was that both Calafiori and Tzolis should be available at Anfield, which is the only genuinely good news in the paragraph.
Rotation as policy, not panic
Arteta's response has been to rotate hard and early rather than wait to be forced. Nine changes tonight at Fleetwood. A triple substitution on 68 against Everton with the game won. Zubimendi, barely a starter this season, given the holding role in the cup. Asked on Monday whether fringe players could earn promotion, the manager was careful: "Let's see tomorrow where everybody is because individually as well we have put so much demand in so many players." The phrase to notice is "so much demand." Arsenal have played thirteen competitive matches in ten weeks and have a Champions League trip to Prague three days after Anfield.
Tactically, two shifts stand out. The first is the set-piece decline covered above, offset by an open-play threat up roughly seventeen per cent on last season. The second is the reconfigured front line. With Gabriel Martinelli sold to Al-Hilal for a club-record £60m and Leandro Trossard and Gabriel Jesus also gone, Arsenal's left side is now Eze by default and Madueke by necessity, with Max Dowman as the emergency teenager. That worked in Munich. Whether it works against a man-marking press at Anfield is Sunday's actual question.
The manager's own yellow cards
One subplot to watch: Arteta himself is a yellow card from a touchline ban. Referee Tony Harrington booked him in the second half against Everton for leaving his technical area, his second caution of the season, and one more brings a one-match suspension from the touchline. His reaction was equal parts joke and grievance. "I do have to be careful, yeah. I'm going to put sensors there!" he said, before claiming that rival managers leave their areas "36 times in 10 minutes, no yellows" and that he is building a case: "So I am clipping all of that so I have a massive storage of images." A manager compiling video evidence against his peers is good copy. A manager banned from the touchline for a title run-in is not.

Hale End
Two Debuts Tonight, and an Under-21 Side Going Nowhere
The academy's best are being promoted faster than ever. The team they leave behind has not won a league game all season.
Start with the good. Ceadach O'Neill makes his Arsenal debut at Fleetwood tonight, as does the summer free signing Illan Meslier in goal. Marli Salmon, 17, starts in central defence alongside Mosquera, and Max Dowman starts in attack. On the bench sit three more academy names in Harriman-Annous, Washington and Hashi. For a club in a title race and a Champions League league phase, that is a remarkable amount of trust to place in a League Two away tie with no VAR and penalties straight after ninety minutes.
Dowman remains the headline act and the reason the academy is a first-team story rather than a footnote. He is 16 until December 31, when a pre-contract agreed in January converts into professional terms. He already holds the records for the youngest Premier League goalscorer (16 years 73 days, against Everton in March), the youngest Premier League title winner, the youngest starter in Premier League history and the youngest player ever to appear in the Champions League, at 15 years 308 days. He scored twice in the Carabao Cup win at Ipswich Town in September and came off the bench against Everton on Saturday. Whether Saka's absence accelerates him into a league starting role is the most interesting selection question Arteta faces before Christmas.
The league table nobody mentions
Below that, it has been a grim autumn. The under-21s lost 4-1 at Leicester City on Saturday, O'Neill scoring the consolation and Salmon playing the first half before being withdrawn with tonight in mind. Six Premier League 2 games have produced four draws and two defeats and no win. Part of that is structural: the best players keep getting taken away, as the Fleetwood team sheet demonstrates. Part of it is simply a bad season. Next up is an EFL Trophy tie away at AFC Wimbledon, again shorn of anyone on first-team duty.
The under-19s have been the better watch. They drew 3-3 at Bayern Munich in the UEFA Youth League on October 21 having been 3-1 down at the break, Hakeem Abubakar opening from left-back, Salmon pulling one back after an unlucky penalty concession and the substitute Emerson Nwaneri equalising in stoppage time. That followed a 3-2 home defeat to Lille in which Porter stood out. Arsenal sit 18th in the Youth League table, which on current format would be enough to reach the knockout rounds, and travel to Slavia Prague next, the leaders, who beat them 5-1 last season.
Emerson Nwaneri deserves his own sentence. In the space of a week he went in goal as an emergency keeper for the under-18s against Tottenham Hotspur after both goalkeepers were injured, then came off the bench in Munich and scored a 90th-minute equaliser. The under-18s won that north London derby, with the recent arrival Mylo Bernard singled out.
Out on loan
On loan, the picture is mixed and the headline case is awkward. Ethan Nwaneri has made seven appearances for Borussia Dortmund without a goal or an assist, was booked inside the opening minute against Union Berlin and was withdrawn on the hour of a goalless draw. Dortmund's own Bundesliga record has held up, which is the part of the loan Arsenal will point to; the lack of a single goal involvement is the part the German press has noticed. A January reassessment feels inevitable rather than imminent.
Arsenal Women
A Derby Win Buys Renée Slegers Some Air
One win in six had turned a Champions League-winning coach into a subject of scrutiny. Then Georgia Stanway and Alessia Russo won the north London derby at Tottenham Hotspur Stadium.
Arsenal went to Tottenham Hotspur on Saturday on a five-game run without a league win, eighth in the table, and trailing inside fourteen minutes to Cathinka Tandberg's header from a corner. They were level three minutes later through Georgia Stanway, who struck the ball as cleanly as anyone in the division does, and ahead on 56 when Alessia Russo converted a penalty awarded after the ball hit Ella Morris's hand in the box. Shekiera Martinez thought she had equalised on 82; the goal went for offside. Leah Williamson celebrated with the away end. It finished 2-1 and lifted Arsenal to sixth.
Renée Slegers was careful not to oversell it. "This will definitely energise us," she said, before calling it "only a step" and stressing the need to back it up quickly. That caution is warranted. Arsenal have nine points from seven games, two wins and three draws, a negative goal difference, and sit ten behind the Chelsea and Manchester City pair on 19. Leads were surrendered at Paris FC in Europe and against Manchester City in the league, and a home draw with newly promoted Birmingham City on October 18 was the low point.
The context for all of it is a summer of almost total turnover. Out went Beth Mead to Manchester City, Katie McCabe to Chelsea, Victoria Pelova to Tottenham Hotspur, Laia Codina to West Ham United and Manuela Zinsberger to Borussia Dortmund. In came Stanway from Bayern Munich, Ona Batlle from Barcelona, Géraldine Reuteler from Eintracht Frankfurt, Élisa De Almeida from Paris Saint-Germain, Misa Rodríguez from Real Madrid, plus Selina Cerci, Lisa Baum and Isabella Damm.
Eight in, ten out. The side that won the Champions League in 2025 and finished second in Europe last season is substantially a different team, and the mystery is less why it has misfired than why anyone expected it not to.
Benfica at Meadow Park
Europe comes next, tomorrow night at Meadow Park. Benfica arrive for matchday three with one win and one defeat, having beaten Juventus 2-1 through two Marit Bratberg Lund goals and then lost 3-0 at home to Bayern Munich. They have won only six of twenty-eight Champions League matches excluding qualifiers and have gone ten European games without a clean sheet, though they have won their last three domestic fixtures by a combined 14-1 and Ivan Baptista's side can press hard and counter. Arsenal won 2-0 in Lisbon last season. They sit on four points from two, having beaten HB Køge 1-0 through a 93rd-minute Mariona Caldentey penalty and drawn with Paris FC after leading 2-0.
The absentee list is long: Caldentey (thigh), Frida Maanum, Chloe Kelly, Selina Cerci, Katie Reid and Michelle Agyemang are all out, with Kim Little back after illness and a small muscle problem. Taylor Hinds impressed at left-back against Spurs and is expected to keep the shirt. The number to watch belongs to Russo: twenty Champions League goals, one short of Fran Kirby's English record.
One more piece of recognition from the Ballon d'Or gala: Russo made the top ten of the women's award and Caldentey finished 13th. For a team sixth in the table, that is a reminder of the individual quality still in the building.
Gunners Abroad
A Night in Munich, and a Gala in London
No international window this week, so Arsenal's travelling was European and ceremonial. Both trips had consequences.
The football first. Arsenal's 2-2 at Bayern Munich is the result that keeps them seventh in a 36-team Champions League league phase with seven points from three, unbeaten, after a 1-0 win at Napoli and a 3-0 home win over Lille in which Timber, Eze and Saka scored. In Munich, Eze finished off Ødegaard's pass on 25, Alphonso Davies levelled from Harry Kane's through ball with his weaker foot, Kane converted a penalty just after the interval for his 58th Champions League goal after the ball struck Timber's hand, and Rice equalised late off a one-two with the substitute Gyökeres. It was Rice's first away goal in the competition. Raya was the reason the night did not get away.
Then London, and the Ballon d'Or. Kane won it, becoming England's first winner since Michael Owen in 2001, ahead of Kylian Mbappé and Lamine Yamal. Three Arsenal players made the top thirty: Rice 15th, up from 26th in 2024 and 27th in 2025, Gabriel 21st and Saliba 29th despite not having played this season. No Arsenal player made the top fourteen in the year after a league title and a Champions League final, which will be read in north London as a slight and elsewhere as a reasonable reflection of a team award. Arteta lost Coach of the Year to Luis de la Fuente. Raya finished second to his Spain teammate Unai Simón for the goalkeeping prize.
The award Arsenal actually won was the one that mattered most. Bukayo Saka took the Socrates Award, introduced in 2022 to recognise humanitarian work, for his involvement with BigShoe: funding surgeries for children in Africa, supporting emergency housing for families displaced by earthquakes in Turkey and Morocco, and providing health check-ups for more than 580 children in Rwanda plus health insurance for around a thousand more people. Instead of speaking about himself he read out the words of Biringiro, a 14-year-old from Rwanda whose eyesight was restored through the charity, and closed with a line worth repeating: "We know how it feels to be a child and having a ball at your feet." He collected it four days after the injury that will keep him out until winter.
Elsewhere abroad, Ethan Nwaneri continued at Borussia Dortmund without a goal involvement in seven games, and Tzolis remains sidelined by the hamstring he injured on Greece duty during the October window. The next international break is not until November, when a thinned Arsenal squad will have rather more to worry about than friendlies.

In Brief
ARTETA TO 2030  The manager signed a new contract on October 7 running to 2030, three weeks after leading Arsenal to a first title in 22 years. He is now the longest-committed manager at any of the traditional top six.
EMIRATES TO 2033  Arsenal extended their shirt and stadium partnership with Emirates in August, taking the longest-running deal of its kind in English football through to 2033.
FLEETWOOD'S FIRST  Tonight's hosts are in the last sixteen of the League Cup for the first time in their history, having beaten Chesterfield, Shrewsbury Town and Sheffield United. Lewis McCann is suspended; Rhys Bennett returns.
EUROPE'S ROAD AHEAD  After Slavia Prague away on November 4, Arsenal's remaining league-phase fixtures are Borussia Dortmund at home, Real Madrid at home on December 9, Real Betis away in January and Sabah at home to close.
NO ARSENAL IN THE TOP 14  Declan Rice's 15th place was the club's highest Ballon d'Or finish. Arsenal won the Premier League and reached a Champions League final last season.
BARTISHVILI WAITS  The Georgian teenager Andria Bartishvili, signed in September, formally joins next summer once he turns 18. He is the latest of several pre-agreements Arsenal have used to get ahead of the market on under-18 talent.
