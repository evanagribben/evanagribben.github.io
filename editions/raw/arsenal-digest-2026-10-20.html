The Lead
Back to Munich, Carrying a Different Kind of Problem
Arsenal arrive at the Allianz Arena as champions of England, unbeaten in Europe and yet to concede a Champions League goal. They have also scored eleven times in seven league games. Wednesday night asks the question this season keeps asking: can a team this good at not losing find a way to actually win something bigger?
T here is a version of this week that writes itself. Arsenal go to Bayern Munich on Wednesday with fourteen consecutive Champions League league-phase wins behind them, two clean sheets from two this season, a Premier League title in the cabinet for the first time since 2004 and a manager who has just signed on until 2030. They are second in the table, three points behind Manchester City, and they have lost once all season. By any ordinary accounting, this is a club in rude health walking into the most glamorous fixture on its calendar.
Then you look at Sunday afternoon. Arsenal went to the City Ground, held seventy-four per cent of the ball, played 629 passes at ninety per cent accuracy, and registered precisely one shot on target. It went in, because Noni Madueke drove inside from the left on eighty minutes and the ball glanced off Jair Cunha and past Matz Sels. Without that deflection this is a goalless afternoon against a side that had not scored at home in the league all season, and the conversation this morning is very different.
That is the real story of Arsenal's autumn, and it is the thing Mikel Arteta will be turning over on the flight to Bavaria. His team have won 1-0 thirteen times in the last 366 days. They have conceded five goals in seven league matches. They have also scored eleven, which is fewer than Brentford, fewer than Chelsea, and barely half of Brighton's twenty-three. A title defence built on a back four and a goalkeeper is a legitimate way to win a league. It is a far harder way to win in Munich.

The shape of the night
The tactical shape of the evening is unusually legible, because both teams are missing the player who would normally bend it. Bayern are without Jamal Musiala, who has a thigh problem, and without Michael Olise, suspended after his red card against Viking on matchday two. Vincent Kompany is recovering from a viral infection, missed training and did not take Tuesday's press conference, which assistant Aaron Danks fronted instead. Manuel Neuer has fluid on his foot. For a club that beat RB Leipzig 6-1 on Saturday, this is still a formidable team, but it is a reshuffled one, and Serge Gnabry is the likeliest man to take Olise's place against the club where he began.
Arsenal's own absence is the one that matters most. William Saliba is still out with the back problem that has cost him most of the autumn, and is only now back in individual training with a return targeted for next month. In his place Ezri Konsa, the £55m arrival from Aston Villa, has formed a partnership with Gabriel Magalhaes that has produced five clean sheets in nine appearances. Arteta was unambiguous about them after Sunday, saying the win "was not possible without them." Konsa also happens to have a good personal record against Kane, who had a thin evening at the Emirates last November.
"We reached a mountain, but not the highest one or the one that I certainly visualise."
Mikel Arteta, on winning the Premier League
That quote, given to HBO Max's Inside the Game, is the key to why this fixture carries more freight than a matchday-three away trip ought to. Last season Arsenal were on for a quadruple and finished with one trophy. They lost the EFL Cup final to Manchester City, went out of the FA Cup to Southampton in the quarter-finals, and lost the Champions League final to Paris Saint-Germain 4-3 on penalties after a 1-1 draw. The league title ended a twenty-two-year wait and it genuinely changed the club. It also left the one trophy Arteta talks about in those terms still unwon, and the shortest route to it runs through nights exactly like Wednesday.
Arteta's choices
The selection questions are real. Jurrien Timber is expected at right-back, sharing the load with Ben White. Martin Zubimendi is back from a minor hamstring issue but has played so little this season that a start would be a surprise, which leaves Declan Rice as the fixed point and a genuine choice between Myles Lewis-Skelly and Bruno Guimaraes alongside him. The midfield triangle against Joshua Kimmich and Aleksandar Pavlovic is where Danks expects the match to be settled, and he is probably right.
Up front, the arithmetic is awkward. Kai Havertz has three goals in nine appearances and has been below his level since the international break, but Arteta picks him for big games and went out of his way last week to argue that Havertz can become a twenty-five-goal striker. Viktor Gyokeres has started both Champions League matches and has not scored for the club this season. Bukayo Saka has four goals in all competitions and was quiet at Forest. The one man in form is Madueke, who scored the winner from the bench on Sunday after being hauled on for Eberechi Eze at half-time, and who is the obvious candidate to start on the left.
Arteta's framing of the whole thing has been notably unhedged. He said he wants Bayern at full strength, expects "96 minutes of man-to-man," and intends to "confront that and go for it." He will not get Bayern at full strength, and he arrives without his best defender. A draw in Munich would keep Arsenal comfortably inside the top eight and would be a perfectly good night's work. A win would be the first proper statement of the defence of a title and of a continental ambition that last May came down to a shootout.
Either way, the number that will tell us most is not the scoreline. It is the shot count. One on target at the City Ground was enough on Sunday. It will not be enough on Wednesday.

The Vacancy on the Left
Gabriel Martinelli is in Saudi Arabia, Christos Tzolis is injured, Eberechi Eze keeps drifting inside and Noni Madueke has just won a match from the bench. Arsenal's left wing has become the most open job at the club.
When Gabriel Martinelli left for Al Hilal for £60m in early September, Arsenal accepted a reality that had been building for a while: the left flank was no longer a settled position. The summer replacement was Christos Tzolis from Club Brugge at £34m, who is a useful footballer rather than a transformative one, and who is currently out with a hamstring problem until roughly the start of November.
Into the gap stepped Eberechi Eze, the £69m signing from Crystal Palace, who scored against Lille by drifting inside from the touchline, which is both his great strength and the reason he keeps being described as a stopgap out wide. At Forest he was anonymous and came off at half-time. Arteta did not name him in his post-match remarks. He did not need to.
The man who has taken the chance is Madueke, and the story of how is worth knowing. Not called up by England over the October break, he stayed at the training ground and worked with Arteta, who afterwards said he had created more chances and taken more touches in the opposition box than anyone there. He started against Leeds United, crossed for Riccardo Calafiori's equaliser in the 2-1 win, and then won the Forest game off the bench. Arteta's verdict was blunt: Madueke "gave me no option," and had been the best player in training for ten days.
It is a left-footer on the left, which is unusual for this manager, and the wider question remains open. Arteta has floated Max Dowman there. Myles Lewis-Skelly can cover it. None of that is the same as an elite option, which is precisely why the January conversation below exists.
 
Transfer News
The January Attacker, and the Line Arsenal Cannot Cross
David Ornstein's assessment is the one to trust: Arsenal know they are short in attack, they wanted to fix it in the summer and did not, and the constraint is financial rather than philosophical.
Nothing has moved publicly on this thread in the last seven days, which is itself worth reporting, because the volume of speculation around it has gone up considerably while the sourcing has stayed exactly where it was. The best account remains David Ornstein's reader Q&A for The Athletic in mid-September, and it is unusually candid. Arsenal lost Leandro Trossard, Gabriel Jesus and Martinelli in one window, loaned out Ethan Nwaneri, and did not "go beyond the signing of Christos Tzolis," who was in effect the Trossard replacement. Jesus was not replaced at all.
On whether they will act in January, Ornstein's words were: "I could see something happening because they wanted to, and I thought they needed to, in the summer and they didn't." He paired that with a warning. Arsenal "have got to be very careful on the financial side of things," and appear close to the line on UEFA's rules after a summer outlay of roughly £200m on Bruno Guimaraes, Konsa, Piero Hincapie and Tzolis. He also said plainly that he did not know of anything brewing.
Who they actually chased
What makes the thread credible rather than idle is the record of who the club actually chased. Ornstein named Morgan Rogers, Vinicius Junior and Bradley Barcola as summer pursuits that failed, and Julian Alvarez as a player the owners were willing to finance. Clubs that go after those names are not short of intent. The complication is that Arteta said last week he believes Havertz can lead the line for twenty-five goals a season, which reads either as faith in his striker or as a manager managing expectations in a window he knows may be quiet.
Certainty: exploratory. No bid, no talks, no named target stood up by a tier-one source. Treat any figure attached to this thread as invention until Ornstein or Fabrizio Romano put a name to it.
 
Three Contracts, One Self-Imposed Deadline
Martin Odegaard, Declan Rice and Jurrien Timber all have deals running to 2028. Arsenal want all three signed before the January window opens, and the November international break is the target.
Having tied Arteta down to 2030 on October 7, Arsenal have turned to the players. The reporting here comes from TEAMtalk, with Football Insider corroborating, and the shape of it is consistent: talks with Martin Odegaard, Declan Rice and Jurrien Timber have been running for months, all three contracts expire in 2028, and the club wants agreements wrapped up around the November break and certainly before January. Odegaard, who is twenty-seven and has four goals in ten appearances this season, is described as the priority.
The logic is straightforward and it is about leverage rather than loyalty. A 2028 expiry is comfortable now and uncomfortable next summer, when a club with money can start making a case. Arsenal have already extended Saliba, Gabriel, Nwaneri and Lewis-Skelly over the past year, and the pattern of this regime is to renew early rather than negotiate from weakness.
Certainty: advanced talks, single-sourced chain. No terms disclosed, no deal agreed, and no tier-one confirmation yet. The Arteta extension is official and signed.
 
Nwaneri in Dortmund: the Recall Clause Starts to Matter
Seven appearances, no goals, no assists, and a German reporter's verdict that he has not been the boost Dortmund hoped for. Arsenal wrote a January exit into the deal, and this is the week it became a live question.
This is the emerging story of the week, and it has not been fully reported in England yet. When Ethan Nwaneri joined Borussia Dortmund on loan on September 1 for a reported £4.3m fee, Arsenal insisted on a January recall option. At the time that read as routine protection for a nineteen-year-old on his first move. It reads differently now.
Nwaneri has made seven appearances without a goal or an assist. He played sixty-two minutes and was booked in a goalless draw with Union Berlin at the weekend, having also started against Werder Bremen. Dortmund are winless in three, having lost to Bodo/Glimt in the Champions League and drawn the two league games either side of it. Sport1's Manfred Sedlbauer delivered the local verdict, saying Nwaneri "has not yet proven to be the immediate boost that was hoped for." That is a German outlet's reading of a German loan, which is the version that tends to matter.
Set that against Arsenal's own position. They are short in attack by their own reckoning, constrained on spending, and own a creative forward they rate extremely highly who is currently playing without end product in a struggling team. Earlier this year Nwaneri committed his future to the club and told BBC Sport he wanted to stay and train alongside Saka, Eze and Odegaard. There has been no suggestion from the club that a recall is being considered. The clause exists, the context is shifting, and this is the thread to watch between now and the new year.
Certainty: the recall clause is confirmed and on the record. A recall itself is speculation, and nobody reputable has reported that Arsenal intend to use it.

The Squad
A Champion Winning Without Scoring
Thirteen 1-0 wins in a year. Five goals conceded in seven league games. Substitutes deciding three matches in a row. Arteta's team has found a formula that works and has not yet found the one that thrills.
The shape is fixed at 4-2-3-1 and the rotation inside it is heavy. Arteta made five changes from Lille to Forest and five the other way before that, which is what a four-competition season demands. What does not change is the spine. David Raya behind Gabriel and Konsa, Rice as the organising intelligence in midfield, Odegaard between the lines, Saka on the right.
The defensive numbers are the story of the season so far. Five goals conceded in seven league matches. Two clean sheets from two in Europe. Nine clean sheets in the last fourteen Champions League group or league-phase games. Against Forest, a side with fourteen attempts produced two shots on target and no chance that Opta would classify as big. Raya was explicit afterwards about where the credit lies, singling out the work on set pieces, and Arteta went further on his centre-backs.
The scoring problem
The attacking numbers are the problem. Eleven league goals in seven games. One shot on target in ninety minutes at the City Ground. A substitute has scored the decisive goal in three consecutive matches, which is a compliment to the bench and a question about the starters. Havertz has three in nine and has dipped since the break. Gyokeres, a £63.5m striker now in his second season, has not scored for the club at all this term after a start disrupted by injury and by Arteta's preference. Saka has four. Bruno Guimaraes, settling in nicely with two league goals including a lovely finish against Leeds, found no room at all in a Forest low block.
There is a structural wrinkle behind the set-piece figures worth keeping an eye on. Last season Arsenal scored a Premier League record nineteen goals from corners and twenty-five from set pieces in all, around thirty-five per cent of their total, under Nicolas Jover. Over the summer the league instructed officials to take a stricter line on holding and blocking in the box at set pieces, a change some have nicknamed the anti-Arsenal rule. The league has never framed it that way, and the counter-argument is that a team which attacks far more corners than it defends might benefit. Seven games is too few to judge, but with the attack this dry, the corner routines are carrying more weight than anyone planned.
Arteta's own diagnosis of Forest was characteristically specific. He said his team won a game they had failed to win in each of the previous two seasons, described Forest's low block as "like a Christmas tree," and noted that Arsenal had created chances that simply did not reach the target. "You have to be clinical," he said. It is the shortest and most accurate summary of the season available.

Hale End
A New Striker, a Fourteen-Year-Old, and a Goalkeeper Reminding Everyone
Mylo Bernard scored twice to beat Tottenham in stoppage time, Jack Porter kept the U19s in a Youth League game they lost anyway, and three academy players were in first-team training on Tuesday morning.
Start with the signing nobody outside the academy was talking about a month ago. Mylo Bernard, recruited from Crystal Palace in September, scored both goals in the U18s' 2-1 win over Tottenham on Saturday, the winner arriving in the seventh minute of stoppage time. It was a fourth straight league win for Josh Smith's side, it ended Tottenham's unbeaten league start, and it lifted Arsenal to fourth, five points behind Chelsea, who have a game in hand. Bernard had already scored for England U17s against Germany before making his U21 debut off the bench a week earlier. The game also produced a genuine oddity: goalkeeper Jack Talbot suffered a concussion, his replacement Max Lo Presti also went off injured, and outfield player Emerson Nwaneri finished the match in goal.
Youth League, and the U21 drought
The U19s lost 3-2 at home to Lille in their first home Youth League tie of the campaign, having opened with a win over Napoli. Josh Ogunnaike and Elijah Upson scored, but the night belonged to Jack Porter. The eighteen-year-old goalkeeper, who has often found himself behind Khari Ranson for U21 starts, made a series of superb saves in a losing cause, which is exactly the sort of performance that reopens a selection debate. Joshua Sesay, Hakeem Abubakar and Theo Odaji-Ukueku all made Youth League debuts. Most strikingly, fourteen-year-old Luis Munoz played and delivered the corner from which Upson scored.
The U21s remain the problem child. A 2-2 home draw with Sunderland, rescued late by Andre Harriman-Annous after James Scanlon's earlier goal, left them still without a league win this season, even against ten men for the closing stages. Marli Salmon captained the side on his return from injury and sixteen-year-old Emerson Nwaneri started on the right wing.
The most interesting academy item of the week, though, came on Tuesday morning. Ceadach O'Neill, Ogunnaike and goalkeeper Remy Mitchell all trained with the first team ahead of Munich, with U18 defender Elyon Mbala joining the second half of the session and Teshaun Murisa, Harriman-Annous, Ife Ibrahim, Jaden Dixon and Upson also involved. O'Neill is the one to watch. He was on the bench for the Lille game, has already won a senior cap for Northern Ireland at eighteen, and is still waiting on a first-team debut for his club.

Arsenal Women
Booed at the Emirates, and Running Out of Season
Eighteen months after winning the Champions League, Arsenal are eighth in the Women's Super League, winless in five, and twelve points behind Manchester City. Renee Slegers has started saying the quiet part out loud.
Saturday lunchtime at the Emirates was the lowest point of a bad autumn. Newly promoted Birmingham City, who had drawn three and won none, went ahead through Oceane Hurtre on fifty-three minutes on the counter. Olivia Smith equalised thirteen minutes later with a strike Lucy Baggaley could not hold. Baggaley then kept out Lisa Baum and Lotte Wubben-Moy on the line, Alessia Russo had already struck the post from a Leah Williamson cross, and Smith missed a presentable chance to win it at the end. Arsenal were booed off.
That is one point from a home game against the side second from bottom, and it leaves Arsenal eighth after six matches with six points: one win, three draws, two defeats. Manchester City lead with eighteen. Chelsea have sixteen, London City Lionesses fifteen. Sky's Izzy Christiansen put it about as gently as it can be put, saying that with results elsewhere going as expected "you would almost write them out of the title race." It is October.
Renee Slegers, who won the Champions League with this club in 2025, has stopped deflecting. Asked about her own position, she said that if she can no longer help the team, "then a change needs to happen." She also identified the recurring issue precisely, which is small details costing points rather than any collapse in quality. The club made clear over the international break that she would not be sacked during it.
The injury list
Any fair account has to put the injury list next to the table, because it is severe. Mariona Caldentey has a hamstring tear and will miss the whole of this block. Selina Cerci, signed from Hoffenheim in the summer, is out for around ten weeks after knee surgery. Goalkeeper Daphne van Domselaar, Michelle Agyemang and Katie Reid all have knee problems, and Chloe Kelly a hamstring issue. Elisa De Almeida is back from a muscle injury. That is a first-choice creator, a first-choice goalkeeper and England's breakout forward all unavailable at once, in a summer when ten players left and eight arrived.
The brightest thread is Olivia Smith. The Canadian had not started since September 19 while the club quietly managed what Slegers called "some issues," but she is now, in the manager's words, "pain-free" and "back to her best." She scored twice for Canada against Denmark over the break and then scored the equaliser on Saturday. Slegers called her "a player who has such a high ceiling," praising her dribbling in tight spaces and her two-footedness. In a team short of goals, she is the most obvious source of them.
Europe, and what comes next
Europe is the consolation. Arsenal have four points from two Women's Champions League games, beating HB Koge 1-0 at Meadow Park and drawing 2-2 at Paris FC, a game they led by two. Russo, Georgia Stanway and Caldentey have the goals. The league phase gets considerably harder from here: Benfica at home on October 28, then away to reigning champions Barcelona on November 11, Lyon at home on November 19 and Inter Milan away in December.
Before any of that comes Saturday lunchtime at Tottenham, live on BBC One, against a derby rival currently several places and several points above them. Slegers warned before the Birmingham game that her squad faces nine matches in six weeks. She needs the first of them to go right.

Gunners Abroad
Munich, Twice Over
The first team and the under-19s make the same trip on the same day, Ethan Nwaneri carries the Arsenal flag in somebody else's Champions League side, and the women's squad came back from the break with thirteen goals between them.
There is no international window this week, so European duty is the whole of it, and Arsenal have an unusual amount of it. The first team are in Munich on Wednesday. So are the U19s, who face Bayern Munich's youth side in the Youth League on the same trip, looking to recover from the home defeat by Lille. Bayern's U19s lost 2-0 to Viking on matchday two, so both sides arrive needing a result.
Ethan Nwaneri is the one Arsenal player in European action for somebody else. Borussia Dortmund return to league-phase duty this week having lost to Bodo/Glimt last time out, and the nineteen-year-old's involvement, after a run of starts without a goal or assist, will be watched closely at Colney as well as in Westphalia. In Scotland, Ismeal Kabia continues a bruising education at St Mirren, who lost 3-1 to Aberdeen, and in Sweden Charles Sagoe Jr is seeing out the final weeks of a Kalmar FF loan that expires in January.
It is worth recording the international break that closed a week ago, because it was a notably good one for the women's squad and a thin one for the men's attack. Slegers reported that Arsenal players scored thirteen goals across the window. Georgia Stanway scored in both England games, with her manager noting how good she is "in and around the box." Olivia Smith scored twice for Canada against Denmark, and Caitlin Foord, Frida Maanum, Geraldine Reuteler, Alessia Russo, Stina Blackstenius and Smilla Holmberg all found the net for their countries.
On the men's side the break was less kind. Viktor Gyokeres did at least score the winner for Sweden against Romania in late September, his only goal of the autumn in any shirt, but Sweden's campaign has since unravelled and head coach Jon Dahl Tomasson has been dismissed. Noni Madueke, by contrast, was not called up by England at all, stayed in London, outworked everybody in training and has started or scored in every game since. There is a lesson in there somewhere.

In Brief
No grand expansion. Arsenal have ruled out a large-scale redevelopment of the Emirates, with an update delivered at an Advisory Board meeting attended by co-chair Josh Kroenke and chief executive Richard Garlick. A working group had looked at options rising towards 80,000 seats, but that would have meant temporary relocation, probably to Wembley, and The Times reported Arteta was wary of the disruption. Capacity stays at 60,704 and the focus turns to hospitality, catering, retail, connectivity and safe standing, which arrives in the North Bank in 2027/28 having been introduced at the Clock End this season.
December is now fixed. Sky Sports has made its final picks and Arsenal's December schedule is confirmed: Brentford away on Wednesday December 2 at 8:15pm UK, the north London derby away on Sunday December 6 at 4:30pm, Real Madrid at home in the Champions League on December 9, Bournemouth at home on December 13, Manchester United at home on December 20 at 4:30pm, Crystal Palace away on December 27 and Fulham away on December 30. With a League Cup fifth-round tie possible, it could be eight games in a month.
A first meeting in Lancashire. Next Tuesday's League Cup round-of-16 tie takes Arsenal to Fleetwood Town of League Two, a club they have never played competitively. Kick-off is 8:00pm UK at Highbury Stadium, which is a coincidence of names the Fleetwood press office has understandably enjoyed.
Arteta, signed and sealed. The manager's new contract, confirmed on October 7, runs to 2030 and followed the club's first league title since 2003/04. It is the longest commitment of his tenure and was agreed before this season's European campaign began.
An old boy's verdict. Serge Gnabry, who joined Arsenal at sixteen and is likely to start for Bayern on Wednesday, said he is not remotely surprised by Arteta's coaching career. "He was certainly a leader within our team back then," Gnabry said, recalling a captain who was approachable and free with advice.
The corner question. The Premier League's summer guidance telling officials to clamp down on holding and blocking at set pieces has yet to produce a measurable effect on Arsenal, who scored a record nineteen corner goals last season. Chelsea, meanwhile, triggered the release clause of Aston Villa's set-piece coach Austin MacPhee, which tells you how seriously the rest of the league now takes the discipline Jover made fashionable.
