AT A GLANCE
August is the month San Francisco stops apologising for its weather and simply hands you the calendar. The Polo Field disappears under Outside Lands (Aug 7–9, and yes, it is sold out), but the better story this month is free: the 89th Stern Grove Festival closes its season with four consecutive heavyweights — Violent Femmes, Patti LaBelle, Public Enemy and, on Aug 16, Al Green. Japantown and Yerba Buena bookend the first two weekends with Nihonmachi and Pistahan; the Giants play seventeen home dates; the Perseids peak on the night of Aug 12; and out past the bridges, Sebastopol presses apples, Napa runs a proper county fair, and Oakland closes six blocks of MacArthur for world music. Bring a jacket to all of it.
THIS WEEKEND’S WEATHER
Sat Aug 1: sunny, high near 69°F / low 56°F, rain chance 5%. Sun Aug 2: clear, high 70°F / low 56°F, rain chance 0%. Into next week it holds: upper 60s by day, mid-50s at night, no measurable rain through Aug 8.
Rain contingency: effectively none needed — the August hazard here is fog and a hard west wind, not water. Anything at Stern Grove, Ocean Beach, the Polo Field or Lands End runs 8–12°F colder than the Mission and can grey over by mid-afternoon. Pack the jacket you think you won’t need. Outdoor events below run rain or shine unless noted.

PICK OF THE MONTH
Al Green closes the Stern Grove Festival
Sunday, August 16, 2026 | Sigmund Stern Grove, 19th Ave & Sloat Blvd, Sunset/Parkside | Free
With Goapele, the Glide Memorial Ensemble and KALW’s DJ Wonway Posibul.

Why this one. Plenty of things in this issue are bigger. Nothing else is this improbable. Al Green is 80 years old and tours almost never; the Reverend has spent most of four decades behind a pulpit in Memphis rather than on a West Coast stage. He is closing the 89th season of a festival that has been giving away its entire summer for free since 1938, in a eucalyptus canyon with natural acoustics that no promoter could build on purpose. Add the Glide Ensemble — a gospel choir from a Tenderloin church that has been feeding this city since the 1960s — behind “Let’s Stay Together,” and you have a bill that is unrepeatable and costs nothing. That is the whole argument.
How to get in: free general admission is distributed by advance lottery (opens six weeks before each concert) and by a day-of Community Box Office of 1,500 tickets, two per person, first come. Reserved tables are sold separately. Gates open late morning; music runs early afternoon — the festival has not published exact set times for this date, so confirm on the day.
Getting there: Muni K/L/M to West Portal then the 23 Monterey, or the 28 down 19th Ave. Street parking around Sloat is a bloodsport by 11am. Bring a blanket, low chairs and a real jacket — the Grove is a fog trap and runs cold even on a bright day.

ON THE HORIZON
Just past the four-week grid, Labor Day weekend (Sat Sep 5 – Mon Sep 7) is shaping up to be the busiest stretch of early autumn. Watch for Opera in the Park, the San Francisco Opera’s free outdoor concert in Robin Williams Meadow that draws 15,000 picnic blankets to Golden Gate Park; the Haight Ashbury Street Fair, with two music stages and 200-plus vendors between Stanyan and Masonic; a free Kite by the Bay day at Candlestick Point State Recreation Area; a free daytime disco on Union Square; and the Bizerkeley Food Festival, the Bay Area’s largest vegan gathering, at North Berkeley BART.
