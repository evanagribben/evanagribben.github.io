The Lead

After the derby, the night that decides Milan's European autumn

Rúben Amorim's side beat Inter 3-1 to climb to fifth in Serie A. Tonight they must turn that energy into points against an unbeaten Ferencváros, because in the Europa League they are 24th of 36.

There are wins that change a table and wins that change a mood. Milan's 3-1 victory over Inter at San Siro on Saturday did a little of the first and a great deal of the second. It handed Cristian Chivu's side their first league defeat of the season and lifted Amorim's team to fifth on 21 points, seven behind leaders Lazio and two off the top four.

It also came three days after the worst night of the new regime: a 4-2 home defeat by Bologna in which Milan led 2-1 at half-time and then collapsed under a Mbangula hat-trick. The derby was the reply. Now comes the harder question: can this team carry it into Europe, where three matchdays have produced one win, two defeats and a red card?

How Amorim won the derby

The game was settled inside 43 minutes, and almost everything ran through Diego Moreira. The €50m summer signing from Strasbourg crossed for Christian Pulisic to head in after four minutes, then delivered the corner that Adrien Rabiot turned in on ten. After Alessandro Bastoni headed Inter back into it from a corner, Moreira curled a left-footed free kick over the wall just before the break.

Moreira became only the second Milan player in 20 years with three goal involvements in a derby, after Rafael Leão in 2022. It was Milan's fourth straight derby win and, per Opta Analyst, their third in a row in Serie A for the first time since 2002 to 2004. Their unbeaten run against Inter now stands at eight.

The second half was about survival. Inter finished with 19 shots to Milan's seven and roughly 1.68 expected goals to 0.62, per the single stats feed we could find (AllFootball), so treat those numbers with care. On 75 minutes referee Marco Guida gave Inter a penalty for a Mario Gila handball, then overturned it after a VAR review showed the ball had struck his midriff.

It was not an amazing game, but it's an amazing night for our fans. That is part of the process.

Rúben Amorim to DAZN, Oct 31

Amorim, speaking to DAZN, was candid about it. Inter were "very strong in the air", he said, so Milan "changed the way we addressed the game", and in the second half "we maintained the ball", a credit he directed at Luka Modrić (SoccerNews). He also named the three players who had just returned from injury: "Gila, Moreira and Rabiot made a big difference."

Why tonight matters more

The Europa League table is unforgiving. Milan opened with a 2-0 home defeat by Benfica, then won 4-0 in Salzburg behind an Alphadjo Cissé hat-trick, then lost 3-0 at Bournemouth after Mike Maignan was sent off. Three points from three games leaves them 24th, the last of the play-off places, with four games left.

Ferencváros arrive with nine points from nine and, per TuttoMercatoWeb, a 20-match unbeaten run. A defeat would leave Milan looking over their shoulder at elimination before the Olympiacos trip on Nov 26. A win would cut the gap to the Hungarians to three points.

Amorim framed it bluntly at his press conference on Wednesday. "We need to forget about Inter; there's a new competition," he said, adding: "This is the most important game, but we'll need to rotate, all players are ready" (Goal). To Sky Sport Italia he called it "a crucial game" in which Milan must "stay humble and attentive to details".

The selection puzzle

Rotation is unavoidable with Genoa away on Sunday. Maignan serves his one-match ban, so Lorenzo Torriani is expected to make his first start of the season. Strahinja Pavlović has been left out to rest, not through injury, per TMW.

Football Italia's probable XI has Torriani behind a back four of Filippo Terracciano, Koni De Winter, Sankhoun Diawara and Davide Bartesaghi; Omari Hutchinson, Ardon Jashari, Yunus Musah and Cissé across midfield; Rabiot behind Gonçalo Ramos. Modrić and Pulisic would start on the bench. Sources disagree on whether Diawara plays centrally or wide.

The key battle is in central midfield. Without Modrić's tempo, Jashari and Musah must stop Ferencváros's 4-2-3-1 finding Kristoffer Zachariassen between the lines. Up front, the night is another chance for Ramos, the €74m club-record signing, whose slow start has become a talking point.

Off the Pitch

A loss on the books, a stadium on paper and a kit deal in the wings

A crowded business week: the first net loss of the RedBird era, the unveiling of the Generali Stadium and a reported return to Adidas.

Milan's shareholders approved the 2025/26 accounts on Oct 29, and they show the cost of a season without European football. The club reported a net loss of €23.7m, the first loss under RedBird, after profits of about €3m the previous year (Calcio e Finanza). The value of production fell 6.1 percent to €464.6m.

Net financial debt rose from €92m to €145m, per Pianeta Milan. There were brighter lines: sponsorship passed €100m for the first time, and average attendance topped 72,000, the best in Serie A for a second straight year. RedBird paid in €53m toward a future capital increase on Aug 19, per the club's figures reported by MilanPress.

The Generali Stadium

The same day, Milan and Inter unveiled the design of their new home. Designed by Foster + Partners and MANICA, it will hold about 71,500 in two tiers. Work is due to start in 2028, with a first match targeted for the 2031/32 season, subject to approvals.

ANSA puts the whole development at about €1.2bn. Generali's naming-rights deal is worth about €650m over 25 years, per Adnkronos, roughly €13m a year for each club.

Adidas, and Cardinale's message

Gazzetta reports that Milan have agreed to return to Adidas from 2028/29 in a deal worth €500m over ten years, about €50m a season, roughly double what Puma pays now (Calcio e Finanza). It is not yet official.

Owner Gerry Cardinale, speaking to Gazzetta on the eve of the derby, said Amorim "is absolutely not in danger", that the rebuild is only about a quarter done, and that Milan will be "aggressive and opportunistic" in January (via Pianeta Milan).

Transfer News

Krösche agreed as head of football, with a January shopping list waiting

Milan's new football chief is lined up, a centre-back is the priority and Cardinale has fallen for a striker Club Brugge will not sell.

Markus Krösche: agreement reached, announcement pending

Milan have found the man to run their football operation. Gianluca Di Marzio reported on Oct 31 that an agreement had been reached with Markus Krösche, 46, who has been released by Eintracht Frankfurt at his own request. Certainty: agreement reached, not yet official.

The timing is still moving. Di Marzio expects an announcement by Dec 1 and Calciomercato.com in December; Bild and Sky Germany say by Jan 1, 2027. Matteo Moretto reported on Nov 5 that the move is finalised and that he starts in January.

Compensation to Frankfurt is reported variously: about €4m (Bild, via 90min), €5m (Sky Germany) and a €7m clause that Milan may not pay in full (Calciomercato.com). His Frankfurt record is the selling point: Randal Kolo Muani and Omar Marmoush arrived free and left for €95m and €75m.

The role matters because of what Cardinale told Gazzetta: he will never again hand "the keys" to a sporting director. Krösche will run things day to day at Milanello and work alongside the owner, not above him.

The centre-back hunt: Inácio first, Sylla as the alternative

Injuries have made defence the January priority. Matteo Gabbia is out until early 2027 with a hamstring tear, and Fikayo Tomori is out for about a month (Calciomercato.com).

The favourite since June, per Fabrizio Romano, is Gonçalo Inácio, Sporting's 25-year-old left-footed centre-back and a former Amorim player. His release clause is €60m; Sporting's asking price is reported at €40m to €45m (Virgilio). The summer move stalled because Sporting wanted a replacement first. Certainty: concrete interest, no talks confirmed.

The cheaper option is Massiré Sylla, Union Saint-Gilloise's 21-year-old, 1.96m Senegalese centre-back, contracted to 2030. Moretto reported scouting, and Pianeta Milan says exploratory contacts have been made. Certainty: monitoring, single-sourced on contacts. Romano expects "at least one, probably two" January signings.

A forward to push Ramos: Tresoldi and Endrick

Ramos has a team-high eight big chances missed in Serie A, per FotMob, and Gazzetta's Luca Bianchin says the club views his start with "some concern". Cardinale, per Gazzetta, was won over by Nicolò Tresoldi when the 22-year-old scored a Champions League hat-trick against Inter on Oct 13. Club Brugge turned down €50m bids in the summer and will not sell in January (Goal). Certainty: owner's interest, club refusing.

Endrick remains a background thread. Gazzetta reported in October that he would choose Milan if Real Madrid let him go on loan, but José Mourinho said anyone who wants him in January "will have to go over me" (DAZN). Certainty: player keen, Real blocking.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Ferencváros: organised, in form and dangerous between the lines

Hungary's most decorated club arrives at San Siro with three Europa League wins from three and a long unbeaten run.

Ferencváros have been the surprise package of the league phase. They won 3-1 at Celtic, beat Viktoria Plzeň 2-1 and thrashed Torreense 4-1, scoring nine and conceding three, which puts them fourth in the 36-team table (UEFA). TuttoMercatoWeb counts 20 matches unbeaten in all competitions.

Head coach Balázs Borbély, appointed in June, sets them up in a 4-2-3-1 (Ferencváros). The danger man is Kristoffer Zachariassen, the Norwegian attacking midfielder with nine goals and six assists in 19 appearances this season. Krisztián Lisztes and Bamidele Yusuf carry the threat up front, and Yusuf and Mariano Gómez both scored against Torreense.

Where Milan can hurt them

Domestically they have been less convincing: sixth in the Hungarian league with 14 points from nine games as of late October, after a 1-1 draw with Kisvárda. They have conceded in all three Europa League games. Ötvös (knee) and Botka are out and Kovačević is doubtful, per Sports Mole.

The Digest found no previous competitive meeting between the clubs, so this is new ground. Milan's set-piece record cuts both ways: Moreira's delivery created two derby goals, but Milan conceded from corners against both Inter and Bologna.

Next up after that: Genoa

Sunday's trip to the Ferraris is a real test. Daniele De Rossi's Genoa are 11th on 13 points but have won three straight, including 2-0 over Juventus and 2-1 at Lecce. Milan won 2-1 there in May. The Squad

A back four, a crowded treatment room and a midfield that scores

Amorim has moved away from his trademark back three, and the injury list is now long enough to shape the January window.

Milan began the season in Amorim's familiar 3-4-2-1 but switched to a back four from around the Sassuolo game in October, moving Gila to right-back (Fantamaster). Gazzetta called the derby shape a fluid 4-2-3-1, with Rabiot pushing up behind Ramos.

Mike Maignan is captain and Gabbia his deputy. The goals have come from midfield: Rabiot leads the Serie A scoring with five, plus three assists, and Moreira has four goals and three assists, per FotMob. Cissé, 20, has four in Serie A and a Europa League hat-trick.

Chukwueze's long road

The club confirmed on Nov 4 that Samuel Chukwueze had surgery in London on a ruptured tendon in his left hip, an injury from the Atalanta game on Oct 18 that Milan first tried to treat without an operation. The club gave no timeline. Corriere dello Sport says at least three months, Gazzetta possibly the spring. With Saelemaekers also out, a winger has joined the January wish list.

Set pieces and the Ramos question

Moreira now takes corners and free kicks, and the derby showed why. The worry is at the other end, where Bastoni's header and Bartesaghi's own goal against Bologna both came from dead balls. Ramos has about 4.1 expected goals in Serie A, per FotMob, but has missed eight big chances, the most in the squad.

The Academy

Primavera hit five, Milan Futuro climb to third

A rout in Monza ends a slump for Giovanni Renna's side, while the Serie D team wins twice in four days.

The Milan Primavera won 5-0 at Monza on Oct 31, ending a run of four league defeats in five games. Anastasio, Grassini and Messaoudi scored, and Zaramella added two goals and an assist (acmilan.com). Per SempreMilan, the win lifted coach Giovanni Renna's side to eighth. They host AlbinoLeffe on Sunday.

Milan Futuro, in Serie D Group B after relegation from Serie C, won 3-2 at Virtus Verona on Nov 1 through Menon, Perera and Pagliei. On Wednesday they completed a 2-1 win over Piacenza, finishing a game abandoned at half-time in October when the referee was hurt; Ossola and Asanji scored (acmilan.com). They have 20 points, third in the group per Il Giorno.

Youngsters in the first team

Christian Comotto and Camarda have both played in the Europa League this season, and Torriani is set for his first start tonight. Bartesaghi has become a regular substitute. Both Comotto and Camarda are expected to go out on loan in January.

Women's Team

Milan Women lose at Como, with the derby next

Suzanne Bakker's side have six points from five games and face second-placed Inter on Saturday.

AC Milan Women lost 1-0 at Como Women on Sunday. Bernardi scored on 54 minutes, and Nevado hit the junction of post and bar on 80. "We finished strongly, but we were also competitive during the first 65 minutes," coach Suzanne Bakker said (acmilan.com).

The rossonere have won two of five: 2-1 against Juventus and 3-0 against Ternana, with defeats to Como 1907, Roma and Como Women. Scotland international Grimshaw leads their scoring with three. They sit level with Juventus on six points, per Virgilio.

Derby day at the Vismara

Saturday's derby (6:00 AM Pacific) is a steep test. Inter are second on 13 points, two behind leaders Roma. It is shown in Italy on DAZN and Rai; we could not confirm a US outlet. Milan are already out of the Women's Cup after the group stage. No transfer news this week.
