The Lead

Carrick's reprieve runs into the international break

A first win in ten against Roma bought Michael Carrick a week. A wild 4-4 with Aston Villa left United 18th, and the people who decide his future now have a fortnight to make up their minds.

For a few seconds on Sunday evening, Old Trafford believed again. Patrick Dorgu rose to head in Bruno Fernandes's corner in the 96th minute, and United had drawn 4-4 with Villa after trailing by two goals twice. Then the table refreshed. United are 18th, eight league games without a win, with one victory from ten.

That is the context for the fortnight ahead. The international break gives the club's hierarchy room to do what it did not do in late October: decide, calmly and without a match looming, whether Michael Carrick is the man to lead the climb. The next game is at Liverpool on November 22.

A week of two faces

The week began with relief. On Tuesday, Roma led 2-0 at Old Trafford inside 17 minutes in the Champions League, the second a penalty for handball. Fernandes scored a retaken penalty on 58 minutes. Matheus Cunha then struck twice late on, the winner coming on 86 minutes, to end a run of nine games without a win (Sky Sports).

The numbers flattered nobody's doubts about effort. United's xG was 2.72 to Roma's 1.53, and Roma did not have a shot after half-time, according to Sports Illustrated. Cunha was then sent off for a second booking in stoppage time, his first yellow coming for celebrating shirtless in the crowd.

Carrick refused to frame it as personal salvation. "I really 100% wasn't thinking about myself," he told ESPN. His captain went further: "We don't need to save anyone's job," Fernandes said to Sky Sports.

Sunday's chaos, and what it exposed

Villa scored after 59 seconds through Alysson, and Nicolas Jackson made it 2-0 after three minutes and 40 seconds, the earliest United have ever been two down in a Premier League game, according to Opta Analyst. Senne Lammens spilled the shot that led to the second.

United came back through a Fernandes penalty and Youri Tielemans before half-time. Villa went ahead again through a John McGinn penalty, after Diogo Dalot fouled Alysson, and a Leon Goretzka finish on 53 minutes. Joshua Zirkzee and Dorgu, both substitutes, rescued a point in stoppage time (ESPN).

“It's not going to happen, no.”

Michael Carrick, asked after the Villa draw whether he would resign (via Goal)

The record books noticed both halves. No Premier League side had come from two goals down twice to draw a game. But United also have no clean sheet in their first ten league games for the first time since 1986-87, and the 18th place is their lowest at this stage of a top-flight season since that same year (Opta Analyst).

Inside the boardroom

The decision sits with Omar Berrada and Jason Wilcox, with Sir Jim Ratcliffe above them. After crisis talks in late October they gave Carrick more time. David Ornstein of The Athletic reported on November 5 that the club had issued a vote of confidence with a three-match assessment, and that a lack of an obvious replacement was a factor (Football365).

Since then United have lost at Chelsea, beaten Roma and drawn with Villa. Laurie Whitwell of The Athletic said on Sunday that a reassessment is planned around the break but there is no immediate urgency. Fabrizio Romano said United have made no official contact with other coaches and expect a decision before January (Football365).

If they do act, Sky Sports reported on November 5 that the club is unlikely to appoint another interim (Sky Sports). The same Sky report listed Julian Nagelsmann, Fabian Hurzeler and Eddie Howe among the names being weighed, and the Guardian reported in late October that Howe was Wilcox's preference (Irish Examiner). None of that is confirmed by the club.

What to judge him on

Carrick's case rests on credit earned last season, when he took over from Ruben Amorim in January, finished third (Sports Mole) and signed a deal to 2028 in May (ESPN). The case against is this autumn: a back line that concedes in clusters, a goalkeeper short of confidence, and one league win in ten.

Before the Villa game he admitted the club had given him no assurances. "Whatever is going to happen is going to happen," he said (Goal). The next fortnight will tell us which way it goes. Anfield, on November 22, may be the first game of a new era or the first test of a renewed mandate.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Liverpool at Anfield: the worst possible fixture at the worst possible time

Liverpool are fourth under a new head coach, but they are carrying injuries in midfield and United have won the last two meetings.

United's first game after the break is at Liverpool on Sunday, November 22, moved by Sky Sports to a 4:30pm UK kickoff, which is 8:30am Pacific (Sky Sports). Liverpool are fourth with 18 points from ten games, ten clear of United, after a 3-1 win over Crystal Palace on Sunday in which Alexis Mac Allister, Florian Wirtz and Alexander Isak scored (ESPN).

This is a new Liverpool. Andoni Iraola replaced Arne Slot in the summer. We could not confirm his preferred shape at Anfield, but his Bournemouth sides were built on an intense, high press, which is exactly the kind of pressure that has rattled United's build-up from the back this autumn.

Who to stop

Isak is Liverpool's top scorer with seven goals in all competitions. Wirtz scored his first league goal of the season against Palace, and Mac Allister has four goals. Summer signing Bradley Barcola adds pace on the flank, which will test whoever plays full-back for United.

Liverpool have worries. Dominik Szoboszlai hyper-extended his knee against Palace and faces checks (This Is Anfield). Ryan Gravenberch has a heel problem, and Conor Bradley is still regaining sharpness (Anfield Index). Their form has wobbled too: they lost 1-0 to Arsenal on November 1.

History offers United a sliver of hope. They won the last two meetings: 2-1 at Anfield in October 2025, sealed by Harry Maguire's late header, and 3-2 at Old Trafford in May 2026 (Sports Illustrated).

Transfer News

United set to move for a striker in January, whoever is in charge

With Benjamin Sesko injured again, a traditional number nine is the clearest need. Fabrizio Romano says something will happen; the names are less certain.

The most solid transfer line of the week came from Fabrizio Romano, who said on November 6: "I expect Man United to do something in terms of new strikers" (Football365). On October 30 he described the target as a traditional number nine, wanted regardless of who is manager. Certainty: concrete interest.

The driver is fitness. Benjamin Sesko has a recurring shin problem and may not return until 2027, according to Sports Mole. Joshua Zirkzee's form off the bench has been encouraging, but Romano called his future one to watch for 2027.

The name most often attached is Crystal Palace's Jean-Philippe Mateta, linked with a January move by Football Insider rather than a top-tier insider (Football365). Certainty: rumour.

Leeds striker Dominic Calvert-Lewin has been considered, but Laurie Whitwell called a move remote and Leeds are pushing a new contract (CaughtOffside). Certainty: link, cooling.

Contract clocks tick for Martínez, Shaw and the captain

Three senior players are approaching the final stretch of their deals, and each situation is moving.

Lisandro Martínez is under contract to summer 2027, with a club option for 2028. The Sun reported on October 31 that he wants to leave on a free next summer with Atlético Madrid interested (Sky Sports Paper Talk). Talks with his camp are expected in November. Certainty: concrete interest from Atlético, single-sourced.

A claim on social media that he has agreed personal terms with Atlético has not been backed by any reliable outlet, and we are not treating it as fact. Martínez served a one-match domestic ban against Villa after his red card at Chelsea and is available for Liverpool.

Luke Shaw's contract expires next summer, and Romano said on November 6 that his departure in 2027 is a serious, possible scenario (Football365). Certainty: possible exit, per Romano.

Bruno Fernandes is contracted to 2027 with a club option. Reports via TEAMtalk say he wants a deal to 2030 and that talks have stalled (CaughtOffside). That is single-sourced. He was dropped for the Bournemouth game but kept the captaincy (ESPN).

Rebuilding the back line

United reportedly want at least three new defenders over the next two windows.

Dean Jones of Flashscore reported that United want at least three new defenders, each matched by an exit (Football365). That is single-sourced, but it fits a season in which United have conceded freely.

One name is Como centre-back Jacobo Ramón, valued at about €50 million and targeted for summer 2027. Real Madrid hold a buy-back option, which complicates any deal (Goal). Certainty: rumour/link.

The Squad

Injuries pile up in midfield as the goalkeeper debate grows

Carlos Baleba is out for weeks, Sesko is out for longer, and two senior players are aiming to return at Anfield.

Carlos Baleba limped off against Roma with a hamstring injury, and Carrick confirmed he will miss a number of weeks, taking him past the break (Hayters). Manuel Ugarte is still out with a knee injury and has no return date.

There is better news on Marcus Rashford (hip) and Luke Shaw, who are both aiming for Liverpool on November 22, according to Sports Mole. Patrick Dorgu returned from a hamstring problem against Villa and scored the equaliser.

Selection talking points

Senne Lammens is under the most scrutiny after Villa's first two goals (Yahoo Sports). Shea Lacey has forced his way into the conversation: he set up Cunha's winner against Roma and then filled in at right-back.

Fernandes remains captain despite being dropped for the Bournemouth game in October (Sky Sports). He has now scored penalties against both Roma and Villa, and his 28 Premier League penalties since February 2020 put him level with Mohamed Salah for that period (Opta Analyst).

Cunha will be available at Anfield, but his red card against Roma brings an automatic one-match Champions League ban, and UEFA decided not to add to it (Sports Illustrated). That rules him out at Sporting CP on November 25.

Carrington

Thompson's first goal, Armer's red and a busy week for the youth sides

Draws for the U21s and in the UEFA Youth League, a home defeat for the U18s, and a first-team breakthrough for Shea Lacey.

The U21s drew 1-1 at Burnley on Sunday in Premier League 2. Mantato scored on 16 minutes from Tynan Thompson's pass, but captain Dan Armer was sent off on 64 minutes and Burnley equalised early in the second half (The Peoples Person). Jaydan Kamason stood out at right-back.

In the UEFA Youth League on Tuesday, the U19s drew 1-1 with Roma at Leigh. Thompson scored his first United goal on 39 minutes before a disputed handball penalty levelled it (The Peoples Person). United have four points from four games and go to Sporting CP on November 25.

The U18s lost 2-0 at home to Wolves on Saturday (manutd.com). They remain second in the U18 North league, one point behind Manchester City, according to FotMob.

The breakthrough

The academy story of the week is Shea Lacey. He came off the bench against Roma, assisted Cunha's winner and then covered at right-back, before appearing again against Villa.

Loan watch

André Onana kept a clean sheet with six saves as Trabzonspor beat Gaziantep 2-0 on November 1 (ESPN). We could not confirm this week's performances for United's other loanees, who include Altay Bayındır (Celta Vigo), Sékou Koné (Lausanne) and Dan Gore (Luton), per the season page.

Manchester United Women

Zigiotti's header extends the unbeaten run to 11

Eva Olid's side won 1-0 at Aston Villa and sit in the WSL's top five, close to the Champions League places.

United won 1-0 at Villa on Sunday. Julia Zigiotti Olme came off the bench and headed in Lundkvist's cross on 72 minutes, with Andrea Medina, back from illness, involved in the move (Yahoo Sports). Hinata Miyazawa hit the bar.

The win stretched the unbeaten run to 11 games in all competitions and seven in the WSL. Reports differ on their exact place, fourth or fifth, but United are within touching distance of the Champions League places (Yahoo Sports).

Storylines

Zigiotti leads the team with four league goals. The creative gap left by Melvine Malard's move to Chelsea is still being filled, and Elisabeth Terland has two goals on limited service. Eva Olid, appointed in August after Marc Skinner left, has Maya Le Tissier as captain (season page).

United host Crystal Palace on Saturday, November 14 at 12:45pm UK, which is 4:45am Pacific, live on Sky Sports in the UK (Yahoo Sports). A League Cup tie against Everton follows on November 18, then a trip to Birmingham City on November 22.
