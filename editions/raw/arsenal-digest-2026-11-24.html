The Lead

Four contracts in three days: Arsenal lock down the champions

While the football stopped, Andrea Berta kept working. Rice, Timber, Raya and Hincapie have all agreed fresh terms, Calafiori is next in the queue, and the most prominent name still without a new deal is the captain.

The loudest thing that happened to Arsenal this past week was a collapse. Two goals conceded in thirteen second-half minutes at St James' Park, a one-goal lead turned into a one-goal deficit, and a point salvaged only because Noni Madueke cut inside and found the bottom corner with twelve minutes left.

The quietest thing was four sets of signatures. Over a season, the signatures will matter more.

Between Monday, November 16 and Wednesday, November 18, while most of the squad was scattered across three continents on international duty, sporting director Andrea Berta closed out agreements with four of the most important players at the club. None was running down a contract. None had a suitor at the door with a bid prepared. That is precisely what makes the week worth writing about.

Rice first, and then the rest

Declan Rice went first. The Athletic's David Ornstein reported on November 16 that the 27-year-old had agreed a new five-year deal running to the summer of 2031. It replaces terms due to expire in June 2028 with a one-year option attached. Rice has 80 England caps and is the one player in this squad whose absence would change the shape of every phase of play.

Jurrien Timber agreed terms the same day, also reported as running to 2031. David Raya followed on November 17, with a deal reported to 2030, his previous terms having been set to run out in the summer of 2028. Then came Piero Hincapie, whose new and improved contract Ornstein reported as running to 2032, adding twelve months to a deal that already ran to June 2031.

Hincapie is the instructive one. The Ecuador defender only joined permanently in the summer, for a reported £34.5 million plus add-ons after his loan from Bayer Leverkusen was converted, and he has made three starts this season. Gabriel and Riccardo Calafiori are ahead of him. He got a longer deal and better terms anyway.

Why now, when nobody was leaving

There is a cost to this, and it is worth naming. Extending contracts years before they expire locks in a higher fixed wage bill for longer, and it does so without the leverage a club normally enjoys when a player has eighteen months left. Rewarding a rotation defender before his starting role is settled is not an obviously efficient use of a wage budget.

None of the four was close to running out of contract. That is the point, not the objection.

The argument the other way is the one Arsenal have clearly made internally. This squad won the league in May for the first time in 22 years and reached a Champions League final. Every member of it is now worth more to somebody else than he was in April. Real Madrid have circled William Saliba for the better part of two years. Barcelona and Inter are both watching Cristhian Mosquera. The cheapest moment to renew a player is before his agent takes a phone call, not after.

Read alongside Mikel Arteta's own extension to 2030, signed on October 7, the picture is of a club spending the autumn closing every exit that could open in the next three summers rather than reacting to one that already has.

Calafiori next, Odegaard the open question

Riccardo Calafiori is next in the queue. BBC Sport reported that Arsenal intend to open talks with the 24-year-old, whose current deal runs to 2029, so this one sits at a much earlier stage than the others. Real Madrid have been linked with him since the summer, and Calafiori has been open in interviews about one day going back to Roma, the club whose academy produced him. Hincapie's new deal, signed by a player who covers the same ground, is not an accident of timing.

The genuinely open file is the captain's. Martin Odegaard has not signed, extended or agreed anything this autumn, and reporting in England has linked Kai Havertz to the same list. Nothing in the reporting this week suggests a problem. But in a window where Arsenal have tied down a reserve centre-back until 2032, the absence of an Odegaard line is the one thing in the ledger that does not yet balance.

Forty-five good minutes, then the familiar unravelling

Arsenal were superb for a half at St James' Park and second to everything for twenty minutes after it. It was the second time in three weeks the same thing happened.

For 45 minutes on Saturday evening, Arsenal did something genuinely difficult: they took a hostile ground and made it quiet. Sixty-six per cent of the ball. Ten shots from inside the penalty area across the match against five for the hosts. Madueke hit a post early after good hold-up play from Eberechi Eze, and on 33 minutes Kai Havertz read a loose touch from Lewis Hall, raced onto it and slid Eze in behind for the opener.

The only complaint at the break was the scoreline. Arsenal should have been out of sight.

Then Newcastle manager Matthias Jaissle made a triple substitution at half-time and the game turned inside four minutes. Martin Odegaard, under pressure from Joe Willock, sliced a clearance in midfield. The ball fell to Will Osula, who released Harvey Barnes into open grass, and Barnes finished past Raya for Newcastle's first home goal in 312 minutes.

On the hour, Dan Burn was left entirely unmarked at the back post from a corner and nodded back across goal for Malick Thiaw to bundle in. Burn is six foot seven. It was, by some distance, the clearest defensive error Arsenal have made this season.

What followed is the part Arteta will have replayed. Arsenal did not respond for the better part of fifteen minutes. They lost second balls, they misplaced passes, and the first substitutions did not arrive until the 70th minute. Raya had to be helped off on 69 after an innocuous-looking collision.

By the time Madueke restored parity on 78, finishing a move that ran the length of the pitch from Gabriel through Calafiori and Christos Tzolis to Havertz and then Odegaard, Arsenal had managed one shot in the second half.

Arteta's own summary was that his team had "lost two points". The more uncomfortable detail is that the same pattern had appeared three weeks earlier at Slavia Prague, where a controlled first half gave way to a nervous second and a 2-1 win that should have been comfortable. Once this Arsenal side loses its grip on a match, it has taken a long time to find it again. At 28 points from 11 games that is a footnote. Against City on Sunday it would not be.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Manchester City, and a title race that has stalled at the wrong end

Enzo Maresca's first season has gone from seven straight wins to four games without one. Arsenal get them on Sunday, at the Emirates, six points behind.

Manchester City arrive in north London in the strangest form in the division. They opened the season with a club record seven consecutive league wins and sat top of the table. They have not won a Premier League match since: defeats to Aston Villa, Brighton and Nottingham Forest, then a 1-1 draw at home to Fulham on Saturday in which Erling Haaland had a goal disallowed. Four games, one point, 22 on the board and third on goal difference behind Brighton.

The context is a change of manager. Enzo Maresca replaced Pep Guardiola in the summer after ten years, and has spent the past fortnight asking for patience. His line is that "it's not easy to suddenly change" after a decade of one idea. Europe has been kinder: City won 3-2 away at RB Leipzig on matchday four and hosted Napoli on Tuesday.

Who Arsenal have to stop

Erling Haaland remains the league's leading scorer and the reason City's bad run has not become a catastrophe. Phil Foden and Rayan Cherki are the two carrying the creative load around him, and Cherki in particular has been City's best moment-maker in the difficult weeks, including a top-corner half-volley at Forest.

The vulnerability is the back line. City have been conceding at a rate that would have been unthinkable under the previous regime. Antoine Semenyo is sidelined with a knee problem, Marc Guehi has been carrying a hip issue, and Abdukodir Khusanov picked up a knock at Forest. Matheus Nunes is available again, which at least restores an option at right-back.

The recent head-to-head favours Arsenal heavily. These two met in the Community Shield at Wembley on August 16 and Arsenal won 3-0. Since City are the one side within realistic touching distance, Sunday is the clearest three-point swing available before Christmas: win it and the gap over City is nine.

Transfer News

Nusa: the player says yes, the club says not yet

Arsenal have made the RB Leipzig winger their priority for January. The gap is a few million pounds and a German club that does not want to sell in winter.

The most advanced thread in Arsenal's January book is Antonio Nusa. The 21-year-old Norway winger has six goals and eight assists in 15 appearances for RB Leipzig this season. Arsenal lost Gabriel Martinelli and Leandro Trossard in the run-up to this campaign, which has left the left flank thin.

Where it sits on the paper trail: concrete interest, with the player's approval secured. Norwegian outlet Nettavisen reported on Tuesday that Arsenal have held talks and that Nusa has given his blessing. Dean Jones, via TEAMtalk, put Arsenal's proposition at around £62.5 million, a few million short of the asking price in the same report. That is not a chasm.

Be careful with the numbers: they do not agree. Romano earlier put Leipzig's starting valuation at €70 million to €75 million, other reporting has gone above €80 million, and the pound figures quoted around them vary accordingly. What every version agrees on is the direction.

The obstacle is not money alone. Fabrizio Romano has said Leipzig "are not pushing to sell" in January, and German reporting has them intending to keep him until at least the summer. Nusa is contracted to 2029 with no release clause, so Leipzig hold every card. Paris Saint-Germain and Tottenham have both been linked, the latter reportedly exploring a swap.

Certainty: concrete interest, personal terms indicated, no fee agreed. A summer deal remains the likelier outcome.

Kroupi, and a hundred-million-pound price tag

Arsenal's interest in the Bournemouth forward is real and long-standing. So is the number Bournemouth have attached to him.

Eli Junior Kroupi is the centre-forward file. Bournemouth are reported to be ready to ask as much as £100 million, and David Ornstein hinted last week that the player has already formed a view on whether his future lies at Arsenal or at Barcelona.

The reason the file exists at all is the state of the Arsenal attack. Viktor Gyokeres has been used mainly in Europe this season. Kai Havertz has led the line in the league. Arsenal scored 17 goals in their first 11 league matches, and were the better team at Newcastle for 45 minutes without turning that into a second goal. The analysis writes itself.

Certainty: interest, no bid, no timeline. Competition is reported from Barcelona and Paris Saint-Germain.

Mosquera: wanted in Milan, watched in Barcelona, short of minutes in London

Arsenal's £13 million defender has played 378 minutes this season. Several European clubs have noticed.

Cristhian Mosquera has become the most likely senior departure. The 22-year-old arrived from Valencia in July 2025 for a reported initial £13 million, made 989 Premier League minutes in last season's title win, and has been squeezed out this year by the arrival of Ezri Konsa and the return to fitness of William Saliba.

Italian outlet TuttoMercatoWeb reported on November 17 that Inter want to rebuild their defence with Premier League players, and Mosquera is understood to be their main target for next summer. Spanish outlet El Nacional has Barcelona monitoring him. Arsenal are reported to value him at €50 million to €60 million, with Italian media floating 21-year-old midfielder Aleksandar Stankovic, a player Arsenal have chased twice, as a makeweight.

Certainty: external interest, nothing agreed. A January exit is seen as highly unlikely.

The Squad

A goalkeeping scare, a centre-back puzzle, and a winger who took his chance

Raya avoids surgery. Saliba is back. And Noni Madueke has spent five weeks proving Arsenal can survive without Bukayo Saka.

Raya: better news than Saturday suggested

When David Raya walked off at St James' Park holding his left shoulder, the immediate read was grim. It was the first time he had failed to finish a match he started for Arsenal. The collision with Will Osula looked like nothing; the reaction did not.

Mikel Arteta's Monday update was considerably brighter. Raya will not need surgery, the first scan was better than the club expected, and the manager said the injury "doesn't look too serious". He missed Tuesday's Champions League tie, with further tests deciding whether he is available against Manchester City on Sunday. That matters: Raya has kept the joint-most clean sheets in the Premier League this season with seven.

In his absence, Kepa Arrizabalaga steps in, with Illan Meslier the alternative on the bench.

Saliba or Konsa, and a problem worth having

The more interesting selection question is at centre-back. William Saliba has been working his way back from the back injury sustained at the World Cup and was on the bench at Newcastle after a run of behind-closed-doors minutes. In his absence Ezri Konsa, the £51 million summer signing from Aston Villa, has partnered Gabriel so convincingly that Arteta left Saliba out of the starting eleven even once he was fit to be involved.

Konsa then went away with England and headed in the second goal in Madrid. Arteta now has two first-choice right-sided centre-backs and one shirt, which is a luxury, and it has pushed Cristhian Mosquera to the margins.

Madueke, and the Saka question answered for now

The most convincing individual story of the autumn is Noni Madueke. Signed from Chelsea in the summer of 2025 to ease the load on Bukayo Saka, he has instead carried the right flank entirely since Saka's quad injury against Bayern Munich on October 21. He scored the winner at Nottingham Forest, scored in the League Cup, and rescued the point at Newcastle with his best performance in an Arsenal shirt. The one thing still missing is consistency in front of goal.

Elsewhere, Bruno Guimaraes remains the squad's live tactical argument. Arteta started him at his former club; he was withdrawn on 70 minutes, and Myles Lewis-Skelly, who replaced him, was the one who steadied things. Lewis-Skelly was named in the starting eleven on Tuesday.

Set pieces, both ends

One detail from Saturday should concern Arsenal more than the result. Newcastle's second goal came from a corner on which a six-foot-seven defender was left completely unmarked at the back post. Arsenal's own set-piece reputation is built on exactly that sort of attention to detail. Conceding one of that description, days before facing a side with Erling Haaland in it, is the kind of error that gets fixed quickly or gets punished.

Hale End

Dowman takes two records off Bellingham. Nwaneri comes home in the wrong shirt

A 16-year-old broke England Under-21 history inside three days. A 19-year-old returned to the Emirates with Borussia Dortmund, and his loan has not worked.

Max Dowman had the week of his young life. Called into Lee Carsley's England Under-21 squad despite being several years younger than almost everyone in it, he came off the bench against Portugal on November 13 to become the youngest player ever to appear for the age group, a record previously held by Jude Bellingham. Three days later he scored the dramatic winner in a 3-2 victory over France, taking Bellingham's youngest-scorer record too. Two records in three days.

Dowman is already the youngest Premier League title winner in history, having been 16 years and 139 days old last May. He turns 17 on December 31, at which point he can convert the pre-contract agreement he signed in January 2026 into full professional terms. He picked up the first red card of his senior career in October, in the EFL Cup tie at Fleetwood.

Nwaneri's loan has stalled

Ethan Nwaneri was back at the Emirates on Tuesday night, in black and yellow. The 19-year-old Hale End graduate joined Borussia Dortmund on a season-long loan on September 1, and UEFA rules permit him to face his parent club, unlike the Premier League's arrangement.

The numbers are not what anybody wanted. Thirteen appearances, no goals, three starts, all in the Bundesliga, and none since October 17. He has never completed more than 65 minutes in a match. Arteta's assessment this week was blunt: "moments are not enough". This is a player who started ten of fifteen matches between January and April 2025 when Saka was out, and who spent the back half of last season on loan at Marseille. A second loan that has not produced minutes is a real problem in his development, and January is the obvious moment to fix it.

The younger groups

The Under-18s go into this week needing a response after a defeat to Chelsea. For the UEFA Youth League tie against Dortmund, Vincent Joseph, Maalik Hashi and Marli Salmon were named in the provisional Under-19 squad, while goalkeeper Jack Porter was absent from it after training with the first team ahead of the senior fixture. Arsenal also registered Angelinou Pedro and Theo Odaji-Ukueku in their Champions League squad.

Arsenal Women

Six home draws, no home wins, and a head coach under real pressure

Arsenal Women have not won a league game at the Emirates all season. They were booed off on Sunday. Renee Slegers took responsibility and said she loves the club.

This is the hardest section in the edition to write, because the numbers are unambiguous. Arsenal Women drew 0-0 with West Ham at the Emirates on Sunday. It was their sixth consecutive home draw in the Women's Super League, and it means they will end the calendar year without a single home league win. West Ham avoided defeat against Arsenal for the first time.

Arsenal had 67 per cent of the ball, 22 shots to 11, and 14 corners to one. They also created zero big chances to West Ham's two. That gap between volume and threat is the season in a sentence.

The reaction at full time was unhappy. Some players looked visibly emotional. Renee Slegers, asked whether she was considering her position, said she takes her responsibilities because the results have not been good enough. She signed a contract to 2029 in January, having taken over from Jonas Eidevall on an interim basis two years ago. For now she remains in charge, though ESPN described her future as highly in question and multiple outlets reported increased pressure after Sunday.

Lyon exposed the gap

Three days earlier, Arsenal had been comprehensively beaten at Meadow Park by OL Lyonnes, who won 2-0 through Jule Brand on 24 minutes and Melchie Dumornay on 57. Lyon had eight efforts on goal by the 28th minute to Arsenal's none. Arsenal's only shot on target came from Emily Fox deep in stoppage time. Misa Rodriguez made several significant second-half saves to keep the score where it was.

It was Arsenal's first European defeat of the season and it sent Lyon through to the quarter-finals as a top-four side. Arsenal sit eighth in the league-phase table with eight points from five, three behind fourth-placed Chelsea, with one round to play. Automatic qualification is now out of their hands.

The table, and two other things

Arsenal are sixth in the WSL on 15 points from 11 games, with three wins, six draws and two defeats, and a goal difference of exactly zero: 11 scored, 11 conceded. For a squad rebuilt in the summer around Ona Batlle, Georgia Stanway, Misa Rodriguez and Geraldine Reuteler, that is a long way from the standard.

Two other notes from the week. Leah Williamson made her 300th appearance for the club. And there was a worrying moment involving Lisa Baum, the 19-year-old German signed from RB Leipzig in the summer, who was short of breath on the pitch; Slegers said afterwards that she had been told Baum was in a stable condition.

Gunners Abroad

Two Arsenal defenders ended a 43-game Spanish unbeaten run

The November window closed on November 17 and produced the best away result England have had in years, with Arsenal players at the centre of it.

Nineteen Arsenal players went away for the final international window of 2026, and the headline night was Sunday, November 15 in Madrid, where as many as eight of them could have shared a pitch.

England beat Spain 3-2 at the Metropolitano, ending a Spanish unbeaten run that had stretched to 43 matches and 968 days. Harry Kane converted a penalty on the stroke of half-time. Eight minutes after the break Ezri Konsa headed in from a Declan Rice corner, and four minutes after that Ben White made it three. Mikel Oyarzabal and Nico Williams pulled two back, and England held on with ten men to top Nations League Group A3 and reach the quarter-finals. Myles Lewis-Skelly was among the substitutes.

On the other bench, David Raya, Mikel Merino and Martin Zubimendi were with Spain. Zubimendi came off injured in that match with the hamstring problem that still has him out.

Elsewhere across the window: Max Dowman broke two England Under-21 records; Kai Havertz and Jurrien Timber met in Berlin when Germany hosted the Netherlands on November 16; Viktor Gyokeres was with Sweden chasing promotion to Nations League A; Martin Odegaard captained Norway; Riccardo Calafiori played for Italy against France and Belgium; Christos Tzolis featured for Greece; and Cristhian Mosquera was with Spain's Under-21s, whose window ran to November 17.

Two Arsenal players stayed home by design. William Saliba was left out of the France squad following talks between the federation and the club as he rebuilt his fitness, and Piero Hincapie was omitted by Ecuador to recover from a fitness concern. Bukayo Saka was unavailable through injury, and Noni Madueke, the in-form Arsenal attacker, again missed out on an England call.

There are no international fixtures in the coming week. The next window is in March.

Standings snapshot

Arsenal are first in the Premier League on 28 points from 11 games, six clear of Brighton and Manchester City on 22, with Liverpool fourth on 21. Sunday's visitors are the closest of those three in the fixture list, and a win would stretch the gap over City to nine.

In the Champions League, Arsenal went into Tuesday's matchday five third in the league-phase table, unbeaten, with 10 points from four matches and three goals conceded. Tuesday's result is not reflected in that line.
