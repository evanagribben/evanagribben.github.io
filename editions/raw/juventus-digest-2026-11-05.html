The Lead

Ten days on a knife edge: Spalletti's Juventus fight for air

A defeat at Genoa, a retreat, a rare speech from the chief executive, then wins over Napoli and AZ Alkmaar. The reprieve is real, but it is thin.

Luciano Spalletti wants the Allianz Stadium to become a fortress again. On Sunday night, with his own crowd jeering from the opening whistle and hundreds of ultras protesting outside the Curva Sud before kickoff, Juventus beat Napoli 2-0 on the club's 129th birthday. It was not a cure. It was oxygen, and the days that followed showed how little of it there is.

The win came four days after the low point of the season: a 2-0 defeat at Genoa on October 28, with goals from Ehizibue and Baldanzi inside half an hour (club report). It was a third league loss in nine rounds. Juventus had 14 points, 11 behind leaders Lazio and six off fourth place, as Fanpage noted the next morning.

The response was a mandatory retreat before Napoli and a stern dressing-room address from chief executive Giovanni Carnevali. "The most important speech was made by Carnevali, because they are not accustomed to hearing his voice," Spalletti said afterwards (Football Italia). His own message before the game was blunter: "Physical strength can fade, but mental strength cannot" (Goal).

The noise around the bench

Outside, the speculation ran hot. Mediaset reported on October 29 that Antonio Conte was Carnevali's first choice should a change be made; Conte said on Instagram there were no advanced talks. La Repubblica, as relayed by CalcioNapoli24, named Stefano Pioli and Didier Deschamps as alternatives.

Fabrizio Romano reported that the club was backing Spalletti, had contacted no other coach, and was nonetheless unhappy, with Napoli framed as the key game (via L'Interista). Mediaset called Napoli, AZ Alkmaar and Fiorentina "ten decisive days".

How the Napoli game turned

Spalletti returned to a back four: Vicario; Kalulu, Bremer, Lucumí, Cabal, with Douglas Luiz, Koopmeiners and McKennie in midfield behind Alajbegović, Woltemade and Kolo Muani (club line-up). Vicario's early reflex save from Lorenzo Lucca's diving header kept it level, and Cabal headed a corner against the post.

The first half was thin, and Weston McKennie did not come out for the second, his adductor tightening. Federico Gatti replaced him and, as Football Italia saw it, Juventus shifted to three at the back. With a pure marker on him, Lucca faded.

Edon Zhegrova replaced the whistled Kolo Muani on 59 minutes. Eleven minutes later he beat Rrahmani and found Kerim Alajbegović, whose left-footed shot from about 20 yards went in off the far post for his first Serie A goal.

“You can say Spalletti's no good, that player's no good, get rid of everyone, but right now we need to be united, and this stadium must return to being our fortress.”

Luciano Spalletti, after Napoli

Napoli's Luca Marianucci was sent off on 75 minutes for a second booking, for stepping on Cabal's foot. Jérémie Boga, on for Alajbegović, cut inside and curled the second into the far corner on 87 minutes, set up by Andrea Cambiaso (Football Italia).

Juventus finished with 18 shots to Napoli's seven, five on target to one, by Napoligrafia's count. Napoli were led from the bench by assistant Marco Landucci, with Massimiliano Allegri suspended.

A squad of thirteen

The win did not hide the arithmetic. "Lo dico eh, siamo in 13," Spalletti said: I'll say it, there are 13 of us (SOS Fanta). "All the substitutions I made were forced ones," he added, with Sarr out with a fever and McKennie, Cabal and Alajbegović all needing checks.

On Tuesday, at the shareholders' meeting, Carnevali closed the question for now: "Spalletti è l'allenatore della Juventus e noi lo sosteniamo" (Spalletti is the Juventus coach and we support him), Calciomercato.com reported.

Alkmaar: the second breath

Thursday night in the Netherlands brought a second win in five days. With eight players unavailable, Juventus won 2-1 at AZ Alkmaar to reach 10 points from four Europa League games (Juventus News 24).

Spalletti went with three at the back: Vicario; Gatti, Lucumí, Kelly; Kalulu, Sarr, Koopmeiners, Cambiaso; Alajbegović and Woltemade behind Kolo Muani (Football Italia line-ups). Bremer and Douglas Luiz started on the bench.

The man whistled off on Sunday decided it. On 19 minutes Randal Kolo Muani beat his man and cut the ball back from the byline for Teun Koopmeiners to finish past De Busser. On 37 minutes Kolo Muani played a one-two with Woltemade and volleyed in his fourth goal of the season (Corriere dello Sport).

AZ pulled one back through Mexx Meerdink on 88 minutes, and in stoppage time Vicario saved Meerdink's header before De Wit's rebound was ruled out for offside (Football Italia). Jeff Ekhator made his first official appearance off the bench.

Sunday in Florence is the last stop before the international break. Fiorentina are 16th but won 4-2 at Sassuolo on Monday, and Juventus have not beaten them in four meetings. The organised groups of the Curva Sud will not be there: they are boycotting the trip over ticketing (Juve Oggi).

Off the pitch

Exor's €250m lifeline comes with a warning attached

Shareholders approve a €66m loss and a capital increase; Carnevali promises to buy "who we can" within UEFA's limits.

The other big Juventus meeting of the week took place on Tuesday at the Allianz Stadium, and it was about money. Shareholders approved the 2025-26 accounts, a net loss of €66.0m against €58.1m a year earlier, with 91.8% of the vote (Football Italia).

They also authorised the board to raise up to €250m in fresh capital, passed with 99.9%. Exor, which holds 65.4%, guarantees its share and any part other shareholders do not take up, and has already paid €60m in advance (Di Marzio).

Roughly half of the money rebuilds equity; about €100m is earmarked for investment, mainly the first team and transfers, according to Calciomercato.com.

No spree in January

Carnevali said the UEFA settlement agreement still limits spending and that there would be no extraordinary outlays. "Compreremo chi potremo," he said: we will buy who we can (Corriere dello Sport). Chief financial officer Stefano Cerrato expects another loss this season because Europa League revenue is lower than the Champions League's.

President Gianluca Ferrero described Tether as the second-largest shareholder at 11.5%, said the club was open to dialogue but had no joint initiatives, and ruled out delisting. He also floated a second, smaller stadium closer to the city for the women's team and Next Gen, while admitting there is no executive project (Calcio e Finanza).

The floor was hostile. Small shareholders attacked John Elkann and called for club great Del Piero as president (Calcio e Finanza).

Others criticised former chief executive Damien Comolli, and one called Randal Kolo Muani's fee excessive. Ferrero cut off one persistent critic mid-speech.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Fiorentina: dangerous going forward, leaky at the back

Paolo Vanoli's side are 16th but scored four at Sassuolo on Monday, and Juventus have not beaten them since April 2024.

Fiorentina sit 16th with 8 points from 10 games (W2 D2 L6), with 15 goals scored and 23 conceded (Sky Sport table). Fabio Grosso was sacked on September 6 after three straight league defeats in which his side scored once and conceded nine; Paolo Vanoli returned the next day (Reuters via The Star).

Form has picked up. They won 4-2 at Sassuolo on Monday (Pellegrino, Njie, Mastantuono, Valdepeñas), drew 2-2 with Atalanta on October 29, and lost 2-1 at Inter on October 25 to goals in the 91st and 98th minutes.

System and the men to stop

Vanoli has used a 4-3-2-1 in the last two games: De Gea; Jiménez, Dragušin, Viery, João Mário; Ndour, Fagioli, Brescianini; Mastantuono, Njie; Pellegrino. Franco Mastantuono is the club's top scorer with at least four league goals and their highest-rated player.

Mateo Pellegrino has at least three goals as the lone striker. Former Juventus midfielder Nicolò Fagioli sets the tempo, and David de Gea is in goal.

Where they can be hurt

Fiorentina concede 2.3 goals a game, joint third-worst in the league, and they average 397 passes a game (15th) against Juventus's 545 (4th), per theScore. But they also average 1.5 goals and 16.4 shots a game, slightly more than Juventus.

Sottil, Atta, Pongračić, Parisi, Ranieri and Gosens were listed as injured, and Njie came off after 63 minutes at Sassuolo. No suspensions have been reported.

Recent history is poor for Juventus: a 2-0 home defeat in May 2026, a 1-1 draw in Florence in November 2025, a 3-0 loss there in March 2025 and a 2-2 draw in December 2024. The last win was 1-0 in April 2024.

Transfer News

Juventus want a striker in January, but must sell to buy one

Balogun and Šeško lead a long list; Zhegrova is named as the likeliest departure.

The clearest thread in Turin this week is a centre-forward. Calciomercato.com, via Football Italia, reported on Wednesday that Juventus expect to invest in a striker in January. Monaco's Folarin Balogun, under contract to 2028, and Manchester United's injured Benjamin Šeško head the list.

Sunderland's Brian Brobbey and Frankfurt's Ebnoutalib are also named. All are at the interest stage. No bid has been reported for any of them.

Marco Conterio of TMW, reported via Tuttojuve, adds the catch: a striker arrives only if someone leaves first. His list includes Balogun, Atlético Madrid's Alexander Sørloth and free agent Mauro Icardi, with Jeff Ekhator a candidate for a loan exit.

Conterio names Edon Zhegrova as the most likely sale. That report is single-sourced. The Kosovo winger has two league goals and set up the Napoli winner, yet he has not started a game all season and is not on the Europa League list.

The list rule explains that last part: when Locatelli's injury opened a place, UEFA required a locally trained replacement, so Ekhator went in instead (Goal Italia). Carnevali's line on Tuesday, "we will buy who we can", suggests the January budget will be shaped by who leaves.

A price on Cambiaso, and a Højbjerg idea for midfield

A €25m to €30m valuation on the wing-back; Marseille's midfielder linked while Koopmeiners is called "not untouchable".

CalcioMercato.it reported on Tuesday that Juventus would consider offers of €25m to €30m for Andrea Cambiaso, and that he could leave in January as Juan Cabal's form pushes him down the order (report). No suitor is named.

Claims elsewhere of a renewed Barcelona approach could not be traced to a primary source, so the Digest is treating them as unconfirmed. Cambiaso came on for eight minutes against Napoli and set up Boga's goal.

In midfield, TMW (via Football Italia) names Marseille's Pierre-Emile Højbjerg as the top target and ties any deal to a possible sale of Teun Koopmeiners, described as "not untouchable". Fiorentina are reported to be interested in Højbjerg too. This is rumour, not negotiation.

 

Loan options: Vicario a formality, Grabara heading back

Four summer loans, four different outcomes taking shape.

Tuttosport reported on Tuesday that buying Guglielmo Vicario from Tottenham is now "a formality", at a reported €8m excluding bonuses. He has started every Serie A match and is the only Juventus player in Italy's new squad.

Kamil Grabara, on loan from Wolfsburg with an €11.5m option, ruptured his ACL in September and is expected to go back to Wolfsburg rather than be bought (Goal Italia).

Pape Matar Sarr's deal carries a €28m option that, per Fabrizio Romano's summer reporting, becomes an obligation if Juventus qualify for Europe and Sarr plays half the matches (Calciomercato.com).

The terms on Nick Woltemade are reported inconsistently. Goal Italia described a paid loan from Newcastle to June 2027 with no option; TMW's Conterio this week referred to an obligation to buy. Until the club clarifies, treat his future as open.

The Squad

Spalletti's thirteen: the injury list that shapes every choice

Back four or back three, the coach is picking from a shrinking group, with the captain out until the new year.

Luciano Spalletti began the season with a back four, mostly a 4-2-3-1, through to the 1-1 draw at Cagliari on October 11. Since then a back three has dominated: it has been used in all four Europa League games and in the league against Lazio, Lecce and Genoa.

Against Napoli he went back to four, then to three when Gatti came on. The constant is personnel: Douglas Luiz, Kolo Muani, Lucumí, Bremer and Kalulu are the regulars, and Vicario has started every Serie A game, with Neto and Pinsoglio behind him.

The captain's long absence

Manuel Locatelli, the captain, had his right lateral meniscus repaired in September and is out until early 2027 (Sky Sport). Bremer wore the armband at Lecce.

Kenan Yildiz broke the base of his left fifth metatarsal in the opener at Frosinone and had surgery. Quotidiano Sportivo expects him back in team training in early December, with Monza on December 14 or Roma on December 19 the targets.

Conceição (shoulder), Nico González (calf) and Çelik (hamstring) are aimed at the Venezia game after the break, according to DAZN. McKennie stayed home from Alkmaar as a precaution and is targeting Fiorentina (Corriere dello Sport).

Kolo Muani's test, and the cards to watch

Kolo Muani was whistled off against Napoli. "He has to react to certain moments," Spalletti said, adding that the first step and the courage to dare must come from the player himself. He answered in Alkmaar with a goal and an assist, taking him to four goals in all competitions, one more than Woltemade.

No player is suspended for Fiorentina. By the Digest's count from match reports, Lucumí has three Serie A bookings and Douglas Luiz two. Woltemade has taken and scored both of the team's penalties, against NEC and Rennes.

Next Gen

Next Gen stuck near the bottom as Durmiši knocks on the door

A 1-1 draw with Pro Vercelli, a goalless derby for the Primavera, and a hard week for the loanees.

Massimo Brambilla's Juventus Next Gen drew 1-1 with Pro Vercelli on Monday, Merola scoring on 31 minutes and Bertoli equalising after the break (Juventus News 24). They are 17th of 20 in Serie C Group A with 11 points from 12 games, two wins, five draws and five defeats.

The bright spot is Arman Durmiši, an Albanian striker born in 2008, who has three Serie C goals and got his first senior call-up for the trip to Genoa, alongside Pagnucco and Owusu (Tuttosport). With the first team stretched, Spalletti took four Next Gen players to Alkmaar (Juvenews).

Primavera and the loanees

The Primavera drew 0-0 in the derby at Torino on Sunday; Beydts was their best player and Ali Camara went off injured (Mondo Primavera). They are 14th in Primavera 1 with 10 points from nine games and host Empoli on Saturday.

Among the loanees, Loïs Openda is still out at Lyon with a broken right fibula (Sky Sport), and Fabio Miretti is sidelined at Beşiktaş with a shoulder injury until early December.

Vasilije Adžić started Sassuolo's 4-2 home defeat to Fiorentina on Monday and was taken off after 59 minutes; in the same game João Mário played 90 minutes at left-back for Fiorentina (Goal Italia). Michele Di Gregorio remains a cup goalkeeper at Bournemouth (Football Italia).

Juventus Women

Two away wins put Guerrero's Juventus Women back on track

Four goals in Vienna, a first league win on the road in Florence, and Lyon on the horizon.

Isaac Guerrero's Juventus Women answered three straight league defeats with two away wins. In the Women's Champions League on October 29 they won 4-1 at Austria Wien, with goals from Pavan, Redondo (a penalty after a VAR check), Sylla and Bartel (Solo Calcio Femminile).

That took them to six points from three league-phase games, after a 2-1 home loss to Benfica and a 3-0 win at Häcken.

On Monday they won 2-1 at Fiorentina's Viola Park in Serie A Women, Alba Redondo scoring on 28 minutes and Kysha Sylla adding her first league goal for the club on 56 before Severini's late reply (FIGC). It was their first away win of the league season.

Lyon next week

The table is still uncomfortable: Juventus are joint seventh on six points from five games, level with Milan and nine behind leaders Roma. Sassuolo visit Biella on Saturday, then OL Lyonnes come to Turin on Wednesday in the Champions League (club schedule).

Redondo and Sylla have scored in both games this week, and Julia Bartel has a goal and an assist. The Supercoppa final against Roma is set for January 10 in Taranto (Juventus News 24).

Players Away

Vicario alone in Italy's squad; the November break begins Sunday

No international games this week, but call-ups are in and Juventus loanees were busy in Europe.

Italy named 31 players on Thursday for the Nations League games against France at San Siro on November 12 and Belgium in Brussels on November 15. Guglielmo Vicario is the only Juventus player; Gatti and Cambiaso were left out (FIGC).

Other federations had not published their squads by press time, and the club usually rounds up its internationals around November 9. In European club action, Jonathan David, on loan at Atlético Madrid, came off the bench against Bayern in the Champions League on Tuesday, his only European minutes of the week.

The women's team has no international window this month: Juventus Women play on November 7, 11 and 15.

In Brief

The Curva Sud's organised groups will boycott Sunday's game in Florence, citing too few tickets and a presale that opened on the day of the AZ trip (Juve Oggi).

Before the Napoli game, ultras protested outside the Curva Sud with a banner reading "129 years of glory, our shirt is history, give us back our Juventus" (video).

"When the public pays, they can say and do what they want," Spalletti said of the boos, "so you have to accept both approval and dissent."

The Venezia game after the break is on Sunday November 22 at 12:30 in Italy, which is 3:30am Pacific (Fantacalcio), not the Monday date in the club's original schedule.

Carnevali was confirmed as a director and chief executive at Tuesday's meeting, where Ferrero also ruled out taking the club off the stock market.

Jeff Ekhator, registered on the Europa League list in place of the injured Locatelli, made his first official Juventus appearance in Alkmaar.
