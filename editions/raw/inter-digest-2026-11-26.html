The Lead

Inter's week of extremes: a 4-4 at Bergamo, then a clean sheet against Stuttgart

Pio Esposito's first Serie A hat-trick could not stop Atalanta clawing back two leads in the final 20 minutes. Three days later, with Çalhanoğlu injured and Lautaro back, Chivu's side answered with control, a penalty and a clean sheet.

For 52 minutes at the New Balance Arena on Sunday night, Inter looked like the most dangerous team in Italy. Pio Esposito scored at 16 minutes, headed a second from Dimarco's delivery at 32, and completed his first Serie A hat-trick at 52 from Marcus Thuram's pass. It was 3-1, and it should have been over.

Then the floor gave way. Atalanta had already pulled one back through Elmas before half-time, and at 72 minutes Charles De Ketelaere headed in. Sučić restored the two-goal cushion from the spot at 76, but De Ketelaere struck again at 80, and Franck Kessié converted a stoppage-time penalty after Bastoni handled. 4-4.

xG: 3.38 to 3.10 at Bergamo; 2.68 to 1.02 against Stuttgart (ESPN).

Injuries: Çalhanoğlu 3 to 4 weeks; Bastoni scans due; Zieliński and Bonny out.

Two points given away

Cristian Chivu did not dress it up. "We were leading 2-0, 3-1 and 4-2, so this is the regret," he told DAZN, as reported by Football Italia. "I see this as two gifted points." He went further: "We are conceding too many goals for a club that has high ambitions."

The numbers back him on both counts. Inter had 59 per cent of the ball and 3.38 expected goals to Atalanta's 3.10, according to ESPN. Yet Atalanta put ten of their 15 shots on target, and Inter have now conceded 17 league goals in 12 games, the most of any side in the top six.

“I think we failed the exam in the final minutes.”

Cristian Chivu, after Atalanta 4-4 Inter

The Stuttgart reply

Wednesday's visit of VfB Stuttgart was a different kind of test. Without Çalhanoğlu (hamstring) or Piotr Zieliński (foot), Chivu kept his 3-5-2 and handed Sučić the regista's role. Inter took 20 shots to Stuttgart's 13, won 11 corners and conceded only two shots on target, per ESPN.

The breakthrough came at 57 minutes: Federico Dimarco, on for the injured Bastoni since the 41st, crossed against Vagnoman's arm, VAR confirmed the penalty, and Sučić scored. At 74 minutes, five minutes after replacing Thuram, Lautaro Martínez finished Dimarco's delivery after an Esposito dummy. It was the captain's first goal in more than a month.

Nicolò Barella was UEFA's man of the match and hit the post. Chivu praised "mentality, organisation, intensity" and stressed that "Champions League games are not easy" (Football Italia). In front of 74,003 at San Siro, it was a third straight European win after defeats to Real Madrid and Club Brugge.

What it means

The two results describe the same team. Inter create plenty: they have scored in all 12 league games and in 22 straight Serie A matches, Sky Sport Italia notes. The issue is game management when the opponent goes direct, and a defence that will now be without Bastoni for at least a few days if his scan on Friday confirms a problem.

In Serie A, Inter sit third on 29 points, three behind Lazio and one behind Roma. In Europe they are 12th on nine points, one off the top eight, with Dortmund away, Liverpool at home and Slovan Bratislava away to come. Genoa visit twice in four days first, in the league on Saturday and the Coppa Italia on Tuesday.

Secondary feature: the Pio Esposito moment

Esposito's hat-trick took him to six league goals, the most in the squad according to Sky, and seven in all competitions by the Digest's count from match reports. He arrived at Bergamo after scoring three times for Italy in the November window, two against France and one against Belgium, which took him to ten international goals (inter.it).

Against Stuttgart he did not score, but Gazzetta praised his work and his dummy helped create Lautaro's goal. With Lautaro back and Bonny injured, Chivu now has a genuine selection call: Esposito, Thuram and the captain for two places. On this week's evidence, Esposito has earned his.

Transfer News

Renewals first: Carlos Augusto agreed, Thuram stalled, Çalhanoğlu on the clock

Marotta says every open contract will end with a yes. The detail this week suggests one is done, one is drifting and one is a race against time.

Giuseppe Marotta spoke to Sky before Stuttgart and was unequivocal: Carlos Augusto, Thuram and Çalhanoğlu "all want to continue with us", and the club will bring every case "to a positive result" (Corriere dello Sport). Inter also exercised a one-year option on Dimarco this month, moving his deal from 2027 to 2028 (Fantacalcio).

Carlos Augusto: personal terms agreed, signature imminent

Carlos Augusto has agreed an extension to June 30, 2031, up from 2028, with the signature expected on Friday, according to Yvonne Alessandro at TMW and FCInterNews. Calciomercato.it puts the package above €4 million net a year including bonuses, and says Juventus had tried for him. Status: agreed, not yet official.

Thuram: first offer short of his demands

Marcus Thuram is under contract to 2028 with an €85 million release clause, per Calciomercato.it. Tuttosport reported Inter's first offer at €4.5 to 5 million fixed plus bonuses to 2031, against roughly €6.5 million now (via TMW). The offer has not convinced him and talks have stalled. Ekrem Konur claims PSG would offer €7 million a year (via Calciomercato.it): a single-source rumour.

Çalhanoğlu: talks aimed at Christmas

Hakan Çalhanoğlu is the one first-teamer whose deal expires in June 2027. Gazzetta reported both sides aim to settle by Christmas (via FC Inter 1908), with Inter preferring a shorter, bonus-heavy deal under Oaktree's wage line. Tuttosport says Juventus would offer about €6 million on a free if talks fail (via Sportmediaset). His new injury will not help his leverage.

Varela: the striker everyone is watching

Monza's breakout forward has Inter's scouts in the stands. The question is whether anyone moves before June.

Gustavo Varela has become the most-discussed name around Inter. Gianluca Di Marzio said it would take €25 to 30 million and that Inter would rather plan for June, with Arsenal and Barcelona also keen (via FCInterNews). Fabrizio Romano put Monza's January price at €30 to 35 million and said Inter cannot be ruled out (via FC Inter 1908).

Tuttosport reports a €25 million starting point, confirmed in principle by Monza's sporting director, while Benfica hold a 20 per cent sell-on and a matching right (via FC Inter 1908). Marotta's only comment: "a player that not only Inter like." Sportmediaset links any Inter move to a Bonny sale (Sportmediaset). Status: concrete interest; summer more likely.

January: no buying unless the defence breaks

Sky's reading is that the summer signings get their chance. Bastoni's scan could change that.

Sky's Andrea Barzaghi reported from pitch-side on Wednesday that Inter will trust the summer arrivals and only act in January for physical emergencies, especially in defence; if John Stones stays fit, there is no plan to buy (via FC Inter 1908). Stones had managed only 144 minutes by mid-November.

The defensive notebook is long but mostly summer-dated: Stuttgart's Finn Jeltsch, who played against Inter on Wednesday, with Stuttgart asking €60 million (Il Giorno via TMW); Arsenal's Cristhian Mosquera (TMW); and a January loan idea for Matthijs de Ligt from TEAMtalk only (via Fantamaster), which we treat as an unconfirmed rumour.

Going the other way: Palacios and Luis Henrique

Tomás Palacios is on loan at Estudiantes until January with an option they are unlikely to use. River Plate are in advanced talks; Inter value him at €6 million and River now prefer a loan with an option, per Christian Liotta at FCInterNews. Luis Henrique, injured since mid-October, is expected to leave in January, though no club has been named (TMW).

Scouting the Next Opponent

Genoa, twice in four days

De Rossi's side arrive in form but short of a striker, and Inter host them in the league on Saturday and the Coppa Italia on Tuesday.

Genoa are tenth on 16 points but have won four of their last five league games, including a 2-0 win over Juventus on October 28 (Juventus.com) and a 2-0 at Sassuolo on Sunday, when Osmajić scored twice (bloooog.it). Their only defeat in that run was 2-1 at home to Milan.

Daniele De Rossi sets up with a back three, usually 3-4-2-1. Osmajić is the focal point, Junior Messias the creative spark, Ellertsson a goal threat from wing-back, and Baldanzi links play. Lorenzo Colombo tore his right ACL at Sassuolo and is out for around six months (ANSA), leaving Osmajić with little cover.

The weakness is output: 13 goals in 12 games and five in five away matches. Genoa also see little of the ball, with 36 per cent even in the win at Sassuolo. Against an Inter side that has won all six home league games and scored 19, the test will be whether Genoa can stay compact for 90 minutes.

History is firmly on Inter's side: unbeaten in 14 meetings, with wins the last three times, including 2-1 in Genoa in December 2025 (Fox Sports) and 2-0 at San Siro in February.

The Squad

Life without Çalhanoğlu

Inter's metronome is out until around the Supercoppa. Sučić got the first audition and passed it.

Hakan Çalhanoğlu strained his right hamstring at Bergamo and is expected to miss three to four weeks, per Sportitalia. That covers both Genoa games, Frosinone, Dortmund, Torino and Lecce, with the Supercoppa against Lazio on December 22 his target.

Petar Sučić took the role against Stuttgart and is now the penalty taker too, scoring in both games. "He's a good taker," Chivu said (SOS Fanta). Stanković, back from injury, is the other option in that role.

Bastoni and the back line

Alessandro Bastoni came off after 41 minutes against Stuttgart with soreness in his left thigh. Chivu said: "I don't think there is a tear," adding it was a problem he had carried for a few days. Scans were scheduled for Friday, November 27 (FC Inter 1908); he is a doubt for Genoa. Carlos Augusto covered at left centre-back.

Lautaro is back after six to seven weeks out, and Stanković returned from an adductor problem. Piotr Zieliński remains out with a foot injury (FC Inter 1908), and Ange-Yoan Bonny is still recovering from the hamstring strain suffered against Como (Fantacalcio).

The Academy

Primavera top the table; a hard week for the U23s

The Primavera beat Juventus 2-0 at the Konami Centre on Saturday through Kukulis's header and a late Grisoni Fasana counter, with left-back Edoardo Rocca the standout (MondoPrimavera). Under coach Bigica they lead on 22 points, one ahead of Parma, and Cristian Carrara tops the scoring chart with eight (table).

Europe was harsher. The Youth League side lost 3-0 at home to Stuttgart on Wednesday and sit on four points; Tuttosport says progress now needs a win in Dortmund on December 9 and help elsewhere (Tuttosport).

Inter U23

Stefano Vecchi's Serie C side beat leaders Potenza 2-1 on Saturday, with Mattia Marello and substitute Agbonifo scoring (Calciomagazine), then lost 3-0 at Audace Cerignola on Wednesday (inter.it). They are ninth on 23 points and visit Barletta on Sunday.

Loan watch

Davide Frattesi, on loan at Lazio with an option, chipped in the third goal at 89 minutes of a 3-0 win over Lecce on Saturday that kept Lazio top (Sportmediaset). We could not confirm this week's displays for the other loanees, including Topalovic at Carrarese and Cocchi at Padova; Palacios's future is covered in Transfer News.

Inter Women

Four at Narni, and second place held

Inter Women won 4-0 at Ternana on Sunday: Tomasevic at 18 minutes, Cambiaghi at 43, Glionna at 49 and Raphino in stoppage time. Haley Bugeja made her 100th appearance for the club (inter.it).

David Sassarini's side are second on 19 points, five behind leaders Roma, after a 3-2 home defeat to Juventus on November 15. In the Women's Champions League they are 13th of 18 on four points after a 3-1 loss at Real Madrid on November 18, with Arsenal at the Brianteo on December 16 to come (inter.it).

There are no club fixtures until Roma away on Saturday, December 12, because of the international window. Inter have 18 players on call-up duty (see Players Away).

Players Away

No men's window this week; Inter Women scatter

There was no men's international football between November 19 and 26. The November window ended before the Atalanta game; highlights included Esposito's three goals for Italy, Sučić scoring against England for Croatia, and Zieliński playing 81 minutes for Poland against Sweden (inter.it).

The women's window opens now, with 18 Inter players called up (inter.it). Bartoli, Cambiaghi, Glionna, Santi and Polli are with Italy for a World Cup qualifying play-off against Finland on December 1 and 5. Raphino plays for Haiti against Mexico in a CONCACAF Women's Championship quarter-final on November 28.

Off the Pitch

San Siro's long road and a club in the black

Il Foglio reported on November 19 that the San Siro project faces three types of threat: a city council motion demanding a health-impact assessment, mayoral candidates refusing to back the deal, and a Court of Auditors complaint alongside a prosecutors' probe. The Council of State hears appeals against the sale on May 4, 2027 (Il Giorno).

The clubs unveiled the new Generali Stadium on October 29: about 71,500 seats, designed by Foster + Partners and MANICA (inter.it). Off the field, the 2025/26 accounts showed revenue of €518 million and a €22.7 million net profit (Inside World Football). In the front office, Dario Baccin now leads the sporting side after Piero Ausilio's September exit (Calcio e Finanza).
