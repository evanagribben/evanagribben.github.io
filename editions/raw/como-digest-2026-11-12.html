The Lead

Back to earth at San Siro: what Inter exposed, and what the break must fix

Four days after a record 9-1 at Lens, Como were second best in a 2-0 defeat at Inter that ended a six-game winning run. Fabregas now has a fortnight to steady his midfield and get his injured forwards back before Cagliari.

Four days is a long time in a debut Champions League season. On Wednesday, November 4, Como scored nine times at Lens. On Sunday evening at San Siro they managed a single shot on target, lost 2-0 to Inter and watched a six-match winning run end inside the first 19 minutes.

Francesco Pio Esposito opened the scoring after 12 minutes, set up by Djed Spence according to Fox Sports' box score, and Marcus Thuram doubled the lead seven minutes later. Those were the only goals. The gap in chances was wider than the scoreline: Inter had 14 shots to five, and four on target to Como's one.

Como had the ball, and it did not help them. They kept 57 per cent of possession and completed 92 per cent of their 292 passes, per VAVEL's match data. Inter let them circulate it, pressed the loose touches and attacked the space behind.

Cesc Fabregas named the problem himself on DAZN: "Mi fa male come abbiamo perso, con palloni persi con troppa facilità" (what hurts is how we lost, giving the ball away far too easily), per Fantacalcio. He added that the confidence of recent weeks simply "wasn't there".

The half-time rethink

At half-time Fabregas took off Máximo Perrone and Martin Baturina and sent on Samuele Ricci and Ivan Smolčić. This was not a chase. "Ho cercato più solidità con Smolcic... Serviva più fisicità" (I wanted more solidity with Smolčić; we needed more physicality), he said, in the same interview.

In his press conference he was blunter still. With only two fit central defenders, he said, "when I saw that this time we might be the ones conceding nine, my responsibility was to fix the defensive phase," per SOS Fanta. Trevoh Chalobah was among the absentees.

The second half was steadier. Marc-Oliver Kempf stopped a Thuram breakaway around the 81st minute, and Como's best moments came late: a Jacobo Ramón header over the bar and a curling Nico Paz shot that struck the woodwork five minutes from time. Josep Martínez was rarely extended.

“Inter deserved the victory, we have to shake their hands and move on.”

Cesc Fabregas, after the defeat (via Football Italia)

The verdict

The Italian press was not gentle. CalcioMercato.com gave Fabregas 4.5 under the headline "Fabregas torna sulla terra" (Fabregas comes back down to earth). Tuttosport's ratings were headlined "Ramón horror". Sky Sport Italia were kinder, at 5.5 for the coach.

The one starter most outlets agreed had held his level was Luis Milla, rated 6 by both Sky and CalcioMercato. Fabregas leaned on his words afterwards: Milla, he said, keeps telling the squad they are "troppo buoni" (too nice). Inter committed 13 fouls to Como's handful, the coach noted, "and they do it well".

Inter's Manuel Akanji twisted the knife this week. "What phenomenon? I've played against them four times and won all four," the defender said, per Sports Mole. "They still have a lot to learn."

What it means, and what comes next

The table cost is modest but real. Como slipped from fourth to fifth on 23 points, six behind leaders Lazio and one behind Milan in fourth, with Frosinone three points back in sixth, per Sky Sport Italia's standings. Fourth is the last guaranteed Champions League place, and it is a point away.

The international break arrives at a useful moment. Como do not play again until Cagliari visit the Sinigaglia on Saturday, November 21 (6:00 a.m. Pacific). AEK Athens follow in the Champions League three days later, then Juventus on November 29.

Fabregas said before the Inter game that Chalobah has "two weeks to prepare for Cagliari", per Fantacalcio. Assane Diao is chasing the same date after a hamstring strain, and Moise Kean is targeting Juventus, per Gazzetta's round-up via TuttoCagliari.

The number Fabregas will watch most closely is 1,103: Tasos Douvikas's minutes so far, by Gazzetta's count, in a piece headlined "emergenza attacco". With Kean and Diao out there has been no understudy at centre-forward, and the Greek is now away for two Nations League matches. San Siro was a reminder of the ceiling. The next fortnight decides how quickly Como get back to it.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Cagliari: a defence that leaks, a keeper who saves, and Maldini

Pisacane's side ended a four-game losing run last weekend. They arrive at the Sinigaglia with nine players on the injury list and Daniel Maldini in career-best form.

Cagliari arrive at the Sinigaglia on November 21 having just stopped the bleeding. Michel Adopo's 38th-minute strike beat Frosinone 1-0 last Saturday and ended a run of four straight league defeats, per Unione Sarda. They are ninth on 16 points, seven behind Como.

Fabio Pisacane has flexed between systems. Against Frosinone it was a 3-4-2-1, with Daniel Maldini and Yael Trepy behind Paul Mendy, per Fantacalcio's line-ups; at Lazio a week earlier it was a back four. Maldini is the man to stop: five league goals already, a career best, per TuttoMercatoWeb.

The other key figure is at the other end. Elia Caprile has four clean sheets and was man of the match against Frosinone, tipping a shot onto the bar, per Flashscore. Cagliari have scored only ten league goals, so a goalkeeper-led, low-scoring game is their likeliest route to a result.

The vulnerability is obvious from the record. Cagliari conceded four at Monza and four at Roma in October, 12 goals in four straight defeats before the Frosinone win, per statz.ai's results list. Their treatment room is fuller than Como's: Unione Sarda listed nine absentees on Tuesday, including striker M'Bala Nzola and Como loanee Alieu Fadera, both doubtful.

Como are unbeaten in their last three meetings with Cagliari. They won 2-1 in Sardinia in March, Baturina and Da Cunha scoring either side of a Sebastiano Esposito equaliser, per CalcioMercato.com. The one blank was a 0-0 at the Sinigaglia a year ago, which is the warning against assuming a home win.

Transfer News

Ludi sets the tone: a quiet January, and two 'signings' from the treatment room

Como's sporting director says the squad needs completing, not rebuilding, and that the biggest January additions will be Kean, Diao and Jesús Rodríguez returning from injury.

Como's sporting director Carlalberto Ludi used the Social Football Summit this week to lower expectations for January. "Siamo molto soddisfatti della rosa," he told Gianluca Di Marzio: the club is very happy with the squad and knows which areas "can be completed, I don't say improved". His summary: "non faremo tanto" (we won't do much).

He went further with CalcioMercato.com. January, he said, could effectively be the recovery of Moise Kean, Assane Diao and Jesús Rodríguez, and there will be no "operazioni incredibili". Players with little game time could leave. If an opportunity appears and there is a need, Como will act.

Read that as the shape of the window rather than a promise of silence. The forward line is the obvious pressure point: Kean has barely played since his €40m move from Fiorentina, Diao is injured and Douvikas has carried the attack. That is where the one live rumour sits.

Paz and Ramón: the Real Madrid buy-backs, explained

Both stay this season. What happens in summer 2027 depends on Madrid, and on how much Como can make the most of the time in between.

Every November brings a fresh round of Nico Paz to Real Madrid stories. Ludi joked this week that every November and every March brings talk of Madrid taking him back, per Sports Illustrated. The facts are settled. In June Como bought him outright for about €60m, with Madrid keeping a buy-back for 2027-28 reported at €80m, per Goal Italia and Calcio e Finanza.

Fabrizio Romano was categorical on November 7: "a gennaio non va da nessuna parte" (in January he is going nowhere), noting that no clause applies mid-season, per SOS Fanta. Ludi called Paz "100% a Como player" and said a Madrid buy-back "would be a sale that would enhance our work", per Sports Illustrated.

Jacobo Ramón is the quieter version of the same story. Madrid also hold a buy-back on the centre-back, and Matteo Moretto reported in September that Madrid expect to use it for the summer 2027 window, per Sports Mole. Ludi closed the January door this week: "sicuramente resterà con noi per tutta la stagione" (he will definitely stay with us all season), per Di Marzio.

Ramón, called up by Spain for the first time this week, credited Como's coach. "I left Real Madrid to carve out a place for myself at the top level," he said, per Football Italia via Yahoo. "Cesc believed in me straight away."

Camello: the striker Como keep coming back to

Rayo Vallecano's forward is the one name linked for January. Certainty: concrete interest, not talks.

Sergio Camello of Rayo Vallecano has been followed by Como for months as cover for Douvikas and Kean, according to CalcioMercato.com, relayed by Labaro Viola and Sportmediaset. A January deal would take about €10m. His contract runs to 2027.

The complication is that Atlético Madrid reportedly retain a buy-back option on the 25-year-old. No bid has been reported and Ludi did not name him. Treat this as concrete interest from a club with a clear need, not a deal in motion.

Shape, captaincy and the goalkeeper question

Cesc Fabregas has settled on a 4-2-3-1: Máximo Perrone and Luis Milla as the double pivot, Nico Paz central behind Tasos Douvikas, with Lucas Da Cunha and Baturina or Caqueret wide. Da Cunha is captain and Paz vice-captain, a hierarchy set in September, per TuttoMercatoWeb.

One pattern worth tracking: Jean Butez started at Lens and Robert Sánchez at Inter. Two matches is not a policy, but it fits a Champions League and Serie A split. Fabregas also admitted at Lens that he has "a weakness for left-footed players", per Football Italia; five were on the pitch together that night.

The scoring has narrowed. Douvikas has 11 goals in all competitions, five in Serie A and six in the Champions League, where he is the competition's top scorer, per statz.ai and ESPN. Paz and Baturina follow on six and five. Como have 37 goals in 15 games, 21 of them after half-time.

Paz by the numbers

Paz's numbers are the other headline. He has three goals and three assists in nine Serie A games and three goals and five assists in four Champions League games. That eight-goal involvement in Europe was the most in the competition after matchday four, per Calcionews24.

On contracts, Douvikas signed to 2031 in late October, per CalcioMercato.com. Perrone's renewal has been described as close since early October but is not yet official. Da Cunha's deal already runs to 2031.

Off the Pitch

The new curva goes to court, and the club pays a fine

A residents' challenge to the Sinigaglia works reaches the regional administrative court on December 10. Meanwhile the losses, and the owners' cheques, keep growing.

The fight over the Stadio Sinigaglia has moved to the courts. The residents' committee Tutela Zona Stadio, represented by lawyer Veronica Dini, appealed to the regional administrative court (TAR) on August 8 against both the overall project and the new Curva Ovest. The hearing is set for December 10, per EspansioneTV.

The same report says the city fined Como for starting work before the required 30 days after filing, a fine the club has paid, and that a second filing on August 8 scaled the works back. The curva itself, 4,550 seats and built in 89 days, is already in use, per Archistadia.

The full Populous redevelopment is a separate, slower process. La Provincia di Como reported in May that it was more than a year behind, with capacity trimmed to about 14,000 and the hotel dropped. A public-interest declaration, a second planning conference and the concession are all still to come.

Fines and finances

There was also a sporting-court fine: €10,000 for a roughly two-minute delay in coming out for the second half at San Siro, treated as a repeat offence, per Calcionews24.

On the money, the last filed accounts (to June 2025) showed a consolidated loss of €132m on about €55m of group revenue, with wages at €86m, per Pambianco. Last year's accounts were reported in December, per Calcio e Finanza, so the 2025-26 figures may land within weeks. UEFA's squad-cost and loss limits now apply.

A guest analysis on The Esk, built from UK company filings, traces control to Robert Budi Hartono of the Djarum group and counts €297.4m of owner funding by November 2025. Its author estimates a further €100m or more will be needed this season. That is an estimate, not filed accounts.

The president speaks

President Mirwan Suwarso was everywhere. He told TNT that "in terms of football DNA, we want to become like Barcelona", with Brighton and Brentford as the business model, per Football Italia via Yahoo. He also said he had built a Como management-game prototype himself in three days, due for staff testing from November 9, per TuttoMercatoWeb.

On Fabregas's future, Suwarso was striking on Piers Morgan Uncensored: "He can go wherever he wants, whenever he wants... I don't even need any compensation," per Sports Mole. The coach's contract runs to 2028. One reported suitor cooled this week, with Moretto saying Mauricio Pochettino will be Manchester United's next manager (Napoli Magazine).

The Academy

Comeback in Florence, and a call from Mancini

The Primavera came from 2-0 down to win at Fiorentina, Riccardo Cassano earned a senior Italy call-up at 17, and Matteo Papaccioli's debut goal still echoes.

Como's Primavera, coached by Gianluca Falsini, came from two goals down to win 3-2 at Fiorentina's Viola Park in Primavera 1 last weekend, with Ballone, Zamagni and Riccardo Cassano scoring, per Football Scouting. The win leaves them eighth on 15 points from ten games; Parma lead on 21.

Cassano, born in 2009, then received a first senior call-up from Italy coach Roberto Mancini for the Nations League games against France and Belgium, per TuttoMercatoWeb. He moves on to Italy's squad for the Under-17 World Cup in Qatar afterwards; Italy open against Jamaica on November 20.

In the UEFA Youth League the Primavera beat Manchester United 2-1 on October 21 but lost 2-1 at Lens on November 4, Cassano scoring, per LiveScore. They have four points and sit outside the play-off places.

Papaccioli and Liberali

The academy's headline of the season remains Matteo Papaccioli. Back from about nine months out with a cruciate ligament injury, the 18-year-old scored in stoppage time on his Champions League debut at Lens. TuttoMercatoWeb reported he is the second-youngest Italian to score in the competition. That record claim is single-sourced.

Mattia Liberali, 19, is now a first-team regular and won his first senior Italy call-up this week, per TuttoMercatoWeb.

Women's Team

A Roma reality check, then the derby nobody else has

Como 1907's women lost 4-0 at home to the leaders but remain third. On Sunday they meet Como Women, the city's other Serie A club.

First, a clarification for new readers. Como 1907 Femminile is the club's own women's team, promoted as Serie B champions last season. Como Women is a separate club, owned by the US fund Mercury13 and based in Meda. Both play in Serie A Women this season, as Il Post explained. This section covers Como 1907.

The promoted side had flown out of the blocks: 4-1 against Milan on opening day, 2-1 at Napoli, then a 4-2 win at Juventus in Biella with two goals from Bonfantini, per Juventus.com. Draws with Sassuolo (2-2) and Ternana (1-1) followed.

Saturday was a reality check. Leaders Roma won 4-0 at Lambrone, with Manuela Giugliano involved in all four goals, per OA Sport. Como 1907 remain third on 11 points (W3 D2 L1, goals 13-11), seven behind Roma, per DAZN's table.

Mazzantini's squad, and Sunday's derby

Coach Selena Mazzantini, with Patrizia Panico as assistant and Heather O'Reilly running the women's section, has a squad built to compete. Tessa Wullaert, last season's Serie A top scorer, rescued a point against Sassuolo with a stoppage-time penalty; captain Vero Boquete, Valentina Giacinti and Emilie Haavi, who scored twice against Milan, add depth.

Next is the derby. Como 1907 host Como Women on Sunday, November 15, at 15:00 (6:00 a.m. Pacific) at Lambrone in Erba, per the club's ticket page. Como Women are sixth on eight points after losing 1-0 at Parma. The sides' summer Women's Cup meeting ended 2-1 to Como Women with a red card each.
