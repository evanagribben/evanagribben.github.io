The Lead

Five Clear, Seven Clean Sheets, One Winger Missing

Arsenal reach the last international break of the year on top of the Premier League, with the division's meanest defence and without their best winger. The numbers behind the title defence, and why the sixteen days after November 21 will decide its shape.

There is a particular kind of quiet that settles over Arsenal when things are going well. Not the giddy quiet of a club that has just found something. The flat, administrative quiet of a club that has decided what it is. Ten Premier League games into the defence of their title, Mikel Arteta's side have won nine, lost one, scored fifteen and conceded five. They sit five points clear. Nobody at the Emirates sounds especially excited about it.

They should not, because the football has not been exciting. It has been correct.

The 1-0 win over Hull City on November 7, the last act before the players scattered to their national teams, was the purest expression of the method yet: 67 per cent of the ball, 21 shots to seven, ten on target to two, and a single goal, scrambled in on 72 minutes by Martin Odegaard after Konstantinos Tzolakis had parried Noni Madueke's low drive into his path.

It was Odegaard's fifth of the season and Arsenal's 34th Premier League 1-0 win under Arteta, more than any other side has managed since his first game in December 2019, according to Sky Sports. Hull's goalkeeper made nine saves. Arsenal's made two. The expected goals read 1.08 to 0.34. Reduced to its bones, this is a team that creates a great deal, converts a little of it, and then simply refuses to let anything back.

The defence is doing something historically rare

The clean sheet against Hull was the seventh in ten league games and the tenth in sixteen matches across all competitions. Arsenal arrived at the break on a run of 444 minutes without conceding a Premier League goal, by Sky Sports' count. They have also won their last ten home games in all competitions, seven of them in the league. Arteta's longest such run remains twelve, set in November 2022.

Five goals conceded in ten games is the number that should frighten the rest of the division, and it has been achieved with a centre-back pairing nobody would have predicted in August.

Ezri Konsa, signed from Aston Villa for an initial 51 million pounds, has slotted in beside Gabriel Magalhaes and against Hull won all seven of the duels he contested while completing 51 of 53 passes. William Saliba, Arsenal's best defender for three seasons, has not played a competitive minute this season.

"We need to praise our players for the way they go about every game." Mikel Arteta, after the Hull City win
The one defeat still explains the season

The single blemish came at the Amex on September 19, where Brighton won 3-0. It remains the only time Arsenal have conceded more than once in the league, and it is also the reason the table looks the way it does: Brighton's goal difference of plus nineteen is the best in the division, and the gap at the top is five rather than eight. Arsenal have the points. Brighton have the goals.

Arteta's answer has been to lean further into control rather than chase the arithmetic. Bruno Guimaraes, bought from Newcastle United for 75 million pounds in August and now the man who goes back to face his old club on November 21, has taken over as the metronome in a 4-2-3-1 that asks very little of its full-backs in transition. Against Hull, Arsenal completed 490 of 569 passes. There was no counter-attacking to speak of because there was nothing to counter.

Saka's absence is the live problem

Bukayo Saka injured a thigh muscle in the 2-2 draw at Bayern Munich on October 21 and has now missed five matches. Arteta's only timeframe has been a flat "weeks", with a further note that Saka will "start hitting the pitch" after the international break. There is no date. There has been no club bulletin. The vice-captain was not in England's squad, and Arsenal have won four of the five games since he limped off, drawing the other at Fleetwood Town.

That last fact is the trap. Madueke has been outstanding in his place, to the point where Arteta told Sky Sports he "has become a different player", and Christos Tzolis, the 34 million pound arrival from Club Brugge, has had a run of starts on the left. But neither has Saka's capacity to decide a game that has gone flat, and Arsenal's margins are thin by design. Six of their nine league wins have come by a single goal.

Why the sixteen days after the break matter most

Arsenal come back on Saturday November 21 at St James' Park. Three days later they host Borussia Dortmund in the Champions League, where they lie third in the league phase on ten points from four unbeaten games. Five days after that, Manchester City come to the Emirates. Then Brentford away on December 2, and Tottenham away on December 6.

That is five matches in sixteen days against two of the three best teams in England, one of the best in Germany, and a derby.

There is at least no Carabao Cup to navigate, because Fleetwood Town knocked Arsenal out on penalties on October 27. Arteta spent the week before the break publicly playing down the schedule and privately managing Declan Rice, who came off on 63 minutes against Hull and about whom the manager said: "We are really looking after him more than any other season."

The title defence is in excellent order. It is also being run with a very small number of spare parts, in a month where the fixture list stops being kind. Saliba's return, now genuinely close, may be the most valuable thing to happen to Arsenal in November.

Barcelona, the Camp Nou, and the Hardest Night of Renee Slegers' Reign

Arsenal Women go to the Spotify Camp Nou on Thursday sitting sixth in the Women's Super League, missing five forwards, and eighteen months removed from beating the same opponent in a European final.

Eighteen months ago Arsenal beat Barcelona 1-0 in Lisbon to win the Women's Champions League. On Thursday evening they walk out at the Spotify Camp Nou for the first time in the club's history, for the first meeting between the two sides since that final, and almost nothing about their situation is the same.

Arsenal are sixth in the WSL on thirteen points from nine games, with three wins, four draws and two defeats and a goal difference of exactly zero. Manchester City are twelve points ahead of them. Renee Slegers, who took the team to that trophy, has had the kind of autumn in which a manager's name starts appearing in sentences it did not use to appear in.

The 2-1 win at Anfield on November 8 was a genuine reprieve, and a tactically interesting one. Alessia Russo scored both goals, the first from a low Emily Fox cross and the second from a Lisa Baum delivery. Both came from the right. Liverpool set up to force Arsenal down their left, and Arsenal went the other way instead. Slegers said afterwards that Liverpool "wanted to have us on our left side".

Fox was the story. Playing, in her own description, "more of a free role", she was advanced inside twenty-five seconds, created four chances, and carried the ball some forty yards on one occasion. Arseblog called it possibly the best individual Arsenal performance of the season. It is worth noting that she signed a contract extension in late September.

The injury list going into Barcelona is brutal. Stina Blackstenius, who scored the goal in Lisbon, is out with an Achilles problem. Mariona Caldentey has a hamstring injury, Michelle Agyemang a knee, Katie Reid an ankle and Selina Cerci a knee. Chloe Kelly is back on grass but not fit. Daphne van Domselaar is not on the Champions League squad list.

Barcelona, who beat Lyon 4-0 in the 2026 final in Oslo, have lost Alexia Putellas and Mapi Leon to London City Lionesses and Salma Paralluelo to Lyon, and have Aitana Bonmati back from a knee injury. Claudia Pina and Ewa Pajor remain.

Slegers has been candid about what Thursday will involve. "We will have to suffer," she said, adding that Arsenal will need to "put bodies on the lines" and be "really disciplined". Around a thousand supporters are travelling from London. The match was moved from Wednesday to Thursday because of a general strike in Spain.

Arsenal are fifth in the league phase on seven points from three, by arseblog's count, having beaten HB Koge 1-0, drawn 2-2 at Paris FC and beaten Benfica 3-0. A top-four finish avoids the knockout play-off round, and the games that decide it are this one and Lyon at home on November 19. It is, as arseblog put it, a make-or-break week.

Transfer News

Zubimendi Says No. Spain Keeps Saying Yes.

Down to 100 Premier League minutes and linked with Atletico Madrid by three Spanish outlets inside three days, Arsenal's 2025 signing from Real Sociedad went to Marca and shut the door. The reporting has not stopped.

The story broke in Spain, as these stories usually do. RNE Deportes reported that Martin Zubimendi was close to a move to Atletico Madrid. Relevo followed with the detail that did the damage: that the midfielder's relationship with Mikel Arteta had gone "cold" after "friction towards the end of last season". AS added that Arsenal would want to recoup the roughly 50 million pounds they paid.

What he actually said

Zubimendi answered it himself, on Spain duty, in an interview with Marca published on November 10. The quotations below are the English renderings carried by Arseblog and ESPN. "There's absolutely nothing to it, and I'm not close to anything," he said. He added that he had not "wasted any energy on it" and had not "got in touch with anyone", and that he was "busy enough trying to earn a place, get playing time, and fight to win this Premier League".

Asked directly whether he would see out the season at Arsenal: "Yes, I hope so. I'm happy there. I'm happy in London."

He was also unusually frank about why he has fallen out of the side. "I played an enormous number of minutes last season, and, to be honest, I was tired," he told Marca, and said he had thought about psychological help without going down that path.

The minutes are the real story

The reporting exists because the selection record is extraordinary for a player who started all 38 league games a year ago. Zubimendi has 100 Premier League minutes this season across seven appearances, and 380 minutes in total across twelve. In the Hull City match he came on in the 89th minute. Ahead of him in league minutes: Martin Odegaard on 830, Declan Rice on 827, Myles Lewis-Skelly on 432, Bruno Guimaraes on 390 and Mikel Merino on 203.

His contract runs to June 2030, which is the single most important fact in the file. Arsenal are not under pressure. FourFourTwo's own understanding is that the club would rather sell in the summer than in January, if at all.

Where it sits

Concrete interest, reported in Spain, publicly denied by the player, with no bid, no named Tier-1 journalist attached and no word from either club. Real Madrid and Chelsea have also been linked, more loosely. Treat the "close to leaving" framing with care: the English coverage of it is an aggregation of Spanish radio and a Spanish website, not an Ornstein or Romano report.

The Wide Shortlist: Endrick, Nusa, Baena, Kroupi

With Saka out and no cup competition left to blood anybody in, four attacking names have surfaced inside a week. None of them is close. One of them tells you how Andrea Berta works.

The most repeated of the four is Endrick, the 20-year-old Real Madrid forward who has played 114 minutes this season. talkSPORT reported that Arsenal are eyeing a January move, a claim relayed through the BBC's gossip column. The interesting wrinkle is the route: the report says sporting director Andrea Berta is working through a relationship with the player's agency.

Against that, AS report that Roma and Sporting CP want Endrick on loan to the end of the season, and that the player is not ready to give up on Madrid. Asked about January while on Brazil duty, he said he did not know what would happen. No bid, no fee, no loan structure and no word from Madrid.

The Independent's three names

Separately, The Independent listed three wide options Arsenal are said to be weighing: Antonio Nusa, the 21-year-old Norway winger at RB Leipzig; Alex Baena of Atletico Madrid; and Eli Junior Kroupi of Bournemouth. Kroupi is reported to be Arteta's own preference, and Bournemouth's position is that he is not for sale. All three are interest, not negotiation, and all three come via a single outlet relayed by the BBC.

The Ornstein baseline

Worth holding on to: in a reader Q&A for The Athletic in September, David Ornstein said he could "see something happening" for Arsenal in an attacking position in January, while also saying they "don't look like they need it" and, pointedly, "I don't know of anything brewing." Nothing in the past week has changed that from the one source who would know.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Newcastle United, away, Saturday November 21

A 100 million pound sale, a 75 million pound sale to Arsenal, a new German head coach and twelfth place. St James' Park is still St James' Park.

Newcastle United have had the strangest summer of any club in the division and the table shows it. They sit twelfth on twelve points from ten games, with three wins, three draws and four defeats, thirteen goals scored and sixteen conceded. Their recent league form reads draw, defeat, win, defeat, defeat. The most recent game was a 1-1 draw at Fulham on November 7, preceded by a 1-0 home defeat by Everton on November 2.

The upheaval was structural. Eddie Howe stepped down on July 31 and Matthias Jaissle was appointed six days later. Then the squad was dismantled at the top end: Sandro Tonali to Tottenham for 100 million pounds, Anthony Gordon to Barcelona for 69.3 million, and Bruno Guimaraes to Arsenal for 75 million on August 8. That is 244 million pounds of midfield and attack sold in five weeks. Kieran Trippier left on a free.

Roughly 214 million pounds went back in, spread wide rather than deep: Nico Gonzalez from Manchester City for 47 million, Bazoumana Toure from Hoffenheim for 42.8 million, Amar Dedic from Benfica for 30.7 million, Aladji Bamba from Monaco for 30 million, Sean Steur from Ajax for 19.6 million, and two goalkeepers. Three of those signings play in central midfield. None of them is Bruno Guimaraes.

Who Arsenal have to stop

Anthony Elanga was Newcastle's leading league scorer in the early weeks and Harvey Barnes has been the other reliable source of goals.

The two names worth more attention, though, are Jacob Murphy, who leads the squad for big chances created with three, and Toure, who leads it for shots on target per match at 1.5. With sixteen conceded in ten, Newcastle have been as leaky as they have been blunt, which argues for Arsenal taking the ball and squeezing, exactly as they did at Anfield and at Slavia Prague.

Availability

Joelinton is carrying a knock with a return flagged for late November and Ewen Jaouen has a hamstring injury flagged for early December. Jacob Ramsey was sent off for a second yellow card against Everton. Newcastle have no European football this season, which means a clear fortnight to prepare for this, where Arsenal will have come straight back from ten different senior international camps.

The Squad

The Saliba Question Nobody Expected to Be Asking

Arsenal's best defender has not played a competitive minute this season. The man who replaced him just won every duel he contested. Arteta has a decision, and no bad options.

William Saliba aggravated a back problem on France duty at the World Cup and has been out of the Arsenal side ever since. He avoided surgery.

He played 45 minutes behind closed doors, then 45 minutes for the under-21s against Aston Villa on November 8, and he was an unused substitute at Slavia Prague on November 4. Arteta's language has moved from cautious to warm: "He's in a really good place," he said before the Hull match. "His evolution has been as expected, which is not always the case. He feels really comfortable."

And yet Zinedine Zidane left him out of France's squad for this window, which is probably the right call for a player four months into a back problem and ten days from a run of five games in sixteen. Saliba has spent the fortnight at the training ground with the other senior players left behind: Noni Madueke, Kepa Arrizabalaga and Illan Meslier.

The problem is that Konsa has been excellent

Ezri Konsa arrived in August for an initial 51 million pounds and was widely read as a squad upgrade rather than a starter. Instead he has formed a centre-back pairing with Gabriel that has conceded five league goals in ten games. Against Hull he won seven duels out of seven and misplaced two passes in 53. Sky Sports spent the weekend asking whether he can hold Saliba off, which is not a question anyone would have written in August.

The likeliest resolution is the dullest. Saliba comes back through the bench at St James' Park, Konsa keeps his place while the fixtures pile up, and by the time Manchester City arrive on November 29 Arteta has three Premier League-standard centre-backs plus Piero Hincapie and Cristhian Mosquera. That is the position every manager in the league wants to be in.

Saka, and the honest version of the timeline

Bukayo Saka's thigh injury, sustained at Bayern Munich on October 21, has cost him five matches. Arteta has given two useful sentences and no date. "We are talking about weeks," he said before Hull, and separately that Saka would "start hitting the pitch" after the international break, while adding that he "is going to be out for a while" and that "it's still quite early".

Websites have variously nominated the Manchester City game on November 29 and the Brentford game on December 2 as targets. Neither is an Arsenal statement, and the Digest is not going to pretend otherwise.

Load management is now a visible policy

The most revealing thing Arteta said all week was about Declan Rice, withdrawn on 63 minutes with no sign of a problem: "We are really looking after him more than any other season."

Ezri Konsa was taken off at Slavia Prague purely as a precaution, with Arteta explaining that he had "played a lot, so many games in the last few weeks". Konsa then played the full ninety against Hull but will miss England's game against Croatia while following his own programme, with a return to the group expected for the trip to Spain.

This is a squad being driven with the handbrake half on, by a manager who has worked out that the title is won in April and lost in December. It is also why Martin Zubimendi, a 38-game starter last season, has 100 league minutes: there is nothing wrong with him, and there has been nowhere to put him.

Set pieces, the captain, and the shape

The system has settled into a 4-2-3-1 with Bruno Guimaraes and Rice behind Martin Odegaard, who remains captain and is now Arsenal's leading scorer with five. Christos Tzolis has held the left while Saka is out, with Eberechi Eze and Mikel Merino used as the second-half levers and Viktor Gyokeres rotating with Kai Havertz through the middle.

Arsenal took seven corners against Hull without scoring from one. Of their 1.08 expected goals in that match, 0.91 came from open play and only 0.17 from set plays, which for a side with their reputation from dead balls is worth noting even over a single afternoon.

Hale End

A First Win, a Sixteen-Year-Old, and a Goalkeeper in the Essex Senior Cup

The under-21s finally got off the mark, the under-18s are through in the Premier League Cup, and the under-19s are 23rd in the Youth League with two games to save their season.
Under-21s 5-2 Aston Villa

David Horseman's side won their first Premier League 2 game of the season at Meadow Park on November 8, and did it with a senior international at centre-back. William Saliba played the first 45 minutes, was replaced by Elijah Upson at half-time as planned, and according to both arseblog and Jeorge Bird's Arsenal Youth was assured and expansive in possession.

Villa led inside five minutes through Cole Brannigan. Maalik Hashi equalised on 24 after good work from Theo Julienne, and Andre Harriman-Annous scored twice, on 27 from a Marcell Washington cross and again on 48. Max Dowman, playing as an attacking midfielder, hit the post and then scored on 78. Evan Mooney made it five on 90 from a Julienne pass. Jack McGrath got Villa's second on 87.

Dowman is the name to hold. Suspended from first-team football after his red card at Fleetwood Town, and sent off again for England's under-19s, he has instead been promoted into Lee Carsley's under-21 squad for friendlies against Portugal and France. Marli Salmon captained the under-21s and is with England's under-19s this window.

Under-18s 3-0 Sunderland

A Premier League Cup win on November 7 that put the under-18s into the knockout stages and extended their winning run across all competitions to eight. Mylo Bernard opened the scoring, set up by Mikael Bonaventure Yetna, Emerson Nwaneri converted a penalty and substitute Callan Hamill finished it.

Yetna is the story here. He is sixteen, still a schoolboy, a defensive midfielder who can play higher, and he made his under-19 debut against Club Brugge last season while still an under-15. Arsenal Youth reports he is very likely to be offered a scholarship contract before next season. He also featured for the under-21s in pre-season.

Under-19s 0-1 Slavia Prague

The sour note. Arsenal's under-19s lost in stoppage time in Prague on November 4 to a Miguel Solarte goal, having competed well for most of the match. That leaves them with one win, one draw and two defeats, in 23rd place in the UEFA Youth League, where only the top 22 advance. Their last two league-phase games are at home to Borussia Dortmund and to the current leaders, Real Madrid. Andre Harriman-Annous led the line well.

Out on loan

Khari Ranson is the new one. The nineteen-year-old goalkeeper, an England youth international who has spent close to a decade in the academy and has been named on the first-team bench, joined National League South side Billericay Town on an initial 28-day loan confirmed on November 10.

He kept a clean sheet in a 7-0 Essex Senior Cup win over Little Oakley. Non-league loans are a well-worn route for young goalkeepers. His departure makes Jack Porter the under-21s' first choice, with Lucas Nygaard as cover.

Ethan Nwaneri at Borussia Dortmund is the one that matters most and is going least well. Give Me Sport's figures, relayed on November 3, put him at 44 minutes across Dortmund's last four matches, and he spent the entire Bayern Munich game on the bench. He is at the Emirates on November 24 with Dortmund, and he is eligible: UEFA permits loan players to face their parent clubs, as the BBC confirmed on November 4.

Tommy Setford is at Stevenage and has been called into England's under-21 squad alongside Nwaneri. Ismeal Kabia is at St Mirren and Charles Sagoe Jr is at Kalmar FF until January.

Arsenal Women

Sixth in the League, Fifth in Europe, and Alessia Russo Carrying It

Four draws in nine WSL games have left Arsenal twelve points off the top. The European campaign is in better shape, and the next ten days decide what kind of season this is.

The Women's Super League table is unkind reading. Arsenal are sixth on thirteen points from nine games, with three wins, four draws and two defeats, ten goals scored and ten conceded. Manchester City lead on twenty-five, Chelsea are on twenty-three and London City Lionesses, in their second season at this level, are third on nineteen. Arsenal are closer to tenth than to first.

The four draws are the problem rather than the two defeats. A 1-1 at home to Aston Villa on November 1, a 1-1 at home to Manchester United in September, a goalless home draw earlier that month and another 1-1 at home in October. The heaviest loss was 4-2 at Manchester City on October 4. Renee Slegers has said publicly that Arsenal are not in the title conversation.

Anfield was the reset

The 2-1 win at Liverpool on November 8 was Arsenal's best performance of the season and Alessia Russo's second decisive one in a fortnight, after the hat-trick that beat Benfica 3-0 in the Champions League on October 28. Both Arsenal goals came from the right: a low Emily Fox cross finished first time, then a Lisa Baum delivery headed in.

The tactical point is a good one. Liverpool pressed to funnel Arsenal left; Arsenal went right instead, repeatedly getting two players against one, and Fox was given licence to go early and high. She created four chances, level with Caitlin Foord for the match high, and at one point carried the ball roughly forty yards. "I had more of a free role," she said. She extended her contract in late September, which now looks like good business.

Leah Williamson, whose form has been questioned, led Arsenal for clearances and interceptions at Anfield and made eighteen passes into the final third. Slegers, asked about the criticism, reached for a Dutch proverb about the tall tree catching all the wind.

Europe is where the season can still turn

Arsenal are fifth in the Women's Champions League league phase on seven points from three: a 1-0 home win over HB Koge on September 22, a 2-2 draw at Paris FC on September 30, and the 3-0 win over Benfica. Thursday takes them to Barcelona at the Spotify Camp Nou, a ground the club has never visited, in their first meeting with Barcelona since the 2025 final in Lisbon that Arsenal won 1-0 through Stina Blackstenius.

Blackstenius is one of five forwards unavailable, alongside Mariona Caldentey, Michelle Agyemang, Selina Cerci and, through a knee problem, Katie Reid at the back. Chloe Kelly is back running on grass but will not be fit. Ona Batlle travelled after missing Anfield. Slegers' assessment was blunt: "We will have to suffer."

Then OL Lyonnes come to the Emirates on November 19. A top-four league-phase finish avoids the knockout play-off round, and these are the two fixtures that decide it. The league phase ends with nine simultaneous games on December 16, when Arsenal are away at Inter.

Gunners Abroad

Twenty Arsenal Players, Ten Countries, One Flight to Singapore

The final international window of the year empties the training ground. Eight Arsenal men are involved on both sides of Spain against England, and two are flying to south-east Asia for a pair of friendlies.

The headline fixture is Spain against England in Madrid on Sunday November 15, at the Riyadh Air Metropolitano, and Arsenal supply both dressing rooms. David Raya, Martin Zubimendi and Mikel Merino are with Spain, who top the Nations League group on twelve points. Declan Rice, Eberechi Eze, Ben White, Myles Lewis-Skelly and Ezri Konsa are with England, who are second having won three of four.

Before that, England host Croatia at Wembley on Thursday November 12 at 11:45am Pacific, needing only a draw to reach the March quarter-finals. Konsa will miss it while following his own recovery programme, and is expected back with the group for the trip to Spain. White's recall after a strong start to the season was one of the week's better pieces of news for Arsenal; Noni Madueke, in the form of his life, was left out again.

The long haul

Gabriel and Bruno Guimaraes are with Brazil for two friendlies in south-east Asia, against Japan on November 14 and Singapore on November 17, both staged in Singapore. Arteta was asked about the travel at his Friday press conference. They will be back at the training ground with perhaps three days to prepare for St James' Park, which is the kind of detail that decides a 1-0.

Everyone else

Martin Odegaard captains Norway against Wales on November 14 and in Denmark on November 17, with his country facing relegation from the top tier of the Nations League after losing three of four across September and October.

Riccardo Calafiori has Italy against France on November 12 and Belgium away on November 15. Kai Havertz is with Germany in Serbia on November 13 and at home to the Netherlands on November 16, the same match that takes Jurrien Timber to Berlin after the Dutch host Greece on November 13.

That Netherlands game also involves Christos Tzolis, who then has Greece against Serbia on November 16. Viktor Gyokeres has Sweden against Bosnia and Herzegovina on November 14 and Poland away on November 17. Piero Hincapie is in Quito with Ecuador for friendlies against Costa Rica and Bolivia in the middle of next week.

In the age groups, Cristhian Mosquera is with Spain's under-21s against Japan and Albania, Max Dowman is with England's under-21s against Portugal on November 13 and France on November 16 alongside Arsenal loanees Tommy Setford and Ethan Nwaneri, and Marli Salmon has three European Championship qualifiers with England's under-19s against Slovenia, Belarus and Austria.

Not travelling: William Saliba, omitted by France despite returning to training, Madueke, Kepa Arrizabalaga and Illan Meslier. Bukayo Saka is injured.

In Brief

Raya and Russo in the World 11. David Raya was named goalkeeper in the 2026 FIFPRO Men's World 11 and Alessia Russo made the Women's XI, announced on November 9. The teams were chosen by 26,317 professional footballers across 75 countries, voting for the men's and women's elevens. Raya drew 10,278 votes, more than any other man on the ballot. The Ballon d'Or winner Harry Kane missed out, polling 27.6 per cent among forwards.

Berta's award. Sporting director Andrea Berta has been named Best Executive in Tuttosport's Golden Boy awards, the same set of prizes in which Alessia Russo took Golden Player Woman and Pau Cubarsi of Barcelona was named Golden Boy.

A Christmas advert with Colin Firth in it. Arsenal released a Love Actually-inspired festive film on around November 5 featuring the actor alongside Gabriel, Riccardo Calafiori and Russo. Make of it what you will.

Arteta on Madueke. "Noni Madueke has become a different player," the manager told Sky Sports, pointing to a new level of creativity and efficiency. Madueke scored at Slavia Prague and forced the save that led to the winner against Hull, and still cannot get into an England squad.

The VAR row rumbles on. Sky Sports ran a dedicated segment on the Havertz non-penalty against Hull, in which the former referee Mike Dean said the decision should have been a penalty rather than an offside. Arsenal have had no explanation.

Nine points in a week. Arsenal beat Liverpool 1-0 at Anfield on November 1 through Kai Havertz, won 2-1 at Slavia Prague on November 4 through Noni Madueke and Viktor Gyokeres, and beat Hull City 1-0 on November 7. Nine points and one goal conceded in a week.
