The Lead

La Roja Reign Again — and a Golden Generation Passes the Torch

Spain outlast a ten-man Argentina at MetLife to win a second World Cup, sixteen years after the first. Beneath the confetti, the biggest tournament ever staged doubled as a farewell for the game’s old kings — and a shop window that will define the summer’s transfer market.

On a humid Sunday evening at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey, the first 48-team, tri-nation World Cup delivered a final worthy of its scale: Spain 1, Argentina 0, settled in extra time. Ferran Torres swept home the only goal in the 106th minute, moments after the defending champions had been reduced to ten men by Enzo Fernández’s second yellow late in regulation. It is Spain’s second world title, and their first since Johannesburg in 2010 — the bookend to a golden era that has now, improbably, produced two.

For long stretches this was a coronation rather than a contest. Luis de la Fuente’s side dominated possession and territory, pinning a tiring Argentina back and forcing Emiliano Martínez into a goalkeeping display for the ages before Torres finally broke through. When the red card came, the tactical arithmetic tilted decisively; Spain simply squeezed until the dam burst. “Destiny was written,” Torres said afterward, and it was hard to argue — this was a team that had spent two summers refusing to lose.

“Destiny was written.” Spain came to North America having already won a European Championship — they leave having refused to lose a match that mattered for two years running.

The individual honours told the story of Spain’s control and of one Frenchman’s enduring brilliance. Rodri, metronome of the midfield, took the Golden Ball as the tournament’s best player, with Lionel Messi second and Kylian Mbappé third. Unai Simón claimed the Golden Glove for seven clean sheets; nineteen-year-old centre-back Pau Cubarsí was named Best Young Player, the emblem of a Spanish spine that will contend for a decade. And Mbappé, even in defeat to Spain in the semi-finals, walked away with the Golden Boot on ten goals and, more lastingly, the record books: his 22nd career World Cup goal, struck in the third-place game, moved him clear of Messi and Miroslav Klose as the all-time leading scorer in the men’s tournament.

The scale of the thing bears repeating. This was the first 48-team World Cup, 104 matches spread across sixteen host cities in the United States, Canada and Mexico, with a newly minted Round of 32 bolted onto the front of the knockouts. It produced the romance the format’s critics said it would dilute. Debutants Cape Verde went unbeaten through the group stage before falling 3–2 to Argentina in extra time in the Round of 32, their 40-year-old goalkeeper Vozinha becoming an overnight folk hero. The co-hosts had their moment too: the United States beat Bosnia and Herzegovina before losing 4–1 to Belgium in the Round of 16, in what was reported as the most-watched soccer match in American history.

There was rancour to match the romance. The tournament’s officiating became a story in itself, with a Wikipedia page dedicated to its controversies before the trophy was even lifted. But the sourest note was political: after Folarin Balogun was sent off in the United States’ win over Bosnia, a phone call from the White House to FIFA president Gianni Infantino preceded the suspension being overturned, freeing Balogun to face Belgium — by most accounts the first reversal of a World Cup red-card ban since 1962, and one UEFA called “unprecedented, incomprehensible and unjustifiable.”

And then the farewells. Messi, the tournament’s second-top scorer at 38, retreated from the celebrations to grieve the defeat privately and is widely expected to step away from the national team, a decision manager Lionel Scaloni says will crystallise around Argentina’s autumn friendlies. This was also Cristiano Ronaldo’s final World Cup, with international curtain-calls anticipated for Luka Modrić, Manuel Neuer and others. A tournament that began as a logistical experiment ended as a generational hinge. The kings have taken their bow; the auction has begun.

The Shop Window: Whose Stock Soared in North America

A month of prime-time football turned unknowns into targets and targets into auctions.

No player’s value has moved faster than Yan Diomandé, the 19-year-old Ivorian winger who lit up the tournament and turned RB Leipzig’s asking price into a bidding war. Germany’s Felix Nmecha and the Netherlands’ Crysencio Summerville — the latter already moved to Al Hilal — are others whose cameos priced them up. The market cuts both ways: Julián Álvarez has become the summer’s most-chased striker, while an injury-hit season left Dušan Vlahović a free agent with a diminished market.

Transfer News

Chelsea Break Their Record for Rogers — and Wave Garnacho Away

Chelsea completed the marquee move of the window on July 21, signing England forward Morgan Rogers from Aston Villa for a club-record fee reported at £117m (~$158m) — the biggest transfer of the summer anywhere, and a statement from a squad now reported to be under Xabi Alonso after a chaotic 2026. Out went Alejandro Garnacho, who joined Aston Villa on July 23 on loan with an obligation to buy near £42.6m (Romano). Chelsea also agreed a deal for France centre-back Maxence Lacroix from Crystal Palace for a fee near €60m. [OFFICIAL / FEE AGREED]

Maresca’s City Pay £116m for Anderson; Spurs Spend a Quarter-Billion

In his first window since replacing Pep Guardiola, Enzo Maresca sanctioned a club-record £116m for Nottingham Forest’s Elliot Anderson; Manchester City also sold Manuel Akanji to Inter and Bernardo Silva (free) to Real Madrid. No club has been busier than Tottenham under Roberto De Zerbi: in flew Sandro Tonali (£92.5m from Newcastle), Mateus Fernandes (£85m from West Ham) and Jan-Paul van Hecke (£52m from Brighton). Yet captain Cristian Romero is, in Romano’s words, “over” at Spurs, with Inter the frontrunners. [OFFICIAL · Romero: ADVANCED]

Mourinho’s Madrid Go Grown-Up; Barça Bet on the Wings

Real Madrid begin life under José Mourinho — confirmed by a re-elected Florentino Pérez — with a lurch toward experience: in came Marc Cucurella (~£51.8m), plus free transfers Bernardo Silva and Ibrahima Konaté and right-back Denzel Dumfries. The marquee chase is Leipzig’s Yan Diomandé, with a nine-figure bid already rebuffed. Barcelona rebuilt their forward line with Anthony Gordon (~£69m from Newcastle) and Karim Adeyemi (from Borussia Dortmund), as Robert Lewandowski left on a free for MLS. The Julián Álvarez saga rolls on, with Atlético refusing to sell. [OFFICIAL · Álvarez: CONTESTED]

Italy’s Coaching Carousel and a Scramble for Strikers

Champions Inter, now under Cristian Chivu, made Manuel Akanji permanent and tied down Pio Esposito and Yann Bisseck to 2031. At AC Milan, new boss Ruben Amorim signed PSG striker Gonçalo Ramos, added Mario Gila, and renewed 40-year-old Luka Modrić. Juventus are racing an internal early-August deadline set by Luciano Spalletti: personal terms are agreed with Randal Kolo Muani and Emiliano Martínez, but both deals have stalled on fees, and Loïs Openda is close to a Lyon loan. [OFFICIAL · FEES STALLED]

PSG Tie Down Luis Enrique; Marseille Cash In; MLS Gets Griezmann

Paris Saint-Germain are reportedly set to extend Luis Enrique to 2030 (L’Équipe) while working the winger market hard. Marseille banked their biggest-ever sale, Mason Greenwood to Fenerbahçe for ~€40m. In MLS, Antoine Griezmann scored on his Orlando City debut; Robert Lewandowski joined Chicago Fire, who sold Golden Boot leader Hugo Cuypers to CF Monterrey for a reported $7.5m-plus. [OFFICIAL]

Around the Leagues

Premier League

England’s top flight enters 2026–27 barely recognisable in its dugouts. Pep Guardiola’s reign at Manchester City ended in the spring, with Enzo Maresca installed. Liverpool sacked Arne Slot on May 30 after a fifth-place finish and began pre-season with the Anfield job still being resolved even as Alexander Isak arrived and Ibrahima Konaté left for Madrid. Michael Carrick steadied Manchester United; Roberto De Zerbi survived a relegation scrape to lead Tottenham; and Chelsea, after cycling through three men in 2026, are reported to have turned to Xabi Alonso.

Off the pitch, the league’s new financial architecture is live this season, with Squad Cost Ratio rules capping on-pitch spend at 85% of football revenue plus player-sale profit — reframing the summer’s nine-figure deals as a calculated bet against the new ceiling. In the women’s game, Alexia Putellas left Barcelona after fourteen years to join London City Lionesses in the WSL, part of a statement spree alongside Mary Earps, Kosovare Asllani and Grace Geyoro. (Arsenal’s dealings are covered in our companion Arsenal edition.)

La Liga

Spain’s summer belongs to two boardroom dramas. At Real Madrid, José Mourinho’s appointment has ushered in an older squad and a public pursuit of Yan Diomandé. At Barcelona, Hansi Flick’s side prepares to reopen a partially rebuilt Camp Nou in August while chasing Julián Álvarez against Atlético’s flat refusal. Elsewhere, Real Betis under Manuel Pellegrini added goalkeeper Diego Conde on loan from Villarreal.

Bundesliga

Under Vincent Kompany, Bayern Munich have entered a “sell-first” phase; João Palhinha is in advanced talks with Aston Villa, while Konrad Laimer was renewed to 2029 and Michael Olise declared not for sale. Borussia Dortmund sold Karim Adeyemi to Barcelona and chase Köln’s Said El Mala. The German summer’s plot is at RB Leipzig, weighing nine-figure interest in Diomandé and Antonio Nusa — the Diomandé auction may set the window’s ceiling.

Serie A

Italy’s benches were reshuffled wholesale: Allegri to Napoli, Amorim to Milan, Sarri to Atalanta, Gattuso to Lazio, Spalletti at Juventus, Chivu at champions Inter. Finances have shaped the market: Lazio are blocked from buying until they sell, while Champions League-bound Como under Cesc Fàbregas chase both Trevoh Chalobah and Moise Kean.

Ligue 1

Paris Saint-Germain remain French football’s gravitational centre, moving to extend Luis Enrique to 2030 and working the winger market from continental strength. Marseille reshaped by cashing in, Mason Greenwood’s record sale chief among the exits, while pursuing Memphis Depay and Keito Nakamura. Monaco, called “in crisis” by French media, agreed to sell young defender Aladji Bamba to Newcastle for north of €40m.

Major League Soccer

MLS returned from its World Cup break with a shock at the top: Nashville SC lead the Supporters’ Shield race on 39 points, clear of Lionel Messi’s Inter Miami in the East. Miami eased back with a 3–2 win over Chicago Fire, Luis Suárez scoring twice. Out West, Vancouver and San Jose set the pace, with the MLS-versus-Liga MX Leagues Cup kicking off August 4.
