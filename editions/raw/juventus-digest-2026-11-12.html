The Lead

Six points off fourth, and a fortnight to fix it

Bremer's header rescued a point in Florence, but another uneven night left Spalletti searching for consistency as the pressure follows Juventus into the international break.

Juventus left Florence on Sunday night with a point, a grievance and a familiar diagnosis. Juventus needed Gleison Bremer’s 66th-minute header to draw 2-2 at Fiorentina, having twice come from behind at the Artemio Franchi. The result left them seventh on 18 points, six behind fourth-placed AC Milan and 11 behind leaders Lazio after 11 rounds.

The match followed a pattern Luciano Spalletti has described all autumn. Fiorentina scored after four minutes, Mateo Pellegrino heading in from a corner. Nick Woltemade levelled on 32 minutes from Randal Kolo Muani’s dribble and cross. Alieu Njie restored the lead on 54 minutes, tapping in after Guglielmo Vicario parried a shot, before Bremer beat David De Gea to a Douglas Luiz corner (Juventus.com match report).

There was controversy too. Juventus wanted a penalty when the ball struck João Mário’s arm, and Radu Drăgușin’s 82nd-minute header for Fiorentina was ruled out for a two-handed push on Bremer. Juventus had complaints about referee Andrea Colombo. Asked about the penalty replay, Spalletti said he did not need one: he had already seen it on the pitch (Football Italia).

“We tend to drown in a glass of water sometimes, and lose confidence in the way we play.”

Luciano Spalletti to DAZN Italia, November 8

He added that his side “just can’t seem to be consistent throughout a match, as a team or as individuals.” A day later, asked about the media criticism, he struck a different tone: “Every time, you always ask the same questions, and this motivates us,” he told reporters, adding that he was “super motivated” (beIN Sports).

What the numbers say

Since the start of October Juventus have alternated almost perfectly in the league: a draw at Cagliari, a 3-0 home defeat by Lazio, a 2-0 win at Lecce, a 2-0 loss at Genoa, a 2-0 win over Napoli and Sunday’s draw. That is eight points from six games. In Europe they are unbeaten, with 10 points from four Europa League matches.

All five league wins have come with clean sheets, a Stats Perform figure reported by SoccerNews after the Napoli game. The trouble starts when the first goal goes against them. In Florence it took four minutes, from a set piece, and the response was spirited rather than controlled: two equalisers, never a lead.

The boardroom view

The club’s public line has not moved. A Tuttomercatoweb analysis published on Thursday (via Napoli Magazine) says the club has confirmed its “full trust” in the coach, yet describes him as “increasingly alone and besieged”. Antonio Conte, it says, remains a ghost over Turin, and Zinedine Zidane has spoken of wanting to coach Juventus one day.

Late October was more fraught. Sports Mole, citing La Stampa, reported on October 29 that a change “could happen quickly”, with Conte the favourite and Stefano Pioli and Didier Deschamps also named. Calciomercato framed Napoli, AZ and Fiorentina as a three-game test (Get Italian Football News). Juventus took seven points from nine: a pass, if not a convincing one.

CEO Giovanni Carnevali, confirmed in post at the November 3 shareholders’ meeting, said in September that changing coaches “is not in my nature” (Football Italia). Tuttomercatoweb reads the January window as the real verdict: whether Spalletti gets the signings he wants will show whether he is still at the centre of the project.

The two weeks ahead

Help is coming from the treatment room. Tuttosport reports that Francisco Conceição, out since dislocating his left shoulder against Lazio on October 18, trained with the group on Wednesday. Nico González is recovering from a soleus strain and Andrea Cambiaso is recovering from fatigue; both are targeted for Venezia. Kenan Yildiz is not expected back before early December.

Spalletti gave the players not on international duty four days off after Wednesday’s session, with work resuming on Monday. Venezia, 19th with six points, visit the Allianz Stadium on Sunday, November 22. On paper it is the kindest fixture of the autumn. Under this much scrutiny, it is also the one Juventus can least afford to fumble.

 
Second feature

Kolo Muani's night in Alkmaar, and the warning beneath it

A goal and an assist won a tight game in the Netherlands, but AZ created the better chances and had a stoppage-time equaliser ruled out.

Juventus won 2-1 at AZ last Thursday, and the night belonged to Randal Kolo Muani. He set up Teun Koopmeiners on 19 minutes, a goal against the club where the Dutchman made his name, with his brother Peer in the AZ starting line-up. On 37 minutes Kolo Muani finished himself after an exchange with Nick Woltemade. Flashscore named him man of the match.

The underlying numbers were less flattering. Per beIN Sports, Juventus produced 0.57 expected goals to AZ’s 2.1, and only 0.12 after the break. Mexx Meerdink pulled one back on 88 minutes, and in stoppage time Guglielmo Vicario made what Football Italia called an extraordinary save before Mees de Wit’s finish from the rebound was ruled out for offside.

The win ended AZ’s 17-match unbeaten home run in Europe, according to Flashscore, and kept Juventus unbeaten in the competition with a goal difference of plus seven. The top eight after eight matchdays go straight to the round of 16. Omonia visit Turin on November 26 (UEFA fixtures).

Scouting the Next Opponent

Venezia: bottom-three, but no longer pointless

Davide Nicola has given the Lagunari a pulse. Juventus must break a side built to defend.

Venezia arrive in Turin 19th with six points from 11 games (two wins, nine defeats, goal difference minus 13), according to FotMob. They lost their first six league games before turning to Davide Nicola, the specialist in relegation escapes, in mid-October (Washington Post).

Under Nicola they have beaten Napoli 1-0 and Udinese 2-1 at home, and lost 1-0 at Genoa, 2-0 at home to Inter and 1-0 at Como. Six points from five games is relegation-survival form, and the narrow margins tell you how they play: compact, combative and hard to open up.

Who to stop

Akor Adams, the striker signed from Sevilla in the summer for a reported €16 million plus add-ons, has two league goals. Antoine Hainaut, with two goals and two assists, is their most creative outlet and has created the most big chances in the squad. Captain Gianluca Busio also has two assists, and Albion Rrahmani takes the most shots per game.

FotMob lists Andrea Adorante (back) and Marin Šverko (thigh) as out, with Pasquale Mazzocchi, Thierry Correia and Bartol Franjić doubtful. No Venezia suspensions are listed.

Where Juventus can hurt them

Nobody at Juventus is suspended after round 11 (JuveFC, on the sporting judge’s November 10 rulings), though Kolo Muani, Bremer and Cabal each took their first booking of the season in Florence. The test is patience: against Napoli the breakthrough came only on 70 minutes. Set pieces may matter: Bremer, a centre-back, leads the squad with three league goals, and his equaliser in Florence came from a corner.

Transfer News

Spalletti's January list: a striker and a left-back

With Yildiz still sidelined and the attack thin, the names are flowing. None has gone beyond the rumour stage.

Juve Oggi reported on Thursday that Luciano Spalletti has two precise requests for January: a striker and a left-back. The striker names in circulation are Gianluca Scamacca of Atalanta, Alexander Sørloth of Atlético Madrid and Folarin Balogun of Monaco, a shortlist also carried in a OneFootball roundup (via Yahoo).

Of those, only Scamacca has a reported structure: Tuttojuve says Juventus are considering a loan with an option to make it permanent (JuveFC). Certainty: rumour, single-sourced, no talks reported.

For the left side, Juve Oggi cites La Gazzetta dello Sport on Manchester City’s Rayan Aït-Nouri, possibly on loan, with Nicolás Tagliafico and Maxi Araújo as alternatives. These are early names, not negotiations.

Money frames all of it. Shareholders approved a capital increase of up to €250 million on November 3, but chief financial officer Stefano Cerrato told the meeting that the outlook for those funds is tied to sporting performance (Tuttosport).

Vicario's option and Calhanoglu's contract

On the permanent front, JuveFC reported on Wednesday that Juventus are increasingly considering triggering the €8 million purchase option on Guglielmo Vicario, on loan from Tottenham, and that Spurs are not keen to take him back (JuveFC via Yahoo). No primary outlet is named, so treat it as a lead. His two match-saving interventions this week make the case.

Calciomercato reports that Juventus will keep working towards Hakan Calhanoglu, whose Inter contract has not been renewed, with a free transfer the scenario (JuveFC via Yahoo). Certainty: interest reported by one outlet; no talks.

The Squad

Kolo Muani out wide, and a new idea up front

Three returns are due for Venezia. Spalletti is weighing how to fit them into an attack that has found goals hard to come by in Serie A.

Randal Kolo Muani has been used on the right of the front three lately, and Spalletti praised his willingness to play anywhere after Fiorentina. Kolo Muani himself said his position does not matter as long as he has space to attack (Juventus.com press room). He has four goals in all competitions, level with Woltemade.

That leaves the middle. Tuttosport reports Spalletti is considering Nico González, described as the squad’s best header of the ball, as a central striker for Venezia, alongside Kolo Muani and Woltemade. Goal listed a 4-3-3 in Florence; against Napoli, Football Italia saw Juventus switch to a 3-5-2 shape after half-time.

Rotation and form

Europe has been a proving ground. At AZ, Pape Matar Sarr, Lloyd Kelly, Federico Gatti and Andrea Cambiaso all started, and Juventus won. Vicario has been the standout of the week in both games. Woltemade’s goal in Florence was his first in the league, and Bremer’s three league goals make him the squad’s top Serie A scorer from centre-back.

With captain Manuel Locatelli out until the new year, the midfield has leaned on Douglas Luiz, whose corners produced Bremer’s equaliser in Florence and Cabal’s header against the post versus Napoli. Koopmeiners has scored in both competitions this season.

Next Gen

A heavy night in Lecco

Next Gen are in the Serie C relegation places; the Under 20s drew the derby.

Juventus Next Gen lost 4-0 at Lecco on Sunday, November 8, in Serie C Group A (JuveFC). Massimo Brambilla’s side have won just one of their last 12 matches and sit 18th of 20 with 10 points from 11 games (Italian League). Before that came a 1-1 home draw with Pro Vercelli on November 2.

They host Treviso on Sunday, November 15 (17:30 in Italy, 8:30 a.m. Pacific), then visit Giana Erminio on November 21. Ferrero told shareholders the club wants a dedicated stadium near Turin for Next Gen and Juventus Women.

Under 20 and the youth sides

The Under 20s drew 0-0 with Torino in the Primavera 1 derby on November 1 (Juventus.com). In the youth ranks the week of October 26 brought a 3-2 Under 18 win over Como, a 2-2 Under 17 derby draw with Torino and a 2-0 Under 19 women’s win over Inter (club roundup).

Jeff Ekhator earned an Italy Under 21 call-up after coming off the bench in both of the first team’s games this week.

Loan watch

Loïs Openda (Lyon) suffered a hairline fracture of his right fibula at Lens in October; he had one goal in four Ligue 1 appearances before it (Gradient Sports). Jonathan David (Atlético Madrid) has three goals and three assists in 11 appearances (VAVEL). No fresh update this week on Fabio Miretti (Beşiktaş), Michele Di Gregorio (Bournemouth) or Vasilije Adžić (Sassuolo).

Juventus Women

Lyonnes deliver a hard lesson in Turin

Two league wins lifted the mood. Then the eight-time European champions arrived.

Juventus Women lost 6-0 at home to OL Lyonnes on Wednesday, November 11, in the fourth round of the Women’s Champions League league phase. Marie-Antoinette Katoto opened the scoring on 31 minutes and Wendie Renard added the sixth in stoppage time (ESPN).

Lyonnes came in on seven points from three games, one ahead of their Italian hosts (UEFA). Isaac Guerrero’s side now have six points from four (two wins, two defeats) and were eighth of 18 in ESPN’s table after the round.

League form

The week had started well. Cecilia Salvai scored the winner in a 2-1 home win over Sassuolo on Saturday, November 7 (Juventus.com via Yahoo). That followed a 2-1 win at Fiorentina on November 2, captain Martina Rosucci’s 200th appearance, which she said mattered “after three defeats” (Juventus.com).

Salvai had warned that Lyonnes would be “a completely different affair”. Next up in Europe is OH Leuven in Biella on Wednesday, November 18, then Paris Saint-Germain away on December 16 (UEFA).
