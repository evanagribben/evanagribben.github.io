The Lead
Europe Reopens on a Map Nobody Recognises
The Champions League league phase began on Tuesday with the holders in disarray at home, a returning heavyweight in the Real Madrid dugout and a Manchester City side that has spent a decade being one thing learning to be another. Eighteen matches across three nights, and the shape of the season is already arguing with itself.
There is a version of this competition that reads exactly as you would expect. Real Madrid beat Inter. Manchester City won at Porto because Erling Haaland scored twice. Borussia Dortmund came from behind in front of the Yellow Wall. Read the six Tuesday scorelines cold and you would conclude that European football has spent the summer standing still.

It has done the opposite. The men in the dugouts have almost all changed. The champions of Europe are 14th in their own league. The best player in the Premier League for a decade is playing in Turkey. And the two clubs who contested last season's final have between them lost a manager, a talisman and, in one case, an entire idea of themselves. Matchday one was not a continuation. It was an audit.

Start at the Bernabéu, because that is where the noise is. José Mourinho returned to Real Madrid in June on a three-year deal, installed by a president who had just survived an election fought over a trophyless season, and his first European night back ended 2-1 against Inter. It should have been a coronation. Instead it was a survival exercise. Kylian Mbappé finished neatly on 14 minutes, Fede Valverde punished a calamitous piece of goalkeeping three minutes later, and then Madrid spent an hour missing chances while Thibaut Courtois made save after save. Carlos Augusto scored late. There were whistles at the final whistle of a home win.

That anxiety has a cause, and it is not the Champions League. Four days earlier Madrid lost 1-0 at Real Betis, beaten by a Troy Parrott goal and by Álvaro Valles saving a stoppage-time Mbappé penalty. Madrid demanded a retake for encroachment; the referee and VAR said no, correctly under the letter of the law, since Marc Bartra still had a foot planted outside the area. Mourinho's response was pure Mourinho. "If the VAR did not order the penalty retaken because they do not know the rulebook," he said, "they need to go home." Madrid are third in La Liga, three points off Barcelona, with Rodrygo out until 2027 and Ferland Mendy out until April.
Four matches without a win, seven points behind Monaco, and Paris Saint-Germain responded by extending the manager who won them back-to-back European Cups until 2030. On the same day they lost.
Which brings us to the genuine oddity of this September. Paris Saint-Germain are the double defending champions of Europe. They beat Arsenal on penalties in Budapest in May after a 1-1 draw, then beat Aston Villa 2-1 in the Super Cup in August. They are also, as of Sunday night, 14th in Ligue 1 with two points from three games, beaten at the Parc des Princes by Monaco and beaten before that by Lens in the Trophée des Champions.

Luis Enrique is not having it. Asked whether his players were physically short after a summer in which most of them went deep into a World Cup, he answered "No, no, no," pointed at the expected-goals numbers, and called the Monaco defeat "inexplicable". His club backed him on the spot: on Friday, hours before kick-off against Monaco, PSG announced contract extensions for Luis Enrique to 2030 and for João Neves, Willian Pacho, Lucas Beraldo, Senny Mayulu and Fabián Ruiz. The French press has settled on the word "préoccupant". Concerning, not yet a crisis. It is worth remembering they sold Bradley Barcola to Liverpool for a reported £106m and Gonçalo Ramos to Milan, and are integrating Ferran Torres and Maghnes Akliouche in their places.

Manchester City, meanwhile, look ominously well. Enzo Maresca took a job nobody has held since 2016 and has won his first three league games and now his first European one, 2-0 at the Estádio do Dragão. Haaland scored on 67 and in stoppage time to reach 59 goals in 59 Champions League appearances, four days after his winner against Coventry City became the 300th goal of his career. City spent something close to £320m rebuilding a midfield they had gutted, selling Rodri to Barcelona and buying Elliot Anderson, Ayyoub Bouaddi and, on deadline day, Enzo Fernández from Chelsea. "The squad is complete," Maresca said. On the evidence of eleven days, he is not wrong.

The romance came from further down the pots. AEK Athens won a Champions League match for the first time in 20 years, Răzvan Marin bending in a free kick to beat LASK. Real Betis, in their first Champions League game in 21 years, went to Lille, fell behind twice and won 3-2 with two Marc Bartra headers either side of half-time and a Parrott finish on the hour. Aston Villa, who won the Europa League in May and have not scored a Premier League goal all season, went to Club Brugge and won 3-2 through John McGinn and Nicolas Jackson. That is the competition doing what the 36-team format promised: giving good clubs somewhere to be extraordinary.

Borussia Dortmund's 3-2 over Villarreal was the strangest of the six: five goals in the second half, an own goal by Renato Veiga, two from Serhou Guirassy including a penalty he won himself, then a Guirassy own goal in the fourth minute of stoppage time. It was also overshadowed. Konstantinos Karetsas, the 18-year-old signed from Genk in the summer, collapsed with circulatory problems in the 27th minute and was taken to hospital. The club said afterwards that he "is doing well under the circumstances", which was the only line from the night that mattered.

The rest of matchday one lands across Wednesday and Thursday, and it is the better half of the card. Liverpool host Atlético Madrid at Anfield, which pitches Andoni Iraola's high-line, high-pressing Liverpool against the one manager in Europe who has built a twelve-year career out of making that style look naive. Iraola has inherited a club that finished fifth, sacked Arne Slot, lost Mohamed Salah on a free transfer and replaced him with £161m of Barcola and Jérémy Jacquet. Diego Simeone arrives with Julián Álvarez, Ademola Lookman, Cristian Romero and Jonathan David, and having just been beaten 3-0 at Athletic Club. Anfield against Atlético has never once been dull.

Elsewhere on Wednesday, Barcelona open at a Camp Nou now licensed for 62,652 and still being rebuilt around them, having scored 17 goals in four league games. Napoli, two straight league defeats in and missing Scott McTominay, host a visitor Massimiliano Allegri has publicly told his players is worth chasing 12 points in this phase for. And PSG, mercifully, get Slovan Bratislava. Thursday is the competition's exclusive night: Bayern Munich host Bodø/Glimt, Manchester United are back in the Champions League against Sabah, Roma return after seven years away at Fenerbahçe, and Como play the first Champions League match in their history at the Stadio Giuseppe Sinigaglia, a 13,602-seat ground on the lake that UEFA formally cleared only five days ago.

Eight games each, one table of 36, and a final in Madrid on June 5. This edition closed before Wednesday's results were confirmed, so treat the card below as the state of play at press time. The early reading is that the favourites are Manchester City and Barcelona, that Real Madrid are a better team than their nerves suggest, and that the holders have a great deal of work to do before anyone should write them off. Champions of Europe rarely stay 14th for long.

The Benches
The Summer the Dugouts All Emptied at Once
Guardiola stepped away. Klopp came back, for a country. Mourinho went home. Zidane took France. If you stopped watching in May, almost nothing you knew about who stands on the touchline is still true.
The scale of it only registers when you list it. Pep Guardiola resigned at Manchester City on May 22 after a decade, and did not take another club: he is now a global ambassador for City Football Group. Arne Slot was sacked by Liverpool eight days later, a year after winning the league, having taken 20 defeats in all competitions. Eddie Howe stepped down at Newcastle United at the end of July after nearly five years and a 12th-place finish. Neither Slot nor Howe has taken a new job.

The vacancies cascaded. Enzo Maresca left Chelsea in January and, after a spell out, took the City job in June. Xabi Alonso, who left Real Madrid by mutual consent in January, is now at Stamford Bridge on a four-year deal, having succeeded a caretaker regime that followed Liam Rosenior's April dismissal. Andoni Iraola went from Bournemouth to Liverpool; Marco Rose replaced him. Oliver Glasner left Crystal Palace for Nottingham Forest, and Pierre Sage came the other way from France. Álvaro Arbeloa, who managed Real Madrid for part of last season, is at Fulham. Marco Silva left Fulham for Benfica, where he replaced Mourinho.

Mourinho, of course, went to the Bernabéu, appointed on June 11 and confirmed as one of Florentino Pérez's explicit election promises. Pérez was re-elected on June 7 with 64.79 per cent against the challenger Enrique Riquelme, whose ticket had promised Jürgen Klopp as coach and Raúl as sporting director. Klopp went somewhere else entirely: on July 24 he was appointed manager of Germany, taking over from Julian Nagelsmann on a contract to 2030 after Germany went out of their own generation's World Cup in the round of 32, beaten on penalties by Paraguay. His Red Bull contract contained an exit clause written for precisely this job. He has not yet taken charge of a competitive game. His first is on September 27.

Italy changed almost as completely. Rúben Amorim is at Milan, Massimiliano Allegri at Napoli, Luciano Spalletti at Juventus, Gian Piero Gasperini at Roma, Maurizio Sarri at Atalanta, Gennaro Gattuso at Lazio, Cristian Chivu at Inter. Nine of the twenty Serie A clubs changed coach before a ball was kicked, and Fiorentina have already changed again. In France, Zinedine Zidane has succeeded Didier Deschamps as national coach, Filipe Luís has taken Monaco to the top of Ligue 1, and Davide Ancelotti has his first job in charge of a major club at Lille.

What ties it together is the World Cup. A tournament that finished on July 19 compressed every pre-season in Europe, delayed the Premier League by a week and the Bundesliga by another, and gave clubs a natural moment to end things. The cost is being paid now, in September, by squads that had four weeks instead of eight and by managers being asked to install new ideas in players who have not stopped running since February. Read the tables in that light and the surprises look less like accidents.

Manchester City Spent £320m Rebuilding a Midfield They Had Just Sold
Rodri out, Enzo Fernández in, and a British record equalled on deadline day. Status: all official.
The single largest reallocation of capital in this window happened in one club's engine room. Manchester City sold Rodri to Barcelona for £65m on August 18, having already lost Bernardo Silva to Real Madrid on a free and Tijjani Reijnders to Al Qadsiah for £52m. Then they rebuilt from scratch.

Elliot Anderson arrived from Nottingham Forest for £116m. Ayyoub Bouaddi, 19, came from Lille for a reported £81.4m on August 26. And on September 1, with the window closing, City equalled the British transfer record by paying Chelsea £125m for Enzo Fernández. They also took Iliman Ndiaye from Everton for £65m, with Jack Grealish going the other way on loan as part of the arrangement.

"The squad is complete," Enzo Maresca said afterwards, which is the sort of line a manager only gets to use once. Three league wins from three and a 2-0 in Porto suggest the money has landed. The open question is the one nobody at the club will discuss on the record: the 130 charges the Premier League brought in 2023 remain, on every reachable public record, unresolved. We could not confirm any hearing outcome, and we are not going to imply one.
 
Newcastle Sold Their Spine and Still Ended Up Net Spenders
Gordon, Tonali and Guimarães all went. £265.3m came back in. The club insists none of it was panic.
On paper it reads like a distress sale. Newcastle United sold Anthony Gordon to Barcelona for £69.3m on July 1, Sandro Tonali to Tottenham for £92.5m five days later, and Bruno Guimarães for £75m on August 8. Their manager of nearly five years, Eddie Howe, had resigned at the end of July. Three of their four best players and the man who built the side, gone inside six weeks.

The arithmetic argues otherwise. Newcastle's outgoings totalled £236.8m and their incomings £265.3m. Nico González arrived from Manchester City for £47m, Matias Fernandez-Pardo from Lille for £51m on deadline day, Bazoumana Touré from Hoffenheim for £42.8m, Aladji Bamba from Monaco for £34m, Amar Dedić from Benfica for £30.7m and Sean Steur from Ajax for £23m. That is not a club raising cash. That is a club changing its mind about what it wants to be.

Sporting director Ross Wilson has said the summer was "not chaotic" and that the club had prepared for Howe's exit. Matthias Jaissle, appointed on August 5, has taken five points from three games: a 2-2 with Liverpool, a 2-0 win at Tottenham and a 2-2 at home to Bournemouth. Nobody we could reach has linked the sales to profit and sustainability rules, and we are not going to assert it. One correction worth making, because it circulates: Alexander Isak's £125m move to Liverpool happened in September 2025. He is not part of this story.
 
Liverpool Let Mohamed Salah Walk, and Sold a Third of Themselves
A free transfer to Trabzonspor, a £106m replacement from Paris, and a new ownership consortium at Anfield.
Mohamed Salah left Liverpool on May 24 with his contract expired, after announcing in March that he would go. He pursued Beşiktaş first; those talks collapsed over shirt revenue and agent commission. On August 6 he signed a two-year deal at Trabzonspor, wearing number 10 after being welcomed in 61, the city's area code. He scored twice in his second start. Nine years, and it ended on a technicality of contract law.

Liverpool replaced him at the top of the market. Bradley Barcola arrived from Paris Saint-Germain on August 31 for a reported £106m plus £17m in add-ons on a five-year deal, joining Jérémy Jacquet from Rennes at £55m and Víctor Muñoz from Osasuna at £34.5m. Ibrahima Konaté left for Real Madrid on a free, Curtis Jones to Inter for £25.7m and Andy Robertson to Tottenham. Ronald Araújo came in on loan from Barcelona with a €55m option.

The bigger change was upstairs. On August 14, Fenway Sports Group agreed to sell a 38 per cent stake to 1892 Holdings, a consortium led by Amit Bhatia and reported to include Jeff Bezos, Eduardo Saverin and the Mittal family, with FSG retaining 62 per cent. Bhatia, who becomes vice-chairman subject to regulatory approval, called being a Liverpool investor "the stuff of dreams". No valuation has been published, and Liverpool's own channels have carried nothing on the deal, so treat the percentage and the named investors as reported rather than club-confirmed.

One live thread: Alexis Mac Allister said on Monday that he is "very sad" not to have been offered a new contract. For a club that has just watched a generational forward leave for nothing, that is a sentence with some history behind it.
 
Barcelona Bought a Premier League Midfield and Registered All of It
Rodri, Gordon, Adeyemi and a deadline-day Gabriel Jesus, with no registration scandal for the first time in three years.
The most consequential detail of Barcelona's summer is administrative. For the first time since 2023 they got to the first matchday with every signing registered and nobody stranded outside the squad. Rodri (£65m), Anthony Gordon (£69.3m) and Karim Adeyemi (€22m from Borussia Dortmund) were all lodged with La Liga around August 21, and Gabriel Jesus, signed for roughly €10m plus €4m on deadline day, was registered immediately and played the same week.

The room was created by selling. Ferran Torres went to Paris Saint-Germain for €48.5m, Robert Lewandowski left on a free for Chicago Fire, Ronald Araújo and Marc-André ter Stegen went out on loan to Liverpool and Ajax, and Ansu Fati joined Monaco for €11m. Barcelona are back on La Liga's 1:1 spending rule, though the structural squeeze has not gone away.

Gabriel Jesus was, on the record, Plan B. Matteo Moretto, speaking on Radio Marca on August 21, reported that Barcelona had concluded a move for Julián Álvarez was "prácticamente imposible" and would instead buy a striker of similar profile at much lower cost. Moretto also confirmed the club had held meetings over Lautaro Martínez and found Inter's demands unrealistic. Both threads are dead for now. Neither is buried.
 
Italy's Window: €149m at Fiorentina, and the Coach Was Gone by September 6
Serie A's biggest spenders have no points. Atalanta made a €97m trading profit. Como paid €60m for a player Real Madrid had just bought back for €9m.
Fiorentina spent about €149.2m this summer, more than any club in Serie A. They signed Inao Oulai for €26m, Arthur Atta for €25m, Mateo Pellegrino for €22.5m, Beto for €18m and took deadline-day loans for Willy Gnonto and Pedro Gonçalves. Three games later they had no points, one goal and nine conceded, their worst start since 1935-36, and Fabio Grosso had been sacked.

The manner of it was ugly. According to Corriere dello Sport, the decision followed a late-night phone call in which Grosso told sporting director Fabio Paratici he had strong doubts about the squad the club had assembled, and refused to resign. He was dismissed shortly before 23:30 on Sunday. Paolo Vanoli, who managed the club until June, was reappointed on Monday on a reported two-year deal at €1.2m net. La Nazione reports the separation is not amicable and could reach a courtroom.

The counter-example is Atalanta, who sold Marco Palestra and Honest Ahanor to Chelsea and banked a net trading profit of roughly €97m. And the strangest deal in Europe belongs to Como: Real Madrid exercised a €9m buy-back on Nico Paz, then sold him straight back to Como for €60m payable over four years, retaining a large sell-on and an €80m buy-back option next summer. Paz has since scored in a 4-1 win at Genoa and will play in Como's first ever Champions League match on Thursday.

Elsewhere: Milan spent €165.05m gross under Rúben Amorim, headlined by Gonçalo Ramos at €74m, and recouped €104.25m including Rafael Leão to Galatasaray for €38m. Napoli bought Rasmus Højlund from Manchester United for €44m. Lazio, constrained by Italy's liquidity index, made every meaningful arrival a loan and funded it by selling Mario Gila to Milan for €30m. They are third with three wins from three.
 
MLS Broke Its Own Records, in Both Directions
A $12.25m intraleague trade, a $23m teenage defender sold to Hull, and a deadline moved to September to match Europe.
The MLS secondary window closed at midnight on September 2, a new date chosen specifically to align with the rest of the world, and it closed on the biggest cash-for-player trade in league history. St. Louis CITY paid the Colorado Rapids up to $12.25m for Rafael Navarro, the Brazilian designated player who had scored 44 goals in 114 appearances in Denver. Colorado retained a sell-on. Navarro came off the bench three days later in a 3-1 win at Vancouver.

Colorado had already set the outbound record. On August 12 they sold Lucas Herrington, an 18-year-old Australian centre-back they had bought from Brisbane Roar for under $1m a year earlier, to Hull City for up to $23m with $17m guaranteed: the largest fee ever received for a defender by an MLS club. Six days later Real Salt Lake sold Zavier Gozo, 19, to Crystal Palace for a reported $15m, a club record. Colorado's total outgoing business for the year is around $35m.

Coming the other way, the league added a striking amount of European pedigree on free transfers: Robert Lewandowski to Chicago Fire, Casemiro to Inter Miami, Antoine Griezmann to Orlando City, Allan Saint-Maximin to Charlotte, Sergi Roberto to the LA Galaxy. Breel Embolo cost Atlanta United $18m from Rennes and scored a penalty on debut. Sports Illustrated rated Dejan Joveljić, $6m from Sporting Kansas City to Seattle, the best signing of the entire window. Seven club-record signings were made across the league in 2026.

Richarlison and Trabzonspor. The free-agent list. Because the window has shut, the only players clubs can still sign are those without one, and the roster is remarkable. What Rome wants in January. Italian analysis of the closed window identifies Roma's missing piece as a specialist attacking winger, a position Gasperini has requested and the club has not filled for more than two years, made more urgent by Paulo Dybala's injury record. Atalanta are expected to intervene on the same flank. Inter need a right-back after selling Denzel Dumfries. Tier: informed analysis of stated needs, not links to named players.
 
Around the Leagues
Premier League
Three matchweeks in. A promoted side has conceded nothing, two established clubs have scored nothing, and the derby is on Sunday.
Manchester City lead on a perfect nine points after Erling Haaland's 26th-minute header beat Coventry City, the 300th goal of his career, in front of 60,765. Behind them the most arresting name in the table is Hull City, third on seven points, back in the division after nine years and yet to concede a goal. Sergej Jakirović's side beat Manchester United 2-0 on the opening weekend, won 1-0 at Coventry City and drew 0-0 at home with Aston Villa. Nobody in the Premier League has a better defensive record, and only two clubs in the whole division are unbeaten.

At the other end sit the two most alarming stories of the early season, and they are alarming for opposite reasons. Tottenham spent £92.5m on Sandro Tonali and £52m on Jan Paul van Hecke, took Andy Robertson and Marcos Senesi on frees, and have one point and no goals from three games: beaten 3-0 at Brentford, 2-0 at home by Newcastle, then a 0-0 at Nottingham Forest. Roberto De Zerbi has publicly questioned why underperforming players remain in his squad, left Richarlison out of his registered list and, per Paul Merson, criticised the medical staff as well as his forwards. Aston Villa, meanwhile, also have one point and no league goals, having sold Morgan Rogers for £117m and Emiliano Martínez and Youri Tielemans besides. Then they went to Bruges on Tuesday and scored three.

Matchweek three was, above everything, a referendum on VAR. Neco Williams had a goal disallowed for handball in Forest's draw with Tottenham, with subsequent analysis questioning the call. Matty Cash had a penalty appeal turned down at Hull. Sky's Ref Watch spent Monday asking why a challenge on Cody Gakpo at Ipswich was not a spot kick. Four contested decisions in ten matches, in a season whose only announced officiating change is that gambling firms may no longer appear on the front of shirts. Elsewhere, Alexander Isak scored twice inside nine minutes to give Andoni Iraola his first league win at Liverpool; Michael Carrick's Manchester United were pegged back at 90+6 by Ainsley Maitland-Niles at Everton; and Tyrick Mitchell scored twice as Crystal Palace won 3-2 at Fulham, who along with Frank Lampard's Coventry City have lost all three. On Sunday, United host City at Old Trafford.

Off the pitch, the Independent Football Regulator has begun to bite, though not yet at this level. Operational since September 2025 under the Football Governance Act, it issued its first formal reprimand in August, to Morecambe of the National League North, for failing to answer information requests. The provisional licence application window opens in November and closes in February, and all 116 regulated clubs must hold at least a provisional licence to compete in 2027-28. That is the autumn's quiet deadline.
 
La Liga
Barcelona have won four out of four and scored seventeen. Alavés are second and have never started better. Valencia are bottom and have scored one.
Hansi Flick's Barcelona have a goal difference of plus fifteen after four games, having won 5-0 at Elche and 5-0 at Valencia, beaten Athletic Club 2-0 and Rayo Vallecano 5-2. Raphinha leads the Pichichi with six. Lamine Yamal, who scored twice at Mestalla, has been added to the club's five-man captaincy group at 19, behind Raphinha, Pedri, Eric García and Frenkie de Jong, a restructure forced by the departures of Marc-André ter Stegen and Ronald Araújo.

The story of the weekend was in Vitoria. Deportivo Alavés beat Osasuna 5-2 with a Lucas Boyé hat-trick, his first as a professional, to reach ten points from four games: the best start in the club's La Liga history, beating marks set in 1930 and 2001. Quique Sánchez Flores was named coach of the month for August. Behind them, RC Deportivo are unbeaten and fifth on their return, with Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang, signed from Marseille, scoring the winner at Villarreal.

Refereeing dominated the round. Beyond the Mbappé penalty row at La Cartuja, Espanyol had a legitimate-looking Omar El Hilali goal ruled out against Sevilla for positional offside interference, and the released VAR audio caught the referee saying he thought a defender "may not see the ball" before disallowing it anyway. That in a season in which La Liga has become the first national league to use connected-ball technology in every match, with fourteen sensor-equipped balls per game and a proprietary data layer it licenses to broadcasters. At the bottom, Valencia have not won a league game since the final matchday of last season, and Carlos Corberán has publicly refused to resign. Sevilla, operating on the lowest salary limit in the division at €22.1m, completed 27 operations in one window.
 
Bundesliga
Two matchdays old, and a club playing its first ever season in the top flight is above Bayern Munich.
The German season started a week later than the rest of Europe, on August 28, pushed back by the World Cup. After two rounds the table is led by FC Augsburg on six points and a goal difference of plus six, having beaten Schalke 04 3-0 and then won 4-1 at Eintracht Frankfurt. Level with them are SC Freiburg, Borussia Dortmund and, most improbably, SV Elversberg, the 59th club in Bundesliga history, who beat Bayer Leverkusen 3-2 on their debut and then won 4-3 at Borussia Mönchengladbach from behind. They host Bayern Munich on Sunday.

Bayern are sixth. Vincent Kompany's side won the Supercup 2-1 at Dortmund, thrashed Stuttgart 5-1, then were held 0-0 at the Veltins-Arena by promoted Schalke, for whom Robin Gosens, a boyhood fan signed from Fiorentina, was outstanding. "I think we have all gone to the limit today, if not even over it," he said. Harry Kane is on the Ballon d'Or shortlist alongside teammates Michael Olise, Luis Díaz and Dayot Upamecano, after a 2025-26 in which he scored 36 Bundesliga goals, 14 in the Champions League and 73 in all competitions, the second-best single season in European history.

Dortmund are the form side. Niko Kovač has taken 107 points from his first 50 Bundesliga games, a rate of 2.14 per game and a club record ahead of Favre, Tuchel, Klopp and Hitzfeld. "The stats are what they are," he said. "When I'm not here anymore, someone else will do better." At the bottom, Hamburger SV have lost twice, scored nothing and conceded seven, including 0-5 at home to Mainz. All eighteen Bundesliga clubs came through the DFB-Pokal first round; the shocks were beneath them, with fifth-tier SSV Jeddeloh II beating Heidenheim 5-2 and Rot-Weiss Essen knocking out St. Pauli.
 
Serie A
Roma, Inter and Lazio have all won three from three. Matchday three was a festival of comebacks, and a president has been banned from his own dressing room.
Gian Piero Gasperini's Roma top the table on goal difference: three wins, ten scored, one conceded. Donyell Malen, a €25m signing from Aston Villa, leads the scoring charts with five, including a hat-trick against Fiorentina. Gasperini has compared him to Higuaín. On Saturday Roma trailed Atalanta until the 90th minute and won 2-1 through Mario Hermoso and Matías Soulé, with Atalanta's Gianluca Gaetano sent off for protesting after the final whistle.

Italian media called the round "un festival di rimonte", and the numbers back it. Inter were 2-0 down at home to Napoli inside 53 minutes and won 3-2, Lautaro Martínez scoring in the 56th and the 91st either side of a Marcus Thuram equaliser. Gennaro Gattuso's Lazio came from behind at Udinese, Albert Guðmundsson winning it on his debut. Juventus drew 1-1 with Milan through a 92nd-minute Federico Gatti header, in a game whose only VAR controversy was contact by Koni De Winter on Randal Kolo Muani in the build-up to Milan's goal, which referee Maurizio Mariani and his VAR judged a normal challenge.

The Giudice Sportivo has been busy. Bologna manager Domenico Tedesco was banned for two matches and fined €5,000 for abusing officials during a 2-2 with Sassuolo, and president Joey Saputo was inhibited until September 30, barred from dressing rooms and pitch areas, for offensive language on the pitch and in the tunnel. Bologna are appealing. At the foot, Fiorentina and Genoa have no points: Daniele De Rossi's Genoa have lost their opening three for the first time since 1957-58. Three games in, Como are fourth under Cesc Fàbregas and about to host RB Leipzig in a Champions League tie at a 13,602-capacity ground on Lake Como.
 
Ligue 1
Monaco have won all three. Paris FC are second. The European champions are fourteenth, and Marseille conceded three at home to a club playing its second top-flight season.
Filipe Luís took over at Monaco on July 6 and has won every game, most recently 2-1 at the Parc des Princes, where Flávio Nazinho and Stanis Idumbo overturned a Marquinhos opener and goalkeeper Lukas Hrádecký made nine saves. Monaco are seven points clear of Paris Saint-Germain, who have drawn at Rennes and Lille after leading in both, lost at home to Monaco, and lost the Trophée des Champions 1-0 to Lens. The French press has not reached for the word crisis, and neither should we: PSG extended Luis Enrique to 2030 on the morning of the Monaco defeat.

Marseille's Sunday was worse. Paris FC, in their second season up under Liam Rosenior, won 3-2 at the Vélodrome with a Lassine Sinayoko hat-trick, the first goal arriving after 23 seconds. Bruno Génésio, appointed in July, was blunt: "On s'est fait contrer comme une équipe de gamins." We got countered like a team of kids. Sinayoko, an €8m signing, is now joint top scorer in France on four.

The most consequential French story is financial. Lyon's executives gave a full accounting last week: their owner went bankrupt in June, the paperwork on Michele Kang's takeover was signed on the very day of their DNCG licensing hearing, and general director Michael Gerlinger described the timing as "très très juste". Lyon sold €70m of players, cut losses by €200m, are still not profitable, and face at least three years of restructuring. They are third. Auxerre, under Will Still, are bottom with no points and eleven conceded.
 
Major League Soccer
Nashville are chasing a points record, Messi is chasing a third straight MVP, and the league has one season left in the calendar it has always used.
Nashville SC lead the Supporters' Shield race by ten clear points, having taken 53 from 23 games under B.J. Callaghan, with Sam Surridge, Hany Mukhtar and Cristian Espinoza contributing 53 goals and assists between them across all competitions. At 2.3 points per game they are on course to threaten Inter Miami's single-season record of 74 set in 2024. Vancouver lead the West on 43 but lost 3-1 at home to St. Louis CITY on Saturday, ending a long unbeaten run.

Lionel Messi announced his retirement from international football on August 31, at 39, in an Instagram post that followed the World Cup final defeat to Spain and the death of his father in August. "There's not much time left, and chapters are coming to a close," he wrote. "This is one that hurts me deeply." His first match after it, a 2-2 with Atlanta United in a game delayed by tropical storms, brought two assists and took him to 30 goal contributions for the season, making him the first player in MLS history to reach 30 in three separate campaigns. He is contracted to Inter Miami through 2028 and tied with Petar Musa on 18 goals in the Golden Boot race.

Matchday 24 produced 47 goals in 14 matches, two hat-tricks and some genuine oddities. Vincent Janssen scored the first hat-trick in Portland's MLS history, in the club's 522nd league game, in a 5-4 win over Minnesota. Josh Sargent scored his first professional hat-trick in a 4-4 between Toronto and Chicago. Cavan Sullivan, 16, scored again for Philadelphia, a fifth consecutive game with a goal contribution, and celebrated in the style of LeBron James. Five clubs are still being run by interim head coaches with eleven games left.

Two structural notes. The Leagues Cup final on Sunday in Houston went to Toluca, 2-0 over Monterrey, the first non-MLS winner since 2021, after all four MLS quarter-finalists lost to Liga MX opposition. Toluca also won the Concacaf Champions Cup in May, completing a regional double in a year when the World Cup was played on American soil. And this is the final full season MLS will play in the spring-to-autumn format: the Board of Governors has approved a move to a summer-to-spring calendar from 2027-28, bridged by a 14-game sprint season from February to May 2027.

In Brief
BALLON D'OR. Six Bundesliga players are on the 2026 shortlist, including Harry Kane, Michael Olise, Luis Díaz and Dayot Upamecano. Paris Saint-Germain have ten nominees, the most of any club, but Désiré Doué and Bradley Barcola were both left out, France Football citing their "interchangeable" status and inconsistency across a season of good spells and bench time.
VINÍCIUS RENEWED. Real Madrid confirmed on August 6 that Vinícius Júnior has extended to June 2032, ending a long and public contract standoff. He is now vice-captain, with 375 appearances and 128 goals for the club.
NEGREIRA. The judge in the Barcelona payments case has rejected Real Madrid's request for a further six-month extension of the investigation. Barcelona paid between €7.3m and €8.4m to a former referees' committee vice-president between 2001 and 2018. Spanish reporting projects the instruction closing at the end of 2026 and a trial in autumn 2027, though that is a projection rather than a court order.
WSL EXPANDS. The Women's Super League has grown from 12 clubs to 14 and began on September 4. Charlton Athletic are back in the top flight for the first time in 18 years via the play-off, with Birmingham City and Crystal Palace promoted. Alexia Putellas made her debut for London City Lionesses, who beat Manchester United 2-1.
SINIGAGLIA CLEARED. UEFA granted formal approval on September 4 for Como to host Champions League football at the 13,602-capacity Stadio Giuseppe Sinigaglia, after the club built a new stand and upgraded safety infrastructure. President Mirwan Suwarso has given up the directors' box seats to older supporters for the club's European debut.
INTER IN PROFIT. Inter posted a €15m net profit for 2025-26, a second consecutive profitable year, on revenue of €505m, down from €567m after an early Champions League exit. Separately, Brookfield completed its acquisition of Oaktree on August 3; Oaktree retains control of the club and nothing changes operationally.
CAMP NOU, SLOWLY. Barcelona are playing at a licensed capacity of 62,652 and expect 72,000 during the autumn, 82,000 to 85,000 by early 2027 and a final 104,600 once the roof is complete. The schedule slipped after a construction worker died on site on July 24 and work was halted.
LA LIGA TV. Spain's domestic rights for 2027 to 2032 have gone to DAZN and Movistar Plus+ for €6.135bn, a nine per cent increase on the current cycle, with five matches per round each.
OPEN CUP. The Lamar Hunt U.S. Open Cup semi-finals are on September 16, with Columbus Crew, Houston Dynamo, Chicago Fire and Orlando City left. The final is on October 21. Holders Nashville SC did not enter because of Concacaf commitments.
