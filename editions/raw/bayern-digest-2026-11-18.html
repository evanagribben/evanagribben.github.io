The Lead

Kompany comes home: Bayern's coach returns for Köln, and the unbeaten interregnum ends

Twenty-eight days after collapsing at the edge of a reserve-team session, Vincent Kompany is expected back on the bench on Friday. He inherits a team that never lost without him, top of the league on goal difference alone, and a squad thinner than at any point this season.

The scene at Säbener Straße on Monday morning was ordinary in every respect but one. Vincent Kompany ran the session, as he has done for more than two years, but with his left arm strapped in a sling. It was the first training he had led since October 23, when he collapsed while watching the reserves and was taken to hospital (dpa via Tagesspiegel).

The diagnosis was meningeal irritation, a Hirnhautreizung, and the fall dislocated his shoulder (ZDF). He spent six days in hospital before being sent home to rest (dpa). His last match on the touchline had been the 2-2 against Arsenal on October 21, a night he coached while already reported unwell.

The next step is now mapped out for Bayern. Sport1 reports that Kompany will take the mandatory matchday press conference on Thursday, his first since the collapse (Sport1/SID), and dpa reports he returns to the bench for Friday's visit of 1. FC Köln (dpa). Hoeneß had predicted as much on November 8: after the international break, he said, Kompany would be back on the bench (dpa via Volksstimme).

“He is under total stress. He works twelve hours a day, six days a week. It was simply too much.”

Uli Hoeneß on BR's Sonntags-Stammtisch (translated)

What Danks built in his absence

The striking thing about the past four weeks is how little Bayern's results moved. Assistant Aaron Danks took five games and won four of them, drawing the other 2-2 with Dortmund. The run included Bayern's first-ever away win at Atlético Madrid, a 2-0 in which Lennart Karl chipped Jan Oblak and Konrad Laimer was named UEFA's player of the match (FC Bayern).

The last of the five, a 2-1 at Mainz on November 7, showed both sides of the side Kompany left behind. Bayern had 68.7 percent of the ball and 26 shots to Mainz's 10 (FC Bayern match stats), and an expected-goals tally of 3.25 to 0.82 (Bundesliga.com). Yet the winning margin was one goal, and Phillip Tietz's stoppage-time header made the finish nervier than it should have been.

What he comes back to

The table is the first thing on his desk. Bayern have 21 points from nine games, unbeaten at six wins and three draws, but so are Dortmund, and the champions lead them only on goal difference, +20 to +9 (Bundesliga table). Across all competitions Bayern are unbeaten in 16 matches this season (ZDF).

The second thing is the treatment room. Manuel Neuer has a partial tendon tear in his right foot, suffered in his record-breaking 550th Bundesliga game at Mainz, and is out for weeks (dpa). Alphonso Davies came home from Canada with a muscle-fibre tear in his left hamstring (dpa via Volksstimme). Serge Gnabry and Ismael Saibari were both hurt against Dortmund on October 31; Saibari is not expected back until 2027.

There is better news further up the pitch. Jamal Musiala and Luis Díaz returned to team training on Monday, (dpa), and Sky reports Bayern hope to have Musiala back in the squad against Köln (Sky Sport). Jonas Urbig, who finished the Mainz game in goal, is in Bundesliga.com's predicted line-up (Bundesliga.com).

The Köln problem: patience against a back five

Köln arrive 15th, with nine points and four league games without a win, and they have taken one point from four away trips (Hessenschau results). Bundesliga.com expects René Wagner to set up with three centre-backs and wing-backs, which on Friday will mean a deep, crowded box for Bayern to break down (predicted line-ups).

That is exactly the test Mainz half-passed. The key battles sit on the flanks: Michael Olise against Köln's left-sided wing-back, and, at the other end, whichever full-back replaces Davies against Said El Mala on the break. With Davies out, Josip Stanišić or Nathaniel Brown are the likeliest options on the left, a selection Kompany will make for the first time in a month.

The bigger selection question is load. Bayern play four times in twelve days: Köln on Friday, Lille in the Champions League next Wednesday, Hamburg away on November 28 and Hertha in the DFB-Pokal on December 1 (FC Bayern fixtures). Seven Bayern players spent the break with Germany alone, and Harry Kane played two Nations League games for England.

History favours Bayern heavily. They beat Köln 5-1 at the Allianz Arena last May, with a Kane hat-trick (FC Bayern), and won 3-1 in Cologne in January. A win on Friday guarantees nothing at the top, but a slip would give Dortmund the chance to go clear this weekend, on the very night Kompany comes home.

Second Feature

Hoeneß's “dilemma” over Musiala opens a rift, just as the player returns

The honorary president's public doubts about Jamal Musiala's future have drawn a rebuke from Oliver Kahn and reached the supervisory board. On the grass, Musiala is finally back in training.

The remarks came on Bavarian broadcaster BR's Sonntags-Stammtisch on November 8. Asked about Jamal Musiala, honorary president Uli Hoeneß said the club was “of course in a dilemma”, describing an illness “that is not easily curable, but is manageable to some extent through medication” (dpa via Volksstimme). Translations are the Digest's own.

Hoeneß then began a sentence, “but whether he will ever again have the performance he once had”, and left it unfinished. He added that Musiala continues to be paid, and asked his audience to imagine the reaction if FC Bayern were to question the contract now.

The background, as reported by dpa, is that Musiala collapsed twice in pre-season friendlies and later disclosed so-called absences. The club says his medication was changed and no further episodes have been reported (dpa). Separately, he has been out since September with a torn muscle fibre in his right thigh (ZDF).

The fallout

Former chief executive Oliver Kahn replied on LinkedIn on November 13 that the club “bought the risk along with” the player when it signed him to a long contract (t-online). Sport1 reports that some supervisory board members share Hoeneß's doubts, but that Bayern will not challenge the deal (Sport1).

The Abendzeitung reported that Musiala was on the agenda at Monday's supervisory board meeting (AZ). Sport Bild, as relayed by FCBinside, claims his contract guarantees six months' pay while injured, with insurance covering payments after six weeks; that detail is single-sourced and unconfirmed (FCBinside).

The irony is the timing. Musiala returned to team training on Monday after roughly eight weeks out, and Sky reports Bayern hope to include him against Köln (Sky Sport). Whatever the boardroom thinks, the first-team picture is simpler: a fit Musiala makes this squad better.

Transfer News

The goalkeeping succession is now a deadline, not a debate

With Manuel Neuer injured again and out of contract next summer, Bayern want his answer by Christmas and are already shortlisting an experienced No. 2.

Kicker reports that Bayern planned to sit down with Manuel Neuer during the international break and want a decision on his future before Christmas. The same timetable applies to Sven Ulreich, 38. Both contracts expire in June 2027 (kicker via FCBinside).

Neuer turned 40 in March and became Bayern's oldest Bundesliga player at Mainz, only to suffer a partial tendon tear in his right foot that day. Kicker says retirement next summer looks the likelier outcome and that the club leans that way, though nothing is decided. In that scenario Jonas Urbig, 23, becomes No. 1.

That leaves a vacancy behind him. Sky's Florian Plettenberg has Fulham's Bernd Leno, 34, on Bayern's list, complicated by Fulham's option to extend his deal to 2028 (Sky via FCBinside). Kicker adds Loris Karius and Oliver Baumann as names discussed (kicker via FCBinside). Certainty: shortlist only.

One young goalkeeper may go the other way. Sky reports extension talks with 17-year-old Leonard Prescott have stalled, with Chelsea among the interested clubs (Sky via FCBinside).

January: Eberl says no, the injury list says maybe

The sporting director sees “zero reason” to spend this winter. Sky's weekend round-up shows who Bayern are tracking anyway.

Max Eberl said in October that he saw “zero reason” for January spending (Bulinews), then on November 2 that the club is “watching the market” (FCBinside). Since then Davies has torn a hamstring, Neuer has torn a foot tendon, and Gnabry and Saibari are still out.

Sky's November 16 rundown is the best guide to the board (Sky Sport). The most concrete name is Club Brugge's Nicolò Tresoldi, 22, with 14 goals in 17 games, but Sky says Kane's new contract makes a January deal uncertain and a move elsewhere more likely. For the summer, Liverpool teenager Rio Ngumoha remains a live interest.

In defence, Bild's Christian Falk says Bayern want a new centre-back next summer and have Benfica's Tomás Araújo in view (FCBinside). That links to the future of Kim Min-jae, 30, whom TEAMtalk describes as a sale candidate with Turkish interest. Treat the Kim story as a rumour.

Kane signs on; Olise talks have not begun

The Ballon d'Or winner is tied down to 2029. The next big contract question is Michael Olise.

Harry Kane's extension to 2029 became official on November 5. He said he feels “at home” at the club, and president Herbert Hainer called him one of the greatest personalities in sport (Sport1).

Eberl has told DAZN there have been no talks yet with Michael Olise, whom he wants to keep beyond 2029, noting that “there is a third party involved” (Bulinews). English outlets keep linking Arsenal (TEAMtalk); there is no sign of an approach, and the Digest rates it a rumour.

Scouting the Next Opponent

1. FC Köln: a back five, a hot young forward and a poor away record

René Wagner's side have won twice all season and arrive under pressure. Their threat is on the counter.

Köln sit 15th with nine points from nine games (two wins, three draws, four defeats) and have scored ten goals. Their last five league games read: a 2-1 home win over Gladbach, then a 1-0 loss at Frankfurt, 0-0 with Schalke, 1-1 at Union and a 2-1 home defeat to Leverkusen (Hessenschau). ZDF describes the pressure on coach René Wagner as growing (ZDF).

Bundesliga.com predicts a 3-3-2-2: Marvin Schwäbe in goal, a back three of Krauß, Simpson-Pusey and Schmied, and Said El Mala and Marius Bülter up front (Bundesliga.com). El Mala scored against Leverkusen and also scored in last May's 5-1 in Munich; Schwäbe made several key saves in that Leverkusen game.

Where Bayern can hurt them

The numbers point one way. Köln have not won away from home this season (one draw, three defeats, three goals scored, eight conceded), and ten goals in nine games is among the lower totals in the division. A deep back five will invite Bayern's wide players to cross and cut inside, and Bayern's box occupation will matter more than possession.

The risk is the transition. With Davies out, Bayern's left side is less quick to recover, and El Mala is the kind of runner who punishes a high line. Striker Ragnar Ache sat out the Leverkusen game as a precaution; no other absences were reported by the time of writing.

The Squad

Urbig's moment, a left-back puzzle and a crowded front line

Kompany returns to a squad with three injured regulars and two key attackers just back. The decisions start in goal.

For the first time in his short Bayern career, Jonas Urbig has a run of games ahead of him. He started against Freiburg and Dortmund while Neuer nursed foot pain, then replaced Neuer at Mainz. With Neuer out for weeks, the 23-year-old is now the starter in all competitions.

Left-back is the open question. Davies's injury, his latest thigh problem, leaves Stanišić, who started there at Mainz, and Nathaniel Brown, who has played further forward this autumn. Davies himself told OneSoccer he had been afraid surgery would be needed (dpa); the club says it will not be.

Attack: Karl's opening

With Gnabry and Saibari out, Lennart Karl has been the beneficiary. The 18-year-old scored in both Atlético Madrid and Mainz, Bayern's last two games. Musiala's return creates competition for the same central attacking spaces, and Kompany must decide how quickly to restore a player who has not played for about eight weeks.

In midfield, Joshua Kimmich comes off a strong break with Germany, while Dayot Upamecano left France's camp on November 13 for personal reasons and returned to Munich (ran.de). There is no indication he will miss Friday.

The Campus

A 16-year-old's double and a reserve side unbeaten in nine

Bayern II keep climbing, the U19s lead their league by six points, and Campus graduates keep breaking into the first team.

FC Bayern II beat Bayreuth 3-1 on November 7, with 16-year-old Bastian Assomo scoring twice and David Santos Daiber adding a third in stoppage time (BFV). The club says the side is unbeaten in nine (FC Bayern Campus news). They are 10th in the Regionalliga Bayern with 24 points from 16 games, with games in hand on most rivals.

Last weekend's trip to Ansbach was called off and moved to December 12 (FC Bayern). Next up is Eltersdorf away on Saturday at 14:00 in Germany, 5 a.m. Pacific (DFB data centre). Guido Della Rovere is out for weeks with a thigh injury.

U19s and the first-team pathway

Leo Haas's U19s won 3-1 at Stuttgart in a top-of-the-table game and 1-0 at Ingolstadt, and are six points clear and already through to the next stage of the DFB youth league (FC Bayern). In the UEFA Youth League, Dante's side have four points from four games after a 4-1 defeat at Atlético Madrid (laola1).

The pathway is open. Tim Binder made his competitive debut and Santos Daiber, 19, scored his first senior goal in the 6-1 DFB-Pokal win over Magdeburg on October 28 (FC Bayern). Santos Daiber then played for Portugal's U20s this month, and Filip Pavić is in Croatia's squad for the U17 World Cup.

Bayern Women

Ten from ten: Bayern Women win at Wolfsburg and pull six clear

A 4-1 win in Wolfsburg extended an unbeaten league run to 52 games, days after a 2-1 Champions League win in Paris.

FC Bayern Women made the title race look one-sided on Saturday. Bernadette Amani headed in from a corner on 36 minutes and again on 82, Linda Dallmann scored in first-half stoppage time, and Janina Minge turned into her own net before Cecilie Fløe Nielsen's late consolation for Wolfsburg (DFB).

The DFB notes it was the 52nd league game in a row without defeat, a club record; the last loss was 2-0 at Wolfsburg in October 2024. The table now reads ten wins from ten and a six-point lead (Sport1 table). Before that, Bayern beat Werder Bremen 3-1 at home on November 6 (Weser-Kurier).

Paris, and the push for the top four

On November 10, José Barcala's side won 2-1 at Paris Saint-Germain: an own goal from a Pernille Harder shot and a Klara Bühl strike before half-time, then a PSG penalty (FC Bayern). Bayern have eight points from four league-phase games and are unbeaten, after draws with Manchester City and Real Madrid and a win at Benfica (UEFA).

Next up is bottom side Austria Wien on Thursday, then Hoffenheim at home in the league. Lena Oberdorf (knee), Vanessa Gilles (muscle) and Kassandra Potsi (knee knock) were missing before Wolfsburg, and Bayern are also through to the DFB-Pokal quarter-finals after Harder's penalty beat Leverkusen 1-0 (dpa).

Players Away

Kane makes history, Olise keeps scoring, Davies comes home hurt

Sixteen Bayern men were called up for the November window. Here is how each one fared.

England: a record for the captain

Harry Kane won his 126th cap against Croatia on November 12, becoming England's most-capped men's player ahead of Peter Shilton, and scored the 64th-minute penalty that won it 2-1 (Al Jazeera). Three days later he won and scored a penalty as ten-man England beat Spain 3-2 in Madrid, a rare defeat for the world champions (Inside World Football).

Germany: seven Bayern players, two wins

Jürgen Klopp's Germany won 3-1 in Serbia on November 13 and beat the Netherlands 5-0 in Berlin on November 16 to win their Nations League group (DFB). Joshua Kimmich captained the side in Belgrade, started both games and assisted the opener in Berlin; Jonathan Tah and Aleksandar Pavlović started both, and Pavlović's pass led to Florian Wirtz's goal in Belgrade (FC Bayern).

Nathaniel Brown started against the Netherlands; Tom Bischof came off the bench in both games; Lennart Karl got eight minutes in Berlin. Jonas Urbig did not play.

France, Croatia, Austria

Michael Olise scored after ten minutes in France's 3-1 defeat in Italy, his fifth goal in five Nations League games, then started the 2-1 win over Türkiye (FC Bayern). Dayot Upamecano played the Italy game, then left camp for personal reasons. Josip Stanišić played every minute of Croatia's 2-1 loss to England and 2-1 win over Czechia.

Konrad Laimer captained Austria to a 1-0 win over Ireland that sealed promotion to League A (Flashscore), then played most of a 1-1 draw with Israel, his saved shot leading to Saša Kalajdžić's equaliser (t-online).

Asia and North America

Hiroki Ito started at left-back as Japan beat Brazil 3-2 in Singapore (J.League) and came off the bench in a 2-1 loss to Paraguay. Kim Min-jae played the full 3-2 defeat to Saudi Arabia, which drew criticism of Korea's defence (JoongAng Daily), and started a 1-0 win in Qatar.

The bad news came from Guatemala. Alphonso Davies lasted six minutes of Canada's 3-2 win in the CONCACAF Nations League before a hamstring tear sent him back to Munich (Sky Sport).
