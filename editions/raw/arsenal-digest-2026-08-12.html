THE LEAD

Champions, in name only, until Cardiff

Arsenal arrive at the Principality Stadium on Sunday as Premier League champions for the first time in 22 years — and as a team that has shipped six goals in two pre-season defeats with its two best defenders in the treatment room. The Community Shield against Manchester City is a trinket. What it will reveal about Mikel Arteta's back four is anything but.

There is a particular kind of unease that settles over a champion in August, and Arsenal are living in it. Eighty-three days have passed since Mikel Arteta's side were confirmed as Premier League champions, ending a wait that had stretched to 22 years. Seventy-four days have passed since the club watched Paris Saint-Germain lift the European Cup in Budapest after a shootout in a final Arsenal did not lose in 120 minutes — Havertz inside six minutes, a Dembélé penalty on the hour, 4-3 on spot-kicks, and gone. And in the fortnight since the squad reassembled properly at London Colney, the reigning champions of England have lost two pre-season matches in a row and conceded six goals doing it.

On Sunday afternoon in Cardiff they face Manchester City in the FA Community Shield, a fixture displaced from Wembley — booked out for concerts — to the Principality Stadium for the first time since 2006. It kicks off at 3pm British time, 7am on the West Coast, with Sam Barrott refereeing. It is, by the standards of what this squad achieved last season and expects of itself this one, a nothing game. It is also the first competitive evidence anyone will get about whether the champions have a defence.

The problem is not conceptual, it is nominal. William Saliba is in what Arteta called "rest mode" — a back injury that curtailed his World Cup with France and which the manager described this week in terms that should worry anyone reading closely. "William is in rest mode at the moment for two weeks," Arteta said. "He had to do almost nothing, don't aggravate the injury. It's going to take some time to heal." Jurriën Timber, recovering from groin surgery in June, is "still weeks away" and will miss the start of the Premier League season. Louie Copley, the 18-year-old midfielder who had been one of the genuine stories of the summer, ruptured his anterior cruciate ligament ten minutes into the defeat in Dublin. That is a first-choice centre-back, a first-choice right-back and an emerging squad option, gone before a ball has been kicked in anger.

"William is in rest mode at the moment for two weeks. He had to do almost nothing, don't aggravate the injury. It's going to take some time to heal." — Mikel Arteta, on William Saliba

What is left is a back line assembled out of parts. Gabriel Magalhães is fit and will partner, most likely, Piero Hincapié — whose loan from Bayer Leverkusen was made permanent for £34.5m in June and who scored the consolation against Real Betis. Cristhian Mosquera and Ben White, the latter only lately back to full training after two injury-bitten seasons, are the right-back options, with the 18-year-old Theo Julienne — a midfielder by trade — pressed into service there through pre-season and, on the evidence of the Emirates Cup, entirely unfazed by it. Riccardo Calafiori covers the left. It is a functional group. It is not the group that won 26 league matches and conceded 27 goals across a title-winning campaign.

The two defeats have been instructive in different ways. Against Real Betis in Dublin on August 5, a Kepa Arrizabalaga error helped Betis to a 2-0 lead inside the opening exchanges and Arsenal lost 3-1, Rodrigo Riquelme and Nelson Deossa scoring before Pablo Fornals settled it. Arteta said his players were "fuming" afterwards and made clear he considered that a good sign. Four days later, in the Emirates Cup against Borussia Dortmund, they were 2-0 down inside half an hour again — Samuele Inacio after seven minutes, then the 18-year-old Konstantinos Karetsas punishing a Calafiori turnover — and lost 3-2, with Joane Gadou scoring from a badly defended corner. "We conceded some poor goals the other day, some poor goals today, in the way we competed," Arteta said. The shootout win afterwards was a courtesy.

Set against that, the attacking signs are good, and they are new. Christos Tzolis, the £34m arrival from Club Brugge, has been the most consistently threatening Arsenal player of pre-season: a goal on debut in the 4-1 win at Girona, two dangerous free-kicks and an assist for Ethan Nwaneri against Dortmund, and the foul won off Jobe Bellingham that Viktor Gyökeres converted from the spot. He has started pre-season on the left wing, which is not an accident and which has consequences we address below. Max Dowman, who is 16, has a goal and an assist and looks like a footballer rather than a project.

And then there is the midfield, which is now the most expensive Arsenal have ever assembled. Bruno Guimarães signed from Newcastle United on August 8 for £75m and will wear 39. Whether he starts in Cardiff is genuinely open — he has had barely a week on the training ground — but the idea of him alongside Declan Rice and Martín Zubimendi, with Martin Ødegaard and Eberechi Eze ahead, is the most obviously frightening thing about this Arsenal team. Arteta will have to choose two of three, every week, and that is a problem other managers would like to have.

City, for their part, arrive as FA Cup holders and as the side that finished seven points behind Arsenal last season with a goal difference two adrift. Pep Guardiola's teams have historically treated the Shield as a scrimmage and then won it anyway. The tactical question is narrow and specific: can an Arsenal back line without Saliba hold a high line against City's runners for ninety minutes, and if it cannot, does Arteta drop the line and concede the territory he has spent six years teaching this squad to take? On Sunday it costs nothing to find out. On August 21, against Coventry City under the Friday-night lights at the Emirates, it starts costing points.

The vacancy on the left that nobody has filled

Vinicius Junior re-signed at Madrid. Morgan Rogers went to Chelsea. Galatasaray have bid €40m for Gabriel Martinelli and been told no — because Arsenal cannot sell a left winger they have not yet replaced.

The single defining failure of Arsenal's summer, if it ends as it currently stands, will be the left wing. Andrea Berta spent the window chasing an elite left-sided forward and has so far come away with Tzolis — a very good signing at £34m, and not the signing the club set out to make.

The headline pursuit was Vinicius Júnior. Arsenal monitored his contract situation closely as he entered the final year of his deal at Real Madrid; Sky Sports News had reported for more than a week that he would stay, and on August 6 he signed a six-year extension through 2032. Those close to the player always expected as much, and there is a reasonable reading in which Arsenal's interest served chiefly to improve his terms. Morgan Rogers went to Chelsea for a reported £117m. Bradley Barcola is available in principle from Paris Saint-Germain but at a valuation reported at €150m, with Liverpool also in the conversation. Nico Williams has a release clause at Athletic Club of roughly £77m and, per TEAMtalk, Berta has held discussions to assess whether it is doable. The Independent has named Julián Álvarez and Iliman Ndiaye on the same shortlist. Nothing has moved to a bid.

Into that vacuum has walked Galatasaray, who submitted a €40m (£34m) offer for Gabriel Martinelli — first flagged as an exploratory approach by Ben Jacobs, then reported as a formal bid by The Sun. Arsenal rejected it on valuation, believing the 25-year-old is worth more in the current market, and the club has been unambiguous with anyone who asks: Martinelli does not leave until a replacement arrives. He is said to be open to the move. He scored once in 30 Premier League appearances last season, 11 goals and seven assists across all 52. He is also, right now, the only senior left-sided forward at the club with Premier League title medals and a functioning relationship with the way Arteta wants that flank to work.

The arithmetic is uncomfortable and it is also historic. Arsenal's record sale remains Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain's £35m move to Liverpool in August 2017 — nine years of market inflation and not one player sold above it. Galatasaray's £34m sits just under that line, which is part of why it was refused; reported valuations of Martinelli in the £45m-£50m range would break the record outright, and the Mirror framed Wednesday's developments in exactly those terms. Selling well is exactly what a well-run club does. Selling in the wrong order, with £144m already committed in fees this summer and no replacement secured, is how a champion arrives at September 2 light in its weakest area. The window closes at 11pm on Tuesday, September 1.

TRANSFER NEWS

£75m for Bruno: Arsenal buy a captain, and Newcastle lose one

Confirmed, August 8. Four-year deal, squad number 39, and a Brazilian who started all five of his country's World Cup matches. Newcastle did not want to sell.

Arsenal completed the signing of Bruno Guimarães from Newcastle United on August 8 for £75m, with around £7m of that figure due to Olympique Lyonnais under a sell-on clause. The 28-year-old has signed a four-year contract and will wear 39. It is the second-largest fee in Arsenal's history and the clearest statement of intent the club has made since winning the title.

Guimarães leaves Newcastle after four and a half years, 195 appearances, 31 goals and 32 assists, having captained the club to the 2025 League Cup — their first domestic trophy in seventy years. The Daily Mail reported this week that Newcastle did not want to sell and that a "very emotional" Guimarães asked to leave. He arrives in form: he started all five of Brazil's matches at this summer's World Cup and registered four assists.

Andrea Berta, the sporting director, framed it as a leadership signing as much as a footballing one: "Bruno is a player with a great mentality and quality, who will bring strong leadership to our squad," he said, noting the depth it creates alongside Rice and Zubimendi. Guimarães himself was blunter. "I need a challenge like this," he said. "I want to win trophies, I want to make history."

The tactical consequence is a genuine selection headache and a squeeze further down. Rice, Zubimendi, Guimarães, Mikel Merino and Ødegaard cannot all play. Myles Lewis-Skelly, 19, who featured in the Champions League semi-final against Atlético Madrid and in the final against PSG, is the player most obviously affected — see The Rumour Mill below.

The centre-back hunt: Konsa reignited, Romero a non-starter, Stones free

With Saliba's back injury open-ended and Timber weeks away, Arsenal have returned to Aston Villa for Ezri Konsa. The gap is money, and it is wide.

Arsenal have reignited their interest in Ezri Konsa, the 28-year-old Aston Villa and England defender, with the BBC and the Mirror both reporting movement on Wednesday and Nicolò Schira independently confirming the interest. The picture from the various reports is consistent on the shape and inconsistent on the numbers: Arsenal's opening offer was rebuffed, Villa value Konsa at around £60m, and Arsenal accept they must significantly improve on an initial proposal reported in some quarters at around £30m and in others considerably higher. Konsa's contract runs to 2028, which is the lever Arsenal are leaning on. The Guardian reports a second bid is expected. Treat the specific figures as unsettled.

The appeal is obvious and it is not only about Saliba: Konsa plays centre-back and right-back to a high standard, which in a squad missing Timber and Copley is worth a premium. Whether Arsenal should pay Villa's premium in the third week of August, with the champions' leverage at its lowest, is the argument being had inside and outside the club.

The alternatives are thinner than the volume of speculation suggests. The Athletic's Phil Costa reported on August 6 that Arsenal admire Cristian Romero but that Tottenham's refusal to sell to their north London neighbours makes it "a non-starter"; the Argentine has since agreed personal terms with Atlético Madrid after Inter had verbally agreed a €40m fee, and a move away from the Premier League looks the likely end point. John Stones, a free agent after leaving Manchester City and fresh from a strong World Cup, is the cheapest serious option on the board — Arsenal are interested, and Chelsea are competing. Iván Fresneda of Sporting CP has also been floated as a right-sided option. Fabrizio Romano has said Arsenal want a centre-back and a winger before the deadline and are "nowhere near done."

THE SQUAD

A settled spine, an unsettled back four, and a left wing in limbo

Arteta's selection questions for Cardiff are almost entirely defensive. Everything from the base of midfield forwards has, if anything, too many answers.

Begin with what is certain. David Raya returns as first choice and as a World Cup winner, having been part of the Spain squad that beat Argentina 1-0 after extra time in the final in July. Kepa Arrizabalaga and the newly arrived Illan Meslier, a free transfer from the relegated Leeds United, complete a three-deep goalkeeping department. Rice and Zubimendi are the base. Ødegaard is the captain and, along with Martinelli, was given an extended break after international duty. Gyökeres and Havertz returned early after Sweden and Germany exited the World Cup at the group and early knockout stages respectively, and both have looked sharp.

The defensive picture is the whole story. Fifteen Arsenal players went to the World Cup, representing nine nations — the largest contingent from any Premier League club, and a direct cause of the compressed, ragged pre-season the champions have had. Saliba came back from it with a back complaint that is now open-ended. Arteta's phrasing — two weeks of doing "almost nothing", then a reassessment — is the language of a club that does not yet know. Timber had groin surgery in June and will miss both the Shield and the opening weekend. Ben White is fit but has been fragile for two years. That is the entirety of the argument for spending £50m-plus on Konsa in the last three weeks of a window.

Tactically, two threads are worth watching on Sunday. The first is the height of the line: without Saliba's recovery pace, the risk profile of Arsenal's pressing scheme changes materially, and both Betis and Dortmund scored goals in the first half-hour that looked like the system rather than the individuals. The second is set pieces at both ends. Arsenal have been the league's most productive dead-ball side under Nicolas Jover; in pre-season they have been conceding from them, with Gadou's goal for Dortmund coming from a corner Arsenal defended badly. Champions do not usually regress in the one area they have monopolised. It is worth watching whether the return of the full first-choice group fixes it.

Forward, the shape of the front line has quietly changed. Tzolis has started pre-season on the left, Bukayo Saka is back in training after the World Cup and is untouchable on the right, Gyökeres leads the line with Havertz and Merino behind him, and Eze and Ødegaard rotate through the ten. Noni Madueke gives width on either flank. That leaves Martinelli and Jesus — two Brazilians with title medals — as the squad's most saleable assets and its most obvious sources of August friction.

HALE END

Dowman at 16, Julienne at right-back, and a golden boot in Shanghai

A brutal blow for Louie Copley, a genuine emergence for Theo Julienne, and an academy season that starts this Saturday.

The hardest news first. Louie Copley, the 18-year-old midfielder whose progress had been one of the quiet pleasures of Arsenal's summer, confirmed on August 7 that he ruptured his anterior cruciate ligament in the defeat to Real Betis, having been withdrawn after ten minutes. "The road ahead won't be easy," he wrote, "but I'll give everything to come back stronger." He will miss the season.

His absence has opened a door for Theo Julienne. The 18-year-old, a midfielder who became a fixture in the U21s during the second half of last season, has featured in every first-team friendly and made his Emirates debut off the bench against Dortmund — largely at right-back, a position he has taken to out of necessity with Timber out and Copley gone. Arseblog's assessment is that he "has always carried the aura of a senior professional." He started against Como on Wednesday alongside Marli Salmon, with Max Dowman also in the XI and Josh Ogunnaike among the substitutes. On Tuesday, Julienne trained with the first team alongside Ife Ibrahim and Demiane Agustien — Ibrahim and Julienne singled out as the two who have most impressed.

Dowman remains the headline act. Sixteen years old, a goal and an assist in the 4-1 win at Girona, and a starter rather than a cameo in three of the four friendlies. He made his Premier League debut last season. There is no sensible version of this campaign in which he is not involved with the senior squad.

Abroad, Arsenal's U17s were declared joint winners of the Shanghai Future Star Cup on August 9 after the final was cancelled by a typhoon. Jaden Maghoma took the golden boot with four goals and an assist, having scored in a 1-1 draw with Athletic Club on August 6 from an Emerson Nwaneri assist; Abraham Owusu-Gyasi, who is chasing a professional contract, scored the decisive penalty in a 4-3 shootout win over Shanghai FA in the semi-final. Separately, Nathan Tchoumba, a 15-year-old defensive midfielder from the Colorado Rapids academy, trained with the U18s in a friendly this week before returning to MLS.

ARSENAL WOMEN

Slegers rebuilds: five frees, one fee, and a clear run at September

No summer tournament, no Champions League qualifiers, six new senior faces and a seven-week runway to the WSL opener at Brighton. Arsenal Women have not had a pre-season this clean in years.

Renée Slegers has had the sort of summer a coach dreams about and rarely gets: a squad rebuilt through the free-agent market, no major international tournament pulling players away, and — because Arsenal qualified directly for the Women's Champions League league phase by finishing second in the WSL — no August qualifying rounds to disrupt the build-up. As one assessment put it this week, there "looks to be a real chance to hit the ground running."

The rebuild was substantial and it was necessary. Beth Mead, Katie McCabe, Manuela Zinsberger, Laia Codina, Victoria Pelova and Naomi Williams all left in May. In came five outfield players, four of them on free transfers: Georgia Stanway from Bayern Munich, filling the defensive-midfield hole left by Lia Wälti; Ona Batlle from Barcelona, a direct answer to McCabe's departure and capable on either flank; Géraldine Reuteler from Eintracht Frankfurt, the most positionally flexible player in the squad; and Selina Cerci from Hoffenheim, a third forward option behind Alessia Russo and Stina Blackstenius with 32 goals in 43 Frauen-Bundesliga appearances. The one fee went on Lisa Baum, 19, from RB Leipzig for around £510,000 — the youngest player to score six or more goals in a Bundesliga season.

Baum has already justified the outlay. She scored, along with Chloe Kelly, in a 2-1 pre-season win over Crystal Palace on Saturday — both goals in the second half. Frida Maanum did not feature; Steph Catley, Leah Williamson and Cerci were unavailable, having only recently rejoined full training. The squad then travelled to La Manga for a training camp, with a friendly against AC Milan arranged during the trip and a further behind-closed-doors fixture likely.

Two departures completed the tidying-up. Jenna Nighswonger joined NWSL side Bay FC for a reported £150,000 — the 25-year-old American made only five competitive appearances after arriving from Gotham FC in January 2025 and spent the second half of last season on loan at Aston Villa, where a purchase option went unexercised. Jessie Gale, the young striker, joined Newcastle United on loan in WSL2 on August 7.

The Emirates hosts every WSL home game again this season, for the second year running, with league-phase Champions League and domestic cup ties staying at Meadow Park. Home European football at the Emirates resumes only from the quarter-finals — should Arsenal get there.

GUNNERS ABROAD

A quiet week away — and a World Cup still being paid for

There is no international window this week and no European club football until September. The relevant story abroad is the tournament Arsenal have just come back from.

For once this section reports an absence rather than a result: there were no competitive international fixtures involving Arsenal players in the last seven days, and no European club football. The next senior international window opens in early September; the Champions League league phase begins on September 8-10, with the draw at UEFA headquarters in Nyon on Thursday, August 27 at 5pm BST — 9am Pacific.

The World Cup that finished on July 19 is still the dominant fact of Arsenal's summer. Fifteen players from the title-winning squad went, representing nine nations. Three came back with winners' medals: David Raya, Mikel Merino and Martín Zubimendi, after Spain beat Argentina 1-0 in extra time through Ferran Torres. Bruno Guimarães, then still a Newcastle player, started all five of Brazil's matches and provided four assists — form that is a large part of why Arsenal paid £75m for him three weeks later. Saka, Rice, Eze and Madueke were all in the England squad. Saliba's tournament with France was cut short by the back injury that still has him sidelined.

Among those out on loan and abroad, Cecily Wellesley-Smith continues her spell at FC Rosengård in Sweden through December, and Jessie Gale has just begun her loan at Newcastle United in WSL2. Arsenal's men's loan list for 2026/27 is still being assembled — several deals are expected before the September 1 deadline, and we will track each of them here weekly once they are done.

IN BRIEF

■ Arsenal and Emirates announced an extension of the longest-running shirt sponsorship in the Premier League, taking the partnership — which dates to 2006 — through to 2033 at a reported £70m a year, up from £50m. Chief executive Richard Garlick called the airline "an integral part of this success"; Emirates president Sir Tim Clark said the two had "grown alongside" each other. The package covers shirt, training kit, stadium naming rights and the women's and youth teams — more comprehensive than either Manchester City's £67.5m Etihad deal or Manchester United's £60m with Qualcomm.

■ The Champions League league-phase draw takes place in Nyon on Thursday, August 27 at 5pm BST. Six English clubs are in the pot: Arsenal, Liverpool, Manchester City, Chelsea, Manchester United and Aston Villa. The final is at the Estadio Metropolitano in Madrid on June 5, 2027.

■ Arsenal will open their title defence at home to newly promoted Coventry City on Friday, August 21, live on Sky Sports at 8pm BST — noon Pacific. Aston Villa away follows on August 29, then Chelsea at the Emirates on September 5.

■ Jaden Maghoma won the golden boot as Arsenal's U17s were named joint winners of the Shanghai Future Star Cup, the final having been cancelled because of a typhoon.
