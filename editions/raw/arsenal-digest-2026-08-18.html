The Lead

Champions Again, and Already One Defender Short

Arsenal open the defence of a first league title in twenty-two years on Friday night against Coventry City. The Community Shield said one thing about this squad. The treatment room says another.

Twenty-five seconds. That is how long Arsenal waited to score the first goal of their post-championship life. Myles Lewis-Skelly slid a ball into the space that Manchester City had not yet decided to defend, and Riccardo Calafiori arrived at the end of it.

By the time the Principality Stadium had settled into its seats on Sunday afternoon the Community Shield was already tilting. It finished 3-0. It could reasonably have finished by more.

That is the version of this football club the rest of the league will be looking at on Friday evening, when Coventry City arrive at the Emirates for the curtain-raiser of the 2026-27 season. It is not the whole picture.

The whole picture includes a centre-half who cannot play, a right-sided defender who cannot play, and a sporting director with under a fortnight to do something about both.

The injury that shapes everything

William Saliba came off after half an hour of France's World Cup semi-final in July and has not been right since. Arsenal confirmed on July 23 that specialist reviews had found a back injury requiring "a period of rehabilitation," and that he would be out for an extended spell.

Surgery was considered and rejected, which is the good news: the operation would have cost four to five months. The BBC's Sami Mokbel has since put the realistic floor at two to three months, pointing at the home fixture with Leeds United on October 10 as a plausible re-entry point.

Jurrien Timber, who had a small procedure on a stubborn groin problem, is further along but not close. Mikel Arteta said on August 10 that there were still "a few weeks to go."

Losing Saliba is not a squad-depth inconvenience. It is a structural one. Sky Sports noted alongside the club's July statement that Arsenal's win rate falls from 68 per cent to 50 per cent in matches he does not start.

The reason is visible in the way Arteta asks his team to play. The high line that lets Arsenal compress the pitch and win the ball 60 yards from their own goal is underwritten by a defender who can recover 40 metres in a straight line without panicking. Take him out and either the line drops, which costs territory, or it does not, which costs goals.

On Sunday the answer was Cristhian Mosquera alongside Gabriel Magalhaes, with Ben White at right-back and Calafiori at left, and it worked well enough that the question got quieter for ninety minutes.

Manchester City, under Enzo Maresca for the first time in a showpiece, never really got Erling Haaland into the game. Jeremy Doku was doubled up on the touchline every time he took possession. David Raya made the two saves that mattered.

But a Community Shield in the third week of August is not a Tuesday in Naples in November, and Arteta knows the difference. Asked afterwards about the window, he did not bother with the usual manager's shrug.

 

"We know there are certain areas that through injuries we are weaker and we need to strengthen. We're trying to do that and we've been trying to do that for weeks." Mikel Arteta, August 17

Selection, and too many good players

The selection questions for Friday sit further forward. Kai Havertz scored the second goal on Sunday and started ahead of Viktor Gyokeres, who came off the bench, and a real debate has opened about which of them leads the line in the games that decide seasons.

Gyokeres was Arsenal's leading league scorer last season with 14: a respectable debut return for a 55-million-pound striker, and a slightly underwhelming one for a player who arrived from Sporting CP with a reputation as an unstoppable force. Havertz offers the link play that makes Martin Odegaard's job easier, and Odegaard was the best player on the pitch in Cardiff.

There is also the matter of who plays on the right. Bukayo Saka went to the World Cup, played in England's bronze-medal match against France on July 18, and returned late; he was a substitute on Sunday. Noni Madueke started in his place and kept it through most of the game.

Arteta has more players than shirts, which is the position every champion wants to be in and the position that creates a specific problem. Gabriel Jesus, Gabriel Martinelli and Ethan Nwaneri were all left out of the Shield squad entirely: all three fit, all three now the subject of transfer conversations.

Do not dismiss the visitors

Coventry City should not be treated as a formality even though the bookmakers treat them as one. Frank Lampard's side won the Championship by eleven points, which is not a play-off scramble but a procession.

They have spent the summer sensibly: Carl Rushworth made permanent in goal after his loan from Brighton, six new arrivals in all, Gustavo Hamer and Taiwo Awoniyi to carry the attack. They beat Monaco 2-0 and Espanyol 3-1 in pre-season.

A newly promoted team playing its first top-flight match in twenty-five years, away, in front of the champions' crowd, under lights, is a team with nothing to lose and a manager who will have spent a fortnight thinking about nothing else.

The larger frame is what makes Friday interesting rather than routine. Arsenal have not defended a Premier League title since 2004, and the last time they tried they were coming off an unbeaten season and still finished second.

The 2025-26 team won it by seven points with the best defence in the division, and it has been strengthened rather than dismantled: Bruno Guimaraes for 75 million, Christos Tzolis for 34, Piero Hincapie made permanent. What has not been solved, three days before kickoff, is the one thing that broke on its own.

The 75-Million-Pound Rewiring of the Arsenal Midfield

Bruno Guimaraes played 45 minutes in Cardiff. It was enough to see what Arteta has bought, and what it does to everybody else.

The signing of Bruno Guimaraes from Newcastle United on August 9, for a fee reported at 75 million pounds on a four-year deal with a further year at the club's option, was the most expensive piece of business Arsenal have done this summer and the least obviously necessary.

This was a squad that already contained Declan Rice, Martin Zubimendi, Mikel Merino and Odegaard. Adding a 28-year-old Brazilian international to that group looks, on a team sheet, like hoarding.

Watch the Community Shield and it looks like something else. Guimaraes started in a three with Lewis-Skelly and Odegaard, which is not the shape anybody predicted, and the effect was to give Arsenal a midfielder who breaks lines with a pass rather than with a carry.

Rice destroys and drives. Zubimendi circulates. Guimaraes plays the ball that skips a phase. Against a City side pressing in a mid-block, that was the difference between working the ball around the outside and getting through the middle of it.

He was withdrawn at half-time for Rice, which reads less like a hook than like a manager who signed a player nine days ago and is not going to break him in week one.

The knock-on effects are real. Lewis-Skelly, nineteen, started in midfield rather than at left-back and produced the pass for the opening goal. Zubimendi, a World Cup winner five weeks ago, came off the bench. Merino did not get on. That is why the outgoing side of this window has become as consequential as the incoming one.

 

Transfer News

Thirteen Days to Find a Centre-Half

Konsa, Quansah, Kounde, Pubill. Four names, four different problems, and a deadline that does not move.

The furthest advanced of the four is Ezri Konsa, and "furthest advanced" is doing heavy lifting in that sentence. Arsenal have made at least two approaches to Aston Villa for the England defender, with reported figures in the 30 to 45 million range.

Villa have valued him at 60 million and not blinked. As of Monday the second proposal had fallen short and the two clubs were not close. Certainty: bid made, fee not agreed.

The alternative that emerged over the weekend is Jarell Quansah, the 23-year-old former Liverpool defender now at Bayer Leverkusen. ESPN reported on August 16 that Arsenal had opened talks and that Leverkusen want somewhere around 50 million euros.

This is the deal that makes the most institutional sense: a Premier League-schooled defender still on the right side of 24, at a club Arsenal have just done business with over Hincapie, at a price that does not require selling anybody. Certainty: early talks.

The glamour option is Jules Kounde. Catalan outlets including Mundo Deportivo and SPORT reported in the middle of last week that Arsenal were prepared to pay a substantial fee for the Barcelona defender, with Liverpool also circling.

Treat this one with care. It is sourced entirely to the selling market, no bid has been confirmed, and Barcelona's financial calendar has a way of generating stories about players they might sell. Certainty: concrete interest, single market, unconfirmed.

The fourth is already dead. Arsenal made contact with Atletico Madrid about Marc Pubill, and Fabrizio Romano and Jorge Garcia Hernandez of AS both reported around August 13 and 14 that Atletico had declared the defender non-transferable, pointing at a release clause of 500 million euros by way of punctuation. Certainty: rejected.

Read those four together and a pattern emerges. Andrea Berta is not shopping for a squad player. He is shopping for someone who can start thirty games at the heart of a title defence and then hand the shirt back when Saliba returns.

That is a strange and expensive brief, and it explains why the club has been willing to look at four different leagues rather than settle for the obvious. It also explains Arteta's unusually direct language on Sunday. Managers do not normally say they are weaker.

Nwaneri at the Crossroads

Four clubs, two countries, and a buy-back clause. The most delicate decision of Arsenal's summer involves a player who is not going to start.

He was fit. He had trained. He had played through pre-season. And on Sunday, Ethan Nwaneri was not in the Community Shield squad at all, which is the loudest thing Arteta has said about him all summer.

The manager's public phrasing was more diplomatic. Nwaneri, he said, "needs consistent minutes at this stage of his career."

The nineteen-year-old spent the second half of last season on loan at Marseille, a move that went wrong for reasons largely outside his control. Roberto De Zerbi, the coach who wanted him, left shortly after he arrived, and under Habib Beye he managed a little over 400 minutes across nine appearances, with two goals.

He came back to a club that had just won the league and then added Tzolis and Guimaraes on top of a forward line that already had Saka, Madueke, Eberechi Eze and Martinelli in it.

The most concrete reporting so far comes from Italy. Carlo Pellegatti has described RB Leipzig as being in advanced talks, with the German club prepared to offer between 35 and 40 million euros for a permanent transfer, and Arsenal exploring a buy-back clause valued at 55 to 60 million.

Leipzig expect funds from an outgoing sale of their own and have capped individual spending at 45 million this summer, which makes the arithmetic plausible. AC Milan made an enquiry in late July and have been linked with a loan. Borussia Dortmund, Fulham and Galatasaray have all been mentioned. Certainty: exit expected, structure undecided.

The complication is that Nwaneri is contracted to 2030, so Arsenal face no pressure at all. Selling a Hale End graduate who broke the Premier League's youngest-player record is the kind of decision that gets re-litigated for a decade if it goes wrong.

A loan protects the asset and solves nothing. A sale with a buy-back solves the minutes problem and transfers the risk to whether the buy-back price stays sane. Somebody at London Colney has to pick one within a fortnight.

The Turkish Channel

Galatasaray bid for Martinelli and were turned down. What else was on the table in that conversation is the more interesting story.

David Ornstein reported on August 14 that Galatasaray had bid 45 million euros, around 38.4 million pounds, for Gabriel Martinelli, and that the Brazilian had declined the move.

Arsenal are understood to be open to selling him at the right number, but to a club in one of Europe's leading leagues rather than to Turkey, and Martinelli's own preference appears to run the same way. He scored eleven goals in 53 appearances last season and was left out of the Shield squad. Certainty: bid made, declined by player.

The detail worth filing away came from the BBC's Sami Mokbel, also on August 14. In the course of those talks, the future of Victor Osimhen is understood to have been discussed as well.

Berta and his assistant Maurizio Michele have long-standing relationships in Turkey, and Berta's brief this summer explicitly includes making Arsenal active sellers rather than only buyers.

Nothing about this constitutes a pursuit of Osimhen. It does tell you that the channel between the two clubs is open in both directions, which is worth remembering if a striker becomes available in the last week of the window. Certainty: single-source, exploratory, no bid.

The Rumour Mill

The smaller threads, the ones still simmering, and where each of them honestly sits.

Pedro Neto

The noisiest name of the past week and the least likely to move. Chelsea are willing to sell, but the asking price has travelled from around 70 million on August 10, per Simon Phillips, to close to 100 million by Monday, and Arsenal are in a queue with Manchester City and Tottenham. Given that Tzolis arrived in July and produced six assists and a goal in six pre-season matches, spending nine figures on another wide forward would be a strange use of the remaining budget. Certainty: interest, no bid.

Nico Williams

Berta held discussions over the Athletic Club winger, whose release clause sits at around 90 million euros, after moves for other wide targets fell away earlier in the summer. Reported by Graeme Bailey on August 6 and quiet since. Certainty: interest, dormant.

Gabriel Jesus

In talks with Napoli at agent level, per Fabrizio Romano on August 7 and 8. His new representative also represents the Napoli coach, which is how these conversations tend to start. Arsenal want a permanent sale in the region of 20 million rather than a loan, and Napoli must resolve Romelu Lukaku's future first. Certainty: agent-level talks.

Martin Zubimendi

Interest from Real Madrid surfaced through Spanish reporting and football.london's Tom Canton on August 10, with a figure around 90 million euros attached. Arsenal have no intention of selling and Madrid are reported to be internally divided on whether to pursue it. Certainty: rumour.

Myles Lewis-Skelly

Chelsea made a second formal enquiry, reported by Sky Sports on August 12, with Manchester United also long-term admirers. The teenager has signed a new five-year contract and Arsenal have no interest in selling to a domestic rival. Arteta's response was the only correct one: "if there is speculation about our players, it's a good sign." Certainty: enquiry rebuffed.

Salma Paralluelo

On the women's side, the forward is a free agent following her release by Barcelona, with Arsenal named alongside Chelsea and OL Lyonnes among interested parties. Nothing has been confirmed by any of the three. Certainty: unconfirmed link.

 

The Squad

What Cardiff Told Us, and What It Did Not

Ninety minutes against Manchester City is a poor sample and a good sign. Reading it honestly.

Arteta sent out Raya behind White, Mosquera, Gabriel and Calafiori, a midfield of Guimaraes, Lewis-Skelly and Odegaard, and a front line of Madueke, Havertz and Tzolis.

Read that back and note how many players you would call automatic starters are missing from it: Saka, Rice, Zubimendi, Gyokeres, Eze. All five were on the bench. All five came on or could have.

The press, and the corner

The pressing was the most striking feature. Arsenal scored inside half a minute because they were already hunting the ball when City tried to play out, and they never really stopped.

This is the pattern that produced 27 goals conceded and 19 clean sheets last season, and it survived the loss of the defender who anchors it, at least for one afternoon.

Set pieces remain the club's most distinctive weapon and the one most likely to suffer if the personnel change. Arsenal scored 18 goals from corners in 2025-26, an all-time Premier League single-season record, beating a mark that had stood for 32 years.

More than 40 per cent of their league goals arrived from dead-ball situations. That routine depends heavily on aerial threat from centre-backs. It is another quiet argument for signing a defender who can head a ball, not just defend a counter.

The armband

The captaincy was settled without drama. Odegaard retained the armband after a vote of players and staff that Arteta described as unanimous "by a big, big 100 miles," and then won man of the match in the Shield. He has held the role since 2022, but the manner of the confirmation matters in a dressing room about to shed several senior names.

 

Availability / Men's First Team

William Saliba, back. Injured on World Cup duty with France, surgery ruled out in favour of rehabilitation. Arsenal said on July 23 he would miss an extended period. The BBC's estimate is a minimum of two to three months, pointing at October.

Jurrien Timber, groin. Underwent a small procedure and is doing individual pitch work. Arteta said on August 10 there were still "a few weeks to go," with a late-September return the realistic target.

Ben White, knee. Fully recovered from surgery, back in training since late July, started the Community Shield at right-back.

Louie Copley, knee. The under-21 defender suffered a season-ending anterior cruciate ligament injury in the pre-season friendly against Real Betis.

Form and the number nine

On individual form, the debate that will not go away is Havertz against Gyokeres. Havertz started in Cardiff and scored. Gyokeres came off the bench.

The case for the German is the one Arteta has always made, that a centre-forward in this system is a connector as much as a finisher, and Havertz's passing numbers are considerably better. The case for the Swede is that 14 league goals in a first season in England, at a club that scored 71, is a platform rather than a ceiling. Expect them to share it, and expect the question again in November.

The one to watch quietly is Tzolis. Six assists and a goal in six pre-season appearances, then the header that set up Havertz for the second in the Shield, and a body of work at Club Brugge that reads 51 goal involvements in 52 matches. At 34 million pounds, in a market where Chelsea want three times that for Pedro Neto, he already looks like the shrewdest thing Berta has done.

 

Hale End

A New Academy, Top to Bottom

Mertesacker gone, De Maesschalck in, and a new under-21 head coach appointed yesterday. The pathway that produced Saka and Lewis-Skelly has new management.

The most recent piece of news here is the freshest thing in this edition. On Monday, Arsenal confirmed David Horseman as the new under-21 head coach, filling the vacancy created when Max Porter left for Stoke City.

It completes a summer of change. Per Mertesacker stepped down as academy manager after eight years, a decision confirmed in January and effective at the end of last season, and has since taken a senior role with the German federation.

His replacement started on August 1 with a new job title. Pascal De Maesschalck arrives as Academy Director, rather than academy manager, from a technical director role at Strasbourg and with previous academy stints at Monaco and Club Brugge.

Berta cited his "outstanding track record in youth development." He inherits a department with an outstanding recent record for producing first-team players, a rather less outstanding one in the youth leagues themselves, and the perennial problem of holding on to the best fifteen-year-olds in London.

The season starts now

Competitive football is only now beginning. The under-21s open their EFL Trophy campaign today away at Barnet, in a group that also contains Leyton Orient and AFC Wimbledon, and start their Premier League 2 season at home to Crystal Palace on Saturday.

That competition has been restructured again: 31 teams across three tiers, 20 league games each, top 16 into the play-offs. The under-18s started their season on August 15 away at West Bromwich Albion.

The names to know, according to Jeorge Bird's pre-season review published on August 13, are Marli Salmon and Theo Julienne. Salmon, a centre-back who turns 17 later this month, was the second-youngest Englishman ever to appear in the Champions League last season, at 16 years and 103 days.

Julienne was described as the biggest winner of pre-season and played 62 minutes at right-back in the first-team friendly against Como at the Emirates on August 12, alongside Josh Ogunnaike, who signed his first professional contract on July 17.

And then there is Max Dowman, who is the reason the rest of English football keeps checking the Hale End team sheets. He does not turn 16 until December and is already the youngest player and youngest goalscorer in Premier League history, at 16 years and 73 days for the latter.

He scored, assisted and was named man of the match in the pre-season win over Girona on August 1, and he was on the bench in Cardiff on Sunday. He has signed scholarship terms; a professional contract follows when he turns 17.

Nine first-year scholars were confirmed for 2026-27, among them Emerson Nwaneri, a midfielder and the younger brother of Ethan, who scored a hat-trick against Chelsea at under-16 level. The one bad piece of academy news is Louie Copley's cruciate injury, which ends his season before it starts.

 

Loan Watch

Unusually thin this year. Two names, both this week.

Charles Sagoe Jr, 21, at Kalmar FF in Sweden until January. Scored and assisted against Mjallby, scored again at IFK Goteborg on August 9, then started and played 73 minutes in a 4-0 defeat to Hammarby on Sunday.

Ismeal Kabia, 20, joined St Mirren on loan until May 2027 on Saturday and made his debut a day later, playing 59 minutes in a 5-1 Scottish League Cup defeat. Manager Craig McLeish said the club had beaten off competition from Britain and Europe to get him.

 

Arsenal Women

Seven In, Six Out, and Three Weeks to Build a Team

Renee Slegers has rebuilt half a squad in a summer. La Manga produced a defeat, a first goal for a new signing, and a very welcome sight at centre-back.

The most significant thing that happened to Arsenal Women last week was not the result. It was Leah Williamson heading in an equaliser from an Olivia Smith corner in the 46th minute against AC Milan in La Manga on August 13.

The England captain spent much of last season managing recurring knee, hamstring and calf problems, and missed the June World Cup qualifiers with a hamstring picked up in rehabilitation. Seeing her rise for a set piece at competitive tempo is the best piece of news of the women's pre-season.

The match itself finished 3-2 to Milan, and it ended the Spanish training camp on a sour note. Thea Kyvag put the Italians ahead on 25 minutes after a defensive mix-up, Williamson levelled straight after the break, and Sara Stokic restored the lead around the 70th.

Selina Cerci, one of seven summer arrivals, scored her first Arsenal goal with a strike from outside the area before Monica Renzotti won it in stoppage time. New goalkeeper Isabella Damm, signed from Brondby in July, made a smart double save.

The rebuild, in full

Seven players in, all but one on free transfers: Georgia Stanway from Bayern Munich, Ona Batlle from Barcelona on a four-year deal with an option, Geraldine Reuteler from Eintracht Frankfurt, Cerci from Hoffenheim, and goalkeepers Misa Rodriguez from Real Madrid and Damm from Brondby.

The exception is Lisa Baum, 19, signed from RB Leipzig for a reported 600,000 euros, understood to be a record fee for a German women's league transfer.

The departures cut deeper than the arrivals suggest. Katie McCabe left for Chelsea after ten years and the captaincy. Beth Mead went to Manchester City on a three-year deal. Laia Codina joined West Ham and Victoria Pelova crossed north London to Tottenham.

Manuela Zinsberger went to Borussia Dortmund and Rosa Kafaji to London City Lionesses. That is a captain, a European Championship winner and a first-choice goalkeeper out of one dressing room in a single window.

What they are rebuilding from

Arsenal finished second in the Women's Super League last season, four points behind Manchester City, who won their first title in a decade and ended Chelsea's six-year run. Second place was secured on the final day with a 3-1 win at Liverpool, Alessia Russo scoring twice.

In Europe, the defending champions finished third in the league phase, beat Chelsea 3-2 on aggregate in the quarter-finals and lost the semi-final 4-3 on aggregate to OL Lyonnes.

They did add a trophy: the inaugural FIFA Women's Champions Cup, won 3-2 after extra time against Corinthians at the Emirates on February 1, with goals from Olivia Smith, Lotte Wubben-Moy and Caitlin Foord.

Slegers herself is secure, having signed an extension understood to run to the summer of 2029. Behind her, set-piece coach Chris Bradley left after two seasons and was replaced on July 31 by Nikita Runnacles, a former Ipswich Town goalkeeper and Brighton assistant, who will also help with goalkeeper coaching.

Gunners Abroad

The World Cup Bill Comes Due

There is no international break in August. There is, however, a squad still absorbing the cost of a tournament three Arsenal players won.

The 2026-27 calendar has no August window, with the next international break falling in September in an extended format. So there are no Arsenal internationals to report on this week.

The European calendar is equally quiet: the UEFA Super Cup was played in Salzburg on August 12 between Paris Saint-Germain and Aston Villa, with no Arsenal involvement.

What is still shaping the season is the summer that has just finished. Spain won the World Cup on July 19, beating Argentina 1-0 after extra time, and three Arsenal players were world champions: David Raya, Martin Zubimendi and Mikel Merino.

A day earlier, Declan Rice and Bukayo Saka had played in England's extraordinary 6-4 win over France in the third-place match, a game in which William Saliba lined up on the losing side.

The consequences are visible in every team sheet. Raya, Zubimendi and Merino did not return to training until around August 10. Saka and Rice came back late enough that both began the Community Shield on the bench.

And Saliba came back with an injury that will cost him the first two months of the title defence. Players from nations eliminated earlier, among them Kai Havertz with Germany, Viktor Gyokeres with Sweden, Piero Hincapie with Ecuador and Gabriel and Martinelli with Brazil, were back in mid to late July and are correspondingly further ahead.

In Brief

CHAMPIONS LEAGUE DRAW. Arsenal are in pot one for the 2026-27 league phase, drawn on Thursday, August 27. Matchday one falls between September 8 and 10. Pot one also contains Paris Saint-Germain, Real Madrid, Bayern Munich, Barcelona, Inter and Atletico Madrid. Liverpool and Manchester City are seeded there as well, but English clubs cannot be drawn against each other.

PFA AWARDS. Three Arsenal players are shortlisted for the PFA Players' Player of the Year, announced on August 25: Gabriel Magalhaes, David Raya and Declan Rice. Raya has already collected the Premier League Golden Glove for a third consecutive season, with 19 clean sheets.

THE EMIRATES QUESTION. Arsenal have engaged the architects Populous, who designed the current stadium, to study expansion options. Reporting through the spring suggested anything from an additional 5,000 hospitality seats to a full redevelopment towards 80,000. No planning application has been submitted and executives are said to feel no pressure to decide.

SHIRT SPONSOR. From 2027-28 the front of the shirt will read simply "Emirates," dropping the "Fly Better" tagline. The new agreement covers the men's, women's and academy teams, training kit and stadium naming rights, and was negotiated by chief commercial officer Juliet Slot, who leaves the club later this year.

THE ACCOUNTS. Arsenal's most recently published figures, for the year to May 2025 and released in March, showed record revenue of around £690m against a pre-tax loss of roughly £1m, with a wage bill of £346.8m at just over 50 per cent of turnover. The title and the run to the Champions League final should push the next set higher: the European campaign alone was worth about £123m in UEFA prize money.

PRE-SEASON, FOR THE RECORD. Arsenal beat Girona 4-1 and MK Dons, drew 1-1 with Como at the Emirates and won the shoot-out 4-3, and lost to Real Betis 3-1 and Borussia Dortmund 3-2. Then they beat Manchester City 3-0 at Cardiff. Draw your own conclusions about the value of July friendlies.
