The Lead

Twenty Four Seconds Into the Rest of It

Arsenal began their first season as champions of England in twenty two years by dismantling Manchester City before most of Cardiff had sat down. On Friday the actual defence starts, and it starts with a back line held together by improvisation.

There is a version of Sunday afternoon in which the Community Shield means nothing at all. Curtain-raiser, glorified friendly, a trophy nobody puts on the honours board with a straight face. Then Riccardo Calafiori hit the ball 24 seconds after kick-off, and the entire pre-match framing collapsed.

Myles Lewis-Skelly found him on the edge of the area with a pass of real disdain for the space Manchester City had left, and Calafiori did the rest. It was the first goal scored inside the opening minute of a Community Shield since Bobby Owen in 1968. Kai Havertz added a second at the back post on 28 minutes from Martin Odegaard’s cross, and Odegaard himself, who was the best player on the field by a distance, finished the thing off three minutes after the interval by leaving two defenders and deceiving Gianluigi Donnarumma. Arsenal 3, City 0, at the Principality Stadium, and not one moment of it in doubt.

Context matters here. The last time Arsenal started a season as reigning champions of England, Arsene Wenger’s Invincibles were the story and Thierry Henry was 26 years old. Last season ended that wait: 85 points, a goal difference of plus 44, 26 wins, the title mathematically secure on 19 May when Manchester City drew at Bournemouth. Four points was the final margin. Sunday was the first competitive occasion on which Mikel Arteta’s players had to walk out and be the team everybody is measuring themselves against, and they treated it like an audition rather than a coronation.

The shape was a 4-3-3 that has been quietly recalibrated. David Raya behind Ben White, Cristhian Mosquera, Gabriel and Calafiori. A midfield of Odegaard as captain, Lewis-Skelly in a left-sided central role and Bruno Guimaraes making his full debut. Noni Madueke, Havertz and Christos Tzolis ahead of them.

Tzolis finished with two assists on debut and three tackles, which is a sentence that tells you most of what Arsenal were buying when they paid Club Brugge a Belgian record fee for him. Guimaraes played 45 minutes, pushed the ball forward relentlessly from deep, and came off with an ice pack that Arseblog described as looking precautionary.

“You need the best facilities, the best stadium, the best supporters, and you need the best squad and the best players in the world.”

Mikel Arteta, this week

Manchester City were awful, and it is worth being honest about why that matters. This was Enzo Maresca’s first competitive match in charge, and a 3-0 defeat equals the heaviest opening-game loss by a City manager in the club’s 120-year history, matching Harry Newbould’s 4-1 reverse against Arsenal in 1906.

City managed 12 shots for an expected-goals figure of 0.8. Erling Haaland and Phil Foden were fed almost nothing. Raya still had to produce one outstanding save from Antoine Semenyo, a reminder that he remains the best goalkeeper in the division and that Arsenal’s cleanest sheets are rarely as clean as the scoreline suggests.

Now the caveats, because a serious reading of Sunday has to include them. William Saliba and Jurrien Timber were both absent injured. Gabriel Martinelli, Ethan Nwaneri, Gabriel Jesus, Fabio Vieira and Reiss Nelson were all left out of the squad entirely, which Arteta called a technical decision and which everybody understood as transfer-window housekeeping. Arsenal beat a team in transition with a side that is itself still being assembled. That is a genuinely encouraging thing and a genuinely incomplete one at the same time.

Friday brings Coventry City to the Emirates for the season’s opening fixture, live and alone in an 8pm slot. It would be easy to file this under formality. Arsenal have played Coventry five times in the Premier League era and won all five, 16 goals scored and two conceded.

But Frank Lampard’s side won the Championship by 11 points with 97 goals, he was named the division’s manager of the season, and promoted teams who arrive with that kind of attacking record tend to be a nuisance before they are a victim. Arsenal also have a specific structural problem for this game that a good Coventry counter-attack will look to find.

That problem is the centre of defence. Saliba is out with the back injury he carried through France’s World Cup and which forced him off in the semi-final; surgery was avoided but Arteta has called it long-term. Timber’s groin is still weeks away. Mosquera partnered Gabriel on Sunday and did it well, with Piero Hincapie on the bench as the third option. It held against a City side that never really came. It is a thinner arrangement than a title defence deserves, and the club knows it, which is why the last fortnight of this window is going to be spent chasing a defender rather than admiring a trophy.

The selection questions for Friday are pleasant ones by comparison. Bukayo Saka was available on Sunday but used only from the bench, and he starts. Havertz scored and looked like a centre-forward again, which puts a genuine question to Viktor Gyokeres about who leads the line in the biggest games. Declan Rice, rationed after a heavy summer, comes back into a midfield that suddenly has more good players than places. And Lewis-Skelly, who at 19 has now been trusted in central midfield in a showpiece against Manchester City, has made himself very hard to drop.

The last Arsenal side to defend a top-flight championship did it in 1934-35, which is the sort of statistic that gets read out at the start of a season and forgotten by October. No English club has retained the Premier League since Manchester City in 2023. Arteta has spent the summer talking about the scale of what he wants rather than the difficulty of holding what he has, and £143.5m on four signings suggests the board is listening. Sunday was a statement. Friday is the first instalment of the bill.

The Hole at the Heart of the Defence

Two of Arsenal’s three best defenders are unavailable, and the fix is not in the building. This is the story that will define the next fourteen days.

William Saliba played through a back problem for months last season. It caught up with him on the biggest stage of all: he was substituted after 30 minutes of France’s World Cup semi-final defeat to Spain, and on 23 July Arsenal confirmed he would miss an extended period. The club took the view that surgery was not the right route, which spares him the longest possible layoff but commits him to a managed rehabilitation programme instead.

Reported timelines put him at roughly two to three months, with an outside chance of the Leeds United fixture on 10 October. Arteta was blunter than that this week, calling it more a long-term injury.

Jurrien Timber’s summer was worse in a quieter way. He withdrew from the Netherlands squad before the World Cup even began with a groin problem, had minor surgery, and the issue flared again in pre-season. Arteta says he is progressing really well but is still weeks away. The knock-on is that Ben White becomes the first-choice right-back by default rather than by plan, and Arsenal lose the player who was arguably their most inventive full-back.

What is left is workable and not much more. Gabriel is the constant. Cristhian Mosquera, 22, partnered him on Sunday and handled Erling Haaland with a composure that will have reassured people. Piero Hincapie, signed permanently from Bayer Leverkusen in June for £34.5m after his loan spell, can play left centre-back or left-back and scored in the friendly defeat to Real Betis.

Calafiori covers across the back four. Behind that, the club has been giving pre-season minutes to the 19-year-old Welsh youth international Brayden Clarke, which is a compliment to him and a warning about the numbers.

Hence the urgency in the market. Arsenal have been in talks with Bayer Leverkusen about Jarell Quansah and have held conversations around Aston Villa’s Ezri Konsa. Neither is close, and both have a price problem. The window shuts at the start of September. If nothing arrives, Arteta spends the autumn asking Gabriel and Mosquera to play almost every minute of a Premier League title defence and a Champions League league phase, and hoping nobody pulls anything.

Seventy Five Million Reasons

Bruno Guimaraes is an Arsenal player, Newcastle have sold a quarter of a billion pounds of talent, and the champions have bought a midfielder they arguably did not need. That is the point.

The headline signing of Arsenal’s summer arrived on 9 August. Bruno Guimaraes joined from Newcastle United for £75m on a four-year contract with the option of a fifth, and took the number 39 shirt. He had made 195 appearances for Newcastle since 2022 and scored 31 goals. He called leaving one of the toughest decisions of his life, and then said the quiet part: “I need a new challenge in my life.”

Sporting director Andrea Berta framed the signing in the language of availability as much as ability. Guimaraes “combines quality with quantity, and has provided goals and assists for his team every year,” he said. That is the distinction that matters. Arsenal already had Declan Rice, Martin Zubimendi and Mikel Merino. What they did not have was a fourth central midfielder of genuinely elite standard who could take 45 minutes out of Rice’s legs in a week with three fixtures and cost the team nothing in control.

His debut suggested the fit is immediate rather than gradual. In 45 minutes against Manchester City he dictated tempo alongside Odegaard and pushed forward passes into the spaces City’s midfield kept abandoning. Odegaard was effusive afterwards, describing him as unbelievable since the first moment.

The wider context is a Newcastle fire sale. Guimaraes follows Anthony Gordon to Barcelona for £69.3m and Sandro Tonali to Tottenham Hotspur for £100m, taking the club past £240m in sales this summer. That is one of the more remarkable single-window dismantlings the Premier League has seen, and Arsenal were the beneficiaries of the biggest piece of it.

The Centre-Back Hunt Enters Its Final Fortnight

Two names, two valuations Arsenal do not like, and a deadline that does not move.

Arteta was asked this week whether the squad was finished and gave the answer managers give when it is not. “We have obviously detected some areas that can be strengthened and that’s what we are trying to do,” he said, and everybody in the room knew he meant a defender. CEO Richard Garlick has separately said Arsenal are still looking for players who “change the dial.”

Sky Sports reported on 14 August that Arsenal are in talks with Bayer Leverkusen over Jarell Quansah, the England defender who left Liverpool for £30m last July. Two things complicate it badly. Leverkusen do not currently consider him for sale, a position Sky Germany’s Florian Plettenberg has reported as a clear internal decision, and Liverpool hold a buy-back clause understood to be worth around £59.8m. Any Arsenal bid would need to clear both. Certainty tier: talks confirmed, seller unwilling.

Ezri Konsa is the more available option and the more expensive conversation. Aston Villa want around £60m for the 28-year-old, and Sky Sports News reports Arsenal have not indicated any willingness to come close to it. Fabrizio Romano described Arsenal as in the mix, with contact made with the player’s representatives, and called him “probably the most easy solution on the market.”

One outlet, Football Insider, went further and said the move was 99 per cent done. That is a single source going well beyond what anybody else is reporting, and it should be read that way. Certainty tier: concrete interest and agent contact, no fee agreement.

Two other names have surfaced and both should be treated coolly. Romano reported Arsenal made contact regarding Atletico Madrid’s Marc Pubill, but that Atletico absolutely want to keep him. Jules Kounde of Barcelona has been floated as a versatile solution, with Romano saying explicitly that there is nothing advanced. And the Cristian Romero link is dead: Tottenham Hotspur have zero intention of selling to a direct rival, and Atletico Madrid are the frontrunners there instead.

Nwaneri at the Crossroads

Left out of the Community Shield squad entirely, courted by Milan, and now the defining subplot of Arsenal’s deadline fortnight.

Ethan Nwaneri did not travel to Cardiff. That is not a small thing for a 19-year-old who has been at the club since he was eight and who became the Premier League’s youngest ever player in 2022. Romano reported on 16 August that Nwaneri will leave Arsenal before the window shuts, “as planned weeks ago.”

The mechanics are where it gets interesting. AC Milan proposed an initial loan with a €40m option to buy. Arsenal rejected it, and have set a clear condition: a permanent transfer, or a loan with an obligation to buy. No options. Galatasaray have enquired, German clubs have made contact, and Romano expects Premier League clubs to join. RB Leipzig and Borussia Dortmund have both been named across multiple outlets.

The backdrop is a loan spell at Marseille in the second half of last season that did not go well, and a manager who has been unusually candid about the calculus. Arteta said on 14 August that he only wants Nwaneri to stay if the club can guarantee him minutes. With Saka, Odegaard, Eberechi Eze, Madueke and now Tzolis in the building, that guarantee cannot honestly be made.

Certainty tier: exit expected and effectively agreed in principle by the club, destination genuinely unresolved as of Tuesday evening. Romano’s framing is worth quoting because it is the right one: “Arsenal will be in control.”

The Clear-Out, and a Manager’s Complaint

Martinelli, Jesus, Vieira, Nelson. Four players who were not in the Community Shield squad, and one rule Arteta wants changed.

Gabriel Martinelli is the most significant of them. He rejected a move to Galatasaray reported at around £34m to £45m, and was then left out of the Cardiff squad altogether.

Napoli have opened talks according to multiple reports on 16 and 17 August, Roma have been mentioned as an alternative, and Matteo Moretto and David Ornstein both surfaced an approach from Al-Sadd of Qatar. Arsenal will not sanction anything without a replacement winger arriving first. All of this is early-stage and should be read as such.

Gabriel Jesus, finally fit again after the anterior cruciate ligament injury suffered in January 2025 and a scorer in pre-season against Girona, is wanted by Napoli too. The block is money: Arsenal want around €20m and Napoli would need to find roughly €6m a year in wages while prioritising defensive signings. He has one year left on his contract.

Fabio Vieira has been at Hamburger SV on loan and both clubs want to make it permanent. Arsenal have reportedly softened their asking price to get it over the line, having initially wanted north of €20m. Reiss Nelson is in talks with West Ham United over a permanent move for a fee reported as low as £3m. Kepa Arrizabalaga is open to leaving for the right opportunity but there is little market for a 31-year-old understudy.

The volume of exclusions produced a genuinely interesting piece of advocacy from the manager. Arteta used the Community Shield to call publicly on the Premier League to expand matchday squads from 20 to 23, bringing the competition into line with UEFA. He had five senior professionals he could not name. It is self-interested, and it is also correct.

The Squad

A Midfield With Too Many Good Players

The tactical picture for 2026-27: a recalibrated 4-3-3, a genuine question at centre-forward, and a captain who looks like himself again.

The most interesting thing about Sunday’s team sheet was not who was in it but where they were. Lewis-Skelly, who came through as a left-back, started in a left-sided central midfield role and produced the pass for the opening goal. He has, in Arseblog’s phrase, cemented a place in the Arsenal midfield. That is a remarkable sentence to write about a 19-year-old at a club that has just spent £75m on a central midfielder.

Which is the point. Arsenal now have Rice, Zubimendi, Merino, Guimaraes, Odegaard and Lewis-Skelly for three positions, and a Champions League league phase, a title defence and two domestic cups to distribute them across. Rice was managed carefully through pre-season after a hamstring and neural issue and the fatigue of a World Cup that ran to a third-place playoff; he was given three weeks off, returned in early August, and sat on the bench in Cardiff. That is not a demotion. It is the first visible evidence of a squad deep enough to rest its best midfielder in a showpiece.

Up front there is a real contest. Havertz started centrally, scored, and looked like the more natural fit for the expansive, false-nine-adjacent version of Arsenal’s attack. Gyokeres, who led the scoring in the title season, was a substitute. Nobody sensible is calling this settled after one August afternoon, but Arteta has two very different centre-forwards and has shown he will pick by opponent rather than by reputation.

Hale End

A New Coach, a Chastening Night, and the Boy With Number 56

David Horseman’s first match in charge of the under-21s went about as badly as a first match can. Elsewhere, Hale End keeps producing.

Arsenal confirmed David Horseman as permanent under-21s head coach on 17 August. He had been the club’s elite player development coach, overseeing the under-21s in the EFL Trophy and the under-19s in the UEFA Youth League, and stepped up on an interim basis through pre-season. He replaces Max Porter, who left for a first-team role at Stoke City, and becomes Arsenal’s sixth under-21 coach since 2018. His background runs through Southampton, Watford, Bristol City and a senior spell at Forest Green Rovers.

His first match was on Tuesday, and it was brutal: Barnet 5, Arsenal under-21s 1, in the EFL Trophy. Jeorge Bird’s Arsenal Youth described the start to Horseman’s tenure as deeply disappointing. The consolations were developmental rather than competitive. Elijah Upson made his debut, and a group of under-18s got a night of senior football that will do more for them in the long run than a comfortable win would have.

Upson is worth knowing about. A centre-back and the son of the former West Ham United and England defender Matthew Upson, he joined Arsenal on a free from Tottenham Hotspur on 21 July after rejecting a Spurs extension, and is reported to have turned down Chelsea, Manchester City, Everton, Bayern Munich, Borussia Dortmund, Bayer Leverkusen and Monaco to do it.

The under-18s opened their U18 Premier League campaign on 15 August with a 0-0 draw away at West Bromwich Albion under Adam Birchall. Daniel McCarron, signed from Dungannon Swifts, made his competitive debut, and Jaden Maghoma, who is technically still an under-16, came off the bench on 69 minutes. The under-21s begin their Premier League 2 season at home to Crystal Palace on Saturday.

Max Dowman is the name everyone wants. He turned 16 in December 2009 and became the youngest player ever to win the Premier League at 16 years and 144 days, surpassing Phil Foden. He pre-agreed a professional contract in January that becomes effective on his 17th birthday on 31 December this year.

He has been given the number 56 shirt for 2026-27, went on the Girona training camp, and featured in all four pre-season friendlies, scoring and assisting in the 4-1 win at Girona. He was on the bench in Cardiff and did not play. Arteta on him, in January: “What he has done with us, me personally, I haven’t seen before.”

Arsenal Women

Seven In, Ten Out, and Three Weeks to Find a Team

Renee Slegers has rebuilt Arsenal Women almost from scratch after a season that produced a trophy, a runners-up medal and a European semi-final. The WSL starts on 6 September.

The scale of the turnover at Meadow Park this summer is hard to overstate. Seven players in, ten out, and among the departures are two of the most recognisable Arsenal footballers of the past decade. Beth Mead joined Manchester City on a free in May after nine years. Katie McCabe crossed London to Chelsea after eleven, a move made more pointed by the fact the two clubs had met in a bruising Champions League quarter-final weeks earlier.

Victoria Pelova went to Tottenham Hotspur, Laia Codina to West Ham United, Manuela Zinsberger to Borussia Dortmund and Rosa Kafaji to London City Lionesses.

The incomings are, on paper, an upgrade in profile if not in familiarity. Georgia Stanway arrived on a free from Bayern Munich on 3 July. Ona Batlle came from Barcelona on 10 July on a four-year deal with an option for a fifth. Geraldine Reuteler, the Switzerland midfielder, joined from Eintracht Frankfurt and took the number 14. Selina Cerci arrived from Hoffenheim, Lisa Baum, 19, from RB Leipzig, the 18-year-old goalkeeper Isabella Damm from Brondby, and Misa Rodriguez, the Spain international goalkeeper, on a free from Real Madrid. No fees were disclosed on any of them.

It is a rebuild launched from a position of frustration rather than failure. Arsenal finished second in the WSL on 51 points, four behind champions Manchester City, losing only one league match all season but drawing six, which is precisely how titles get away. Alessia Russo contributed 25 goals and assists in 22 matches.

In Europe, the holders reached the Champions League semi-final, beating Chelsea over two legs in the quarters before losing 3-1 in Lyon to go out 4-3 on aggregate. OL Lyonnes then lost the final 4-0 to Barcelona in Oslo. There was a trophy: Arsenal won the inaugural FIFA Women’s Champions Cup in February, beating Corinthians 3-2 in the final.

Renee Slegers signed a contract through the summer of 2029, announced in the spring, so there is continuity at the top even as the dressing room changes around her. Kim Little continues as captain with Leah Williamson as vice-captain. Mariona Caldentey set the tone for the season in an interview this summer: “It has to be our time.”

Pre-season has been mixed and is being played out partly at a warm-weather camp in La Manga. Arsenal beat Crystal Palace 2-1 in early August, Lisa Baum equalising and Chloe Kelly completing the comeback, with Slegers making a full eleven-player change at half-time. On 13 August they lost 3-2 to AC Milan, Selina Cerci scoring her first goal for the club before a late collapse. Two more friendlies remain: Paris FC at Meadow Park on Sunday and Charlton Athletic there on Wednesday 26 August.

Gunners Abroad

The World Cup Hangover, and Where Everyone Is

No international window this month, which makes August the right moment to account for a summer that gave Arsenal a World Cup winner, an England hat-trick and one badly damaged back.

Spain won the 2026 World Cup, beating Argentina 1-0 after extra time at MetLife Stadium on 19 July, Ferran Torres scoring the goal. They conceded once in the entire tournament, which no champion had managed before. David Raya, Martin Zubimendi and Mikel Merino were in that squad, which means Arsenal have three world champions in the building. Cristhian Mosquera, who made his Spain debut in March, is not believed to have made the final 26 and we could not confirm it either way.

England had their best World Cup since winning it, finishing third. They lost the semi-final 2-1 to Argentina on 15 July, and then beat France 6-4 in an extraordinary bronze final in Miami. Bukayo Saka scored a hat-trick in that match, two in the first half and a penalty, and earlier in the tournament matched the England record of three assists at a single World Cup, previously held only by David Beckham in 2002 and Harry Kane in 2022.

Declan Rice captained the side in the third-place playoff, scored inside two and a quarter minutes and set up Ezri Konsa’s header, becoming the first England player to score and assist in his first World Cup match as captain. It was his 80th cap. Eberechi Eze and Noni Madueke were also in the squad.

Martin Odegaard took Norway to their first World Cup since 1998 and then to a quarter-final, knocking out Brazil along the way before losing 2-1 to England on 12 July. He registered three assists in his first three matches, matching a record last set by Michael Ballack in 2002. It is the single best explanation for why he looked the way he did in Cardiff on Sunday: he has spent a summer being the best player on very good teams.

The rest is a mixed ledger. William Saliba came off after 30 minutes of France’s semi-final with the back injury that is still keeping him out. Gabriel Magalhaes and Gabriel Martinelli went out with Brazil in the round of 16, Martinelli having scored the winner against Japan a round earlier. Kai Havertz scored an equaliser for Germany against Paraguay in the round of 32 and then missed his penalty in the shootout, making him the first Arsenal player eliminated.

Viktor Gyokeres scored and assisted on his debut against Tunisia before Sweden went out at the same stage. Piero Hincapie was sent off after a VAR review as Ecuador lost to Mexico. Riccardo Calafiori did not go at all: Italy lost the European playoff final to Bosnia and Herzegovina on penalties and missed a third straight tournament. Jurrien Timber withdrew injured before a ball was kicked.

Christian Norgaard is at Everton and had not yet debuted; their season begins on 22 August. Fabio Vieira remains at Hamburger SV while the permanent deal is negotiated. Karl Hein is a Werder Bremen player outright after they triggered their €3m option.
