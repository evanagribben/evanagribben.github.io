The Lead

Stung by the derby, revived in Rotterdam: Inter face Como's test

A first league defeat exposed old habits. Three days later a patched-up side won 3-0 in the Champions League. Sunday's visit of Fàbregas's Como will show which version is real.

For ten minutes on Saturday night, Inter looked like a team that had forgotten what a derby demands. Christian Pulisic headed in after four minutes and Adrien Rabiot scored from a short corner after ten, both set up by Diego Moreira. By half-time Milan led 3-1 and the unbeaten league start was gone. Inter's website carried Chivu's call afterward to “rediscover our humility.”

The numbers made the result stranger. Inter had 57 percent of the ball, 19 shots to Milan's seven and an expected-goals tally of 1.68 against 0.62, according to FotMob. Alessandro Bastoni headed in a Calhanoglu corner after Akanji's flick, and a late penalty for a Gila handball was overturned by VAR. It was still Inter's eighth straight derby without a win.

Cristian Chivu did not hide behind the data. “We had the wrong approach,” he told Sky Italia, adding that Pio Esposito had been unwell and Bonny “had a fever until 4.30pm,” forcing him to improvise up front with Lautaro already injured (Football Italia). Marcus Thuram later spoke of a senior players' meeting and the need for “a change of mentality.”

The reply at De Kuip

Rotterdam on Tuesday was the answer Chivu wanted. Without Bastoni (suspended), Lautaro, Curtis Jones, John Stones and Luis Henrique, Inter beat Feyenoord 3-0. Yann Bisseck pressed, turned between two defenders and scored after eight minutes, his first Champions League goal in his 27th appearance (Football Italia).

Djed Spence crossed for Bisseck and then for Francesco Pio Esposito's header on 34 minutes, becoming the first English player to score or assist for Inter in the Champions League, per beIN Sports. Piotr Zielinski curled the third in off the inside of the post after 54 minutes. It was the club's 87th Champions League win, level with Milan.

What changed between Saturday and Tuesday

The shape changed first. In the derby Chivu used Diouf and Sucic behind a lone Thuram; in Rotterdam he went back to a 3-5-2 with Esposito beside Thuram and Zielinski and Dimarco restored. Carlos Augusto moved to left centre-back for Bastoni. Feyenoord had more of the ball (53 percent), but Inter created the better chances, with expected goals of 1.19 to 0.73 per FotMob.

The pressing changed too. Bisseck's goal came from a defender stepping out to win the ball high, the very aggression that was missing when Milan scored twice in ten minutes. Chivu's own warning stands, though: “Consistency brings results, confidence and belief, and that's what we're trying to find right now,” he told Inter TV.

Why Como matters

Sunday's opponent is the hardest possible check. Como sit fourth on 23 points, two behind Inter, and have won four straight league games. They share the best defence in Serie A with Lazio (eight conceded) and average 62.1 percent possession, second only to Inter's 62.4, per the club's opponent analysis. On Wednesday they won 9-1 at Lens.

The stakes are plain. A win keeps Inter within touching distance of Lazio, who lead on 28, and opens a five-point gap on Como. A defeat would drop Inter to fourth if Roma also win. Inter were unbeaten in four meetings last season (three wins and a 0-0), including a 4-0 at San Siro in December, but this Como side is a different animal.

The New Stadium

A 71,500-seat future: the Generali Stadium is unveiled

Inter and Milan presented the design for San Siro's successor, with a naming-rights deal reported as a record for Italy.

On October 29, Inter and Milan presented the new stadium at the Castello Sforzesco. It will be called the Generali Stadium, designed by Foster + Partners with MANICA, and hold 71,500 spectators, according to Il Post. The two clubs bought the old San Siro and its land from the city for 197 million euros in November 2025.

The money is large. Goal, citing Calcio e Finanza, puts the naming rights at about 650 million euros over 25 years, roughly 26 million a year split between the clubs. The build is estimated at around 1.2 billion euros; neither club has confirmed that figure.

Timelines vary. Il Post reports construction starting in 2028 and the ground ready before summer 2031, in time for Euro 2032, while earlier reports pointed to a 2027 start. Thursday, November 5, was the deadline for the clubs' company, Stadio San Siro SpA, to file its feasibility project with the city after a three-month extension; this edition could not confirm the filing.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Como: Fàbregas's possession machine arrives on a 9-1 high

Fourth in Serie A, winners of six straight and scoring for fun, Como bring the league's joint-meanest defence to San Siro.

Como arrive in rare form. In his third full season in charge, Cesc Fàbregas has them fourth on 23 points (seven wins, two draws, one defeat, a 2-0 loss at Frosinone). They have won six in a row in all competitions; the last five: 4-1 against Manchester United and 9-1 at Lens in the Champions League, plus Sassuolo, Torino and Venezia in Serie A (ESPN).

The system is a 4-2-3-1 with at least four attackers. Anastasios Douvikas scored a hat-trick at Lens and has five Serie A goals. Nico Paz is the creative hub after 12 goals and seven assists last season, with Martin Baturina and Lucas Da Cunha shooting from between the lines. Perrone, Milla and Caqueret rotate in the double pivot.

Where Inter can hurt them

Inter's own opponent analysis is candid about the risks: Como press high and have scored four goals from high turnovers. But they struggle against sides that press their build-up, as Torino and Feyenoord showed, and full-backs Yan Couto and Valle push so high that space opens behind them in transition.

That suits Bisseck's aggression and Spence's running. Como are also short up front: Diao and Kean are out and Chalobah is unavailable, with Kempf partnering Jacobo Ramón at centre-back, per the club analysis and Fàbregas's own remarks.

Transfer News

Renewals first: Dimarco tied down, Carlos Augusto close, Calhanoglu the puzzle

Inter's new sporting chief Dario Baccin has spent the autumn on contracts, and the week brought movement on three fronts.

Inter used their one-sided option to extend Federico Dimarco by a year to June 2028, which president Beppe Marotta confirmed on November 3. Sky's Gianluca Di Marzio describes it as a platform for a longer deal to 2029 or 2030; Dimarco has new agents who have met Baccin (Sky Sport Italia). Status: OFFICIAL option exercised, longer deal in talks.

Carlos Augusto, Inter's third-top scorer this season with four goals in all competitions by the Digest's count, is in advanced talks over a new contract to 2031. Matteo Moretto said on November 2 that November is decisive and Inter want it signed by Christmas (via FC Inter 1908). Status: ADVANCED TALKS.

The Calhanoglu question

Hakan Calhanoglu's contract ends in June 2027 with no option. Sky reports a first meeting between Baccin and agent Gordon Stipic but no offer yet: the player wants at least two years at close to his current 6.5 million euros net, while Inter prefer a lower fixed salary with bonuses (Sky via FC Inter 1908). Como and Juventus are named as interested. Status: TALKS OPEN.

Marotta told Sky on November 3 that Inter have a “very good rapport” with Calhanoglu, and that the club is “not looking for a fifth striker” despite the injuries (Football Italia).

 

Barcelona circle Bastoni again as Thuram's clause comes into focus

Two of Inter's most valuable players are the subject of reports that matter for next summer more than January.

Tuttosport reports that Barcelona are back in for Alessandro Bastoni, and that he wants clarity from Hansi Flick about his role before discussing a renewal; his deal runs to June 2028 (via Calciomercato.com). Status: RUMOUR, single outlet.

The same paper says Marotta and Baccin want to rework Marcus Thuram's 85 million euro release clause, which can be triggered each summer until July 15; his contract runs to 2028 (via FC Inter 1908). Status: EARLY TALKS reported.

The Squad

Chivu's balancing act: a stretched front line and a mentality reset

With the captain out until after the international break, Pio Esposito has become the reference point up front.

Lautaro Martínez suffered a left hamstring strain around the 3-4 home defeat by Club Brugge in the Champions League on October 13 and is expected back after the international break, with Italian reports pointing to the trip to Atalanta on November 22 (Calciomercato.com). One Yahoo roundup on November 5 claimed he could start against Como; three other reports say otherwise, and the Digest treats him as unavailable.

That leaves Francesco Pio Esposito and Marcus Thuram as the likely pair, with Bonny behind them. The good news: John Stones and Curtis Jones returned to group training on November 4 (FC Inter 1908), and Benjamin Pavard played the last minutes in Rotterdam.

Mentality, after the derby

The derby provoked unusually public self-criticism. Chivu said “it's not easy to play every three days with the same hunger,” and Barella, Bastoni and Thuram all called for a change of attitude, with Thuram describing a meeting of senior players (Football Italia). Rotterdam was the first test of that talk, and it passed.

Selection is the next storyline. Andy Diouf, used as an attacking midfielder in the derby, is tipped by Italian previews to start at right wing-back against Como, with Bastoni returning in defence. Bisseck has two goals this season and Carlos Augusto four, a remarkable return for two defenders.

The Academy

A tough week for the youth sides

The Primavera, the Youth League side and the Under-23s all lost, while Frattesi's loan at Lazio stalled.

The Inter Primavera lost 2-0 at home to Roma in Primavera 1 on October 31 (Inter.it). Emiliano Bigica's side had beaten Parma 2-1 the week before.

In the UEFA Youth League on November 3, the Under-20s lost 3-1 at Feyenoord. Vukaj scored Inter's goal on 20 minutes; Haps scored twice for the hosts, once from the penalty spot. Inter have four points from four games and host Stuttgart on November 25 (Inter.it).

Under-23s and loans

Stefano Vecchi's Inter U23 lost 1-0 at Cosenza in Serie C Group C on November 1 (Dalmazzi 26') and sit ninth on 17 points after 12 games, still in the play-off places (Inter.it). They host Salernitana on Saturday.

On loan, Davide Frattesi was rated 5.5 and substituted after 55 minutes in a 2-1 win over Cagliari on November 1 (Calcio Magazine). Mattia Massolin was an unused substitute for Cagliari. The Digest could not verify the other loanees' weeks.

Inter Women

Bugeja double keeps Inter Women in the title race

Five league games without conceding, a derby on Saturday and Manchester City in Europe on Tuesday.

Inter Women beat Lazio 3-0 at the Arena Civica on November 1. A Consolini own goal opened the scoring on 22 minutes and Haley Bugeja added goals on 35 and 58 minutes (FC Inter 1908). Bugeja recently became the club's all-time top foreign scorer.

It was a fifth straight league game without conceding for David Sassarini's side, who are second on 13 points, two behind Roma and two ahead of Como 1907 (FIGC).

Derby, then City

Next comes the derby away to Milan on Saturday, then the Women's Champions League. In Inter's first league-phase campaign they beat Häcken 1-0, drew with Austria Wien and lost 6-0 at OL Lyonnes on October 28. Manchester City visit on Tuesday, November 10, followed by Real Madrid away on November 18 (Inter.it).

Players Away

Sucic is in Croatia's 32-man preliminary list, and England name their squad on Friday, a decision that matters for the returning Stones and Jones. Lautaro will not join Argentina while he recovers. In Europe this week, Inter Women host Manchester City on Tuesday.
