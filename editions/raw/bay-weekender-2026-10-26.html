At a Glance

Five weeks, three holidays, and one deadline this Saturday

This is a long window and it has a clear shape. It opens with Halloween landing on a Saturday, which has pushed almost every good party indoors and spread the rest across both Friday and Saturday night. Then comes the real centre of the edition: November 2, when the Mission builds several hundred altars in a park and walks 24th Street by candlelight, and Oakland closes nine blocks of Fruitvale the day before. There are seven separate Day of the Dead observances here across four counties, and six of the seven are free.

After that the window turns athletic. The Warriors come home 0-3 for the longest homestand of their season, nine Chase Center dates in all. The Earthquakes play two final home matches with playoff seeding still live, and Bay FC says goodbye to a hard year on November 1.

Then the holiday machinery starts: a coffee festival, a tea festival, a chocolate salon, Dickens Fair, Lightscape, and four turkey trots in four counties on Thanksgiving morning.

One thing needs deciding this week rather than later. Dickens Fair's Early Bird pricing, which covers the opening weekends and saves ten dollars a head, is only on sale through October 31.

Weekend weather

Cool, grey, and a real chance of rain on Halloween

The National Weather Service's Bay Area forecast, issued Monday morning, puts San Francisco in the upper 50s on Saturday with a chance of rain, and the lower 60s on Sunday with a slight chance. Inland bay shoreline runs a few degrees warmer. There is also a coastal flood advisory in effect through Thursday afternoon, which matters for anything near the Embarcadero or Aquatic Park at high tide. Costumes on Saturday want a coat over them.

Act now

  • Sold out already. Brandi Carlile and Jessie Reyez at the Fox, Tuba Skinny and Haley Heynderickx at the Freight, Weston Estate at The Independent.

Pick of the Month

Day of the Dead in the Mission

Festival of Altars and the Ritual Procession, Monday November 2

Two events, one evening, and they are designed to be done together. From ten in the morning the community builds altars in Potrero del Sol Park, with a ceremony at sunset and a performance at eight. At seven the procession gathers at Bryant and 22nd and walks toward 24th Street with drums, Aztec dancers and several thousand people moving mostly in silence. Food vendors line the procession route rather than the park. Both are free.

Why this one

Most of what fills a five-week calendar can be caught again next year, or next month, or in another city. This cannot. The Marigold Project has run the Festival of Altars for more than three decades and it only works on November 2, only in that neighbourhood, and only because a few thousand people show up and build it themselves. It costs nothing, it asks something of you, and it is the single most San Francisco thing happening between now and December. Bring a photograph of someone you have lost.

Halloween falls on a Saturday this year, and the forecast says upper 50s with a chance of rain. Indoor options are doing well.

Seven separate observances across four counties, almost all of them free. The Mission is the centre of it but Oakland does the better food.

Dia de Muertos Celebracion at La Pena Sun Nov 1 · Free, donations encouraged Son jarocho, an ofrenda and Latin American home cooking in Berkeley. Free entry.

La Pena has been the Bay Area's Latin American cultural center since 1975, and its Day of the Dead night is small, warm and entirely unhurried. The ceremony is around 5:30pm, with son jarocho music after. Admission is free with donations encouraged; food and drink are sold inside, and the kitchen is the real draw.

Oakland Dia de los Muertos Festival Sun Nov 1 · Free, historically no admission charge Nine blocks of Fruitvale given over to altars, dancers and the Bay's best street food.

Trending and gone viral
The matcha queue, and the backlash to it. Kissaten Hifi at 189 6th Avenue in the Inner Richmond, Nagomi, and Q Specialty Coffee are all running lines, with reported waits around 25 minutes and a queue down the block at Q. Why it caught on: Kissaten Hifi opened in March 2026 as a Japanese-Filipino vinyl cafe with a banana cue latte, and the room itself became the draw as much as the drink. Here is the honest part. The same writer who documented the waits also said the matcha is good but not worth more than fifteen minutes of your life. Lines real, enthusiasm mixed.
Baklava is the city's new pastry obsession. Turquaz is running roughly a dozen varieties, with Cafe Velora doing pistachio and chocolate cherry and Kapari's doing pistachio and walnut. Why it caught on: it slots straight into the pistachio-everything moment, it photographs well, and SF's dessert conversation needed somewhere to go after the cookie and croissant cycles exhausted themselves. It registered as a named trend this autumn rather than on a single viral video.
Onigiri, everywhere. The Wild Fox is doing miso salmon, Do-Re-Mi is making them to order, and Tadaima is adding them at its third Sunset location. Why it caught on: cheap, portable, one item done properly, and it fits the citywide shift away from full restaurants toward counter-service specialists. This is a supply-side trend as much as a demand-side one.
Yemeni coffeehouses as the post-dinner move. Delah Coffee, Haraz Coffee House and Heyma are all open past 10pm, some until 1am at weekends. Why it caught on: San Francisco dinner service ends early and there was a real gap for late, alcohol-free, sit-down socialising. Of the four items here, this is the one that reads as a genuine behavioural shift rather than a photograph trend.
Up and coming
Hi NRG Coffee, 5801 Geary Boulevard, Inner Richmond. The strongest story in this section. Luis Gonzalez and Nathan Kruse ran what the Chronicle called the sceniest place to drink coffee in the city as an Inner Richmond pop-up, and the permanent shop opened quietly on September 16 with no announcement, grand opening a week later. Velvet curtains, a disco ball, a DJ booth, under-seat bins for your coat, and coffee, music and wine on the same menu. The interesting tension is the Chronicle's own framing: the pop-up was a scene, the cafe is for locals. Catch it before it tips.

Chef Kyle McGrath, raised in Bayview and the Fillmore and the grandson of two professional chefs, calls his food Pana Soul: Panamanian flavours, Southern comfort cooking and French technique, aimed at high-end results at an accessible price. No address and no date, so nothing to plan around yet.

Free museum Saturdays for Bay Area residents Sat Nov 7 · Free general admission for Bay Area residents The de Young and Legion of Honor are free to Bay Area residents every Saturday. Book ahead.

General admission at both Fine Arts Museums is free on Saturdays for Bay Area residents, which covers the permanent collections but not special exhibitions.
