The Lead
Six Days to Remake a Forward Line
Arsenal have won their first two matches of the title defence by an aggregate of 6-0 and have not conceded a goal. Underneath that placid surface, the champions are dismantling and rebuilding their attack with less than a week of the window left, and the outcome rests on two men who have not yet decided anything.

There is a particular kind of quiet that settles over a club that has just won the league. Arsenal have spent this month enjoying it. They beat Manchester City 3-0 in the Community Shield at the Principality Stadium on August 16, a match settled inside 24 seconds by Riccardo Calafiori and finished off by Kai Havertz and Martin Odegaard. Five days later they opened the Premier League season at the Emirates by beating newly promoted Coventry City 3-0 on a Friday night, and the visitors did not register a single tackle in the first half. Two matches, six goals, none conceded, and a manager who described the evening as having "a lot to like" about it.

And yet the most consequential Arsenal story of the past seven days did not happen on a pitch. It happened on the phone lines between north London, Riyadh and Madrid, and it concerns the shape of the attack that Mikel Arteta will take into a Champions League campaign that begins in a fortnight. The Premier League window closes at 11pm UK time on Tuesday, September 1, which is 3pm Pacific. That leaves six days for a set of decisions that will define the season more than any of the five signings already made.

The Two Undecided Men

The first is Gabriel Martinelli. David Ornstein of The Athletic reported on August 23 that Arsenal have placed a valuation of around 70 million euros, roughly 60 million pounds, on the Brazilian, and that the player is open to a move to the Middle East. Al Hilal have been circling for a fortnight. Sky Sports reported on Monday that the two clubs are closing on an agreement, with a sell-on clause understood to be part of the structure, and that the decision now belongs to the player himself. Martinelli turned down an approach from Galatasaray last week. He has been left out of both matchday squads so far this season. Whichever figure the deal eventually lands on, it will comfortably break Arsenal's record for a player sale.

The second is the man Arsenal would like to sign with the proceeds, and here the picture is genuinely unresolved. Fabrizio Romano said on Tuesday that Arsenal remain attentive to Julian Alvarez, that the club has an internal feeling it could reach agreement with Atletico Madrid on the financial terms, and that Arsenal will not lodge a formal bid until the striker signals he actually wants to come. That last clause is the whole story.

Alvarez has made no secret of preferring Barcelona, a move Spanish reporting describes as financially close to fiction, and the Argentine press is split on whether Atletico are desperate to sell or determined to keep him and rehabilitate him in front of a crowd that whistled him. Arsenal are not, on the current evidence, prepared to spend anything close to 150 million on a player who regards them as a fallback.

This is a more disciplined position than Arsenal have historically taken in the last week of a window, and it is worth pausing on. Andrea Berta, in his second summer as sporting director, has run a window of unusual decisiveness.

Piero Hincapie made permanent from Bayer Leverkusen for a reported 34.5 million in June, Illan Meslier taken on a free from Leeds United in July, Christos Tzolis bought from Club Brugge for a reported 34 million as the designated replacement for Leandro Trossard, Bruno Guimaraes prised out of Newcastle United for a reported 75 million, and then Ezri Konsa from Aston Villa on the eve of the season for 51 million plus 4 million in add-ons. That is roughly 198.5 million pounds of gross spending before deadline week has even started.

Ian Wright, on Gabriel Martinelli
What the Money Has Not Settled

What that spending has not yet bought is a resolution to the question Arsenal have been circling for two years. Havertz started the opener and scored, having also scored in Cardiff, which left Viktor Gyokeres on the bench and unused. The Swede scored 14 Premier League goals and 21 in all competitions in his first season, numbers that would flatter most centre-forwards and which somehow still left the debate open. Arteta's public position is that the two are complementary rather than competing. His team sheets over the past ten days suggest something slightly firmer than that.

Meanwhile the club is quietly working the other end of the ledger. Ethan Nwaneri, back from a difficult half-season on loan at Marseille, is the subject of loan interest from Newcastle United and others, with Arsenal understood to prefer a temporary move to a sale. Gabriel Jesus has one year left and has been linked with a Premier League exit. Fabio Vieira, Reiss Nelson and Kepa Arrizabalaga are all names the club would move on if the right offer arrives. There is more traffic to come through the gates at London Colney in the next six days than there has been all summer.

The context that makes it urgent is Europe. Arsenal lost the Champions League final in Budapest on May 30, drawing 1-1 with Paris Saint-Germain after Havertz had scored inside six minutes, and going out 4-3 on penalties after Eberechi Eze missed and David Raya saved from Nuno Mendes. Arsenal had four shots that night to PSG's eighteen.

That is the reason a club that has just won its first title in 22 years spent the summer buying a 75 million midfielder and a 55 million centre-back rather than resting on it. The league is being defended. Europe is being attacked. Both plans need a front line that is finished, and as of Tuesday lunchtime, it is not.

Konsa Goes Home on Monday Night
Arsenal's first road test of the season is at Villa Park, four days after signing Villa's best defender. It is also a meeting between a side who have not conceded and a side who shipped four in ninety minutes.

The fixture computer has an unkind sense of humour. On Monday, August 31, at 8pm UK time, which is midday Pacific, Arsenal travel to Villa Park with Ezri Konsa likely to make his debut against the club he served for seven years and left ten days earlier. He missed the Coventry match because he only completed his medical and signed that morning.

Aston Villa arrive in a considerably worse state than anyone expected. They were beaten 4-0 at Brighton on the opening weekend, sit twentieth on goal difference, lost their best centre-back to the champions, and will be without Joao Gomes through an automatic suspension after a red card for violent conduct in that Brighton defeat. Reporting in the West Midlands has Villa chasing Taylor Harwood-Bellis and Club Brugge's Joel Ordonez to plug the hole Konsa left.

For Arsenal the selection questions are more interesting than the opposition. Bruno Guimaraes missed the opener with a muscle problem picked up in the Community Shield, variously reported as thigh and groin tightness, and Arteta's assessment was pointedly relaxed: "We expect that not to be weeks at all." If the Brazilian is fit, Arteta must decide whether to break up a midfield that functioned beautifully at the Emirates, with Declan Rice and Myles Lewis-Skelly behind Martin Odegaard. Lewis-Skelly, playing in central midfield rather than at left-back, had 94 touches at 95 per cent passing accuracy.

At the back, Cristhian Mosquera partnered Gabriel Magalhaes in the absence of William Saliba and did so without alarm. Konsa's arrival gives Arteta a genuine alternative there and, usefully, a second option at right-back where Ben White has started both matches and provided an assist on Friday. Villa Park in late August, against a wounded and short-handed home side, is exactly the kind of fixture the champions dropped points in three seasons running before last year. That is what makes it worth watching rather than merely worth winning.
Transfer News
Martinelli and the Record That Is About to Fall
Status: advanced talks between clubs. Player decision pending.

Seven years after arriving from Ituano as a teenager nobody outside Brazil had heard of, Gabriel Martinelli is on the edge of leaving Arsenal for Saudi Arabia. The reporting has moved quickly and in one direction. David Ornstein established the frame on August 23: Arsenal value the winger at around 70 million euros, roughly 60 million pounds, and Martinelli is open to the move. Sky Sports followed on Monday with news that Al Hilal and Arsenal are close to agreement, with a sell-on clause of about 20 per cent of future profit expected in the structure.

Fee figures reported elsewhere this week have ranged from a 47 million pound opening bid up to a 55 million pound near-complete deal in the Daily Mail. Readers should treat any single number as provisional until the club confirms one. What is not in dispute is the direction of travel: Martinelli has been omitted from both matchday squads this season, he rejected a Galatasaray approach reported at around 38 million pounds, and the decision now sits with him rather than with either club.

It is a strange way for a significant Arsenal career to end. Martinelli was part of the squad that won the title last season, though he managed only one league goal in it, and James McNicholas of The Athletic has reported that the club never explicitly told him he was for sale even as it worked to find him a destination. Ian Wright, never a man to leave a Hale End-adjacent grievance unspoken, said this week that Martinelli deserves better treatment than he has received.

The financial logic is harder to argue with. Arsenal's previous record sale has stood since 2017. Selling a 25-year-old with reportedly one year left on his contract, for a fee in this range, to fund a marquee replacement, is precisely the sort of trade the club has spent a decade being told it should make. Konsa Signs, and Arsenal Buy Insurance They Already Needed
Status: OFFICIAL. Announced Friday, August 21.

Ezri Konsa is an Arsenal player, and the deal is the clearest statement yet of how seriously the club is taking the defensive attrition it carried out of last season. The fee is 51 million pounds fixed with 4 million in add-ons, taking the package to 55 million. Aston Villa had initially wanted 60 million. The contract runs four years with an option for a further twelve months.

Konsa is 28, an England international, and can play centre-back or right-back. He won more ground duels than any other player in the Premier League last season, a statistic that maps almost exactly onto what Arteta asks of his defenders. Andrea Berta called him "a defender of high quality, whose athleticism, composure and tactical intelligence have established him as one of the Premier League's most consistent performers." Konsa's own line was blunter: "I want to keep winning, hungry to win and that's what I'm here for."

Arsenal had reportedly considered Jarell Quansah of Bayer Leverkusen before settling on Konsa. The need was real: William Saliba is out until the winter and Jurrien Timber is not expected back until late September at the earliest. Full details at Sky Sports
The Alvarez Question Arsenal Cannot Answer Themselves
Status: concrete interest, no formal bid. Sources conflict sharply.

This is the thread that will decide whether Arsenal's window is remembered as good or as great, and it is also the one where readers should be most careful about what they believe. Here is what can be stood up. Fabrizio Romano reported on Tuesday that Arsenal remain attentive to Julian Alvarez, believe they could reach a financial agreement with Atletico Madrid, and will only make an official approach once the player indicates he wants to join. Atletico's asking price has been reported at around 150 million.

Beyond that, the Spanish and Argentine reporting diverges almost completely. Infobae, drawing on El Chiringuito and the English press, described Atletico as desperate to sell but unwilling to sell to Barcelona, and reported that Arteta has set one condition: the player must be genuinely committed to the move rather than treating Arsenal as a consolation. La Nacion, on the same days, reported almost the opposite, that Atletico refuse to negotiate below the release clause, that Arsenal and Atletico "never found common ground," and that Alvarez himself has reservations about returning to England after his time at Manchester City.

Both cannot be right. What both agree on is that Alvarez's preference is Barcelona, that Barcelona cannot afford it, and that the clock runs out on September 1. The Daily Mirror reported Arteta is prepared to sanction a push of around 130 million pounds with the same caveat about commitment attached. Treat all of it as live and unresolved. Nwaneri at a Crossroads, and Arsenal Say Loan
Status: loan preferred over sale. Multiple clubs circling. Single-sourced in places.

Two years ago Ethan Nwaneri was the most talked-about teenager in English football. He is 19 now, under contract to 2030, back from a loan at Marseille that did not go as planned, and needs to play. He was left out of the Community Shield squad entirely and named on the bench against Coventry without getting on.

The reporting this week says Arsenal have decided against a permanent sale and are working to place him on loan before the deadline. Newcastle United have been given permission to pursue a deal, with Everton and continental clubs also interested. Sami Mokbel of BBC Sport and the Telegraph have both reported that Arsenal are studying multiple loan offers at home and abroad. Separate reporting has linked AC Milan, RB Leipzig and Galatasaray with permanent or temporary moves, though those items sit lower on the reliability scale and should be treated as links rather than negotiations.

The logic on Arsenal's side is straightforward. Nwaneri is homegrown, which makes him valuable both for squad registration and, in accounting terms, as pure profit if ever sold. The Squad
The Champions Look Like Champions, With Caveats
Two clean sheets, a debutant with two assists, a captain who looks himself again, and a treatment room that still contains two of the best defenders in the country.

Arteta named this XI at the Emirates on Friday: Raya; White, Mosquera, Gabriel, Calafiori; Rice, Lewis-Skelly, Odegaard; Saka, Tzolis, Havertz. Havertz swept in a Calafiori cross on 15 minutes, Saka tucked in a deflected Tzolis cross on 23, and Odegaard made it three four minutes into the second half. Rice and Saka came off on 67 minutes with the game long settled.

The single most striking statistic of the night belonged to the visitors. Coventry City did not make a tackle in the first half, and did not touch the ball in the Arsenal box either. Opta's records, which begin in 2007, contain no other Premier League first half like it.

Their first tackle came in the 69th minute, by which point they were three down. Frank Lampard called it "a big learning curve."

Two individual notes matter more than the scoreline. The first is Christos Tzolis, who was excellent on his league debut, providing both first-half assists and drawing praise for relentless positional intelligence rather than raw speed. Arsenal bought him explicitly to replace Trossard and he looked, on one night, like an upgrade in dynamism if not yet in end product.

The second is Martin Odegaard, who told the club this week he is "feeling great" after a season disrupted by injury and form. He was man of the match in the Community Shield and scored again on Friday. An Odegaard operating at his ceiling changes what this team is capable of.

Elsewhere, Bukayo Saka revealed he took only seven days off after the World Cup, and his selection on Friday was read as a positive signal on the Achilles issue that has shadowed him. Myles Lewis-Skelly's conversion to a central midfielder now looks less like an experiment than a plan. And Viktor Gyokeres, a 63 million pound striker who scored 21 goals last season, watched all 90 minutes from the bench while Arteta gave late minutes to Mikel Merino and Eberechi Eze instead. That is the selection story of the autumn in one substitution pattern.

Hale End
A Bruising Start for Horseman, and a Schoolboy Rescues a Point
Three academy matches in eight days produced one thrashing, two draws, a red card, and a fair amount of encouragement buried inside all of it.

The U21s have a new manager in David Horseman, and his first competitive match was not one to frame. Arsenal lost 5-1 at Barnet in the EFL Trophy on August 18, with Knowles scoring a hat-trick for the hosts and Arsenal's only goal arriving via an own goal. Horseman fielded a makeshift back line, deploying the midfielder Teshaun Murisa at centre-back alongside Josh Ogunnaike with Brayden Clarke unavailable. Jeorge Bird, the most reliable observer of this age group in the country, noted mitigating circumstances (a thin pre-season, players away with the first team) while questioning the shape.

Four days later came the correction. Arsenal drew 1-1 with Crystal Palace in the opening Premier League 2 fixture at the Mangata Pay UK Stadium, Zavier Gozo putting Palace ahead on 50 minutes and Andre Harriman-Annous equalising on 64 after excellent control and a neat pass from Max Dowman. The lineup read: Porter; Julienne, Salmon, Upson, Ogunnaike; Ibrahim; O'Neill, Dowman; Stevens, Mooney, Harriman-Annous. Elijah Upson made an encouraging first appearance at centre-back before coming off at half-time, and Marli Salmon defended well and went close twice at the other end.

The U18s have had the more eventful week of the two. They lost 3-1 at home to Ipswich Town on August 22, undone by a Chris Atherton hat-trick, with Emerson Nwaneri scoring a 90th-minute consolation assisted by Joshua Tahou on his return from injury. Then on Tuesday they went to Blackburn Rovers in the U18 Premier League Cup and drew 3-3 in circumstances that read like fiction.

Oba scored inside the first minute. Blackburn scored three by half-time. Saurap Sampang pulled one back on 67, and the schoolboy Mikael Bonaventure Yetna, on as a late substitute, equalised in the second minute of stoppage time. Van der Straaten was then sent off and Arsenal held on with ten.

Names to keep an eye on this season: Max Dowman, already a first-team bench regular; Marli Salmon and Elijah Upson at the back; Andre Harriman-Annous and Archie Stevens in attack; Maalik Hashi, working his way back to prominence in the U18s; and Gabriel Arteta, the manager's son, who started against Blackburn. Arseblog reported this week that up to five further academy signings are expected to be confirmed before the season is out.

Arsenal Women
Seven In, Ten Out, and a 19-Year-Old Making a Case
Renee Slegers has rebuilt the oldest squad in the WSL around explosiveness. Two pre-season wins in four days suggest the idea is taking.

Arsenal beat Manchester City 2-1 in a behind-closed-doors friendly at the Sobha Realty Training Centre on August 20. Stina Blackstenius headed them in front from a corner, Lauren Hemp equalised with a curled finish, and then Selina Cerci released Lisa Baum to win it late. Three days later, in front of a crowd at Meadow Park, they beat Paris FC 1-0 with Olivia Smith cutting inside and finishing in the 91st minute. Georgia Stanway was excellent in both, Geraldine Reuteler impressed in a 30-minute debut cameo, and Alessia Russo had a goal ruled out for offside after coming on at half-time. Smilla Holmberg went off at the break with a knock.

Baum is the story worth telling properly. She is 19, arrived from RB Leipzig in July with Barcelona, Manchester United and Bayern Munich all reported to have wanted her, and has now scored in pre-season against the reigning WSL champions. She is also the clearest expression of what Slegers has been building.

Arsenal drew six league games last season and lost the title to Manchester City largely because they could not turn control into goals. The recruitment answer has been three Bundesliga forwards (Baum, Cerci from Hoffenheim and Reuteler from Eintracht Frankfurt) chosen for their ability to create a shot from nothing in tight spaces.

The two headline arrivals are Georgia Stanway, back in England from Bayern Munich, and Ona Batlle, signed on a free from Barcelona on July 10. Stanway's early public framing of the move has been notable in itself: she has described Arsenal as "setting the standard with women's football" and said the club is "more than just a football club." Two goalkeepers, Isabella Damm from Brondby and Misa Rodriguez from Real Madrid, have also arrived this month.

The outgoing traffic has been just as heavy and considerably more emotional. Beth Mead, Katie McCabe, Victoria Pelova, Laia Codina and Manuela Zinsberger have all left, with Mead joining Manchester City. Arsenal had the oldest squad in the WSL last season and used fewer players than anyone else in the division. That is no longer true. Leah Williamson and Steph Catley have both renewed, and Kim Little remains captain at 36.

The competitive season starts away at Brighton on Sunday, September 6, with the first home fixture against Crystal Palace a week later. Arsenal are in Pot 1 for the Women's Champions League league phase, whose draw takes place on September 4, with matchday one on September 22 and 23. Gunners Abroad
No Flags This Week, So Follow the Loanees Instead
There is no international window until September, and Arsenal's European campaign has not begun. The Gunners playing away from London Colney this week are the ones on loan.

With the World Cup finished in July and the next FIFA break not arriving until September, no Arsenal player featured on international duty in the past seven days, and the men's team's European season does not start until the Champions League matchdays open on September 8 to 10. That makes this a good week to take stock of the players Arsenal have out in the world.

Ismeal Kabia has joined St Mirren on loan and wasted no time. The winger, who spent last season in the Championship with Shrewsbury Town, completed the move in mid-August and made his competitive debut inside 24 hours, starting a Scottish League Cup tie against Rangers and playing around 59 minutes of a heavy 5-1 defeat. His manager has spoken publicly about being excited to show him off, which is a decent sign of how the loan was pitched.

Charles Sagoe Jr is in the middle of a calendar-year spell at Kalmar FF in Sweden that began in March. The 22-year-old has been a regular, with four goals and eight assists in 19 appearances, though he was quiet in Sunday's 4-0 defeat at Hammarby. Playing through a Scandinavian summer season means he arrives at the winter with a full campaign of senior football behind him, which is the entire point of the exercise.

On the women's side, Cecily Wellesley-Smith is on loan at FC Rosengard in Sweden until December. Expect this section to fill considerably next week: the September international window opens shortly, and with Saka, Rice, Konsa and Lewis-Skelly in the England picture, Odegaard captaining Norway, Saliba and Calafiori in the French and Italian setups and a large Arsenal contingent across the Lionesses squad, the club will be emptied out. In Brief

A bigger Emirates deal. Financial details of Arsenal's extended agreement with Emirates emerged this month: reportedly 65 million pounds a year for 2026 to 2028, rising to 75 million from 2028 to 2032, covering shirt front, training kit and stadium naming rights, with bonuses of 10 to 15 million for winning the Premier League or Champions League. Outgoing chief commercial officer Juliet Slot closed it before leaving the club later this year. Neither party had formally confirmed the terms at the time of reporting. Details

Two trophies in three years. The Community Shield win was Arsenal's second in three seasons, and Martin Odegaard was named player of the match after rounding Josko Gvardiol and Gianluigi Donnarumma to roll into an empty net.

Saka's short summer. The winger revealed he took just seven days off after the World Cup before returning to prepare for the season, which explains both his sharpness on Friday and, perhaps, why Arteta withdrew him on 67 minutes.

The pot that matters. Arsenal are in Pot 1 for Thursday's Champions League league phase draw, alongside Real Madrid, Bayern Munich and Liverpool. Five English clubs have qualified. The final is at the Estadio Metropolitano in Madrid on June 5, 2027.

Academy intake. Arsenal have confirmed their first-year scholars for 2026/27, and Arseblog reported this week that up to five further academy signings are expected to be announced before the season ends.

Villa's own window. Arsenal's Monday opponents are chasing Taylor Harwood-Bellis and Club Brugge's Joel Ordonez to replace Konsa, and will be without the suspended Joao Gomes.
