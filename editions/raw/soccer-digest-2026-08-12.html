The Weekly Soccer Digest

Six leagues, one continent’s worth of noise, and the paper trail behind it

Four of Europe’s big five are still in pre-season — La Liga opens Saturday, Serie A and Ligue 1 the following week, the Bundesliga not until 28 August. So this is a transfer-market edition by necessity, and it is a violent one: a Real Madrid club record, a Newcastle dismantling, a Barcelona balance-sheet rescue and a Dortmund–Köln staring contest over €55m. Arsenal are covered in their own companion edition and are carved out here. Correction to earlier practice: figures are given in the currency each club or reporter used; where outlets disagree, both numbers appear.

THE LEAD

The Reset at the Bernábeu

In one August afternoon Real Madrid broke their transfer record, ended the biggest contract standoff in the game and handed José Mourinho a squad built to win the league back. What they still do not have is the one player he actually asked for — and he appears to be going to Barcelona.

MADRID · ANALYSIS

On Thursday 6 August, Real Madrid published two official communiqués within hours of one another. The first confirmed that Yan Diománde, a nineteen-year-old Ivorian winger, had signed from RB Leipzig on a seven-year contract running to 30 June 2033 — the club’s own wording was that he “will be linked to our club for the next seven seasons.” The second confirmed that Vinícius Júnior had extended to 30 June 2032. Madrid disclosed no fee for the first and no salary for the second, which is the modern way of announcing that you have just spent more money than you ever have.

The Diománde number is where the reporting fractures. Eurosport put it at €130m; RPP and several Spanish outlets at €125m; Semana at €140m all-in with variables; Football365’s sterling table has it at £107m. The safe formulation — and the one this paper will use — is a base of roughly €125–130m rising toward €140m with add-ons, which makes it the most expensive signing in Real Madrid’s history and the third-largest fee of the entire 2026 window. He is a player with exactly one season of top-five-league football behind him. He arrived at Leipzig from Leganés only twelve months ago, in what remains that club’s record sale, then produced twelve goals and eight assists and was named the Bundesliga’s young player of the season. PSG walked away over the fee. Liverpool had a June bid rejected.

The Vinícius half of the day is the more consequential piece of business, because it closed a saga that had run for more than a year. He had entered the final twelve months of his deal earning a reported €15–17m net; several Premier League clubs had done the arithmetic on matching it and concluded they could not. Madrid, with Florentino Pérez personally involved, made a final offer and let the deadline do the work. The club’s statement described a player who “has become one of the most important players in one of the most successful stages of our history.” Vinícius, on Instagram, was less corporate: “Eight years at the Bernábeu is a short time, six more years and forever!”

“Eight years at the Bernábeu is a short time, six more years and forever!”

All of it sits underneath a manager who has been in the job nine weeks. Mourinho was appointed on 11 June 2026 — the club’s communiqué specifies a three-year deal “hasta el 30 de junio de 2029” — and reported for pre-season on 13 July. He is the third man in the dugout inside eight months: Xabi Alonso was dismissed on 12 January, álvaro Arbeloa took the season out as interim and has since gone to Fulham. It is Mourinho’s second Madrid spell, thirteen years after the first ended, and he inherits a club that has now gone two consecutive seasons without a major trophy — a sentence that reads like a misprint and is not.

The tactical objection writes itself. Madrid now have Kylian Mbappé, Jude Bellingham, Vinícius and Diománde — four players whose best work happens in the same two half-spaces, none of whom has ever been asked to defend a full-back for ninety minutes by a coach with any leverage. What Mourinho reportedly asked the board for was not another forward. It was a deep-lying organiser to structure the build-up, the single position in which this squad has been thin since Toni Kroos retired. Madrid have spent the summer chasing exactly that, and this week they appear to have lost.

Rodri — Spain’s captain, a world champion, and the man who took the Golden Ball at the tournament in July — has, on the reporting of the last seven days, chosen Barcelona over Madrid. Goal reported the Madrid strand frozen: Rodri wanted four years, Madrid’s wage offer “fell well short.” By 11 August Fabrizio Romano had it that the Barcelona deal was “advancing very well,” that the fee would exceed €60m and that “all parties are confident.” That is a remarkable thing for Madrid to have permitted: the outstanding defensive midfielder of his generation, in the final year of his contract, moving to the one club they most did not want him at, in a summer when they were willing to spend €130m on a teenager.

Zoom out and the Madrid spend looks less like an outlier than a symptom. This is a window in which the most expensive British footballer in history — Morgan Rogers, Aston Villa to Chelsea for £117m — is only the biggest deal by a nose, with Elliot Anderson’s £116m move from Nottingham Forest to Manchester City a whisker behind. It is a window in which Newcastle have sold three first-choice players for roughly £236m and in which the Saudi Pro League’s largest outlay is £55m — a sharp cooling on 2023. It is also a window playing out under UEFA’s 70 per cent squad-cost cap, which bit hard enough on Strasbourg in July to produce a €13m fine. Real Madrid, notably, registered Denzel Dumfries, Marc Cucurella and Ibrahima Konaté with LaLiga without recourse to any of the financial levers that have defined Barcelona’s last four summers.

And then there is the calendar, which has quietly done Mourinho a favour. Because Spain reached and won the World Cup final, LaLiga postponed four of the six opening-weekend fixtures. Real Madrid’s scheduled home game against Real Sociedad has been pushed to Wednesday 26 August; their first competitive match of the Mourinho era is now away at Espanyol on Saturday 22 August. That is ten more days to integrate a €130m nineteen-year-old, to decide who among four attackers is dropped, and to find out whether the midfield they wanted is instead going to be lining up against them at the Camp Nou in October.

Manchester City named their price. Barcelona have until Friday.

A €20m gap, a report-back date and a £120m domino sitting behind it. The Rodri auction is the most consequential unresolved deal in Europe.

The sequence, as far as it can be established: Barcelona’s opening bid — reported by Sky Sports at around £40m, or €45m plus €5m in variables — was rejected by Manchester City on Friday 7 August as, in the words of the report, “far too low.” City want upwards of £60m; the Spanish press has their ask at €70m (Depor) or €80m (El Español). By 11 August Barcelona had reportedly climbed to €67–68m and Romano had the deal “advancing very well.” Nothing is agreed.

Two hard constraints give the thing a shape. The first is the contract: Rodri is into the final year of his City deal, which expires in June 2027, and City would in principle rather renew than sell — a position Jamie Carragher put bluntly on Sky: “If you’re Manchester City, what’s the point in selling them? You might as well just keep them for the extra year.” The second is the calendar. Rodri is required to report back to City by Friday 14 August; Enzo Maresca, in his first summer as City’s head coach after succeeding Pep Guardiola, said this week that “it seems he will be in Manchester on Wednesday.” A player training at Carrington is a player with less leverage than a player refusing to.

Behind it sits the domino that would make this the story of the window rather than merely a story in it. City’s stated preference to replace Rodri is Enzo Fernández, and here the reporting is genuinely irreconcilable. Romano, on 12 August, has contact made through the agent Javier Pastore, with Chelsea holding at £120m and City “not expected to spend this money.” CaughtOffside the same day has City “confident” at £100m with no formal bid lodged. The Athletic, on 11 August, reports Chelsea now intend to keep him. This paper is not going to pick one. What is safe is that City are not moving for a midfielder until Rodri’s position resolves, and that every hour Barcelona spend closing a €15m gap is an hour Chelsea spend deciding whether they want £120m more than they want a No. 8.

For Barcelona the money has to come from somewhere, and it is coming from the squad — see the transfer pages below. For Madrid, watching the Golden Ball winner walk to their rivals having just spent €130m on a nineteen-year-old winger, this is the week’s quiet humiliation.

TRANSFER NEWS

Newcastle sell the captain, hire the wunderkind, and start again

Three first-choice players gone for roughly £236m, a 38-year-old head coach in the dugout, and a recruitment plan built on volume. This is the most radical thing any big club in England is doing.

Bruno Guimarães, Newcastle United’s captain, completed a move on Friday 8 August for a fee reported at around £75m (€87.5m), on a four-year contract with a further one-year option. It is the third major sale of a brutal summer on Tyneside: Sandro Tonali went to Tottenham on 6 July for a fee variously given as £92.5m (£85m plus £7.5m) and £100m — the outlets have not reconciled — and Anthony Gordon to Barcelona for around £69m. Add them up and Newcastle have banked something in the region of £236m from three players in five weeks.

They have also changed manager. Eddie Howe departed around 30 July after nearly five years, having delivered the club’s first major trophy in seventy years with the 2025 League Cup. His replacement, announced on 5 August, is Matthias Jaissle — 38 years old, two Austrian titles at RB Salzburg, back-to-back AFC Champions League Elite titles at Al Ahli. Newcastle did not disclose the contract length. “It is an incredible honour to become head coach of Newcastle United. This is one of the biggest clubs in European football,” Jaissle said, adding that he “fully and unreservedly” believes in the project. Sporting director Ross Wilson framed it in system terms: Jaissle’s “leadership style and football philosophy are strongly aligned with our sporting vision.”

The reinvestment is deliberately unglamorous and deliberately young: Bazoumana Touré from Hoffenheim (reported c.€49m), Sean Steur from Ajax (c.€27m), Lukáš Horníçek from Braga (£25.7m, 3 Aug) and James Trafford from Manchester City. Reporting on Tyneside points to six to eight arrivals in total. The live thread is Amar Dedić, the 23-year-old Bosnia right-back at Benfica who played under Jaissle at Salzburg: Romano reported club-to-club talks on 11 August with the player keen and no fee disclosed. Status: concrete interest.

What Newcastle are not doing is selling any more. They rejected an approach for Lewis Hall reported at £60m from Manchester United, with Romano relaying the club line verbatim — “we want the player to stay” — and by 11 August his own assessment was that the deal “looks impossible.” They have also made an early enquiry about Felix Nmecha at Borussia Dortmund, where the answer was emphatic. Sporting director Nils-Ole Book: “I would consider that highly unlikely… in Felix’s case, I can pretty safely rule it out this summer.” Dortmund’s asking price is reported at €100–120m; an €80m release clause activates in the summer of 2027. Status: rumour, publicly denied.

One coda worth marking: Pierre-Emile Højbjerg was, by the account of Foot Mercato, hours from joining Newcastle from Marseille for around €15m when he categorically refused. A source close to owner Frank McCourt said McCourt “ne cache pas son profond mécontentement face à certaines attitudes” and warned that if the club did not act, “les conséquences pourraient être extrêmement sérieuses.” Talks are dead. Newcastle open the season at home to Liverpool on 23 August.

Barcelona are selling to buy — and Atlético have gone to FIFA

Araújo to Anfield, Ferran to Paris, and a formal complaint filed in Zurich over the way the Julián Álvarez pursuit has been conducted.

Liverpool announced on 10 August that they had agreed a deal to sign Ronald Araújo on a season-long loan from Barcelona, subject to a work permit and international clearance; he takes the No. 33. The structure matters more than the headline. Per El Desmarque and Romano, Liverpool cover 100 per cent of a salary reported at around €16m gross (€11m net), and there is a non-obligatory purchase option set at €55m — a figure ESPN put in a headline and Liverpool have never disclosed. Araújo, whose Barcelona contract runs to 2031 and who was absent for weeks in December and January with documented mental-health difficulties, said: “I’m super-happy to be here and excited to get started. I’m glad about the interest and it was the right move at the right time.” Status: official.

The second sale is nearly done. Ferran Torres — the man who scored the winning goal in the World Cup final — is on the point of joining Paris Saint-Germain. Foot Mercato reported an “accord de principe” between the clubs on 11 August at €50m fixed plus €5m in bonuses, PSG having opened at €40m; Romano the same day had it at €50–55m with negotiations “entering final stages.” On 12 August, per Mundo Deportivo, Barcelona granted Torres permission to skip training in order to push the move through. Luis Enrique is reported to have phoned him personally to guarantee minutes in rotation. He scored 21 in 49 last season. Status: fee agreed, personal terms pending, not official.

Why the fire sale? Because LaLiga restored Barcelona to the 1:1 spending rule for this summer — driven by Robert Lewandowski’s wage coming off the books, by Spotify Camp Nou revenues running above projection in the first full season back, and by record VIP and seat-licence billing. But 1:1 is relief, not licence. To fund Rodri at €60m-plus, plus a striker, plus João Cancelo from Al Hilal (agreement with the player at around €10m; no agreement with the club), Barcelona need the wages off and the cash in. El Desmarque put the three-target bill at €230m on 11 August.

And then there is the fight. Atlético Madrid have filed a formal complaint with FIFA against Barcelona over the pursuit of Julián álvarez, alleging negotiation with a contracted player during a protected period — the complaint attributed to chief executive Miguel ángel Gil Marín. Barcelona’s reported offer of €100–130m expired with a 31 July deadline set by president Joan Laporta; Atlético point to a €500m release clause and have publicly refused to negotiate, having already turned down a reported €150m from Real Madrid. álvarez himself said in July that “lo mejor para todos es una transferencia.” He was due back at Atlético’s pre-season on 10 August. Status: stalled — and now a legal matter. Single-sourced on the FIFA filing; treat with care.

Dortmund have bid four times for Said El Mala. Köln keep saying no.

A nineteen-year-old winger, a €55m package, and a selling club with no financial reason to sell. The Bundesliga’s defining negotiation.

The ladder, as reported by Patrick Berger of Sky Deutschland and carried by Get German Football News: Borussia Dortmund’s first bid for Said El Mala was rejected; a second at €37m guaranteed plus €8m in bonuses was rejected; a third of around €50m with achievable bonuses was rejected on 8 August; and on 12 August a fourth arrived at €50m guaranteed plus €5m in bonuses. 1. FC Köln want €50m as a fixed fee and, crucially, have “little financial necessity to sell.”

El Mala — nineteen, thirteen goals and five assists last season — is willing. Dortmund’s internal backing is total: sporting director Nils-Ole Book, managing director Lars Ricken, chief executive Carsten Cramer and head coach Niko Kovač, who has spoken to the player personally. AC Milan are monitoring and could enter if Dortmund fail. Dortmund’s fallback is Giannis Konstantelias of PAOK. One caveat this paper will print rather than smooth over: 90min.de, on the same day, reported that no written offer has yet been submitted at all. Status: advanced talks; the reporting conflicts on whether a formal bid exists.

Dortmund have already spent this window. Konstantinos Karetsas, an eighteen-year-old attacking midfielder, arrived from KRC Genk on 3 August for €30m guaranteed plus €2m in bonuses, with Genk retaining a 10 per cent sell-on, on a five-year deal to 2031. Ricken: “With Kos, we’re picking up a player who, at just 18 years of age, already possesses tremendous ability.” Set that against Bayern’s summer — Nathaniel Brown from Eintracht Frankfurt for a Sky-reported €55m and Ismael Saibari from PSV for a Dutch club record (€50m or €55m, depending on the outlet) — and the shape of the German window is two clubs buying and sixteen watching.

Italy’s domino week: Frattesi, Spence, Piccoli — and Lukaku says goodbye

Serie A does not open until 22 August, but the market moved harder here than anywhere in Europe this week.

The one confirmed departure of real weight: Romelu Lukaku left Napoli for Fenerbahçe, made official on 12 August. Corriere dello Sport gives the most granular structure — €6m plus €2m in bonuses, with a further €1m if Fenerbahçe win the Turkish title — on a one-year contract at a salary slightly below the €11m gross he was on in Naples; Sportmediaset reported a flat €7m. Napoli’s farewell noted 45 appearances, 15 goals, a Scudetto and a Supercoppa. Fenerbahçe’s post read: “Big Rom, bigger dreams.”

Inter are the fulcrum of everything else. Davide Frattesi is going to Lazio on a €1m loan with a €14m obligation to buy on easy conditions and a further €3–4m in bonuses — and, per Gianluca Di Marzio, Inter retain 50 per cent of any future sale. Luis Henrique goes the other way in the capital, to Roma, at a base agreement of around €30m plus bonuses. Coming in: Djed Spence from Tottenham, described by Football Italia on 12 August as €31.5m plus bonus on a deal to June 2031 (Corriere has €30m plus €5m plus a 10 per cent sell-on), with a medical scheduled for 13 August. Inter must clear a non-EU roster slot to register him. Their first choice in midfield, should Frattesi leave, is reported to be Curtis Jones of Liverpool. Status: Frattesi and Spence fee-agreed; Jones concrete interest.

Elsewhere: Nahuel Molina completed a move from Atlético Madrid to Roma on 12 August at €13m plus €4m in bonuses. Trevoh Chalobah joined Como from Chelsea on 9 August for around €30m plus add-ons to 2031 — a genuinely startling sentence three years ago. Thomas Kristensen was in Bergamo for a medical on 12 August, joining Atalanta from Udinese on a loan with an obligation at €22m plus €3m, Udinese keeping 10 per cent of a future sale. And a chain runs through Tuscany: Roberto Piccoli to Bologna (loan with an €18m obligation, 10 per cent sell-on), which unlocks Mateo Pellegrino from Parma to Fiorentina at around €24m, which in turn frees Thijs Dallinga to go to 1. FC Köln on loan with an option.

The messiest file in Italy is Rodrigo Mora, the FC Porto forward wanted by Roma. In seventy-two hours, four incompatible structures were published: a €5m loan plus €45m option; a €7–8m loan plus a €42–43m obligation totalling €50m; a co-ownership at €25m for half the registration; and €25m plus 40 per cent of a future sale. Roma have insisted on no buy-back. This paper is not going to print a number. Status: advanced talks; fee genuinely unestablished.

One more, for the connoisseurs. Juventus want a goalkeeper and the mechanism proposed is baroque: PSG buy Zion Suzuki permanently from Parma for around €35m — Romano gave that strand a “here we go” on 11 August, with Foot Mercato reporting a five-year deal — and then loan him straight to Turin for 2026/27. Suzuki is said to be hesitant about signing for one club and playing for another, which is a reasonable position. Juventus are simultaneously in advanced talks for Jhon Lucumí of Bologna: personal terms agreed on 11 August, Bologna asking €20m plus bonuses, Juventus offering €18m plus Juan Cabal as a makeweight. And Dušan Vlahović, released by Juventus, signed a three-year deal at Bešiktaš on 12 August.

PSG rebuild the front line; Marseille are being taken apart by the accountants

One French club is spending European champions’ money. Another is selling everyone it owns because a regulator told it to.

Paris Saint-Germain have turned over their attack. Out went Gonçalo Ramos to AC Milan for a club-record sale — PSG confirmed the move; the fee is reported between €70m and €74m — plus Randal Kolo Muani to Juventus (€38m or €41.2m, unreconciled) and Kang-in Lee to Atlético Madrid at €40m. In came Maghnes Akliouche from Monaco on 6 August for a package reported at €50m including bonuses, a five-year deal to 2031 and the No. 11 shirt — “Signer au Paris Saint-Germain est une immense fierté,” he said — plus Lucas Digne from Aston Villa on 9 August. Ferran Torres and Suzuki, above, would make five and six.

Olympique de Marseille are doing the opposite, and not by choice. The DNCG’s ruling of 26 June imposed an “encadrement de la masse salariale et des indemnités de mutations” — a cap on both wages and transfer spending — after several seasons of nine-figure outlay. The consequence has been a summer-long liquidation: Mason Greenwood to Fenerbahçe for a reported €39–40m, Pierre-Emerick Aubameyang to Deportivo, and inside this window Facundo Medina to Bayer Leverkusen on 11 August (€23m plus €2m, five years) and Gerónimo Rulli to Manchester City on 12 August for €3.5m. Nayef Aguerd passed a medical at Real Sociedad on 12 August — a loan with an option, a year after OM paid €23m for him. Olympique Lyonnais and Monaco are also under salary-mass supervision.

The biggest live French story is a teenager. Ayyoub Bouaddi, eighteen, of Lille, is wanted by Manchester City as the long-term answer to the Rodri question. The Spanish daily AS put the price at €130m and described the deal as “en excellente voie”; Foot Mercato’s own line is more cautious — “plus de 100 M€.” Treat €130m as a newspaper number, not a club-confirmed one. Status: advanced talks, price unverified. Also live: Folarin Balogun, Monaco to Tottenham, with L’équipe reporting discussions opened and an estimate around €60m. And Paul Pogba, 33, is now a free agent: Monaco moved to terminate after a fresh thigh injury in training on 11 August, having got six appearances out of him last season.

MLS shops the veteran aisle: Lozano to the Galaxy, Alexis Sánchez to Montréal

The secondary window is open until 2 September and the roster rules were loosened for exactly this.

The most eye-catching move of the week was intra-league: Hirving “Chucky” Lozano joined the LA Galaxy on loan from San Diego FC on 7 August, through December 2026, as a Designated Player with an option to make it permanent. General manager Will Kuntz: “Hirving is a player whom we tried to acquire three years ago, so this feels like something of a full circle moment.” The Galaxy also signed Sergi Roberto on a free from Como on 8 August, through June 2028 with an option, and sold Edwin Cerrillo to Club América the same day.

CF Montréal signed Alexis Sánchez as a free agent from Sevilla on 11 August, as a Designated Player through 2027 with an option. Houston Dynamo took Marcelo Saracchi from Boca Juniors through 2028–29. Vancouver Whitecaps added the 21-year-old Serb Aleksa Cvetković from OFK Beograd on 12 August under the U-22 Initiative. Two DP slots have just opened and both clubs have said so on the record: Atlanta United, after loaning Emmanuel Latte Lath to Union Berlin — chief soccer officer Chris Henderson: “This move also opens up a Designated Player spot for us which we will look to fill in this summer window” — and St. Louis CITY, after selling Marcel Hartel to Hannover 96 on 11 August.

Traffic runs the other way too. Max Arfsten left Columbus Crew for Middlesbrough on 4 August with the Crew retaining a sell-on; Olwethu Makhanya, the Philadelphia Union’s first-ever U22 Initiative signing, has an agreed move to Rangers. And for context on the scale of what MLS is now buying: Robert Lewandowski joined the Chicago Fire from Barcelona on 29 June as a Franchise Player through 2027–28, and scored his first Leagues Cup goal on 9 August.

THE RUMOUR MILL

What is simmering, and how hot

Everything below is unresolved as of 12:00 Pacific on Wednesday. Certainty is stated for each. Nothing here is official.

Cristian Romero, Tottenham → Atlético Madrid. The best-sourced saga in Europe not involving Rodri. Atlético’s opening €30m was rejected; Spurs want €40m. Per Esto es Atleti on 11 August, the clubs and the agents have been meeting since Monday and “lo que queda por cerrar es el salario y los bonus de la operación, no la voluntad del jugador.” Romano reports Inter’s €40m alternative collapsed when they could not sell Benjamin Pavard, and that Romero has given Atlético the green light. Personal terms agreed; clubs apart on fee.

Bradley Barcola, PSG → Liverpool. Framed as the succession to Mohamed Salah, who left Anfield this summer. Quoted around £130m. Romano reported on 10 August that PSG had responded to Liverpool’s indicated proposal and that club-to-club talks are live. Concrete interest; no agreement.

Enzo Fernández, Chelsea → Manchester City. Three mutually exclusive reports in twenty-four hours (see the secondary feature above). Contact made; everything beyond that is contested.

Carlos Baleba, Brighton → Manchester United. Paused. Baleba damaged ankle ligaments in pre-season and is expected to miss the start of the season; Brighton are “hopeful it is not long-term.” United have instead spent on Andrey Santos and Youri Tielemans and are now prioritising a left-back. On ice, injury-driven.

João Palhinha, Bayern → Aston Villa. Romano reported personal terms agreed on 12 August, with the player having told Bayern he wants to go. Bayern want around €25m against the roughly €51m they paid in 2024; a loan with an option remains possible. Villa’s head coach publicly acknowledged the interest on 8 August. Personal terms agreed.

Kaishu Sano, Mainz → RB Leipzig. Leipzig’s interest accelerated by Christoph Baumgartner’s injury. Contract to 2028, no release clause, and a Mainz chief executive in no mood: Christian Heidel — “You wouldn’t even get one of his legs for €25m” — also calling Sano “the heart of our game.” Liverpool are among others watching. Concrete interest.

Rafael Leão, AC Milan → Galatasaray. A bid of €30m plus €5m was rejected on 6 August as inadequate, per Di Marzio; Galatasaray are preparing to go again. Leão has told Milan he wants to stay and, on 11 August, that “nobody other than my family and lawyers are authorised to speak for me.” Bid rejected; player resisting.

Exequiel Palacios, Bayer Leverkusen → Ipswich Town. A genuine Romano exclusive on 9 August — Ipswich lodged an official bid, with four other Premier League and Bundesliga clubs in the race. By 12 August the bid had been rejected: Leverkusen want €35m and the World Cup winner is reported to want a bigger club. Ipswich have instead agreed a fee with Strasbourg for Julio Enciso. Bid rejected.

Gabriel Jesus → Napoli. Sky Sport Italia placed his agent at Napoli’s training camp on 8 August, with the player said to have approved a move conditional on two Napoli departures. The agent publicly denied talks on 7 August. Rumour, denied. Napoli’s defensive alternative, Benoît Badiashile from Chelsea, has stalled over the formula — Napoli offered a €3m loan with an option; Chelsea raised their price on 12 August.

Mika Baur, Paderborn → Celtic (medical expected 12 August, package reported up to €8m) and Fabio Baldé, HSV → Strasbourg (€7m plus bonuses, travelling to France on 12 August) are the two Bundesliga-adjacent deals most likely to be official by the time you read this. Medical stage.

And one to file away. On 11 August Fabrizio Romano — the single most-read transfer reporter alive — wrote on LinkedIn that he is “probably not gonna continue into the transfer market industry as I’m doing right now for longer: it’s time to change the approach, the view.” He said he would say more after the window closes on 3 September. The post followed a public argument on X over the reporting of the Diománde fee. Single-sourced; his own words, via LinkedIn.

AROUND THE LEAGUES

England · Premier League

The season opens on Friday 21 August and it will begin with the most comprehensively rebuilt set of dugouts in the competition’s history. Twelve of the twenty clubs will start with a different head coach from the one who finished last season. Pep Guardiola has gone from Manchester City and been replaced by Enzo Maresca. Andoni Iraola is at Liverpool. Xabi Alonso, sacked by Real Madrid in January, is at Chelsea. Michael Carrick was made permanent head coach at Manchester United on 22 May, replacing Ruben Amorim, who is now at Milan. Oliver Glasner has moved to Nottingham Forest, Pierre Sage to Crystal Palace, Marco Rose to Bournemouth, álvaro Arbeloa to Fulham, and Matthias Jaissle to Newcastle. The three promoted clubs — Coventry City, Hull City and Ipswich Town — have brought Frank Lampard, Sergej Jakirović and Gary O’Neil with them. Only David Moyes at Everton and Unai Emery at Aston Villa feel like fixed points. For the standings context that shapes all of it: the 2025/26 title went to Arsenal on 85 points, and West Ham were relegated in eighteenth on 39, alongside Burnley and Wolves.

The pre-season evidence is thin and mostly unflattering. Liverpool lost 3–2 at home to Monaco on 9 August in front of 60,216 — Alexander Isak on 16 and Florian Wirtz on 29, then Aleksandr Golovin from the spot on 44, Mika Biereth on 56 and Paris Brunner on 88 — in Iraola’s first game at Anfield. He made eleven changes at half-time and was unusually candid afterwards: “We played a good first half but we don’t have much more in terms of the level required to play like this,” adding that his players “probably don’t have the energy for 90 minutes.” Manchester City beat Atlético Madrid 3–1 in Seoul the same day, Omar Marmoush scoring twice in two minutes with both goals made by Antoine Semenyo — “without him, these goals wouldn’t have been possible; I just had to tap it in,” Marmoush said. Chelsea drew 3–3 with Johor Darul Ta’zim in Malaysia, Liam Delap scoring two penalties. Manchester United drew 1–1 with PSG in Gothenburg on 8 August, Bryan Mbeumo cancelling out Ibrahim Mbaye; Carrick called it “a good challenge… we should’ve scored more goals.” The season’s first silverware is on Sunday: the FA Community Shield, unusually at the Principality Stadium in Cardiff rather than Wembley.

Off the pitch, two structural items matter more than any signing. The first is that PSR is gone: clubs have approved a Squad Cost Ratio system to replace it, while voting down a proposal that would have introduced a salary cap. The second is that Chelsea are operating under a March 2026 settlement with the league — a £10.75m fine and a suspended one-year transfer ban over Abramovich-era payments — which lends a certain edge to a summer in which they have spent £117m on Morgan Rogers and this week added Pep Chavarría from Rayo Vallecano for €19m plus €2m on a five-year deal. In squad news: Micky van de Ven signed a new long-term contract at Tottenham on 10–11 August (“I’ve always loved Spurs, from the first day I stepped in here”); Crystal Palace and Everton executed a straight player-for-player swap on 11 August, Brennan Johnson to Goodison and Dwight McNeil to Selhurst Park, both on four-year deals with no cash element disclosed; and Fulham signed Shea Charles from Southampton for an undisclosed fee widely reported at £30m. The injury list is already ugly: Amadou Onana has ruptured a cruciate ligament at Aston Villa, and Carlos Baleba will miss the start of the season at Brighton.

Spain · LaLiga

Spain is first of the big five out of the blocks — Saturday 15 August, Alavés against Getafe — but only in a technical sense, because LaLiga has postponed four of the six opening fixtures to protect the workload of players who went deep into a World Cup that Spain won. Atlético v Málaga moves to 19 August; Valencia v Real Betis to the 25th; Real Madrid v Real Sociedad to the 26th; Barcelona v Athletic Club to the 27th. The practical effect is that both giants begin away from home in matchday two.

The league itself has changed shape. Girona, the romance story of 2024, are down. Up come Racing de Santander as second-tier champions after fourteen years away, RC Deportivo, and Málaga CF through the play-offs. Racing marked the return by prising Julen Agirrezabala out of Athletic Club permanently — a deal unblocked in forty-eight hours by restructuring around a buy-back clause and a sell-on percentage after Athletic refused a loan and held out for around €7m.

The genuinely underreported Spanish story is the widening gap between the two economies inside one league. Sevilla are operating on a salary limit of roughly €22m — the lowest in the division — and sold Akor Adams to Venezia for €16.8m plus variables purely to create room, having already lost Fede Viñas to Toluca for a figure roughly double their own valuation. Valencia’s limit was raised to €95.6m and they still lost Thomas Meunier to Sunderland because they could only offer a year plus an option against two guaranteed. At the other end, Barcelona have been restored to the 1:1 rule on the back of Lewandowski’s wage coming off the books and a Spotify Camp Nou returning revenue above projection in what is the first full season back at the rebuilt stadium, with a target capacity of 104,600. Whether the VIP-seat income is being counted twice in that arithmetic is an argument currently running in the Catalan press, and it is a better story than most transfer rumours. Barcelona’s Joan Gamper Trophy — against Al Ahly, on 19 August — will be the competition’s first at the Camp Nou since the rebuild.

Germany · Bundesliga

Germany does not kick a competitive first-division ball until Friday 28 August, and the week’s biggest German story was decided not on a pitch but at the Bundeskartellamt. The Federal Cartel Office closed its long-running antitrust proceeding into the 50+1 rule and found for the rule — president Andreas Mundt: “Die 50+1-Regel kann weiterhin mit dem Ziel der Vereinsprägung und der Mitgliederpartizipation gerechtfertigt werden.” But it found against the way the rule has been applied. Four clubs were given homework: Bayer Leverkusen and VfL Wolfsburg must reform the benefactor exemptions tied to Bayer AG and Volkswagen; RB Leipzig must give supporters genuine access to ordinary voting membership; and Hannover 96 were criticised over the handling of Martin Kind’s supervisory-board vote in a 2023/24 secret ballot. The DFL must now equalise membership access across all thirty-six clubs and apply 50+1 consistently in its own internal voting. No deadline has been set; the DFL Präsidium will consult the clubs over the coming weeks. This is the most consequential governance ruling in German football in a decade and it went almost entirely unreported outside Germany.

The division has a new look: Wolfsburg were relegated for the first time in their history after twenty-nine consecutive top-flight seasons, along with St. Pauli and Heidenheim. Up come SV Elversberg, making their Bundesliga debut as the 59th club in the league’s history, Schalke 04 after three years away, and SC Paderborn. Schalke marked the return in the most Schalke way imaginable: 40,000 retro away shirts, released on 6 August with the historic crest, sold out in two and a half hours. Chief executive Matthias Tillmann: “You can’t plan something like that, it’s just crazy” — and, relaying what an Adidas employee told him, “you’re the boss of one of the craziest clubs in Europe.”

Bayern Munich, meanwhile, spent the week lobbying. President Herbert Hainer said flatly that “the Bundesliga is clearly, clearly in the background compared to the Premier League” and that “internationalization cannot be a solo effort by one individual club”; marketing chief Rouven Kasper noted that only nineteen of thirty-six clubs attend the league’s annual meetings. Bayern also look set to keep the architect of their window: Max Eberl is close to an extension beyond summer 2027, with the supervisory board due to decide on 25 August and Uli Hoeneß putting the odds at “100 percent” while crediting Eberl with “a perfect transfer period.” The season proper begins with the Franz Beckenbauer Supercup — Bayern against Borussia Dortmund at Signal Iduna Park on 22 August — followed by the DFB-Pokal first round, in which Bayern and Dortmund have been held over to 1–2 September.

Italy · Serie A

Serie A opens on 22 August, defending a title Inter won for the twenty-first time, but Italian football restarts on Friday with the Coppa Italia round of 32. The managerial map is barely recognisable: Massimiliano Allegri is at Napoli, Ruben Amorim at Milan, Luciano Spalletti at Juventus, Maurizio Sarri at Atalanta, Gennaro Gattuso at Lazio, Fabio Grosso at Fiorentina, Domenico Tedesco at Bologna, Ivan Jurić at the promoted Monza and Ignazio Abate at Torino. Cristian Chivu holds at Inter and Gian Piero Gasperini at Roma, where he is already complaining about squad depth: “There are teams that can’t do business because they’re full of players; we have too few.”

Milan are the club with the most audible friction. Mike Maignan is reported unhappy with the club’s direction and has delayed his return from holiday, with Al-Nassr circling; Adrien Rabiot also extended his break to the week before kick-off. Amorim has confirmed that squad cuts are coming and is studying a role for Rabiot as a trequartista behind Gonçalo Ramos. Napoli, by contrast, are settling: Giovanni Di Lorenzo has agreed an extension to June 2030, described in the Italian press as a rinnovo a vita after 305 appearances, with only the signature outstanding, and Alex Meret said on 12 August the squad is “very happy” under Allegri. Lorenzo Pellegrini’s extension at Roma to 2027 was made official on 9 August.

Elsewhere: Atalanta lost Kamaldeen Sulemana to a serious knee injury in training on 12 August, out at least two months. Juventus beat Palermo 2–0 in Perth on 11 August through Kenan Yıldız on 44 and Arkadiusz Milik on 86. Daniele Orsato, briefing on the 2026/27 refereeing protocol, restated the standard as “Var solo in caso di chiaro ed evidente errore.” And a story worth chasing rather than printing at length: Football Italia reported on 11 August that Lazio were forced to terminate a €19m sponsorship with Polymarket after the platform was declared illegal in Italy. That is single-sourced and this paper flags it as such.

France · Ligue 1

France returns on Friday 21 August, Marseille against Strasbourg, with every match of the opening round streaming exclusively on the league’s own platform, Ligue 1+ — a wholesale rewiring of French football’s economics that passed a million subscribers within a month of launch and remains the subject of an open argument about whether the model works. The division has eighteen clubs: Nantes went down after thirteen years up, alongside Metz; Le Mans and Troyes came up. Nice survived the play-off, beating Saint-étienne 4–1 on aggregate. PSG won a record fourteenth title, their fifth in a row, clinching on 13 May.

The story of the French summer is Olympique Lyonnais. Michele Kang, via Olympe Bidco, acquired 87.78 per cent of the club from Eagle Bidco for $30m in a deal announced on 23 June, subject to regulatory clearance since granted. The transaction eliminated roughly €232.6m of debt, extended creditor repayment deadlines by eighteen months, and commits up to €71m of new capital over two years with €31m injected on completion. John Textor’s Eagle Football Group is out; the holding company reverts to the name Olympique Lyonnais Groupe. Kang, who already controls the women’s club, called it “le plus grand défi de ma vie.” The men’s and women’s clubs will continue to be managed separately.

The dugouts have turned over almost as hard as England’s: Davide Ancelotti to Lille, Dino Toppmöller to Lens, Will Still to Auxerre, Olivier Pantaloni to Nice, Alexandre Dujeux to Lorient. And French football is still absorbing a loss: éric Roy, head coach of Stade Brestois, died on 17 June aged 58. Julien Lachuer has succeeded him. The season’s first trophy is on Sunday, when Lens host PSG in the Trophée des Champions at Bollaert-Delelis, Lens qualifying as Coupe de France winners.

United States · Major League Soccer

MLS is the only one of the six actually playing, and it has spent the week inside Leagues Cup, which runs from 4 August to a final on 6 September with all thirty-six MLS and Liga MX clubs in two league-wide tables. It has not gone well for the American league. Vancouver and Minnesota are out; Charlotte were eliminated on 11 August by an Alan Bautista goal in the first minute of stoppage time off a miscued Tim Ream pass — head coach Dean Smith: “a lapse in concentration on the edge of the box cost us everything” — and FC Cincinnati effectively bowed out the same night. Seattle, the holders, were beaten 3–0 at altitude in Toluca on 5 August with Stefan Frei making fourteen saves; Brian Schmetzer was blunt: “Toluca was the better team. The altitude and their intensity hurt us.”

Lionel Messi made his first start since the World Cup on 5 August and scored twice in Inter Miami’s 4–2 win over Atlético de San Luis, becoming the competition’s all-time leading scorer on fourteen career goals; Noah Allen registered three assists and the match was suspended for around half an hour in the 67th minute for weather. Miami then lost 2–1 at home to Monterrey on 8 August despite Rodrigo De Paul’s goal from a Casemiro assist, both Monterrey scorers being former MLS players, and go into tonight’s meeting with Club León needing to win in regulation to survive. Luis Suárez is unavailable, serving a six-game Leagues Cup suspension. Elsewhere Myrto Uzuni scored a first-half hat-trick for Austin FC against Puebla on 9 August, and Robert Lewandowski scored his first goal in the competition for Chicago.

In the league itself — only one fixture was played in the window, Houston’s 2–0 win at New England on 8 August, their third consecutive clean-sheet victory, and Jack Harrison’s Revolution debut. All thirty clubs return on 15–16 August. Two administrative notes worth filing: Austin FC have promoted Andy Loughnane from club president to chief executive with oversight of both business and soccer, founder Anthony Precourt moving to chairman; and Martí Cifuentes has taken over at Portland, bringing assistants Xavi Calm and Javi Ortiz from Leicester. The wider frame: 2026 is the last full spring–autumn season, with a one-off fourteen-game “Sprint Season” from February to May 2027 before the league restarts in July 2027 on a summer–spring calendar.

IN BRIEF

UEFA changed two rules on 6 August. The yellow-card suspension threshold in all three club competitions rises from three bookings to four — the rationale being that the expanded league phase guarantees at least eight matches before the knockouts — and stadium clocks will now count upward through added time rather than freezing at 45:00 and 90:00.

Olympiacos are out of the Champions League. NEC Nijmegen beat them 2–1 after extra time on 11 August to win the tie 2–1 on aggregate, the biggest scalp of the third qualifying round. Bodø/Glimt edged Union Saint-Gilloise 6–5 across two legs, and Lyon recovered from a first-leg defeat to beat Sparta Praha 4–2 on aggregate. The play-off round pairs Lyon with Fenerbahçe and Celtic with LASK; the league-phase draw is on 27 August.

Benfica put six past Hearts in the Europa League third qualifying round on 6 August, while Rangers lost 2–1 away to Jagiellonia Białystok and Maccabi Tel-Aviv were beaten 3–0 at home by CSKA Sofia. Ajax, meanwhile, are in Conference League qualifying, where they beat Shelbourne 3–1.

The Saudi Pro League season starts on Thursday. Cristiano Ronaldo, now 41, begins it on 976 career goals — twenty-four short of a thousand — having scored 28 as Al Nassr won the title. Nine SPL clubs changed manager over the summer; Ange Postecoglou is now in charge at Al Nassr.

Paul Pogba is a free agent. Monaco moved to terminate his contract, which ran to 2027, after a thigh injury in training on 11 August; he made six appearances last season. A financial settlement is still being negotiated and is expected to be resolved by 1 September.

Chelsea are locking down the academy. Shumaira Mheuka, the 18-year-old Premier League 2 player of the season, is to sign a long-term deal to 2033 before going out on loan; the club has also handed professional or improved terms to eight further academy players.

Charlotte FC lost two players to surgery on 6 August. Nimfasha Berchimas is out roughly twelve weeks with a broken foot and Aron John around five months after a shoulder operation.

Christoph Baumgartner missed the World Cup after a muscle injury sustained warming up for an Austria friendly, has had a successful procedure and is rehabilitating at RB Leipzig — the injury that has driven Leipzig’s pursuit of Mainz’s Kaishu Sano.

And a note on what we could not stand up. Reporting derived from Kicker in the last week refers to Jürgen Klopp as Germany’s new head coach following the World Cup exit to Paraguay. This paper could not obtain a DFB announcement or a second independent confirmation and is therefore not running it as fact. If it is true, it is next week’s lead.
