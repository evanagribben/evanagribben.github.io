The Lead

Two Nights at the Metropolitano, One Hard Lesson

Bayern punished one mistake, Barcelona punished two. In five days a seven-game league run ended, the Champions League table turned awkward and Simeone admitted what everyone could see: the elite are still a step ahead.

For seven weeks Atlético had looked like a side built for more than third place. Seven straight La Liga wins, a 2-1 derby victory over Real Madrid and a 2-0 Champions League win over Manchester United had made the new-look squad feel finished. Then came the two biggest home nights of the autumn, and two defeats.

On Tuesday Bayern won 2-0 at the Riyadh Air Metropolitano. On Sunday Barcelona won 2-1 at the same ground, with Fermín López scoring either side of the break after Giuliano Simeone had put Atlético ahead. The international break now arrives with Atlético third on 28 points, five behind the leaders.

Bayern: control without a cutting edge

Simeone said his side had Bayern exactly where they wanted them. For 53 minutes that was arguable. Atlético sat in a compact 4-4-2 with Sørloth a surprise starter beside Julián Álvarez, and Bayern, led from the touchline by assistant Aaron Danks with Vincent Kompany absent on medical grounds, were kept largely in front of the block (ESPN).

The game turned on one moment. Le Normand was slow on the ball in his own area, Lennart Karl nipped in and lifted it over Oblak on 54 minutes (beIN). Karl, 18, became only the fourth player to reach five Champions League goals before turning 19. Konrad Laimer added the second from a Harry Kane pass on 74.

The numbers back up Simeone's reading of the first hour but not of the night. ESPN's data gave Bayern 1.77 expected goals to Atlético's 0.83, and 58 percent of the ball. Oblak made five saves, one of them tipping an Upamecano shot onto the bar. It was Atlético's first home defeat to Bayern in the Champions League (MundoNow).

Barcelona: the better performance, the same result

Sunday was different in feel. Simeone rehearsed the XI on Friday and made two changes, dropping Le Normand and Sørloth, moving Llorente to the right of a back four and pushing Lee Kang-in into a free role behind Álvarez (Comuniate). It worked early: Álvarez crossed and Giuliano scored on 10 minutes.

Álvarez had already been played through one on one and denied by the Barcelona goalkeeper, and Pubill and Pedri both hit the crossbar before the break. Then Fermín equalised on 45 minutes, and on 66 Lamine Yamal slipped him through for the winner (ESPN). Atlético had 37 percent of the ball but matched Barcelona almost exactly on expected goals, 1.53 to 1.60.

What the week actually tells us

The common thread is not effort or shape. It is margins at both ends. Bayern needed one Atlético error; Barcelona needed two moments of quality. At the other end Atlético created enough against Barcelona to win and finished like a side that had stopped expecting to. Álvarez, to the disgruntlement of some home fans, missed the one-on-one and lobbed over on 55 minutes (ESPN).

Simeone backed him without hedging. "Le queremos y para llegar donde queremos necesitamos al mejor Julián Álvarez. Si no, no vamos a llegar," he said (El Gol Digital). He called his side a team "en construcción" and said he was proud of the players. That is the honest frame: a squad rebuilt with roughly €123m of summer signings (El Desmarque's tally) is good enough to beat most of La Liga, not yet to beat Europe's best on a bad night.

The European picture is the real concern. Atlético are 23rd of 36 on four points, inside the knockout play-off places but well short of the top eight that sporting director Mateu Alemany named as the target before the Bayern game (Esto es Atleti). Viking at home on November 25 is now close to a must-win.

The break comes at the right time. Valdano put the week down to the physical and emotional cost of two huge games in five days (Esto es Atleti). Cuti Romero is targeting the Getafe trip, and Getafe, Viking and Elche come before the December 2 return to Barcelona. Nine points from those three would make this week look like a blip.

Simeone, Aragonés and the Next Contract

The record that slipped away on Sunday matters less than the deal being drawn up for 2029.

The 2-0 win at Levante on October 31 put Simeone level with Luis Aragonés on La Liga coaching wins for the club (Football España). Beating Barcelona would have given him the record outright. It waits now for Getafe on November 22.

The bigger story is the next contract. Simeone's deal runs to June 2027. Onda Cero's Edu Pidal reported on November 9 that the board has decided to extend it by two seasons, to June 2029 (El Gol Digital). Nothing is signed or announced.

Esto es Atleti added on November 10, without naming a source, that Simeone does not want a three or four year deal and would prefer to go season by season (Esto es Atleti). Treat both reports as direction of travel, not done deals.

Scouting the Next Opponent

Getafe: Unbeatable at Home, Toothless Away

Bordalás's side have not lost at the Coliseum this season and have not scored on the road. Atlético go there first after the break.

Getafe are 15th on 12 points with a goal difference of minus 10, two points above the relegation places (LaLiga). The split is extreme: three wins and three draws at home (8 scored, 4 conceded) and six defeats from six away, with no goals scored and 14 conceded. Atlético visit the half of that record that works.

José Bordalás is still in charge and the identity is familiar: duels, set pieces and a compact, deep block. At Villarreal last weekend he picked Soria; Carvajal, Boselli, Djené, Mojica; Mangala, Gudelj, Andrés García, Mario Martín; Satriano, Ünal, and lost 2-0 to two late goals (LaGrada).

The players to stop

Martín Satriano leads the team in both goals (3) and assists (2) in the league. Enes Ünal has two goals and an assist but is nursing an ankle problem. David Soria is in goal, and Bordalás has admitted his side struggle to string passes together (Al Cabo de la Calle).

Getafe will be without Nemanja Gudelj, suspended after his fifth yellow card (FútbolFantasy). Uche (knee ligaments, out until May 2027) and Juanmi are out, Zaid Romero is a doubt with a quadriceps issue and Borja Mayoral is available again after illness.

Where Atlético can hurt them

Only eight league goals in 12 games suggests Getafe are poorly built to chase a game. An early goal, the thing Atlético managed against Barcelona, changes the whole shape of this fixture. Without Gudelj, their most experienced holding midfielder, there should be more room for Barrios and Koke to turn.

Transfer News

Zubimendi Moves to the Top of the January List

Three outlets report concrete interest in the Arsenal midfielder, and two Atlético players have gone public with their approval.

The newest and biggest thread of the week is Martín Zubimendi. RNE's Silvia Barba reported on November 9 that the Arsenal midfielder is "close" to Atlético and could arrive in the winter window. Relevo's Marcos Durán and AS followed on November 10, both confirming the interest (Sport Witness, Yahoo).

The logic is simple. With Barrios and Cardoso both carrying knocks and Koke on a one-year deal, a senior midfielder is the most widely reported need. Durán described Zubimendi's relationship with Mikel Arteta as "cold". AS says Arsenal would want back what they paid, put at €60m; El Desmarque says €70m.

No bid, fee structure or loan proposal has been reported. A swap involving Julián Álvarez appears only as El Desmarque speculation. Certainty: concrete interest, three outlets.

The dressing room has already voted. Álex Baena told Cadena SER "Me encantaría que viniera" (Sport Witness), and Pablo Barrios told RNE "Martín es un jugadorazo" (Esto es Atleti).

A left-back to push Grimaldo

Marca reported on November 10 that Villarreal's Sergi Cardona, 27, is the main option to compete with Grimaldo (El Desmarque). His contract ends in June 2027, which would keep the price modest. The position has been thin since a late move for Tagliafico was dropped and reserve centre-back Dani Martínez was converted to cover. Certainty: link, single origin (Marca); no contact with Villarreal reported.

Sørloth, Juventus and the outgoing column

Juventus are considering a fresh January approach for Alexander Sørloth, per Gazzetta dello Sport as relayed by ESPN. Atlético reportedly wanted around €40m in the summer, and Esto es Atleti says only a "considerable" offer would be entertained (Esto es Atleti). With no goals yet this season and a quiet hour against Barcelona, his case for staying is not getting stronger. Certainty: link.

On Álvarez, TEAMtalk reported in late October that Atlético have told Arsenal and PSG he is not for sale in January (Football365); that remains single-sourced. Marcos Llorente's renewal is unresolved, and from January 1 he can negotiate freely with other clubs. Nothing new was reported this week.

The Squad

Selection Calls, Sick Notes and a Striker Question

Simeone used the week to test a new right side and a free role for Lee. The break gives Romero and Cardoso time.

Simeone switched between two shapes in five days. Against Bayern he paired Sørloth with Álvarez and played Llorente in midfield. Against Barcelona he moved Llorente to right-back, used Pubill in the middle of defence and gave Lee a free role behind Álvarez (Comuniate). The second version created more.

Barrios told reporters after Bayern that the side missed using Sørloth "arriba y buscar esas segundas jugadas" (Flashscore). Sørloth is still without a goal this season, however, and Lookman was left unused against Barcelona with a change still available (AfricaSoccer).

Form guide

Giuliano Simeone is the man in form: four goals, level with Grimaldo behind Baena's five, and the outstanding Atlético player against Barcelona. Oblak has played every minute of the season (1,440). Le Normand's error decided the Bayern game and he was benched on Sunday; Hancko and Grimaldo had difficult nights against Barcelona's right side.

Jonathan David and Lookman, both bright in the opening weeks, are losing minutes (Esto es Atleti). Pubill leads the squad with five yellow cards.

Availability

Cristian Romero: out since Oct 17 with a muscle problem; not travelling with Argentina, targeting Getafe on Nov 22 (FútbolFantasy).

Johnny Cardoso: returned against Barcelona but has fresh muscle discomfort; withdrawn from the USA squad, individual work at Majadahonda.

Koke: back from a hamstring injury that cost him five matches.

Pablo Barrios: ankle knock against Bayern; trained and joined Spain.

Morten Hjulmand: served his Champions League suspension against Bayern; available.

La Liga suspensions: none.

Off the pitch

Two commercial deals landed this week: a technology agreement with Sportian, the Globant and LaLiga venture, on November 5, and Safeguard Global as official staffing partner for the stadium and the Ciudad del Deporte on November 10 (Esto es Atleti). No values were given. Apollo has held majority control since March, and La Liga set Atlético's 2026-27 squad-cost limit at €361.3m, third in the league (El Gol Digital).

The Academy

Obama Fires the Youth League Side; the Filial Keeps Slipping

A 4-1 win over Bayern's under-19s on the night the first team lost to Bayern, and a painful collapse in Barcelona for Fernando Torres's side.

The Atlético under-19s did what the seniors could not on November 3, beating Bayern 4-1 in the UEFA Youth League at Alcalá de Henares. Joel Sánchez scored his third of the campaign, a Kiala own goal came from a Javi Tena cross, and Gabriel Obama scored twice after the break (Telemadrid).

Obama, 18, now has four Youth League goals in three games (Esto es Atleti). The side have 10 points from four matches. In the División de Honor the Juvenil A beat Sporting Hortaleza 3-2 on November 7 and sit joint second, six points behind Real Madrid.

Atlético Madrileño

Fernando Torres's filial led CE Europa 2-0 at half-time on November 8 through two Marc Domènech goals, then conceded three, the last in the 94th minute (VAVEL). They are 15th of 20 in Primera Federación Group 2 with 11 points and eight games without a win. Domènech has six goals. Teruel visit on Sunday.

Academy graduates keep knocking on the first-team door. Arnau Ortiz was on the bench for both Bayern and Barcelona, and Jorge Domínguez, Atlético's youngest league debutant in August at 16, is with Spain's under-17s this week.

Atlético Femenino

Clean Sheets at Home, Third Place Restored

David González's side beat Badalona 3-0 and remain perfect at Alcalá de Henares.

Atlético de Madrid Femenino beat Badalona Women 3-0 on Monday night to climb back to third in Liga F (VAVEL). Synne Jensen opened the scoring on 62 minutes, Viola Calligaris headed in a free kick on 69, and substitute Kathrine Kühl added a third in stoppage time.

Rosa Otermín, the full-back who was pressed into emergency goalkeeping duty in October, was player of the match and supplied the free kick for Calligaris. Jenni Hermoso came off the bench, had a goal ruled out for offside and hit the post.

Where they stand

Atlético have 19 points from 10 games, eight behind leaders Barcelona and four behind Real Madrid, with Madrid CFF two points back in fourth (dsn.pe). Their three defeats have all come away to the sides around them: Real Madrid, Barcelona and Athletic. At home they have won five from five without conceding, per OneFootball's count.

David González, appointed in June from Bolivia's Oriente Petrolero in his first women's job (Liga F), has no European football to manage this season. Next up is Sevilla away on Sunday, then Eibar on November 21.

Lamine Yamal publicly congratulated Barrios on the call-up (El Nacional).
